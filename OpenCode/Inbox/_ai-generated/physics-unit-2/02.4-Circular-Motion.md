# 2.4 Circular Motion

**Syllabus:** GCE A/L Physics (022) — Unit 2, Topic 2.4

## Definition

Circular motion requires a centripetal force directed toward the centre. Even at constant speed, the changing direction means acceleration exists.

## Key Formulas

| Formula | Variables |
|---------|-----------|
| $a_c = \frac{v^2}{r}$ | Centripetal acceleration |
| $F_c = \frac{mv^2}{r}$ | Centripetal force |
| $v = r\omega$ | Linear-angular velocity relation |
| $T = \frac{2\pi r}{v}$ | Period |

## Worked Example

A 0.5 kg ball on 1 m string swings at 3 m/s. Find tension.

$$T = \frac{mv^2}{r} = \frac{0.5 \times 9}{1} = 4.5\ \text{N}$$

## Common Mistakes

- ❌ Calling centripetal force a "new" force (it's the net force)
- ❌ Forgetting centripetal force is always inward
- ❌ Using $F = ma$ without specifying $a = a_c$
