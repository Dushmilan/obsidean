# Second Law & Entropy

## Definition

Heat flows spontaneously from hot to cold; entropy never decreases in an isolated system:

$$\Delta S_{total} \ge 0 \quad (\text{= only for reversible processes})$$

$$\Delta S = \frac{Q}{T}, \qquad e_{max} = 1 - \frac{T_C}{T_H} \ (\text{Carnot})$$

## The Intuition

Drop an ice cube into hot coffee — it melts. The reverse (coffee spontaneously freezing while heating the air) *could* happen, but the probability is $\sim 10^{-23}$ — you'd wait longer than the age of the universe. The second law is why no perpetual motion machine exists.

## The Physics

| Formula | Meaning |
|---------|---------|
| $\Delta S = \frac{Q}{T}$ | entropy change (J/K) |
| $\Delta S_{total} \ge 0$ | entropy principle |
| $e_{max} = 1 - \frac{T_C}{T_H}$ | Carnot efficiency |

## Derivation

Carnot's theorem: no engine between $T_H$ and $T_C$ beats the reversible Carnot cycle; efficiency $1 - T_C/T_H$ follows from $\Delta S = 0$ around the cycle. Entropy is the state variable whose change equals $Q/T$ for reversible processes. [Full derivations: [[Maths/Pure/04-Calculus/04-Integration/04.4-Integration]]]

## Method

1. Efficiency problems: $e = 1 - \frac{T_C}{T_H}$ with temperatures in Kelvin.
2. Engine: $W = eQ_H$, $Q_C = Q_H - W$.
3. Entropy: $\Delta S = Q/T$ for isothermal transfers; sum over parts for the total.

## Worked Examples

**Setup:** A Carnot engine between 600 K and 300 K. Max efficiency?

**Solution:** $e = 1 - \frac{300}{600} = 50\%$.

**Key insight:** Half the heat becomes work at best — the rest *must* go to the cold reservoir. A law, not a design flaw.

---

**Setup:** Carnot engine 500 K → 300 K absorbs 1000 J. Find $W$, $Q_C$, $e$.

**Solution:** $e = 0.4$; $W = 400$ J; $Q_C = 1000 - 400 = 600$ J.

**Key insight:** Improve efficiency by raising $T_H$ or lowering $T_C$ — why power plants use superheated steam.

## Common Traps

- Temperatures in Kelvin for the Carnot ratio
- $\Delta S \ge 0$ applies to the *total* (system + surroundings), not a subsystem
- Reversible processes are idealisations — real ones always increase entropy
- Confusing "energy conserved" (1st law) with "usable energy decreases" (2nd law)
