# 3.5 Second Law of Thermodynamics

The second law is nature's one-way sign. Heat flows from hot to cold, never the reverse — not because it *can't* go backward, but because the odds are so astronomically stacked against it that it never will. Entropy, the measure of disorder, always increases in isolated systems. This law defines the arrow of time itself.

**The Intuition:** Drop an ice cube into hot coffee. It melts. Now try the reverse — a warm coffee spontaneously freezing while heating the air around it. You've never seen that happen. The second law says it *could* happen, but the probability is so small ($\sim 10^{-23}$) that you'd wait longer than the age of the universe.

**The Math:** Carnot efficiency gives the maximum: $e_{max} = 1 - \frac{T_C}{T_H}$. Entropy change is $\Delta S = \frac{Q}{T}$, and for any real process $\Delta S_{total} \geq 0$. The equality holds only for reversible processes — idealized limits that real systems never quite reach.

| Formula | Variables |
|---------|-----------|
| $e_{max} = 1 - \frac{T_C}{T_H}$ | Carnot efficiency |
| $\Delta S = \frac{Q}{T}$ | Entropy change |
| $\Delta S_{total} \geq 0$ | Entropy principle |

**What does this mean for Physics?** The second law is the reason we can't build a perpetual motion machine. It sets the fundamental limit on [[03-Thermal-Physics/03.4-Heat-Transfer|heat engine]] efficiency: no engine operating between $T_H$ and $T_C$ can exceed $1 - \frac{T_C}{T_H}$. It also explains why [[03-Thermal-Physics/03.3-First-Law|energy conservation]] alone isn't enough — you can have energy in abundance but still be unable to use it if the entropy cost is too high.

---

**Setup:** A Carnot engine operates between 600 K and 300 K. Find its maximum efficiency.

**Solution:** $e = 1 - \frac{300}{600} = 0.5 = 50\%$.

**Key insight:** Half the heat energy becomes work at best — the other half *must* be dumped to the cold reservoir. This isn't an engineering limitation; it's a law of physics. Every real engine does worse, which is why [[03-Thermal-Physics/03.2-Gas-Laws|gas law]] optimization and [[03-Thermal-Physics/03.1-Temperature-Heat|temperature]] management matter so much in practice.

---

**Setup:** A Carnot engine operates between 500 K and 300 K. If it absorbs 1000 J from the hot reservoir, find work done, heat rejected, and efficiency.

**Solution:**
- Efficiency: $e = 1 - \frac{T_C}{T_H} = 1 - \frac{300}{500} = 0.4 = 40\%$.
- Work done: $W = e \times Q_H = 0.4 \times 1000 = 400$ J.
- Heat rejected: $Q_C = Q_H - W = 1000 - 400 = 600$ J.

**Key insight:** Only 40% of absorbed heat becomes useful work. The remaining 60% must be expelled to the cold reservoir — this is unavoidable, not a design flaw. Improving efficiency means raising $T_H$ or lowering $T_C$, which is why power plants use superheated steam.

---

**Setup:** 2 mol of ideal gas at 300 K expands isothermally from 0.01 m³ to 0.03 m³. Find the entropy change of the gas and the total entropy change if the heat comes from a reservoir at 300 K.

**Solution:**
- Entropy change of gas: $\Delta S_{gas} = nR\ln\frac{V_2}{V_1} = 2 \times 8.31 \times \ln 3 = 18.26$ J/K.
- Heat absorbed: $Q = nRT\ln\frac{V_2}{V_1} = 2 \times 8.31 \times 300 \times 1.099 = 5{,}480$ J.
- Entropy change of reservoir: $\Delta S_{res} = -\frac{Q}{T} = -\frac{5{,}480}{300} = -18.27$ J/K.
- Total entropy change: $\Delta S_{total} = 18.26 - 18.27 \approx 0$ J/K.

**Key insight:** The total entropy change is zero — this is a reversible process. For any *real* (irreversible) expansion, the total entropy change would be positive. The second law is satisfied: $\Delta S_{total} \geq 0$, with equality only for idealized reversible processes.

## Common Mistakes

- ❌ Forgetting that entropy is a state function ($\Delta S$ depends only on endpoints)
- ❌ Confusing Carnot efficiency (reversible) with real engine efficiency
- ❌ Using $\Delta S = Q/T$ only for reversible processes or constant temperature
- ❌ Forgetting that $\Delta S_{total} \geq 0$ for all real processes
