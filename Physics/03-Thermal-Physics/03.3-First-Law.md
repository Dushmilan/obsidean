# First Law of Thermodynamics

## Definition

Energy conservation for thermodynamic systems:

$$\Delta U = Q - W$$

$U$ = internal energy, $Q$ = heat added, $W$ = work done *by* the system. With the sign convention: heat in is positive; work out is positive.

| Process | Condition | $\Delta U$ | $Q$ | $W$ |
|---------|-----------|------------|-----|-----|
| Isothermal | $T$ const | 0 | $W$ | $nRT\ln\frac{V_2}{V_1}$ |
| Isochoric | $V$ const | $nC_v\Delta T$ | $\Delta U$ | 0 |
| Isobaric | $p$ const | $nC_v\Delta T$ | $nC_p\Delta T$ | $p\Delta V$ |

## The Intuition

A bank account: heat $Q$ is a deposit, work $W$ a withdrawal, internal energy $U$ the balance. The first law says balance changes by what you put in minus what you spend — you can't spend money you never deposited. Compressing a gas heats it: the work you do has to go somewhere.

## The Physics

| Quantity | Equation | Units |
|----------|----------|-------|
| First law | $\Delta U = Q - W$ | J |
| Work (const. $p$) | $W = p\Delta V$ | J |
| Work (isothermal) | $W = nRT\ln\frac{V_2}{V_1}$ | J |
| Heat capacities | $C_p = C_v + R$ | J/(mol·K) |

## Derivation

The first law is a statement of energy conservation; it defines $U$ up to an additive constant. Isothermal work follows from $W = \int p\,dV$ with $pV = nRT$. $C_p = C_v + R$ because at constant pressure, heat goes into internal energy *and* expansion work. [Full derivations: [[Maths/Pure/04-Calculus/04-Integration/04.4-Integration]]]

## Method

1. Identify the process type → pick the process row.
2. Compute $\Delta U$, $Q$, $W$ from the knowns.
3. Verify $\Delta U = Q - W$ as a sanity check.

## Worked Examples

**Setup:** 2 mol expands isothermally at 300 K from 0.01 to 0.03 m³. Find $W$.

**Solution:** $W = nRT\ln\frac{V_2}{V_1} = 2\times8.31\times300\times\ln3 = 5{,}480$ J.

**Key insight:** With $\Delta U = 0$, all heat added becomes work output — but maintaining $T$ constant needs a reservoir.

---

**Setup:** 1 mol heated 300 → 400 K at constant volume ($C_v = 20.8$ J/(mol·K)).

**Solution:** $W = 0$; $\Delta U = 20.8\times100 = 2{,}080$ J; $Q = \Delta U = 2{,}080$ J.

**Key insight:** At constant volume all heat goes to internal energy — why a pressure cooker heats food faster.

## Common Traps

- Sign of $W$ — work done *by* vs *on* the system
- $\Delta U = 0$ only for isothermal ideal gas
- Using $C_p$ where $C_v$ belongs (and vice versa)
- Forgetting $p\Delta V$ only for isobaric processes
