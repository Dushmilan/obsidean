# Kinetic Theory of Gases

## Definition

Explains macroscopic gas behaviour from molecular motion:

| Quantity | Formula |
|----------|---------|
| Pressure (microscopic) | $pV = \frac13 Nm\overline{c^2}$ |
| Mean KE per molecule | $\overline{KE} = \frac32 kT$ |
| RMS speed | $c_{rms} = \sqrt{\frac{3kT}{m}} = \sqrt{\frac{3RT}{M}}$ |
| Mean speed | $\bar{c} = \sqrt{\frac{8kT}{\pi m}}$ |
| Most probable speed | $c_{mp} = \sqrt{\frac{2kT}{m}}$ |
| Molar heat capacity | $C_V = \frac{f}{2}R$, $C_p = C_V + R$ |
| Adiabatic index | $\gamma = \frac{C_p}{C_v} = \frac{f+2}{f}$ |

$k = 1.38\times10^{-23}$ J/K; $f$ = degrees of freedom (monatomic 3, diatomic 5 at room temp).

## The Intuition

A box of tiny bouncing balls: each molecule zips in straight lines until it hits a wall or another molecule. Temperature controls the speeds; pressure is the cumulative effect of millions of collisions per second. You don't need any single path — only the statistical distribution of speeds.

## The Physics

| Gas type | $f$ | $C_V$ | $\gamma$ |
|----------|-----|-------|----------|
| Monatomic (He, Ne) | 3 | $\frac32 R$ | $\frac53$ |
| Diatomic (N₂, O₂) | 5 | $\frac52 R$ | $\frac75$ |

## Derivation

$pV = \frac13 Nm\overline{c^2}$ from momentum transfer of collisions (Newton's laws per molecule, averaged). Equating with the ideal gas law gives $\overline{KE} = \frac32 kT$ — temperature *is* average molecular kinetic energy. Heat capacities follow from the equipartition theorem ($\frac12 kT$ per degree of freedom). [Full derivations: [[03.2-Gas-Laws]]]

## Method

1. Choose the speed formula by what's given ($M$ vs $m$, $R$ vs $k$).
2. Read $f$ from the gas type → $C_V$, $\gamma$.
3. Link macroscopic ($pV = nRT$) and microscopic ($\frac13 Nm\overline{c^2}$) forms.

## Worked Examples

**Setup:** RMS speed of oxygen ($M = 0.032$ kg/mol) at 300 K.

**Solution:** $c_{rms} = \sqrt{\frac{3RT}{M}} = \sqrt{\frac{3\times8.31\times300}{0.032}} \approx 483$ m/s.

**Key insight:** ~1.4× the speed of sound — sound propagation depends on molecular collisions.

---

**Setup:** Find $\gamma$ for a diatomic gas.

**Solution:** $f = 5$: $C_V = \frac52 R$, $C_p = \frac72 R$, $\gamma = \frac{7/2}{5/2} = 1.4$.

**Key insight:** $\gamma$ governs adiabatic processes — $pV^\gamma = \text{const}$.

## Common Traps

- $M$ (molar, kg/mol) vs $m$ (single molecule, kg) in the speed formulas
- Degrees of freedom change with temperature (vibration kicks in hot)
- $pV = \frac13 Nm\overline{c^2}$ uses mean *square* speed, not mean speed
- Forgetting $\bar{c} \neq c_{rms} \neq c_{mp}$ — three distinct speeds
