# Gas Laws

## Definition

**Ideal gas law:** $pV = nRT$ — pressure, volume, amount, absolute temperature.

- $R = 8.31$ J/(mol·K) · $p$ in Pa, $V$ in m³, $T$ in **Kelvin**.
- **Combined law:** $\frac{p_1V_1}{T_1} = \frac{p_2V_2}{T_2}$.
- **Kinetic theory links:** $pV = \frac13 Nm\overline{c^2}$ and $\overline{KE} = \frac32 kT$, $k = 1.38\times10^{-23}$ J/K.

## The Intuition

A gas is a swarm of particles bouncing off walls and each other. Pressure is millions of collisions per second; temperature controls how fast the particles move; volume controls their space. The ideal gas law is accurate at low pressure and high temperature (molecules far apart, negligible interactions).

## The Physics

| Law | Equation | Constant |
|-----|----------|----------|
| Boyle | $p_1V_1 = p_2V_2$ | $T, n$ |
| Charles | $\frac{V_1}{T_1} = \frac{V_2}{T_2}$ | $p, n$ |
| Pressure law | $\frac{p_1}{T_1} = \frac{p_2}{T_2}$ | $V, n$ |
| Ideal gas | $pV = nRT$ | — |
| RMS speed | $v_{rms} = \sqrt{\frac{3RT}{M}}$ | — |

## Derivation

Kinetic theory derives $pV = \frac13 Nm\overline{c^2}$ from momentum transfer of molecular collisions; combining with $\overline{KE} = \frac32 kT$ yields $pV = NkT = nRT$. [Full derivations: [[03.6-Kinetic-Theory]]]

## Method

1. Use the ideal gas law for one state; the combined law for state changes.
2. Identify the constant in a process (isothermal/isochoric/isobaric) and pick the sub-law.
3. Always convert temperature to Kelvin.

## Worked Examples

**Setup:** 2 mol at 300 K in 0.05 m³. Find $p$.

**Solution:** $p = \frac{nRT}{V} = \frac{2\times8.31\times300}{0.05} \approx 100$ kPa.

**Key insight:** Room-temperature gas at reasonable density behaves like the air around you.

---

**Setup:** Gas at 300 K, 2 atm compressed isothermally to half volume, then heated at constant volume to 600 K.

**Solution:** Isothermal: $p_2 = 2\times2 = 4$ atm. Then $\frac{p_3}{T_3} = \frac{p_2}{T_2}$: $p_3 = 4\times\frac{600}{300} = 8$ atm.

**Key insight:** Pressure scales with temperature at fixed volume — why tires firm up on long drives.

## Common Traps

- Using °C in gas-law ratios — always Kelvin
- Forgetting $R$'s units force Pa and m³
- Applying Boyle where temperature changes
- $v_{rms} = \sqrt{3RT/M}$ — $M$ in kg/mol
