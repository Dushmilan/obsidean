# Heat Transfer

## Definition

Three mechanisms move heat:

- **Conduction:** $\dfrac{Q}{t} = \dfrac{kA\Delta T}{d}$ — molecular relay, needs contact.
- **Convection:** heat carried by bulk fluid motion (currents).
- **Radiation:** $P = \epsilon\sigma AT^4$ — waves, no medium needed ($\sigma = 5.67\times10^{-8}$ W/(m²·K⁴)).

**Carnot efficiency:** $e = 1 - \frac{T_{cold}}{T_{hot}}$ — the theoretical ceiling for any heat engine.

## The Intuition

Conduction is a relay race — molecules pass the "baton" hand-to-hand. Convection is a crowd forming a current, carrying warmth with the flow. Radiation is a shout across an empty room — energy travels as waves through the void.

## The Physics

| Mode | Equation | Notes |
|------|----------|-------|
| Conduction | $\frac{Q}{t} = \frac{kA\Delta T}{d}$ | steady state |
| Radiation | $P = \epsilon\sigma AT^4$ | $T^4$ — very sensitive |
| Carnot efficiency | $e = 1 - \frac{T_C}{T_H}$ | max possible |

## Derivation

Conduction is Fourier's law ($q = -k\,dT/dx$) for a uniform slab. Radiation is Stefan–Boltzmann, an empirical law with a $T^4$ dependence from statistical mechanics. Carnot efficiency follows from the second law and the definition of absolute temperature. [Full derivations: [[03.5-Second-Law]]]

## Method

1. Choose the mechanism (contact? fluid flow? vacuum?).
2. Conduction: identify $k, A, \Delta T, d$; radiation: $T$ in Kelvin.
3. Efficiency: always $T_C/T_H$ in Kelvin; compare to the Carnot ceiling.

## Worked Examples

**Setup:** A metal plate ($k = 200$ W/m·K, $A = 0.1$ m², $d = 0.02$ m) with $\Delta T = 60$ K. Heat flow?

**Solution:** $\frac{Q}{t} = \frac{200\times0.1\times60}{0.02} = 60$ kW.

**Key insight:** High conductivity transfers huge heat from a modest temperature difference.

---

**Setup:** A black body at 2000 K, $A = 0.01$ m². Power? At 4000 K?

**Solution:** $P_{2000} = 5.67\times10^{-8}\times0.01\times(2000)^4 \approx 9.1$ kW. $P_{4000} = \times2^4 = 16\times \approx 145$ kW.

**Key insight:** Doubling temperature ×16 the power — the $T^4$ law is dramatic.

## Common Traps

- Radiation needs $T$ in Kelvin, not Celsius
- Conduction formula assumes steady state and uniform slab
- $T^4$ vs $T$ — hot objects radiate enormously more
- Carnot efficiency is a ceiling — real engines do worse
