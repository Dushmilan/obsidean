# 1.1 SI Base Units — Derivations

**Syllabus:** GCE A/L Physics (022) — Unit 1, Topic 1.1

---

## 1. Definition of the Metre (Length)

**Current definition (1983, reaffirmed 2019):**
> The metre is the length of the path travelled by light in vacuum during a time interval of $1/299\,792\,458$ of a second.

**Derivation from $c$:**
$$c = 299\,792\,458\ \text{m/s} \quad \text{(exact)}$$
$$1\ \text{m} = c \times \frac{1\ \text{s}}{299\,792\,458}$$

**Historical definitions:**
- 1793: $10^{-7}$ of quadrant of Earth's meridian
- 1889: International prototype metre bar (Pt-Ir alloy)
- 1960: $1\,650\,763.73$ wavelengths of Kr-86 orange-red line

---

## 2. Definition of the Kilogram (Mass)

**Current definition (2019):**
> The kilogram is defined by fixing the numerical value of the Planck constant $h$ to be $6.626\,070\,15 \times 10^{-34}$ J·s.

**Derivation from $h$:**
$$h = 6.626\,070\,15 \times 10^{-34}\ \text{kg·m}^2\text{/s} \quad \text{(exact)}$$
$$\text{Since } c \text{ and } \Delta\nu_{\text{Cs}} \text{ are exact, kg is defined via } h$$

**Kibble balance relation:**
$$mg = \frac{h f^2}{c^2} \quad \text{(mechanical power = electrical power)}$$
$$m = \frac{h f^2}{c^2 g} \frac{I V}{f} \quad \text{(in practice via Josephson + quantum Hall effects)}$$

**Historical:** International Prototype Kilogram (IPK), Pt-Ir cylinder, 1889–2019

---

## 3. Definition of the Second (Time)

**Current definition (1967, reaffirmed 2019):**
> The second is the duration of $9\,192\,631\,770$ periods of the radiation corresponding to the transition between the two hyperfine levels of the ground state of the caesium-133 atom.

**Derivation:**
$$\Delta\nu_{\text{Cs}} = 9\,192\,631\,770\ \text{Hz} \quad \text{(exact)}$$
$$1\ \text{s} = \frac{1}{\Delta\nu_{\text{Cs}}} \times 9\,192\,631\,770\ \text{periods}$$

**Uncertainty:** Cs fountain clocks $\sim 10^{-16}$ relative uncertainty

---

## 4. Definition of the Ampere (Current)

**Current definition (2019):**
> The ampere is defined by fixing the numerical value of the elementary charge $e$ to be $1.602\,176\,634 \times 10^{-19}$ C.

**Derivation from $e$:**
$$e = 1.602\,176\,634 \times 10^{-19}\ \text{C} \quad \text{(exact)}$$
$$1\ \text{A} = 1\ \text{C/s} = \frac{e}{\text{s}} \times \frac{1}{1.602\,176\,634 \times 10^{-19}}$$

**Practical realisation:** Via Josephson effect ($K_J = 2e/h$) and quantum Hall effect ($R_K = h/e^2$)

**Historical:** Force between parallel conductors: $F/L = \mu_0 I^2 / 2\pi d$

---

## 5. Definition of the Kelvin (Temperature)

**Current definition (2019):**
> The kelvin is defined by fixing the numerical value of the Boltzmann constant $k$ to be $1.380\,649 \times 10^{-23}$ J/K.

**Derivation from $k$:**
$$k = 1.380\,649 \times 10^{-23}\ \text{J/K} \quad \text{(exact)}$$
$$\text{Thermal energy } E = kT \Rightarrow T = \frac{E}{k}$$

**Triple point of water:** $T_{\text{tpw}} = 273.16\ \text{K}$ (exactly, by definition of Kelvin scale)

**Historical:** Triple point of water = 273.16 K (1954–2019)

---

## 6. Definition of the Mole (Amount of Substance)

**Current definition (2019):**
> The mole is defined by fixing the numerical value of the Avogadro constant $N_A$ to be $6.022\,140\,76 \times 10^{23}$ mol$^{-1}$.

**Derivation from $N_A$:**
$$N_A = 6.022\,140\,76 \times 10^{23}\ \text{mol}^{-1} \quad \text{(exact)}$$
$$1\ \text{mol} = N_A \text{ entities}$$

**Connection to mass:** Molar mass constant $M_u = \frac{m_u N_A}{1\ \text{kg/mol}}$ (no longer exactly 1 g/mol)

**Historical:** Amount of substance in 12 g of C-12 (1971–2019)

---

## 7. Definition of the Candela (Luminous Intensity)

**Current definition (1979, reaffirmed 2019):**
> The candela is the luminous intensity, in a given direction, of a source that emits monochromatic radiation of frequency $540 \times 10^{12}$ Hz and that has a radiant intensity in that direction of $1/683$ W/sr.

**Derivation from $K_{\text{cd}}$:**
$$K_{\text{cd}} = 683\ \text{lm/W} \quad \text{(exact)}$$
$$1\ \text{cd} = \frac{1}{683}\ \text{W/sr} \text{ at } 540\ \text{THz}$$

**Photometric relation:** $I_v = K_{\text{cd}} \cdot I_e \cdot V(\lambda)$ where $V(\lambda)$ is luminous efficiency function

---

## 8. Coherence of the SI System

**All 7 base units now defined by fixing constants:**

| Constant | Symbol | Exact Value | Defines |
|----------|--------|-------------|---------|
| Hyperfine transition | $\Delta\nu_{\text{Cs}}$ | $9\,192\,631\,770\ \text{Hz}$ | s |
| Speed of light | $c$ | $299\,792\,458\ \text{m/s}$ | m |
| Planck constant | $h$ | $6.626\,070\,15 \times 10^{-34}\ \text{J·s}$ | kg |
| Elementary charge | $e$ | $1.602\,176\,634 \times 10^{-19}\ \text{C}$ | A |
| Boltzmann constant | $k$ | $1.380\,649 \times 10^{-23}\ \text{J/K}$ | K |
| Avogadro constant | $N_A$ | $6.022\,140\,76 \times 10^{23}\ \text{mol}^{-1}$ | mol |
| Luminous efficacy | $K_{\text{cd}}$ | $683\ \text{lm/W}$ | cd |

**Coherence check:** All derived units obtained by multiplication/division of base units without numerical factors (except definitions).

## 9. Derived Unit Definitions (from Base Units)

### Force (Newton)
$$[F] = [ma] = \text{kg·m·s}^{-2} \Rightarrow 1\ \text{N} = 1\ \text{kg·m/s}^2$$

### Energy (Joule)
$$[E] = [Fs] = \text{kg·m}^2\text{s}^{-2} \Rightarrow 1\ \text{J} = 1\ \text{N·m} = 1\ \text{kg·m}^2/\text{s}^2$$

### Power (Watt)
$$[P] = [E/t] = \text{kg·m}^2\text{s}^{-3} \Rightarrow 1\ \text{W} = 1\ \text{J/s}$$

### Pressure (Pascal)
$$[p] = [F/A] = \text{kg·m}^{-1}\text{s}^{-2} \Rightarrow 1\ \text{Pa} = 1\ \text{N/m}^2$$

### Charge (Coulomb)
$$[q] = [It] = \text{A·s} \Rightarrow 1\ \text{C} = 1\ \text{A·s}$$

### Voltage (Volt)
$$[V] = [E/q] = \text{kg·m}^2\text{s}^{-3}\text{A}^{-1} \Rightarrow 1\ \text{V} = 1\ \text{J/C}$$

### Resistance (Ohm)
$$[R] = [V/I] = \text{kg·m}^2\text{s}^{-3}\text{A}^{-2} \Rightarrow 1\ \Omega = 1\ \text{V/A}$$

### Capacitance (Farad)
$$[C] = [q/V] = \text{kg}^{-1}\text{m}^{-2}\text{s}^4\text{A}^2 \Rightarrow 1\ \text{F} = 1\ \text{C/V}$$
