# 7.5 Digital Systems

Digital systems are where analog physics meets discrete computation. An ADC takes a continuous voltage (like a microphone signal) and chops it into numbered steps. A DAC does the reverse — it reconstructs a smooth signal from digital numbers. A microcontroller ties it all together: CPU, memory, and I/O on a single chip, running billions of instructions per second.

**The Intuition:** Imagine measuring water level with a ruler marked in millimeters. The real level is continuous, but your reading is always a discrete mark — maybe 34 mm or 35 mm, never 34.7 mm. That's quantization error. An $n$-bit ADC divides the input range into $2^n$ levels, and each reading snaps to the nearest level. More bits = finer steps = less error, but each bit doubles the hardware cost.

**The Math:** The resolution of an $n$-bit ADC or DAC is $\frac{V_{ref}}{2^n}$, where $V_{ref}$ is the reference voltage. The quantization error is $\pm \frac{1}{2} \text{LSB}$ (least significant bit) — you're always off by at most half a step. The conversion time depends on the architecture: successive approximation takes $n$ clock cycles ($t_{conv} = \frac{1}{f_{clk}}$ per bit), flash is instant (one cycle) but requires $2^n - 1$ comparators, and dual-slope trades speed for noise immunity.

**What does this mean for Physics?** The Nyquist-Shannon theorem sets a fundamental limit: you must sample at least twice the highest frequency component to faithfully reconstruct a signal. This connects discrete-time sampling to continuous-wave physics. The resolution error is fundamentally quantum-like — it's an irreducible uncertainty from discretizing a continuous world.

| ADC Type | Speed | Complexity |
|----------|-------|------------|
| Successive approximation | Medium | Medium |
| Flash | Fast | High ($2^n - 1$ comparators) |
| Dual-slope | Slow | Low (noise-immune) |

**Setup:** A 10-bit ADC has a reference voltage of $V_{ref} = 5$ V. Find the resolution and the quantization error.

**Solution:** Resolution $= \frac{5}{2^{10}} = \frac{5}{1024} = 4.88\ \text{mV}$. The quantization error is $\pm \frac{1}{2} \text{LSB} = \pm 2.44\ \text{mV}$. Any input voltage is measured to within $\pm 2.44$ mV of its true value.

**Key insight:** Doubling the resolution (adding one bit) halves the quantization error but doubles the number of digital levels. An 8-bit ADC gives 256 levels; a 16-bit gives 65,536. The cost-benefit tradeoff drives every measurement system design.
