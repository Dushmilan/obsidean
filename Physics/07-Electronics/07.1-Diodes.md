# Diodes

## Definition

A diode conducts current in one direction only. **Silicon forward voltage:** ~0.7 V (a property of the bandgap, nearly constant for reasonable currents). Reverse-biased, it blocks until breakdown.

| Type | Symbol | Use |
|------|--------|-----|
| Standard | → | rectification |
| Zener | → with bar | voltage regulation (reverse breakdown) |
| LED | → with arrows out | display, lighting |
| Photodiode | → with arrows in | light detection |

## The Intuition

A one-way valve for electrons. Above 0.7 V forward it conducts freely; below, it blocks. That fixed 0.7 V drop makes diode circuits easy to analyse — the "fixed-drop model" lets you treat a conducting diode as a 0.7 V battery.

## The Physics

| Quantity | Equation / value | Notes |
|----------|------------------|-------|
| Forward drop | $V_D \approx 0.7$ V | silicon |
| Forward current | $I \approx I_0(e^{V/V_T} - 1)$ | exponential (diode equation) |
| Zener breakdown | $V_Z$ | reverse, regulating |
| Half-wave rectifier | $V_{out} = V_{in} - 0.7$ (during conduction) | — |

## Derivation

The diode equation $I = I_0(e^{V/nV_T}-1)$ comes from the Boltzmann statistics of charge carriers across the junction. The 0.7 V is $V_T\ln$ of a typical current ratio — hence nearly constant. [Full derivations: [[Maths/Pure/01-Algebra/03-Logarithms/03-Logarithms]]]

## Method

1. Assume a state: conducting (0.7 V drop) or blocking (no current).
2. Solve the circuit; check the assumption is consistent.
3. Zener: in reverse breakdown it holds $V = V_Z$ — a voltage regulator.

## Worked Examples

**Setup:** A diode + 5 V supply + 1 kΩ resistor. Find the current.

**Solution:** Conducting: $I = (5 - 0.7)/1000 = 4.3$ mA.

**Key insight:** Subtract the fixed 0.7 V drop, then Ohm's law — that's the whole fixed-drop model.

---

**Setup:** A Zener with $V_Z = 5.1$ V regulates a varying input $6$–$12$ V.

**Solution:** The Zener clamps the load to 5.1 V regardless of input — excess voltage drops across the series resistor.

**Key insight:** Regulation happens in reverse breakdown — the diode's "wrong-way" mode.

## Common Traps

- Forgetting the 0.7 V drop (silicon) or 0.3 V (germanium)
- Reverse-biased diodes are *not* perfect open circuits — tiny leakage exists
- LED forward voltage is ~2 V, not 0.7
- Zener conducts in reverse — don't block it as "off"
