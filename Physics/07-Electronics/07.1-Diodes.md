# 7.1 Diodes

A diode is the simplest semiconductor device — it lets current flow in one direction and blocks it the other way. Think of it as a one-way valve for electricity. The PN junction is the heart of nearly every semiconductor, from the rectifier in your phone charger to the tiny light-emitting pixels on your screen.

**The Intuition:** Imagine a turnstile at a stadium. People (electrons) can walk through freely in one direction, but the gate locks if they try to go backwards. That's exactly what a PN junction does — the junction between p-type and n-type silicon creates a built-in electric field that preferentially allows carrier flow one way. The 0.7 V forward drop is the "cost of admission" — you need to push hard enough to overcome the depletion region before current starts flowing.

**The Math:** The diode's current-voltage relationship is governed by the Shockley equation: $I = I_0(e^{V/V_T} - 1)$. Here $V_T = \frac{kT}{e} \approx 26\ \text{mV}$ at room temperature is the thermal voltage — the average kinetic energy of carriers expressed in electron-volts. The saturation current $I_0$ (typically $\sim 10^{-12}$ A for silicon) depends on the semiconductor material and junction area. For a half-wave rectifier, the average output voltage is $V_{avg} = \frac{2V_0}{\pi}$, where $V_0$ is the peak AC input.

**What does this mean for Physics?** The diode equation emerges directly from statistical mechanics — the exponential term is a Boltzmann distribution of carrier energies. The thermal voltage $V_T = \frac{kT}{e}$ connects thermodynamic temperature to electrical potential, showing that electronics is fundamentally thermal physics at the quantum scale.

**Setup:** A silicon diode ($V_f \approx 0.7$ V) is in series with a $100\ \Omega$ resistor and a $5$ V DC supply. Find the current through the circuit.

**Solution:** Apply KVL: $V_{supply} = V_D + IR$. So $I = \frac{5 - 0.7}{100} = \frac{4.3}{100} = 43\ \text{mA}$.

**Key insight:** The 0.7 V drop is essentially constant for any reasonable current — it's a property of the silicon bandgap, not the circuit. This makes diode circuits easy to analyze once you internalize the fixed-drop model.
