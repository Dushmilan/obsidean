# Transistors

## Definition

A three-terminal semiconductor switch/amplifier. **BJT** (bipolar): base, collector, emitter — collector current controlled by base current:

$$I_C = \beta I_B, \qquad I_E = I_C + I_B \approx I_C$$

**FET/MOSFET:** gate, drain, source — controlled by gate *voltage* (transconductance $g_m$).

## The Intuition

A tap: a tiny turn of the handle (base/gate signal) controls a huge flow (collector/drain current). The transistor is a current (or voltage) amplifier — a microamp signal controls a milliamp load. That's the principle behind amplification, switching, and digital logic.

## The Physics

| Quantity | Equation | Notes |
|----------|----------|-------|
| BJT gain | $I_C = \beta I_B$ | current-controlled |
| Emitter current | $I_E = I_C + I_B$ | ≈ $I_C$ |
| MOSFET | $I_D = g_m V_{GS}$ (linear region) | voltage-controlled |
| Modes | cutoff / active / saturation | switch vs amplifier |

## Derivation

The BJT relation $I_C = \beta I_B$ follows from the transistor's internal current balance — most injected carriers reach the collector ($\beta$ typically 50–300). [Full derivations: [[07.1-Diodes]] — the junction physics]

## Method

1. Identify mode: cutoff ($I_B = 0$, no current), active (amplifier, $I_C = \beta I_B$), saturation (switch on).
2. BJT: given $I_B$, $I_C = \beta I_B$.
3. MOSFET: use $g_m$ for voltage-control problems.

## Worked Examples

**Setup:** A BJT with $\beta = 100$, $I_B = 20\ \mu$A. Collector current?

**Solution:** $I_C = 100\times20\times10^{-6} = 2$ mA; $I_E = 2.02$ mA ≈ $I_C$.

**Key insight:** A microamp controls a milliamp — amplification in one step.

---

**Setup:** A transistor switch drives a relay coil needing 50 mA, $\beta = 100$. Minimum base current?

**Solution:** $I_B = I_C/\beta = 50/100 = 0.5$ mA.

**Key insight:** Saturation means the collector can't rise further — the transistor is a closed switch.

## Common Traps

- $\beta$ varies with temperature and device — circuits must tolerate it
- BJT is current-controlled; FET is voltage-controlled — don't swap
- Active vs saturation mode confusion (amplifier vs switch)
- $I_E \approx I_C$ is an approximation (true when $I_B \ll I_C$)
