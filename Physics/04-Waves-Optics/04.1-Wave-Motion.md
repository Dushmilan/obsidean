# Wave Motion

## Definition

A wave is a travelling disturbance that carries energy **without transporting matter**. Key relation:

$$v = f\lambda, \qquad y = A\sin(kx - \omega t)$$

with wave number $k = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda}$, angular frequency $\omega = 2\pi f$. Wave on a string: $v = \sqrt{T/\mu}$.

## The Intuition

A stadium wave: people stand and sit in place, but the pattern races around the stadium. Each person moves vertically while the wave propagates horizontally — transverse wave motion in a nutshell.

## The Physics

| Quantity | Equation | Units |
|----------|----------|-------|
| Speed | $v = f\lambda$ | m/s |
| Wave number | $k = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda}$ | rad/m |
| Angular frequency | $\omega = 2\pi f$ | rad/s |
| String speed | $v = \sqrt{\frac{T}{\mu}}$ | m/s |
| Wave equation | $y = A\sin(kx - \omega t)$ | m |

## Derivation

The wave equation $v = f\lambda$ comes from "one wavelength passes in one period". The string speed follows from Newton's law applied to a small string element under tension — the restoring force per unit length. [Full derivations: [[Maths/Pure/03-Trigonometry/01-Trigonometric-Functions-Identities/03.1-Trigonometric-Functions-Identities]]]

## Method

1. Identify the type (transverse/longitudinal) and medium.
2. Use $v = f\lambda$ to connect speed, frequency, wavelength.
3. String problems: $v = \sqrt{T/\mu}$ — tension and linear density.

## Worked Examples

**Setup:** A wave has $f = 500$ Hz, $\lambda = 0.68$ m. Find $v$.

**Solution:** $v = 500\times0.68 = 340$ m/s.

**Key insight:** 340 m/s is the speed of sound in air — a familiar sanity check.

---

**Setup:** A string with $\mu = 0.01$ kg/m under $T = 100$ N. Find $v$.

**Solution:** $v = \sqrt{100/0.01} = \sqrt{10000} = 100$ m/s.

**Key insight:** Speed grows with tension but shrinks with density — both inside a square root.

## Common Traps

- $f$ vs $\omega$ — $v = f\lambda$, not $\omega\lambda$
- Wavelength in metres, not cm
- Transverse vs longitudinal — direction of particle motion vs propagation
- Superposition: waves pass through each other, amplitudes add at the crossing point
