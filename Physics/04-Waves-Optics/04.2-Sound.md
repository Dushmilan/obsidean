# 4.2 Sound

Sound is the universe's way of shaking air molecules at you. It's a longitudinal wave — compressions and rarefactions ripple through a medium, carrying energy from source to ear. No medium, no sound. That's why space is silent.

**The Intuition:** Imagine a Slinky. Push one end forward and back repeatedly. A compression pulse travels down the coil while each coil only shifts back and forth locally. Sound does the same thing with air molecules.

**The Math:** The speed of sound in air depends on temperature:

$v = 331 + 0.6\theta$

where $\theta$ is in °C. For a point source, intensity falls off with distance:

$I = \frac{P}{4\pi r^2}$

Sound level is measured on a logarithmic decibel scale:

$\beta = 10\log\frac{I}{I_0}$

where $I_0 = 10^{-12}$ W/m² is the threshold of hearing. 

The Doppler effect shifts frequency when source or observer moves:

$f' = f\frac{v \pm v_o}{v \mp v_s}$

**What does this mean for Physics?** Sound waves demonstrate every wave behaviour — [[04-Waves-Optics/04.1-Wave-Motion|reflection]], [[04-Waves-Optics/04.4-Wave-Optics|diffraction]], [[04-Waves-Optics/04.4-Wave-Optics|interference]] — in a tangible, audible way. The Doppler effect applies equally to [[04-Waves-Optics/04.6-Electromagnetic-Waves|EM waves]] (redshift, radar guns).

---

**Worked Example 1:**

**Setup:** A source moves toward an observer at 30 m/s, emitting 440 Hz. Speed of sound is 340 m/s. Find the observed frequency.

**Solution:**

$f' = 440 \times \frac{340}{340 - 30} = 440 \times \frac{340}{310} = 483 \text{ Hz}$

**Key insight:** The minus sign in the denominator means the source is approaching (compressing wavefronts, raising frequency). Flip the sign and you get recession — lower pitch. Ambulance sirens demonstrate this daily.

---

**Worked Example 2:**

**Setup:** A sound source emits 800 Hz. It moves toward you at 40 m/s while you move toward it at 20 m/s. Speed of sound is 340 m/s. Find the observed frequency.

**Solution:**

Using the Doppler formula with both source and observer moving:

$f' = f\frac{v + v_o}{v - v_s} = 800 \times \frac{340 + 20}{340 - 40} = 800 \times \frac{360}{300} = 800 \times 1.2 = 960 \text{ Hz}$

**Key insight:** When both source and observer move toward each other, the effects compound — the numerator increases (observer approaching) and the denominator decreases (source approaching), both pushing the frequency higher.

---

**Worked Example 3:**

**Setup:** Two organ pipes, one closed at one end (length 0.5 m) and one open at both ends (length 0.5 m), are played. Find the fundamental frequency and first overtone of each. Speed of sound = 340 m/s.

**Solution:**

*Closed pipe (one end closed, one open):*

Fundamental: $\lambda = 4L = 4 \times 0.5 = 2$ m, so $f_1 = \frac{340}{2} = 170$ Hz

First overtone (3rd harmonic): $f_3 = 3f_1 = 510$ Hz

*Open pipe (both ends open):*

Fundamental: $\lambda = 2L = 2 \times 0.5 = 1$ m, so $f_1 = \frac{340}{1} = 340$ Hz

First overtone (2nd harmonic): $f_2 = 2f_1 = 680$ Hz

**Key insight:** Closed pipes only produce odd harmonics (1, 3, 5...) while open pipes produce all harmonics. This is why closed pipes sound "hollower" — they skip every other harmonic, giving a different timbre.

---

**Resonance:** When a sound wave's frequency matches a natural frequency of an air column or cavity, resonance amplifies the sound dramatically. Organ pipes and wind instruments exploit this — different lengths produce different harmonics.

---

## Common Mistakes

- ❌ Forgetting that closed pipe fundamental has $\lambda = 4L$ (not $2L$) — only open pipes use $\lambda = 2L$ for the fundamental
- ❌ Using wrong sign convention in Doppler formula — numerator: $+v_o$ when observer approaches, denominator: $-v_s$ when source approaches
- ❌ Confusing intensity (W/m²) with sound level (dB) — intensity is physical power per area, sound level is a logarithmic perception scale
- ❌ Forgetting that sound needs a medium — it cannot propagate through vacuum
