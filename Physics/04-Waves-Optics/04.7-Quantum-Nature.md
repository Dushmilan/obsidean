# 4.7 Quantum Nature of Light

Light behaves as both wave and particle — and so does everything else. The photoelectric effect shattered the classical wave picture: electrons pop off a metal surface instantly when light hits them, regardless of intensity. Only frequency matters. That's a particle story.

**The Intuition:** Imagine rain hitting a roof. Classical waves say heavier rain (higher intensity) should transfer more energy per drop. But the photoelectric effect is like each raindrop being a coin-operated machine — only coins of sufficient denomination (frequency) work, no matter how many pennies you throw at it.

**The Math:** Each photon carries energy:

$E = hf$

In the photoelectric effect, the maximum kinetic energy of emitted electrons is:

$KE_{max} = hf - \phi$

where $\phi$ is the work function (minimum energy to escape the metal). For matter waves, de Broglie linked wavelength to momentum:

$\lambda = \frac{h}{mv}$

Constants: $h = 6.63 \times 10^{-34}$ J·s, $1 \text{ eV} = 1.6 \times 10^{-19}$ J.

**What does this mean for Physics?** This is the gateway to quantum mechanics. Wave-particle duality means every particle has a wavelength, every wave has quanta. It connects [[04-Waves-Optics/04.6-Electromagnetic-Waves|EM waves]] to atomic physics and the structure of matter.

**Worked Example:**

**Setup:** Light of frequency $1.2 \times 10^{15}$ Hz hits a metal with work function 3.0 eV. Find the maximum kinetic energy.

**Solution:**

$E = hf = \frac{6.63 \times 10^{-34} \times 1.2 \times 10^{15}}{1.6 \times 10^{-19}} = 4.97 \text{ eV}$

$KE_{max} = 4.97 - 3.0 = 1.97 \text{ eV}$

**Key insight:** Increase the intensity and you get more electrons (more current), but each electron still has the same max KE. Only increasing frequency gives individual electrons more energy. Einstein won the Nobel Prize for this, not relativity.

**Threshold Frequency:** Below a certain frequency $f_0 = \frac{\phi}{h}$, no electrons are emitted no matter how intense the light. This was the smoking gun — classical waves had no explanation for a cutoff.
