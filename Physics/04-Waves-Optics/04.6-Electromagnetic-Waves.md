# Electromagnetic Waves

## Definition

Electromagnetic waves are self-sustaining $E$ and $B$ fields, all travelling at $c = 3\times10^8$ m/s in vacuum. Photon energy:

$$E = hf = \frac{hc}{\lambda}, \qquad E_{eV} = \frac{1240}{\lambda_{nm}}$$

$h = 6.63\times10^{-34}$ J·s.

| Type | Wavelength | Source |
|------|------------|--------|
| Radio | > 1 m | AC circuits |
| Microwave | 1 mm – 1 m | magnetron |
| Infrared | 700 nm – 1 mm | hot objects |
| Visible | 400 – 700 nm | atoms |
| UV | 10 – 400 nm | sun, discharge |
| X-ray | 0.01 – 10 nm | decelerating electrons |
| Gamma | < 0.01 nm | nuclear decay |

## The Intuition

Every frequency is the same physics — just different energy. UV triggers the photoelectric effect while radio doesn't, purely because each photon carries more energy.

## The Physics

| Quantity | Equation | Notes |
|----------|----------|-------|
| Speed in vacuum | $c = 3\times10^8$ m/s | constant |
| Photon energy | $E = hf = \frac{hc}{\lambda}$ | J |
| Energy in eV | $E_{eV} = \frac{1240}{\lambda_{nm}}$ | quick form |
| Index | $n = c/v$ | in a medium |

## Derivation

$E = hf$ is Planck's quantum hypothesis — energy comes in quanta $hf$. Maxwell's equations predict EM waves with speed $1/\sqrt{\mu_0\epsilon_0} = c$, identifying light as an EM wave. [Full derivations: [[05-Electricity-Magnetism/05.1-Electric-Fields]]]

## Method

1. Identify the band by wavelength/frequency.
2. Energy: use $E = hf$ (J) or $E_{eV} = 1240/\lambda_{nm}$ (eV) — pick the form matching the units.
3. Longer wavelength → lower energy, lower frequency.

## Worked Examples

**Setup:** Energy of a 500 nm photon in eV.

**Solution:** $E = \frac{1240}{500} = 2.48$ eV.

**Key insight:** The 1240 form makes eV calculations instant — no constants to juggle.

---

**Setup:** Find the frequency of 400 nm violet light.

**Solution:** $f = \frac{c}{\lambda} = \frac{3\times10^8}{4\times10^{-7}} = 7.5\times10^{14}$ Hz.

**Key insight:** Visible light sits near $10^{15}$ Hz — a good order-of-magnitude anchor.

## Common Traps

- $c$ changes in a medium (only vacuum speed is exact)
- $1240/\lambda_{nm}$ requires wavelength in nm and gives eV
- $E$ vs $f$ vs $\lambda$ — convert consistently
- EM waves don't need a medium (unlike sound)
