# 4.6 Electromagnetic Waves

Maxwell predicted them, Hertz detected them, and now they carry everything from radio stations to X-ray images. EM waves are self-propagating oscillations of electric and magnetic fields — they need no medium and travel at $c = 3 \times 10^8$ m/s in vacuum.

**The Intuition:** Imagine flicking a jump rope side to side. Now imagine the rope also wiggles up and down at the same time, at right angles. That's an EM wave — electric and magnetic fields oscillating perpendicular to each other and to the direction of travel.

**The Math:** The speed of light emerges directly from Maxwell's equations:

$c = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu_0 \epsilon_0}}$

The photon energy of any EM radiation is:

$E = hf$

where $h = 6.63 \times 10^{-34}$ J·s. For quick energy calculations in eV:

$E_{eV} = \frac{1240}{\lambda_{nm}}$

The full spectrum spans:

| Type | $\lambda$ range | Source |
|------|-----------------|--------|
| Radio | > 1 m | AC circuits |
| Microwave | 1 mm – 1 m | Magnetron |
| Infrared | 700 nm – 1 mm | Hot objects |
| Visible | 400 – 700 nm | Atoms |
| UV | 10 – 400 nm | Sun, discharge |
| X-ray | 0.01 – 10 nm | Decelerating electrons |
| Gamma | < 0.01 nm | Nuclear decay |

**What does this mean for Physics?** EM waves are the vehicle for [[04-Waves-Optics/04.4-Wave-Optics|wave optics phenomena]] and the bridge to [[04-Waves-Optics/04.7-Quantum-Nature|quantum mechanics]]. Every frequency is the same physics — just different energy. That's why the photoelectric effect works on UV but not radio.

**Worked Example:**

**Setup:** Find the energy of a photon with wavelength 500 nm in eV.

**Solution:**

$E = \frac{1240}{500} = 2.48 \text{ eV}$

**Key insight:** Visible light photons carry a few eV — enough to trigger chemistry (vision, photosynthesis) but not enough to ionize atoms. UV and above cross that threshold, which is why sunscreen matters.
