# Optical Instruments

## Definition

Instruments gather and direct photons:

- **Telescope:** $M = \frac{f_o}{f_e}$ — angular magnification.
- **Simple magnifier:** $M = \frac{D}{f}$, near point $D = 25$ cm.
- **Rayleigh criterion:** $\theta_{min} = \frac{1.22\lambda}{a}$ — the diffraction limit on resolution ($a$ = aperture radius).

## The Intuition

A magnifying glass doesn't create detail — it spreads the image across more retina. A telescope collects more light than your pupil can. An instrument is about gathering photons and directing them usefully.

## The Physics

| Quantity | Equation | Notes |
|----------|----------|-------|
| Telescope magnification | $M = \frac{f_o}{f_e}$ | objective / eyepiece |
| Magnifier | $M = \frac{D}{f}$ | $D = 25$ cm |
| Angular resolution | $\theta_{min} = \frac{1.22\lambda}{a}$ | Rayleigh |
| Resolving power | $d_{min} = \frac{\lambda}{2a}$ | aperture size |

## Derivation

Telescope magnification: the objective forms a real image at its focal plane; the eyepiece magnifies that. Rayleigh criterion comes from the Airy disc — the central diffraction maximum of a circular aperture — and the requirement that two peaks be resolvable. [Full derivations: [[04.4-Wave-Optics]]]

## Method

1. Telescope: ratio of focal lengths; longer objective → bigger magnification.
2. Resolution: larger aperture → smaller $\theta_{min}$ → better resolution.
3. Rayleigh: $\theta_{min}$ in radians.

## Worked Examples

**Setup:** Telescope with $f_o = 120$ cm, $f_e = 4$ cm. Magnification?

**Solution:** $M = 120/4 = 30\times$.

**Key insight:** A long objective and short eyepiece maximise magnification.

---

**Setup:** A telescope aperture of 10 cm observing $\lambda = 550$ nm. Minimum resolvable angle?

**Solution:** $\theta_{min} = \frac{1.22\times5.5\times10^{-7}}{0.05} = 1.3\times10^{-5}$ rad.

**Key insight:** The $1.22$ factor comes from the circular aperture's Airy disc — a square aperture would give $1.0$.

## Common Traps

- $\theta_{min}$ in radians, then convert if needed
- Magnification ≠ resolution — a big image can still be blurry
- Rayleigh criterion assumes diffraction-limited optics (no aberrations)
- Compound vs simple microscope formulas — different magnification expressions
