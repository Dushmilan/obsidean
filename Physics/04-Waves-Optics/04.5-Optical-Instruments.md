# 4.5 Optical Instruments

Optical instruments combine [[04-Waves-Optics/04.3-Geometric-Optics|lenses and mirrors]] to let us see what the naked eye cannot — from distant galaxies to individual cells. Every telescope, microscope, and camera is a lens equation in action.

**The Intuition:** A magnifying glass doesn't create detail — it spreads the image across more of your retina. A telescope collects more light than your pupil can. An optical instrument is really about gathering photons and directing them usefully.

**The Math:** A refracting telescope's angular magnification is:

$M = \frac{f_o}{f_e}$

where $f_o$ is the objective focal length and $f_e$ is the eyepiece focal length. A simple magnifier (loupe) gives:

$M = \frac{D}{f}$

where $D = 25$ cm is the near point. But resolution — the ability to distinguish close objects — is limited by diffraction:

$\theta_{min} = \frac{1.22\lambda}{a}$

This is the Rayleigh criterion, where $a$ is the aperture radius. The resolving power is:

$d_{min} = \frac{\lambda}{2a}$

**What does this mean for Physics?** No matter how perfect your lenses are, diffraction from [[04-Waves-Optics/04.4-Wave-Optics|wave optics]] sets a hard limit on resolution. This connects directly to [[04-Waves-Optics/04.7-Quantum-Nature|quantum observation limits]] — you can't measure everything simultaneously.

---

**Worked Example 1:**

**Setup:** Telescope with $f_o = 120$ cm and $f_e = 4$ cm. Find magnification.

**Solution:**

$M = \frac{120}{4} = 30\times$

**Key insight:** Higher magnification isn't always better. If the objective can't resolve fine detail (diffraction limit), extra magnification just gives you a bigger blurry image. Resolution, not magnification, is the real constraint.

---

**Worked Example 2:**

**Setup:** A microscope has objective focal length 4 mm and eyepiece focal length 25 mm. The objective forms an image 160 mm from the objective. Find the magnifying power.

**Solution:**

For the microscope, we first find the image distance from the objective using the lens equation:

$\frac{1}{v} = \frac{1}{f} - \frac{1}{u}$

The object distance is: $\frac{1}{u} = \frac{1}{f} - \frac{1}{v} = \frac{1}{4} - \frac{1}{160} = \frac{40 - 1}{160} = \frac{39}{160}$

$u = 4.10$ mm

Magnifying power of a microscope:

$M = \frac{v}{u} \times \frac{D}{f_e} = \frac{160}{4.10} \times \frac{250}{25} = 39.0 \times 10 = 390\times$

**Key insight:** The microscope's objective creates a real, magnified image which the eyepiece then acts as a magnifying glass to view. The total magnification is the product of the objective's linear magnification and the eyepiece's angular magnification.

---

**Worked Example 3:**

**Setup:** Two point sources of light are 1 mm apart. Using a telescope with aperture diameter 5 cm and light of wavelength 500 nm, can they be resolved? Use Rayleigh criterion.

**Solution:**

Minimum angular separation for resolution:

$\theta_{min} = \frac{1.22\lambda}{a} = \frac{1.22 \times 500 \times 10^{-9}}{0.025} = 2.44 \times 10^{-5} \text{ rad}$

If the sources are at distance $L$, the minimum resolvable separation is:

$d_{min} = L \times \theta_{min}$

For the sources to be resolved, we need the actual separation to exceed $d_{min}$. Assuming the sources are far enough away that their angular separation is approximately:

$\theta = \frac{1 \times 10^{-3}}{L}$

For resolution, we need $\theta \geq \theta_{min}$, so:

$L \leq \frac{1 \times 10^{-3}}{2.44 \times 10^{-5}} = 41.0$ m

If the sources are closer than 41 m, they can be resolved. Beyond that distance, they blur together.

**Key insight:** The Rayleigh criterion shows that resolution depends only on wavelength and aperture — not on magnification. A bigger telescope doesn't just magnify more, it resolves finer detail. This is why astronomers build ever-larger mirrors.

---

**Microscope vs Telescope:** A microscope magnifies nearby objects (objective close to specimen); a telescope magnifies distant ones (objective collects parallel rays). Different formulas, same physics — both are fighting the diffraction limit.

## Common Mistakes

- ❌ Forgetting that magnification beyond the diffraction limit just gives a bigger blurry image — resolution, not magnification, is the real limit
- ❌ Confusing angular magnification with linear magnification — telescopes use angular, microscopes combine both
- ❌ Using wrong units (mm vs m for focal lengths) — always convert to consistent units before calculating
- ❌ Forgetting that Rayleigh criterion depends on aperture not focal length — larger aperture means better resolution
