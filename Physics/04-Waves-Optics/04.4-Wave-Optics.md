# Wave Optics

## Definition

Interference and diffraction of light:

- **Double slit (bright):** $d\sin\theta = n\lambda$
- **Double slit (dark):** $d\sin\theta = (n+\tfrac12)\lambda$
- **Single slit (dark minima):** $a\sin\theta = n\lambda$
- **Malus's law (polarisation):** $I = I_0\cos^2\phi$

## The Intuition

Throw two pebbles into a still pond: the ripples overlap — some points get bigger (constructive), some cancel (destructive). Light does the same, faster and smaller. Diffraction sets the fundamental resolution limit of any instrument.

## The Physics

| Effect | Equation | Notes |
|--------|----------|-------|
| Double-slit bright | $d\sin\theta = n\lambda$ | $n = 0,1,2,\dots$ |
| Double-slit dark | $d\sin\theta = (n+\frac12)\lambda$ | between bright |
| Single-slit dark | $a\sin\theta = n\lambda$ | $n = 1,2,\dots$ |
| Malus's law | $I = I_0\cos^2\phi$ | polariser angle |

## Derivation

Interference: path difference $\Delta = d\sin\theta$; constructive when $\Delta = n\lambda$ (whole waves), destructive at half-integers. Single slit: each half of the slit cancels the other for the first minimum. Malus: the transmitted field is the projection, so intensity scales as $\cos^2$. [Full derivations: [[Maths/Pure/03-Trigonometry/01-Trigonometric-Functions-Identities/03.1-Trigonometric-Functions-Identities]]]

## Method

1. Identify the setup: double slit (bright/dark), single slit, or polariser.
2. For angles: $\sin\theta \approx \theta$ (radians) for small angles.
3. Fringe spacing on a screen at distance $D$: $\Delta y = \frac{\lambda D}{d}$.

## Worked Examples

**Setup:** Double slit, $d = 0.2$ mm, $\lambda = 500$ nm. Angle to the first maximum?

**Solution:** $d\sin\theta = \lambda \Rightarrow \sin\theta = \frac{5\times10^{-7}}{2\times10^{-4}} = 2.5\times10^{-3}$, so $\theta \approx 0.14°$.

**Key insight:** Slits are tiny and wavelengths tinier — fringes sit at very small angles.

---

**Setup:** Unpolarised light through a polariser, then one at 60°. Final intensity?

**Solution:** First polariser halves it: $I_1 = I_0/2$. Malus: $I = \frac{I_0}{2}\cos^2 60° = \frac{I_0}{8}$.

**Key insight:** Two crossed polarisers ($90°$) transmit nothing — the basis of LCD screens.

## Common Traps

- Using the double-slit formula for single-slit (and vice versa) — note which is bright/dark
- Degrees vs radians in $\sin\theta \approx \theta$
- Malus only applies to already-polarised light (the first polariser halves first)
- Fringe spacing needs $d, \lambda$ in the same units
