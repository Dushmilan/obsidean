# Dynamics (Newton's Laws)

## Definition

Newton's three laws connect force and motion:

| Law | Statement |
|-----|-----------|
| 1st | $F_{net} = 0 \Rightarrow a = 0$ (equilibrium / inertia) |
| 2nd | $F = ma$ — force = mass × acceleration (N = kg·m·s⁻²) |
| 3rd | $F_{AB} = -F_{BA}$ — equal and opposite pairs |

Momentum: $p = mv$; the fundamental form is $F = \frac{\Delta p}{\Delta t}$.

## The Intuition

A shopping cart: push it and it accelerates; stop pushing and it keeps going (until friction wins); a friend pushes back and it stays still. That's inertia, $F = ma$, and action-reaction in plain sight.

## The Physics

| Quantity | Equation | Units |
|----------|----------|-------|
| Force | $F = ma$ | N |
| Weight | $W = mg$ | N |
| Momentum | $p = mv$ | kg·m·s⁻¹ |
| Normal | $R = mg\cos\theta$ (incline) | N |
| Friction (kinetic) | $f = \mu R$ | N |

## Derivation

$F = dp/dt$ is the fundamental statement; for constant mass it reduces to $F = ma$. Weight is the gravitational force $W = mg$. [Full derivations: [[Maths/Applied/02-Dynamics/02.1-Newtons-Laws]]]

## Method

1. **Free-body diagram** — every force on the chosen body.
2. **Choose axes** along the motion.
3. Resolve, write $F = ma$ per axis.
4. **Systems (pulleys):** separate FBD per mass, add constraints ($T$ equal, $a$ equal).

## Worked Examples

**Setup:** A 5 kg block on a frictionless surface is pulled by 20 N. Find $a$.

**Solution:** $a = F/m = 20/5 = 4$ m/s².

**Key insight:** Mass resists acceleration — double the mass, halve the acceleration.

---

**Setup:** 3 kg and 5 kg masses over a frictionless pulley. Find $a$ and $T$.

**Solution:** $5g - T = 5a$; $T - 3g = 3a$. Adding: $2g = 8a \Rightarrow a = 2.5$ m/s². Then $T = 3(g+a) = 37.5$ N.

**Key insight:** The net driving force is the weight difference, accelerating the *total* mass; tension is between the two weights.

---

**Setup:** A 10 kg block ($\mu = 0.3$) pulled by 50 N at 30° above horizontal. Find $a$.

**Solution:** Vertical: $R = mg - F\sin30 = 100 - 25 = 75$ N. Horizontal: $F\cos30 - \mu R = ma \Rightarrow 43.3 - 22.5 = 10a \Rightarrow a = 2.08$ m/s².

**Key insight:** An upward force component *reduces* the normal reaction — hence less friction.

## Common Traps

- Third-law pairs act on different bodies — they don't cancel
- Assuming $R = mg$ when forces act at angles
- Using $F = ma$ where mass changes (use $dp/dt$)
- Forgetting to resolve weight on inclines
