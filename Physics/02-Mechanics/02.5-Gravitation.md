# Gravitation

## Definition

Newton's law of universal gravitation — every bit of mass attracts every other:

$$F = G\frac{m_1m_2}{r^2}, \qquad g = \frac{GM}{r^2}$$

$G = 6.67\times10^{-11}$ N·m²·kg⁻². Orbital velocity $v_{orb} = \sqrt{GM/r}$; Kepler's third law $T^2 = \frac{4\pi^2}{GM}r^3$.

## The Intuition

Two magnets pulling on each other — but gravity works with *every* bit of mass. A planet is a huge collection of atoms all pulling together. The pull drops off fast: double the distance, quarter the force.

## The Physics

| Quantity | Equation | Units |
|----------|----------|-------|
| Gravitational force | $F = \frac{Gm_1m_2}{r^2}$ | N |
| Field strength | $g = \frac{GM}{r^2}$ | m·s⁻² |
| Orbital velocity | $v_{orb} = \sqrt{\frac{GM}{r}}$ | m·s⁻¹ |
| Kepler's 3rd | $T^2 = \frac{4\pi^2}{GM}r^3$ | — |

## Derivation

Orbital velocity: set $GMm/r^2 = mv^2/r$ (gravity = centripetal) and cancel $m$. Kepler's 3rd: substitute $v = 2\pi r/T$. [Full derivations: [[Maths/Applied/04-Circular-Motion/04.1-Uniform-Circular-Motion]]]

## Method

1. Set gravitational force equal to the required centripetal force.
2. For surface gravity: $g = GM/R^2$ — note radius matters a lot ($1/r^2$).
3. Compare planets via the ratios of $M$ and $R$.

## Worked Examples

**Setup:** Orbital velocity of Earth around the Sun ($M = 2\times10^{30}$ kg, $r = 1.5\times10^{11}$ m).

**Solution:** $v = \sqrt{GM/r} = \sqrt{\frac{6.67\times10^{-11}\times2\times10^{30}}{1.5\times10^{11}}} = 3.0\times10^4$ m/s.

**Key insight:** Closer orbits need higher speeds — the Sun's pull sustains Earth at ~30 km/s.

---

**Setup:** Gravity on Mars ($M = 6.4\times10^{23}$ kg, $R = 3.4\times10^6$ m) vs Earth.

**Solution:** $g = \frac{6.67\times10^{-11}\times6.4\times10^{23}}{(3.4\times10^6)^2} = 3.7$ m/s² ≈ 0.38 g.

**Key insight:** Mars is less massive *and* smaller — the smaller radius partially compensates.

## Common Traps

- $g = GM/r^2$ — $r$ is from the *centre*, not the surface
- Treating gravitational potential energy as $mgh$ far from Earth (use $-\frac{GMm}{r}$)
- Forgetting $G$'s tiny magnitude — forces are weak unless masses are huge
- Orbits: $v^2 \propto 1/r$, not $\propto r$
