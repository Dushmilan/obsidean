# Simple Harmonic Motion

## Definition

Motion where acceleration is proportional to displacement and opposite in direction:

$$a = -\omega^2 x, \qquad x = A\cos(\omega t + \phi)$$

with angular frequency $\omega = 2\pi f = \frac{2\pi}{T}$.

| System | Period |
|--------|--------|
| Spring | $T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{m}{k}}$ |
| Pendulum (small angle) | $T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{l}{g}}$ |

Total energy: $E = \frac12 kA^2$.

## The Intuition

A mass on a spring: pull it down, it springs back; overshoot, it pulls back the other way. The force is always proportional to displacement ($F = -kx$) and toward equilibrium. The same math describes a small-angle pendulum.

## The Physics

| Quantity | Equation |
|----------|----------|
| Displacement | $x = A\cos(\omega t + \phi)$ |
| Velocity | $v = \pm\omega\sqrt{A^2 - x^2}$ |
| Max velocity | $v_{max} = \omega A$ (at $x = 0$) |
| Acceleration | $a = -\omega^2 x$ |
| Total energy | $E = \frac12 kA^2$ |

## Derivation

$F = -kx$ with $F = ma$ gives $\ddot{x} + \frac{k}{m}x = 0$, whose solution is sinusoidal. Energy: $E = \frac12 kA^2$ at the extremes ($x = \pm A$, all potential) equals $\frac12 mv_{max}^2$ at equilibrium. [Full derivations: [[Maths/Applied/05-SHM/05.1-SHM-Definition-Characteristics]]]

## Method

1. Identify the restoring force and confirm it's linear ($F = -kx$).
2. Read $\omega$ from the system ($\sqrt{k/m}$ or $\sqrt{g/l}$).
3. $v_{max} = \omega A$; $a_{max} = \omega^2 A$.
4. Energy: $E = \frac12 kA^2$ links amplitude to max speed.

## Worked Examples

**Setup:** A spring ($k = 100$ N/m, $m = 1$ kg) oscillates with $A = 0.1$ m. Find $v_{max}$.

**Solution:** $v_{max} = \omega A = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}A = 10\times0.1 = 1.0$ m/s.

**Key insight:** Max velocity is at equilibrium ($x=0$), not at the extremes.

---

**Setup:** A 0.5 m pendulum at 5°. Find the period ($g = 9.8$).

**Solution:** $T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{l}{g}} = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{0.5}{9.8}} = 1.42$ s.

**Key insight:** Small-angle period is independent of amplitude and mass.

## Common Traps

- $a = -\omega^2 x$ — the minus sign is the definition (restoring)
- Using $\omega$ where $f$ belongs ($\omega = 2\pi f$)
- Pendulum vs spring period formulas — $\sqrt{l/g}$ vs $\sqrt{m/k}$
- Large-angle pendulums break the small-angle approximation
