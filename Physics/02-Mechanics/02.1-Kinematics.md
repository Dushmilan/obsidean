# 2.1 Kinematics

Kinematics is the language of motion — it tells you *where* things are and *how fast* they're moving, without asking *why*. Think of it as tracking a car's GPS coordinates: you see the route, the speed, the turns, but you don't care about the engine or the driver. It's pure description.

**The Intuition:** Imagine throwing a ball. You can describe its path — how high it goes, how far it travels, when it lands — using just four quantities: displacement ($s$), initial velocity ($u$), final velocity ($v$), and acceleration ($a$). Time ($t$) ties them together. This is the backbone of predicting motion before worrying about what causes it.

**The Math:** The four equations of motion for constant acceleration:

| Formula | Use case |
|---------|----------|
| $v = u + at$ | When displacement isn't needed |
| $s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2$ | When final velocity isn't needed |
| $v^2 = u^2 + 2as$ | When time isn't needed |
| $s = \frac{(u+v)}{2}t$ | When acceleration isn't known |

For projectile motion, horizontal and vertical components split cleanly:
- Horizontal: $x = u\cos\theta \cdot t$, with $a_x = 0$
- Vertical: $y = u\sin\theta \cdot t - \frac{1}{2}gt^2$, with $a_y = -g$

**What does this mean for Physics?** Kinematics is the foundation. Every subsequent topic — [[02-Mechanics/02.2-Dynamics|dynamics]], [[02-Mechanics/02.4-Circular-Motion|circular motion]], [[02-Mechanics/02.6-SHM|SHM]] — builds on it. Master these equations and you can decode any motion problem.

---

**Setup:** A ball is thrown upward at $20\ \text{m/s}$. Find max height.

**Solution:** At max height, $v = 0$. Use $v^2 = u^2 + 2as$:
$$0 = (20)^2 + 2(-10)(s) \implies s = \frac{400}{20} = 20\ \text{m}$$

**Key insight:** The negative sign on $g$ matters. Gravity opposes upward motion, so $a = -10\ \text{m/s}^2$ in the vertical direction.

---

**Setup:** A car accelerates from rest at $3\ \text{m/s}^2$ for $10\ \text{s}$, then cruises at constant velocity for $5\ \text{s}$. Find the total distance.

**Solution:** Phase 1 — accelerated motion ($u=0$, $a=3$, $t=10$):
$$s_1 = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2 = 0 + \frac{1}{2}(3)(10)^2 = 150\ \text{m}$$
Final velocity after phase 1: $v = u + at = 0 + 3 \times 10 = 30\ \text{m/s}$

Phase 2 — constant velocity ($v=30$, $t=5$):
$$s_2 = vt = 30 \times 5 = 150\ \text{m}$$
$$s_{total} = 150 + 150 = 300\ \text{m}$$

**Key insight:** Multi-phase problems are solved by splitting them at the point where acceleration changes. The final velocity of one phase becomes the initial velocity of the next.

---

**Setup:** A projectile is launched at $30\ \text{m/s}$ at $60°$ to the horizontal. Find the range, maximum height, and total time of flight.

**Solution:** Resolve initial velocity: $u_x = 30\cos 60° = 15\ \text{m/s}$, $u_y = 30\sin 60° = 25.98\ \text{m/s}$.

Time of flight — at landing, $y=0$:
$$y = u_y t - \frac{1}{2}gt^2 \implies 0 = 25.98t - 5t^2 \implies t = \frac{25.98}{5} = 5.20\ \text{s}$$

Maximum height — at the top, $v_y = 0$:
$$v_y^2 = u_y^2 - 2gh \implies 0 = (25.98)^2 - 20h \implies h = \frac{675}{20} = 33.75\ \text{m}$$

Range:
$$R = u_x \times t = 15 \times 5.20 = 78.0\ \text{m}$$

**Key insight:** Projectile motion is two independent problems running in parallel — horizontal (constant velocity) and vertical (constant acceleration). They share only time. Solve them separately, link them through $t$.

## Common Mistakes

- ❌ Forgetting the negative sign on $g$ when the object moves upward (gravity always acts downward)
- ❌ Using the wrong SUVAT equation — always check which variable is missing and pick accordingly
- ❌ Confusing displacement with distance — they're only equal in one-direction motion
- ❌ Forgetting that horizontal acceleration is zero in projectile motion
