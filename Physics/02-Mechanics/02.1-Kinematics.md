# Kinematics

## Definition

The language of motion — *where* things are and *how fast* they're moving, without asking *why*. Uses displacement $s$, initial velocity $u$, final velocity $v$, acceleration $a$, and time $t$.

**Constant acceleration (SUVAT) — SI units m, m/s, m/s², s.**

## The Intuition

Throwing a ball: you can describe its path — height, distance, landing time — with just four quantities plus time. Kinematics is pure description, before worrying about what causes the motion.

## The Physics

| Equation | Use when |
|----------|----------|
| $v = u + at$ | displacement not needed |
| $s = ut + \frac12 at^2$ | final velocity not needed |
| $v^2 = u^2 + 2as$ | time not needed |
| $s = \frac{(u+v)}{2}t$ | acceleration not needed |

**Projectiles** split cleanly:
- Horizontal: $x = u\cos\theta\cdot t$, $a_x = 0$
- Vertical: $y = u\sin\theta\cdot t - \frac12 gt^2$, $a_y = -g$

## Derivation

Integrate constant $a$: $v = u + at$, then $s = ut + \frac12 at^2$; eliminate $t$ for $v^2 = u^2 + 2as$. [Full derivations: [[Maths/Applied/01-Kinematics/01.2-Equations-of-Motion]]]

## Method

1. Identify knowns and unknowns — pick the SUVAT equation missing the unwanted variable.
2. **Sign convention:** choose a positive direction; upward motion uses $a = -g$.
3. **Multi-phase:** split where acceleration changes; the final velocity of one phase is the next phase's initial velocity.
4. Projectiles: solve vertical and horizontal separately, linked by shared time $t$.

## Worked Examples

**Setup:** A ball is thrown upward at 20 m/s. Find the max height ($g = 10$).

**Solution:** At max height $v = 0$: $0 = (20)^2 + 2(-10)s \Rightarrow s = 20$ m.

**Key insight:** The negative sign on $g$ matters — gravity opposes upward motion.

---

**Setup:** A car accelerates from rest at 3 m/s² for 10 s, then cruises for 5 s. Total distance?

**Solution:** Phase 1: $s_1 = \frac12(3)(10)^2 = 150$ m, $v = 30$ m/s. Phase 2: $s_2 = 30\times5 = 150$ m. Total $= 300$ m.

**Key insight:** Split at the point where acceleration changes.

---

**Setup:** Projectile at 30 m/s at 60°. Find range, max height, time of flight.

**Solution:** $u_x = 15$, $u_y = 25.98$ m/s. Time to peak $t = u_y/g = 2.6$ s, flight $= 5.2$ s. Range $= 15\times5.2 = 78$ m. Max height $= u_y^2/2g \approx 33.7$ m.

**Key insight:** The two components are independent — only the vertical part feels gravity.

## Common Traps

- Wrong sign for $g$ in vertical motion
- Applying SUVAT when acceleration isn't constant
- Treating projectile range with level-ground formulas on slopes
- Mixing displacement (signed) with distance
