# Circular Motion

## Definition

Uniform circular motion: constant speed, continuously changing direction — an inward (centripetal) acceleration and force:

$$a_c = \frac{v^2}{r} = \omega^2 r, \qquad F_c = \frac{mv^2}{r}$$

**Centripetal force is not a new force** — it's the net radial force (tension, gravity, friction, or normal).

## The Intuition

Spin a ball on a string: it wants to fly off in a straight line (inertia), but the string pulls it inward. Snap the string and it flies off **tangentially** — not outward, not inward.

## The Physics

| Quantity | Equation | Units |
|----------|----------|-------|
| Centripetal acceleration | $a_c = \frac{v^2}{r} = \omega^2 r$ | m·s⁻² |
| Centripetal force | $F_c = \frac{mv^2}{r}$ | N |
| Linear–angular | $v = r\omega$ | — |
| Period | $T = \frac{2\pi r}{v} = \frac{2\pi}{\omega}$ | s |

## Derivation

Velocity vectors at two nearby points differ in direction; the change $\Delta\vec{v}$ points to the centre, giving $a = v^2/r$. [Full derivations: [[Maths/Applied/04-Circular-Motion/04.1-Uniform-Circular-Motion]]]

## Method

1. Identify which real force provides the inward resultant.
2. Write $\sum F_{radial} = mv^2/r$.
3. Convert between $v, \omega, T$ as needed.

## Worked Examples

**Setup:** A 0.5 kg ball on a 1 m string swings at 3 m/s. Tension?

**Solution:** $T = \frac{mv^2}{r} = \frac{0.5\times9}{1} = 4.5$ N.

**Key insight:** Tension rises **quadratically** with speed — the string snaps above a threshold.

---

**Setup:** A 1200 kg car rounds a 50 m bend at 20 m/s. Required friction? Skids if $\mu = 0.4$?

**Solution:** $f = \frac{1200\times400}{50} = 9600$ N. Max $f = \mu mg = 4800$ N. Required > available → skids.

**Key insight:** Compare required centripetal force with the maximum available friction.

## Common Traps

- Calling centripetal force a "fifth force" — it's the resultant of real forces
- $\omega^2 r$ vs $\omega r$ (that's $v$)
- Using diameter instead of radius
- Vertical circles: gravity changes the speed — energy conservation needed
