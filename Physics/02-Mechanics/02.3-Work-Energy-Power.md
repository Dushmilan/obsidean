# Work, Energy & Power

## Definition

**Work** is the component of force along the displacement: $W = Fs\cos\theta$ (J = N·m). **Energy** is the capacity to do work; **power** is the rate: $P = \frac{W}{t} = Fv$ (W = J/s).

**Work-Energy Theorem:** $W_{net} = \Delta KE$.

## The Intuition

Work is effort. Push a box across a room — you did work. Push a wall and nothing moves — zero work. Energy is like money: it changes accounts (kinetic ↔ potential) but the total stays constant unless someone takes a cut (friction, air resistance).

## The Physics

| Quantity | Equation | Units |
|----------|----------|-------|
| Work | $W = Fs\cos\theta$ | J |
| Kinetic energy | $KE = \frac12 mv^2$ | J |
| Gravitational PE | $PE = mgh$ | J |
| Elastic PE | $PE = \frac12 kx^2$ | J |
| Power | $P = \frac{W}{t} = Fv$ | W |

**Conservation:** $KE_i + PE_i + W_{nc} = KE_f + PE_f$ (friction is negative $W_{nc}$).

## Derivation

$W = \int F\,ds$; with constant force and straight path this is $Fs\cos\theta$. The work-energy theorem comes from integrating $F = ma$ over displacement: $\int F\,ds = \int mv\,dv = \frac12 mv^2$. [Full derivations: [[Maths/Applied/02-Dynamics/02.3-Work-Energy-Power-Dynamics]]]

## Method

1. Identify which forces do work (normal and string tension do **zero**).
2. Choose: speed-over-distance (no time) → energy; else → forces.
3. Write $KE_i + PE_i + W_{nc} = KE_f + PE_f$ and solve.
4. Friction always does negative work $-\mu N s$.

## Worked Examples

**Setup:** A 2 kg ball falls 10 m. Speed at the ground (no air resistance)?

**Solution:** $mgh = \frac12 mv^2 \Rightarrow v = \sqrt{2gh} = \sqrt{200} = 14.1$ m/s.

**Key insight:** The mass cancels — a feather and a bowling ball hit the ground at the same speed.

---

**Setup:** A 2 kg projectile launched at 20 m/s at 45°. Find KE at the top.

**Solution:** At the top only $v_x = 20\cos45 = 14.14$ m/s remains: $KE = \frac12(2)(14.14)^2 = 200$ J (half the initial 400 J).

**Key insight:** Energy conservation links the launch energy to height + horizontal KE.

## Common Traps

- Counting zero-work forces (normal, tension)
- $\cos\theta$ between force and displacement, not the incline angle
- Friction as positive work
- $KE$ is never negative
