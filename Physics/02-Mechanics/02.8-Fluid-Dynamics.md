# Fluid Dynamics

## Definition

The physics of fluids at rest and in motion:

$$p = \frac{F}{A}, \qquad p = p_0 + \rho gh, \qquad F_B = \rho Vg, \qquad A_1v_1 = A_2v_2, \qquad p + \tfrac12\rho v^2 + \rho gh = \text{const}$$

Continuity: what goes in must come out (mass conserved). Bernoulli: as speed increases, pressure drops (at constant height).

## The Intuition

Put your hand out a car window, palm facing the wind: the air moves faster over the curved top, creating lower pressure there, and higher pressure below pushes your hand up — Bernoulli in action. A garden hose: pinch the nozzle, water speeds up and pressure drops.

## The Physics

| Principle | Equation | Units |
|-----------|----------|-------|
| Pressure | $p = \frac{F}{A}$ | Pa |
| Hydrostatic | $p = p_0 + \rho gh$ | Pa |
| Buoyancy | $F_B = \rho V g$ | N |
| Continuity | $A_1v_1 = A_2v_2$ | m³·s⁻¹ flow |
| Bernoulli | $p + \frac12\rho v^2 + \rho gh = \text{const}$ | Pa |

## Derivation

Continuity: mass conservation ($\rho A_1v_1 = \rho A_2v_2$ for incompressible flow). Bernoulli: the work done by pressure equals the change in kinetic + potential energy along a streamline. [Full derivations: [[Maths/Applied/02-Dynamics/02.3-Work-Energy-Power-Dynamics]]]

## Method

1. **Continuity:** find unknown velocities from $A_1v_1 = A_2v_2$.
2. **Bernoulli:** pick two points on a streamline, cancel equal terms (same height, or $v \approx 0$ at a surface), solve for the unknown pressure/velocity.

## Worked Examples

**Setup:** A pipe narrows from 10 cm² to 5 cm²; wide-section velocity is 2 m/s. Find $v_2$.

**Solution:** $v_2 = \frac{A_1v_1}{A_2} = \frac{10\times2}{5} = 4$ m/s.

**Key insight:** Half the area, double the velocity — flow rate is conserved.

---

**Setup:** Flow 0.02 m³/s; $A_1 = 0.01$, $A_2 = 0.005$ m², $\rho = 1000$. Pressure difference?

**Solution:** $v_1 = 2$, $v_2 = 4$ m/s. Bernoulli (same height): $p_1 - p_2 = \frac12\rho(v_2^2 - v_1^2) = 500(16-4) = 6000$ Pa.

**Key insight:** The faster the flow, the lower the pressure — the $\frac12\rho v^2$ term is the exchange.

## Common Traps

- Bernoulli only along a streamline, steady, incompressible, inviscid flow
- Hydrostatic pressure uses depth $h$, not height above the ground
- Buoyancy uses the *displaced volume* of fluid, not the object's total volume when partially submerged
- Mixing gauge and absolute pressure
