# Errors & Uncertainties

## Definition

No measurement is perfect — every reading carries uncertainty. Quantify, propagate, and report it:

- **Systematic error** — consistent bias in one direction; fixed by calibration.
- **Random error** — scatter; reduced by averaging.
- **Accuracy** — closeness to the *true* value (affected by systematic error).
- **Precision** — closeness of repeated readings (affected by random error).

**Reporting rule:** $x = \bar{x} \pm \Delta x$ — uncertainty to 1 sig fig (2 if it starts with 1), value rounded to the same decimal place, units always included.

## The Intuition

Throwing darts at a bullseye. Darts clustering tightly but away from the centre → precise but not accurate. Darts scattering around the centre → accurate on average but not precise. You want both: tight clusters on the bullseye.

## The Physics

| Combination | Rule | Example |
|-------------|------|---------|
| $z = x \pm y$ | add absolute uncertainties | $\Delta z = \Delta x + \Delta y$ |
| $z = xy$ or $x/y$ | add relative uncertainties | $\frac{\Delta z}{z} = \frac{\Delta x}{x} + \frac{\Delta y}{y}$ |
| $z = x^n$ | relative × $|n|$ | $\frac{\Delta z}{z} = |n|\frac{\Delta x}{x}$ |
| Averaging $n$ readings | $\Delta$ divides by $n$ | $\Delta T = \Delta t / n$ |

## Derivation

The rules come from the chain rule: $dz = \frac{\partial z}{\partial x}dx + \frac{\partial z}{\partial y}dy$. For sums the absolute errors add; for products the relative errors add (logarithmic differentiation: $\ln z = \ln x + \ln y$, then differentiate). [Full derivations: [[01.2-Error-Propagation-Proofs]]]

## Method

1. Classify the error (systematic vs random) and fix the cause (calibrate / average more).
2. Propagate with the combination rules.
3. Report with 1-sig-fig uncertainty and matched decimals.

## Worked Examples

**Setup:** Time 20 oscillations: $t = 40.0 \pm 0.5$ s (reaction time). Find $T$.

**Solution:** $T = t/n = 2.00$ s; $\Delta T = \Delta t/n = 0.025$ s. Report $T = 2.00 \pm 0.03$ s.

**Key insight:** Dividing by $n$ reduces the uncertainty by $n$ — why we time many oscillations.

---

**Setup:** $\rho = RA/l$ with $R = 4.5 \pm 0.2\ \Omega$, $d = 0.50 \pm 0.01$ mm, $l = 1.000 \pm 0.001$ m.

**Solution:** $A = \pi d^2/4$: $\frac{\Delta A}{A} = 2\frac{\Delta d}{d} = 0.04$. Then $\frac{\Delta\rho}{\rho} = \frac{0.2}{4.5} + 0.04 + 0.001 = 0.085$.

**Key insight:** The area uncertainty (squaring $d$) dominates — improving $d$ matters most.

---

**Setup:** Find $g$ from $T = 2\pi\sqrt{l/g}$ with $l = 1.00 \pm 0.01$ m, $T = 2.01 \pm 0.02$ s.

**Solution:** $g = 4\pi^2 l/T^2$: $\frac{\Delta g}{g} = \frac{\Delta l}{l} + 2\frac{\Delta T}{T} = 0.01 + 0.02 = 0.03$. So $g = 9.8 \pm 0.3$ m/s².

**Key insight:** The factor 2 on $\Delta T/T$ comes from the square — powers multiply relative uncertainty.

## Common Traps

- Mixing accuracy and precision — one is closeness to true, the other repeatability
- Forgetting to divide uncertainty by $n$ when averaging
- Reporting uncertainty beyond 1 sig fig
- Mismatched decimals: if $\Delta x = 0.03$, $x$ must go to 2 decimal places
