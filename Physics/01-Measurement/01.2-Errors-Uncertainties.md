# 1.2 Errors & Uncertainties

No measurement is perfect. Every reading carries doubt — and understanding that doubt is what separates a rough guess from a real measurement. This chapter gives you the tools to quantify, propagate, and report that uncertainty.

**The Intuition:** Imagine throwing darts at a bullseye. If all your darts cluster tightly in one spot but away from the centre, you're precise but not accurate. If they scatter around the centre, you're accurate on average but not precise. You want both — tight clusters on the bullseye.

**The Math:** Uncertainty comes in two flavours: **systematic** (consistent bias — fix with calibration) and **random** (scatter — reduce by averaging). Propagation follows simple rules: add absolute uncertainties for $z = x \pm y$, add relative uncertainties for $z = xy$ or $z = x/y$, and multiply relative uncertainty by $|n|$ for $z = x^n$. Always report $x = \bar{x} \pm \Delta x$ with uncertainty to 1 sig fig and the best value rounded to the same decimal place.

**What does this mean for Physics?** A measurement without an uncertainty statement is incomplete. The propagation rules let you track how errors flow from your raw readings into your final calculated quantity — whether that's resistivity, gravitational acceleration, or anything else.

| Type | Characteristic | Detection | Fix |
|------|----------------|-----------|-----|
| Systematic | Consistent bias, same direction | Calibration, comparison with standard | Recalibrate, apply correction |
| Random | Scatter, unpredictable | Repeated measurements, statistics | Average more readings |

| Term | Meaning | Related to |
|------|---------|------------|
| Accuracy | Closeness to true value | Systematic error |
| Precision | Closeness of repeated measurements | Random error |

---

**Setup:** Measure 20 oscillations of a pendulum: $t = 40.0 \pm 0.5\ \text{s}$ (reaction time).

**Solution:** $T = t/n = 40.0/20 = 2.00\ \text{s}$. $\Delta T = \Delta t / n = 0.5/20 = 0.025\ \text{s}$. Report: $T = 2.00 \pm 0.03\ \text{s}$.

**Key insight:** Dividing by $n$ reduces the uncertainty by $n$ — this is why we time many oscillations, not just one.

---

**Setup:** Find resistivity $\rho = \frac{RA}{l}$ given $R = 4.5 \pm 0.2\ \Omega$, $d = 0.50 \pm 0.01\ \text{mm}$, $l = 1.000 \pm 0.001\ \text{m}$.

**Solution:** $A = \pi d^2/4$, so $\frac{\Delta A}{A} = 2\frac{\Delta d}{d} = 2(0.02) = 0.04$. Then $\frac{\Delta \rho}{\rho} = \frac{0.2}{4.5} + 0.04 + \frac{0.001}{1.000} = 0.085$.

**Key insight:** The area uncertainty (from squaring diameter) dominates — improving $d$ matters more than improving $l$.

---

**Setup:** Find $g$ from $T = 2\pi\sqrt{l/g}$ with $l = 1.00 \pm 0.01\ \text{m}$, $T = 2.01 \pm 0.02\ \text{s}$.

**Solution:** $g = 4\pi^2 l / T^2$. $\frac{\Delta g}{g} = \frac{\Delta l}{l} + 2\frac{\Delta T}{T} = 0.01 + 0.02 = 0.03$. So $g = 9.8 \pm 0.3\ \text{m/s}^2$.

**Key insight:** The factor of 2 on $\Delta T/T$ comes from the square — power rules multiply relative uncertainty.

---

**Reporting rule:** Uncertainty to 1 sig fig (2 if first digit is 1), best value matched to same decimal place, units always included. Example: $v = 12.3 \pm 0.1\ \text{m/s}$ ✓ — never $12.34 \pm 0.06$ with mismatched decimals.

## Common Mistakes

- ❌ Mixing up accuracy and precision — accuracy is closeness to the true value, precision is repeatability
- ❌ Forgetting to divide uncertainty by n when averaging — if you time 20 oscillations, ΔT = Δt/20, not Δt
- ❌ Reporting uncertainty to more than 1 significant figure — uncertainty should almost always be 1 sig fig
- ❌ Mismatched decimal places between value and uncertainty — if Δx = 0.03, x must be reported to 2 decimal places
