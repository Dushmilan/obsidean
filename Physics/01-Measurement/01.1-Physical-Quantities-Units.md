# 1.1 Physical Quantities & Units

Physics is built on measurement. Every quantity you'll ever encounter — from the speed of light to the charge of an electron — comes down to a number and a unit. Get the units wrong and your answer is meaningless.

**The Intuition:** Think of SI units like a universal language. A kilogram in Colombo is the same kilogram in London. Since 2019, we locked all 7 base units to exact values of fundamental constants — the speed of light, Planck's constant, the elementary charge — so they'll never drift.

**The Math:** Seven base quantities generate everything else through multiplication and division. Force $F = ma$ gives dimensions $[MLT^{-2}]$, which is why the newton is $kg \cdot m \cdot s^{-2}$. Every derived unit decomposes this way. Prefixes attach directly — $km$, $MHz$, $\mu m$ — but never stack ($\mu km$ is illegal).

**What does this mean for Physics?** Dimensional homogeneity: every term in a valid equation must share the same dimensions. If they don't, the equation is wrong. This is your first sanity check before doing any algebra.

| Quantity | Symbol | Unit | Symbol | Definition |
|----------|--------|------|--------|------------|
| Length | $l$ | Metre | m | Distance light travels in $1/299\,792\,458$ s |
| Mass | $m$ | Kilogram | kg | Fixed by $h = 6.626\,070\,15 \times 10^{-34}$ J·s |
| Time | $t$ | Second | s | $9\,192\,631\,770$ periods of Cs-133 |

**Setup:** Find the dimensions of $G$ from $F = G\frac{m_1 m_2}{r^2}$.

**Solution:** Rearrange: $[G] = \frac{[F][r]^2}{[m]^2} = \frac{MLT^{-2} \cdot L^2}{M^2} = M^{-1}L^3T^{-2}$.

**Key insight:** Isolate the unknown, substitute known dimensions, and simplify.

---

**Setup:** Verify $T = 2\pi\sqrt{l/g}$ dimensionally.

**Solution:** $[T] = T$, $\sqrt{[l]/[g]} = \sqrt{L / LT^{-2}} = \sqrt{T^2} = T$. Dimensions match.

**Key insight:** Dimensional analysis can't find the $2\pi$ constant — it only confirms the form.

---

**Setup:** Convert $72\ \text{km/h}$ to m/s.

**Solution:** $72 \times \frac{1000\ \text{m}}{3600\ \text{s}} = 20\ \text{m/s}$.

**Key insight:** Multiply by conversion factors that equal 1 — the units cancel where they should.

## Common Mistakes

- ❌ Confusing M (mega) with m (milli) — capitalisation changes the prefix by a factor of 10⁹
- ❌ Stacking prefixes (e.g. μkm) — only one prefix is allowed per unit
- ❌ Forgetting angle is dimensionless — radians and degrees have no base dimensions
- ❌ Writing m/s⁻¹ instead of m/s or m·s⁻¹ — the negative exponent belongs to the unit, not the fraction
