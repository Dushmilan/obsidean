# 1.3 Scalars & Vectors

Some quantities just need a number — mass, temperature, time. Others need a direction too — displacement, velocity, force. Mixing them up is one of the most common mistakes in physics.

**The Intuition:** A scalar is like a bank balance — just a number. A vector is like an arrow drawn on a map — it has length (magnitude) and points somewhere (direction). You can't add a northward force to an eastward force by just summing their magnitudes; you need geometry.

**The Math:** Vectors add via the triangle or parallelogram law. Break them into components: $\vec{A} = A_x\hat{i} + A_y\hat{j}$, where $A_x = A\cos\theta$ and $A_y = A\sin\theta$. The **dot product** $\vec{A}\cdot\vec{B} = AB\cos\theta$ gives a scalar (used for work). The **cross product** $\vec{A}\times\vec{B} = AB\sin\theta\,\hat{n}$ gives a vector perpendicular to both (used for torque).

**What does this mean for Physics?** Forces, velocities, and fields are vectors. Calculating resultants, components, and moments all depend on vector arithmetic. Getting the direction wrong — especially with cross products and the right-hand rule — is where most errors live.

| Quantity | Type | Dimensions |
|----------|------|------------|
| Displacement $\vec{s}$ | Vector | L |
| Velocity $\vec{v}$ | Vector | $LT^{-1}$ |
| Force $\vec{F}$ | Vector | $MLT^{-2}$ |
| Energy $E$ | Scalar | $ML^2T^{-2}$ |
| Power $P$ | Scalar | $ML^2T^{-3}$ |

---

**Setup:** Three forces act on a point: $\vec{F}_1 = 3\hat{i} + 4\hat{j}$, $\vec{F}_2 = -2\hat{i} + 5\hat{j}$, $\vec{F}_3 = \hat{i} - 6\hat{j}$ (all in N). Find the resultant.

**Solution:** Sum components: $\vec{R} = (3-2+1)\hat{i} + (4+5-6)\hat{j} = 2\hat{i} + 3\hat{j}$ N. Magnitude: $R = \sqrt{4+9} = \sqrt{13}$ N. Direction: $\theta = \tan^{-1}(3/2) \approx 56.3°$.

**Key insight:** Resolving into components turns vector addition into simple arithmetic — add $x$'s, add $y$'s, recombine.

---

**Setup:** Find the angle between $\vec{A} = 2\hat{i} - 3\hat{j} + \hat{k}$ and $\vec{B} = \hat{i} + 2\hat{j} - 2\hat{k}$.

**Solution:** $\vec{A}\cdot\vec{B} = 2(1) + (-3)(2) + 1(-2) = -6$. $A = \sqrt{14}$, $B = 3$. $\cos\theta = \frac{-6}{3\sqrt{14}} \Rightarrow \theta \approx 122°$.

**Key insight:** A negative dot product means the angle is obtuse — the vectors generally point in opposite directions.

---

**Setup:** A $2\ \text{C}$ charge moves at $\vec{v} = 3\hat{i} + 4\hat{j}$ m/s through $\vec{B} = 5\hat{k}$ T. Find the magnetic force.

**Solution:** $\vec{F} = q\vec{v}\times\vec{B} = 2\begin{vmatrix}\hat{i}&\hat{j}&\hat{k}\\3&4&0\\0&0&5\end{vmatrix} = 2(20\hat{i} - 15\hat{j}) = 40\hat{i} - 30\hat{j}$ N.

**Key insight:** The cross product is anti-commutative — swapping $\vec{v}$ and $\vec{B}$ flips the sign. Always use the right-hand rule to check direction.

---

**Dot product shortcuts:** $\vec{A}\cdot\vec{B} = 0$ means perpendicular. $\vec{A}\cdot\vec{A} = A^2$. Component of $\vec{A}$ along $\vec{B}$ is $\frac{\vec{A}\cdot\vec{B}}{B}$.

**Relative velocity:** $\vec{v}_{AB} = \vec{v}_A - \vec{v}_B$. For a river crossing, add the boat's velocity in still water to the river's velocity to get the ground velocity.

## Common Mistakes

- ❌ Adding magnitudes directly instead of resolving components — |A| + |B| is wrong unless the vectors are parallel
- ❌ Forgetting direction in cross product (RHR) — the result is perpendicular to both inputs, direction via right-hand rule
- ❌ Confusing dot product (scalar) with cross product (vector) — dot gives a number, cross gives a vector
- ❌ Using wrong angle in dot/cross product formulas — θ must be the angle between the two vectors when tail-to-tail
