# Scalars & Vectors

## Definition

- **Scalar:** a quantity with magnitude only — mass, temperature, time, energy.
- **Vector:** a quantity with magnitude *and* direction — displacement, velocity, force.

Components: $\vec{A} = A_x\hat{i} + A_y\hat{j}$, with $A_x = A\cos\theta$, $A_y = A\sin\theta$.

**Dot product** $\vec{A}\cdot\vec{B} = AB\cos\theta$ — a scalar (work).
**Cross product** $\vec{A}\times\vec{B} = AB\sin\theta\,\hat{n}$ — a vector ⟂ both (torque, magnetic force).

## The Intuition

A scalar is a bank balance — just a number. A vector is an arrow on a map — length (magnitude) and direction. You can't add a northward force to an eastward force by summing magnitudes; you need geometry.

## The Physics

| Quantity | Type | Dimensions |
|----------|------|------------|
| Displacement $\vec{s}$ | vector | L |
| Velocity $\vec{v}$ | vector | $LT^{-1}$ |
| Force $\vec{F}$ | vector | $MLT^{-2}$ |
| Energy $E$ | scalar | $ML^2T^{-2}$ |
| Power $P$ | scalar | $ML^2T^{-3}$ |

**Shortcuts:** $\vec{A}\cdot\vec{B} = 0$ ⟺ perpendicular. $\vec{A}\cdot\vec{A} = A^2$. Component of $\vec{A}$ along $\vec{B}$: $\frac{\vec{A}\cdot\vec{B}}{B}$. Relative velocity: $\vec{v}_{AB} = \vec{v}_A - \vec{v}_B$.

## Derivation

Vector addition is the triangle/parallelogram law; component resolution follows from the definitions of $\sin,\cos$ on the triangle. The dot product projects one vector onto the other; the cross product magnitude is the parallelogram area, direction by the right-hand rule. [Full derivations: [[01.3-Vector-Identities-Proofs]]]

## Method

1. **Add vectors:** resolve into components, add $x$'s and $y$'s, recombine with $R = \sqrt{R_x^2+R_y^2}$, $\theta = \tan^{-1}(R_y/R_x)$.
2. **Dot product:** angle between vectors / work — $\cos\theta$ with tails together.
3. **Cross product:** determinant + right-hand rule; anti-commutative ($\vec{A}\times\vec{B} = -\vec{B}\times\vec{A}$).

## Worked Examples

**Setup:** $\vec{F}_1 = 3\hat{i}+4\hat{j}$, $\vec{F}_2 = -2\hat{i}+5\hat{j}$, $\vec{F}_3 = \hat{i}-6\hat{j}$ (N). Find the resultant.

**Solution:** $\vec{R} = (3-2+1)\hat{i} + (4+5-6)\hat{j} = 2\hat{i}+3\hat{j}$ N. $R = \sqrt{13}$ N, $\theta = \tan^{-1}(3/2) \approx 56.3°$.

**Key insight:** Components turn vector addition into simple arithmetic.

---

**Setup:** Angle between $\vec{A} = 2\hat{i}-3\hat{j}+\hat{k}$ and $\vec{B} = \hat{i}+2\hat{j}-2\hat{k}$.

**Solution:** $\vec{A}\cdot\vec{B} = 2 - 6 - 2 = -6$; $A = \sqrt{14}$, $B = 3$; $\cos\theta = \frac{-6}{3\sqrt{14}} \Rightarrow \theta \approx 122°$.

**Key insight:** A negative dot product means an obtuse angle — generally opposite directions.

---

**Setup:** A 2 C charge moves at $\vec{v} = 3\hat{i}+4\hat{j}$ m/s through $\vec{B} = 5\hat{k}$ T. Magnetic force?

**Solution:** $\vec{F} = q\vec{v}\times\vec{B} = 2(20\hat{i} - 15\hat{j}) = 40\hat{i} - 30\hat{j}$ N.

**Key insight:** Cross product is anti-commutative — swapping inputs flips the sign; check with the right-hand rule.

## Common Traps

- Adding magnitudes directly instead of resolving components ($|A|+|B|$ only works if parallel)
- Forgetting cross-product direction (right-hand rule) — the result is ⟂ both inputs
- Dot (scalar) vs cross (vector) — dot gives a number, cross a vector
- Using the wrong angle — $\theta$ must be between the vectors tail-to-tail
