# Electromagnetic Induction

## Definition

A changing magnetic flux induces an EMF (Faraday's law, Lenz's law for direction):

$$\mathcal{E} = -\frac{d\Phi}{dt}, \qquad \Phi = BA\cos\theta$$

- **Motional EMF** (moving conductor): $\mathcal{E} = BLv$.
- **Rotating coil** (AC generator): $\mathcal{E} = NBA\omega\sin\omega t$ — peak when flux changes fastest (coil ⊥ field).

## The Intuition

Moving a magnet near a coil "pushes" charge around it — like stirring water with a spoon. Lenz's law says the induced current opposes the change that created it: the coil fights the magnet, which is why pushing a magnet into a coil feels like pushing against a spring.

## The Physics

| Quantity | Equation | Notes |
|----------|----------|-------|
| Flux | $\Phi = BA\cos\theta$ | Wb |
| Faraday | $\mathcal{E} = -N\frac{d\Phi}{dt}$ | V |
| Motional EMF | $\mathcal{E} = BLv$ | moving rod |
| Generator | $\mathcal{E} = NBA\omega\sin\omega t$ | rotating coil |
| Transformer | $\frac{V_s}{V_p} = \frac{N_s}{N_p}$ | coupled coils |

## Derivation

Faraday: EMF is the rate of change of flux linkage $N\Phi$. Motional EMF: the magnetic force on charge carriers in a moving rod separates charge, giving $BLv$. Transformer ratio follows from equal flux per turn. [Full derivations: [[Maths/Pure/04-Calculus/02-Differentiation/04.2-Differentiation]]]

## Method

1. Compute the flux $\Phi = BA\cos\theta$.
2. EMF = rate of change of $N\Phi$ (magnitude); direction by Lenz (opposes the change).
3. Generator: peak $\mathcal{E} = NBA\omega$.

## Worked Examples

**Setup:** A coil (100 turns, 0.01 m²) rotates at 50 Hz in 0.5 T. Peak EMF?

**Solution:** $\omega = 2\pi\times50 = 314$ rad/s. $\mathcal{E}_{max} = 100\times0.5\times0.01\times314 = 157$ V.

**Key insight:** Peak EMF grows with turns, field, area, and frequency — why generators spin at fixed speeds.

---

**Setup:** A 0.4 m rod moves at 5 m/s ⊥ a 0.5 T field. Motional EMF?

**Solution:** $\mathcal{E} = BLv = 0.5\times0.4\times5 = 1.0$ V.

**Key insight:** Only the component of motion ⊥ to both rod and field contributes.

## Common Traps

- Flux vs flux *change* — steady flux gives no EMF
- Lenz's law sign: the induced current opposes the change
- $BA\cos\theta$ — angle between $B$ and the area normal
- Transformer: works on AC only (needs changing flux)
