# 5.5 Electromagnetic Induction

Faraday discovered that a changing magnetic flux through a loop induces an EMF. The faster the change, the bigger the EMF. Lenz's law adds the crucial detail: the induced current always opposes the change that created it. This is nature's way of enforcing conservation of energy.

**The Intuition:** Imagine spinning a magnet inside a coil of wire. As the magnet approaches, the coil "pushes back" (Lenz's law) — like trying to push two north poles together. The coil resists the change in flux. Spin faster, the opposition gets stronger, and the induced voltage increases. This is how generators work: mechanical energy converted to electrical energy through changing flux.

**The Math:**

Faraday's law:

$\mathcal{E} = -N\frac{d\Phi}{dt}$

Magnetic flux through a surface:

$\Phi = BA\cos\theta$

Motional EMF (moving conductor in a field):

$\mathcal{E} = BLv$

For a rotating coil in a uniform field — the basis of AC generators:

$\mathcal{E} = NBA\omega\sin\omega t$

The peak EMF occurs when the coil is perpendicular to the field (flux changing fastest).

**What does this mean for Physics?**

Induction is the bridge between [[05-Electricity-Magnetism/05.4-Magnetic-Fields|magnetism]] and [[05-Electricity-Magnetism/05.3-Current-Electricity|electricity]]. It enables generators, transformers, and [[05-Electricity-Magnetism/05.7-Electromagnetic-Waves|EM wave propagation]]. Maxwell's equations unify this with the fact that changing electric fields also create magnetic fields.

---

**Setup:** A coil has 100 turns, area 0.01 m², rotating in a 0.5 T field at 50 Hz. Find the peak EMF.

**Solution:**

$\omega = 2\pi f = 2\pi \times 50 = 314$ rad/s

$\mathcal{E}_{max} = NBA\omega = 100 \times 0.5 \times 0.01 \times 314 = 157\ \text{V}$

**Key insight:** Peak EMF is proportional to rotation frequency, number of turns, and field strength. This is why power station generators spin at fixed speeds — frequency determines the peak voltage output.
