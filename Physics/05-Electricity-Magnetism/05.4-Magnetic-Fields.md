# Magnetic Fields

## Definition

Forces on moving charges and current-carrying wires:

$$F = qvB\sin\theta, \qquad F = BIL\sin\theta$$

Field around a long straight wire: $B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi r}$, $\mu_0 = 4\pi\times10^{-7}$ T·m/A.

The force is a **cross product** ($\vec{F} = q\vec{v}\times\vec{B}$) — perpendicular to both velocity and field, direction by the right-hand rule.

## The Intuition

A moving charge feels a sideways push when it crosses field lines — like a river current deflecting a swimmer. The force is zero when motion is parallel to the field and maximum when perpendicular. Magnetic forces never do work (they always act ⊥ velocity).

## The Physics

| Quantity | Equation | Notes |
|----------|----------|-------|
| Force on charge | $F = qvB\sin\theta$ | ⊥ velocity |
| Force on wire | $F = BIL\sin\theta$ | ⊥ wire |
| Long wire field | $B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi r}$ | surrounds wire |
| Circular motion | $r = \frac{mv}{qB}$ | charge orbits |

## Derivation

$B = \mu_0 I/2\pi r$ follows from Ampère's law for a straight wire. Circular orbits: the magnetic force is always ⊥ velocity, so it acts as a centripetal force $qvB = mv^2/r$. [Full derivations: [[Maths/Applied/04-Circular-Motion/04.1-Uniform-Circular-Motion]]]

## Method

1. Right-hand rule for direction (force on positive charge).
2. $\sin\theta$ — use the component of $v$ (or $I$) ⊥ to $B$.
3. Particle in a field: $r = mv/(qB)$ for circular motion.

## Worked Examples

**Setup:** A 0.5 m wire carries 10 A in a 0.2 T field ⊥ the wire. Force?

**Solution:** $F = BIL\sin90° = 0.2\times10\times0.5 = 1.0$ N.

**Key insight:** $\sin\theta = 0$ when parallel — orientation matters completely.

---

**Setup:** A proton ($m = 1.67\times10^{-27}$ kg) at $10^6$ m/s ⊥ a 0.1 T field. Orbit radius?

**Solution:** $r = \frac{mv}{qB} = \frac{1.67\times10^{-27}\times10^6}{1.6\times10^{-19}\times0.1} \approx 0.10$ m.

**Key insight:** Cyclotrons and mass spectrometers exploit this — heavier or faster particles curve less.

## Common Traps

- Magnetic force does **zero work** (⊥ to displacement) — it can't speed particles up, only steer them
- Right-hand rule is for positive charge; electrons flip it
- $B$ from a wire wraps around it (field lines are circles)
- $\sin\theta$ dropped when velocity isn't ⊥ field
