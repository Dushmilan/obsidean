# 5.3 Current Electricity

Current is charge in motion. Every wire in every circuit carries a flow of electrons driven by a potential difference. Ohm's law — $V = IR$ — is deceptively simple but only applies to ohmic conductors where resistance stays constant regardless of voltage.

**The Intuition:** Current through a wire is like water through a pipe. Voltage is the pressure difference pushing the flow. Resistance is friction — narrow pipe, rough walls, more resistance. Ohm's law says: double the pressure, double the flow rate. But non-ohmic devices (like diodes) are like one-way valves — the relationship breaks down.

**The Math:**

Ohm's law and its extensions:

$V = IR$

Resistance of a wire depends on its material and dimensions:

$R = \frac{\rho L}{A}$

where $\rho$ is resistivity, $L$ is length, $A$ is cross-sectional area. Power dissipated:

$P = IV = I^2R = \frac{V^2}{R}$

Kirchhoff's laws govern circuits: junction rule $\sum I_{in} = \sum I_{out}$ and loop rule $\sum V = 0$.

**What does this mean for Physics?**

Current electricity is the foundation of all circuit theory and connects to [[05-Electricity-Magnetism/05.1-Electric-Fields|electric fields]] (the driving force), [[05-Electricity-Magnetism/05.4-Magnetic-Fields|magnetism]] (currents create magnetic fields), and [[05-Electricity-Magnetism/05.8-Electrical-Measurements|measurement techniques]]. The power formulas explain why wires heat up and why high-voltage transmission reduces energy loss.

**Setup:** Three resistors (2Ω, 3Ω, 6Ω) are connected in parallel. Find the equivalent resistance.

**Solution:**

$\frac{1}{R} = \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{6} = \frac{3+2+1}{6} = 1$

$R = 1\ \Omega$

**Key insight:** Parallel resistors always combine to a value smaller than the smallest individual resistor. Adding more paths reduces overall resistance — more routes for current means less total opposition.
