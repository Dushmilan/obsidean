# Current Electricity

## Definition

Charge flow: $I = \frac{Q}{t}$. Ohm's law: $V = IR$. Resistance from geometry:

$$R = \frac{\rho L}{A}, \qquad P = IV = I^2R = \frac{V^2}{R}$$

**Kirchhoff's laws:** junction rule $\sum I_{in} = \sum I_{out}$ (charge conserved); loop rule $\sum V = 0$ (energy conserved).

## The Intuition

Current is a flow of charge; voltage is the pressure pushing it; resistance is the pipe's narrowness. A garden hose analogy: voltage = water pressure, current = flow rate, resistance = hose diameter. High-voltage transmission reduces loss because $P_{loss} = I^2R$ — step up the voltage, drop the current, keep the power.

## The Physics

| Quantity | Equation | Units |
|----------|----------|-------|
| Ohm's law | $V = IR$ | V |
| Resistance | $R = \frac{\rho L}{A}$ | Ω |
| Power | $P = IV = I^2R = \frac{V^2}{R}$ | W |
| Series | $R_{eq} = \sum R_i$ | Ω |
| Parallel | $\frac{1}{R_{eq}} = \sum\frac{1}{R_i}$ | Ω |

## Derivation

Ohm's law is empirical for ohmic materials ($I \propto V$ at constant $T$). $R = \rho L/A$ follows from the drift-velocity model of conduction; power from the work $VQ$ per unit time. Kirchhoff's laws are charge and energy conservation. [Full derivations: [[Maths/Pure/01-Algebra/04-Polynomials/04-Polynomials]]]

## Method

1. Combine series/parallel resistors first.
2. Apply Kirchhoff: one loop equation per independent loop, junction equations for nodes.
3. Power: pick $I^2R$ (knows $I$) or $V^2/R$ (knows $V$).

## Worked Examples

**Setup:** 2 Ω, 3 Ω, 6 Ω in parallel. Equivalent?

**Solution:** $\frac1R = \frac12+\frac13+\frac16 = 1 \Rightarrow R = 1$ Ω.

**Key insight:** Parallel always gives *less* than the smallest resistor.

---

**Setup:** A 60 W bulb on 240 V. Current and resistance?

**Solution:** $I = P/V = 0.25$ A; $R = V^2/P = 240^2/60 = 960$ Ω.

**Key insight:** Power formulas are interchangeable — pick the pair that fits the knowns.

## Common Traps

- Series/parallel formulas flipped (compare with capacitors — opposite!)
- Internal resistance of cells: $V_{terminal} = \mathcal{E} - Ir$
- Ohm's law fails for non-ohmic devices (diodes, filaments at changing temperature)
- Kirchhoff sign conventions — a wrong loop direction flips the sum
