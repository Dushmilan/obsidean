# 5.2 Capacitors

A capacitor is two conductors separated by an insulator — simple construction, powerful behaviour. It stores energy in the electric field between its plates. The key parameter is capacitance, which depends on geometry and the material between the plates, not on voltage or charge.

**The Intuition:** Think of a capacitor like a rechargeable water tank. The plates are two walls, the dielectric is the space between them. Push water (charge) in, pressure builds (voltage). The tank's size (capacitance) determines how much water it holds at a given pressure. Bigger tank = more water at the same pressure.

**The Math:**

Capacitance is defined as charge per unit voltage:

$C = \frac{Q}{V}$

Energy stored in a capacitor:

$E = \frac{1}{2}CV^2 = \frac{1}{2}QV = \frac{Q^2}{2C}$

For capacitors in combination — series adds reciprocally, parallel adds directly (opposite of resistors):

$\frac{1}{C_{eq}} = \frac{1}{C_1} + \frac{1}{C_2}$ (series)

$C_{eq} = C_1 + C_2$ (parallel)

**What does this mean for Physics?**

Capacitors bridge electrostatics and circuits. They appear in [[05-Electricity-Magnetism/05.1-Electric-Fields|electric field]] problems, [[05-Electricity-Magnetism/05.6-AC-Circuits|AC circuits]] (reactance), timing circuits (RC time constants), and energy storage. The energy formula $E = \frac{1}{2}CV^2$ shows energy scales with voltage squared — important for high-voltage applications.

---

**Setup:** A 10 μF capacitor is charged to 100 V. Find the energy stored.

**Solution:**

$E = \frac{1}{2}CV^2 = \frac{1}{2} \times 10 \times 10^{-6} \times (100)^2 = 0.05\ \text{J}$

**Key insight:** Energy scales with $V^2$, so doubling voltage quadruples stored energy. This is why capacitors in camera flashes can deliver enormous power in a short burst.
