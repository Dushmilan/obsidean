# Capacitors

## Definition

A capacitor stores charge: $Q = CV$. Stored energy:

$$E = \frac12 CV^2 = \frac12 QV = \frac{Q^2}{2C}$$

**Combinations:** series $\frac{1}{C_{eq}} = \sum\frac{1}{C_i}$; parallel $C_{eq} = \sum C_i$ (opposite of resistors).

## The Intuition

A capacitor is a water tank: charge is the water level, voltage the height. A big capacitance means a wide tank — lots of charge per volt. The energy $E = \frac12 CV^2$ explains why camera flashes (high voltage, small capacitor) release huge power in a burst.

## The Physics

| Quantity | Equation | Units |
|----------|----------|-------|
| Capacitance | $Q = CV$ | F = C/V |
| Energy | $E = \frac12 CV^2$ | J |
| Parallel plate | $C = \frac{\epsilon_0 A}{d}$ | F |
| RC time constant | $\tau = RC$ | s |

## Derivation

$C = Q/V$ is the definition; for a parallel plate, Gauss's law gives $C = \epsilon_0 A/d$. Energy: the work to build up charge $\int V\,dQ = \int \frac{Q}{C}dQ = \frac12 \frac{Q^2}{C}$. [Full derivations: [[Maths/Pure/04-Calculus/04-Integration/04.4-Integration]]]

## Method

1. Series/parallel: combine the opposite way to resistors.
2. Energy: pick the form matching your knowns ($Q, V$, or $C$).
3. RC circuits: $Q(t) = Q_0(1-e^{-t/RC})$ charging; discharge $Q = Q_0e^{-t/RC}$.

## Worked Examples

**Setup:** A 10 μF capacitor charged to 100 V. Energy?

**Solution:** $E = \frac12(10^{-5})(100)^2 = 0.05$ J.

**Key insight:** Energy scales with $V^2$ — doubling voltage quadruples stored energy.

---

**Setup:** Two 10 μF capacitors in series, then in parallel. Equivalent values?

**Solution:** Series: $C_{eq} = 5$ μF. Parallel: $C_{eq} = 20$ μF.

**Key insight:** Series reduces (same charge, divided voltage); parallel adds (charge divides by voltage).

## Common Traps

- Series/parallel formulas are the *opposite* of resistors — easy to flip
- $E = \frac12 QV$ needs $Q$ in coulombs
- Dielectric changes $C$ by the factor $\kappa$ ($C' = \kappa C$)
- After disconnecting the battery, charge stays constant; connected, voltage stays constant
