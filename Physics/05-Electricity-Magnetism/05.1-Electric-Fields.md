# Electric Fields

## Definition

The force per unit charge — how charges interact at a distance:

$$E = \frac{F}{q} = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{Q}{r^2} \quad (\text{point charge})$$

Electric potential: $V = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{Q}{r}$. Fields obey **superposition** — the total field is the vector sum of all charges' fields.

## The Intuition

A charge creates a "slope" in space; other charges feel a force along it. Potential is the height of the slope; field is its steepness. The field concept replaced "action at a distance" and became central to all field theory.

## The Physics

| Quantity | Equation | Units |
|----------|----------|-------|
| Field (point charge) | $E = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{Q}{r^2}$ | N/C = V/m |
| Force | $F = qE = \frac{q_1q_2}{4\pi\epsilon_0 r^2}$ | N |
| Potential | $V = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{Q}{r}$ | V |
| Uniform field | $E = \frac{V}{d}$ | V/m |
| $k$ value | $k = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0} = 9\times10^9$ | N·m²/C² |

## Derivation

Coulomb's law is the experimental inverse-square law; the field is force per unit charge, and potential is work per unit charge ($V = \int E\,dr$). Superposition follows from forces adding as vectors. [Full derivations: [[Maths/Pure/01-Algebra/02-Indices/02-Indices]]]

## Method

1. Draw field lines: out of +, into −, density = strength.
2. Force between two charges: $F = kQ_1Q_2/r^2$ — opposite signs attract.
3. Superposition: add field vectors component-wise.

## Worked Examples

**Setup:** Charges $+2\mu$C and $-3\mu$C are 0.1 m apart. Force between them?

**Solution:** $F = 9\times10^9\times\frac{2\times10^{-6}\times3\times10^{-6}}{(0.1)^2} = 5.4$ N, attractive.

**Key insight:** The $r^2$ means doubling distance quarters the force — fields fall off fast.

## Common Traps

- Field is a vector (direction = force on a *positive* test charge); potential is a scalar
- $E = V/d$ only for uniform fields
- Superposition must be vector addition, not magnitude addition
- $k = 9\times10^9$ but $\epsilon_0$ appears squared in some formulas — watch the form
