# 5.1 Electric Fields

Electric charges don't just sit there doing nothing — they create invisible fields that reach out and push or pull other charges. Coulomb figured out the math back in the 1780s, and it still holds up. An electric field is simply the force a charge would feel per unit charge placed at that point in space.

**The Intuition:** Imagine throwing a ball uphill on an invisible slope. The slope exists because of how the ground is shaped (like mass warps space). Similarly, a charge creates an invisible "slope" around it. Another charge placed nearby feels a force — pushed down the slope if it's the same sign, pulled up if opposite. The field is the steepness of that slope at every point.

**The Math:**

Coulomb's law gives the force between two point charges:

$F = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{q_1 q_2}{r^2}$

where $\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0} = 9 \times 10^9$ N·m²/C². The field strength at a point is force per unit charge:

$E = \frac{F}{q} = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{Q}{r^2}$

Electric potential — the "height" of the slope — is:

$V = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{Q}{r}$

Fields obey superposition: total field at any point is the vector sum of fields from all charges.

**What does this mean for Physics?**

Electric fields are the mechanism by which charges interact at a distance without touching. This concept extends into [[05-Electricity-Magnetism/05.2-Capacitors|capacitors]], [[05-Electricity-Magnetism/05.3-Current-Electricity|current flow]], and ultimately [[05-Electricity-Magnetism/05.7-Electromagnetic-Waves|electromagnetic waves]]. The field concept replaced the older "action at a distance" idea and became central to field theory.

---

**Setup:** Two charges ($+2\ \mu$C and $-3\ \mu$C) are 0.1 m apart. Find the force between them.

**Solution:**

$F = 9 \times 10^9 \times \frac{2 \times 10^{-6} \times 3 \times 10^{-6}}{(0.1)^2} = 5.4\ \text{N (attractive)}$

The negative signs in the charge product cancel because we're taking magnitudes — the opposite signs tell us it's attractive.

**Key insight:** The $r^2$ in the denominator means doubling the distance quarters the force. Fields fall off fast.
