# 5.6 AC Circuits

In AC circuits, voltage and current oscillate sinusoidally. Capacitors and inductors introduce phase shifts — current leads voltage in a capacitor, lags in an inductor. The total opposition to current becomes impedance, which combines resistance and reactance using phasors, not simple addition.

**The Intuition:** Think of AC current as water sloshing back and forth in a pipe. A resistor is like a constriction — it slows the flow proportionally. A capacitor is like a flexible membrane — it stretches and contracts, letting alternating flow pass but blocking DC. An inductor is like a heavy flywheel — it resists changes in flow, smoothing out rapid oscillations. At resonance, the membrane and flywheel perfectly cancel, and current flows freely.

**The Math:**

Capacitive and inductive reactance:

$X_C = \frac{1}{2\pi fC}$, $X_L = 2\pi fL$

Impedance (phasor combination):

$Z = \sqrt{R^2 + (X_L - X_C)^2}$

RMS values for power calculations:

$I_{rms} = \frac{I_0}{\sqrt{2}}$, $V_{rms} = \frac{V_0}{\sqrt{2}}$

Resonant frequency — where $X_L = X_C$ and impedance is minimum:

$f_0 = \frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{LC}}$

**What does this mean for Physics?**

AC circuits connect [[05-Electricity-Magnetism/05.3-Current-Electricity|DC theory]] to [[05-Electricity-Magnetism/05.5-Electromagnetic-Induction|generators]] and [[05-Electricity-Magnetism/05.8-Electrical-Measurements|measurement instruments]]. Impedance matching is critical in signal processing, telecommunications, and power transmission. Resonance underlies radio tuning, MRI machines, and wireless charging.

---

**Setup:** A series RLC circuit has $R = 100\ \Omega$, $L = 0.1$ H, $C = 100\ \mu$F at 50 Hz. Find the impedance.

**Solution:**

$X_L = 2\pi \times 50 \times 0.1 = 31.4\ \Omega$

$X_C = \frac{1}{2\pi \times 50 \times 100 \times 10^{-6}} = 31.8\ \Omega$

$Z = \sqrt{100^2 + (31.4 - 31.8)^2} \approx 100\ \Omega$

**Key insight:** When $X_L \approx X_C$, the reactive components nearly cancel and impedance equals resistance. Near resonance, even small resistance can produce large currents — useful for tuning circuits, dangerous for power systems.
