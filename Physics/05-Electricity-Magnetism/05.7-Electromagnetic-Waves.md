# Electromagnetic Waves (in this unit)

## Definition

Maxwell's prediction — oscillating electric and magnetic fields that sustain each other:

$$c = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu_0\epsilon_0}} = 3\times10^8\ \text{m/s}, \qquad c = f\lambda, \qquad B_0 = \frac{E_0}{c}$$

Intensity: $I = \frac{E_0^2}{2\mu_0 c}$ (power per unit area).

## The Intuition

A changing electric field creates a magnetic field, which in turn creates an electric field — a self-sustaining ripple that needs no medium. The speed isn't arbitrary: it's determined by two measurable properties of empty space ($\mu_0, \epsilon_0$). That's how Maxwell knew light was electromagnetic.

## The Physics

| Quantity | Equation | Notes |
|----------|----------|-------|
| Speed | $c = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu_0\epsilon_0}}$ | vacuum |
| Wave relation | $c = f\lambda$ | — |
| Field ratio | $B_0 = \frac{E_0}{c}$ | E and B in phase |
| Intensity | $I = \frac{E_0^2}{2\mu_0 c}$ | W/m² |

## Derivation

Maxwell's equations combine Faraday's law and the displacement current to produce a wave equation whose speed is $1/\sqrt{\mu_0\epsilon_0}$. Substituting the measured constants yields exactly $c$ — the identification of light as an EM wave. [Full derivations: [[05.5-Electromagnetic-Induction]]]

## Method

1. Use $c = f\lambda$ to link frequency and wavelength.
2. Field amplitudes: $E_0 = cB_0$.
3. Intensity scales as $E_0^2$.

## Worked Examples

**Setup:** Verify $c$ from $\mu_0 = 4\pi\times10^{-7}$ and $\epsilon_0 = 8.85\times10^{-12}$.

**Solution:** $c = \frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi\times10^{-7}\times8.85\times10^{-12}}} = 3.0\times10^8$ m/s.

**Key insight:** Light isn't arbitrary — it's locked to two constants of free space.

---

**Setup:** An EM wave has $E_0 = 300$ V/m. Find $B_0$.

**Solution:** $B_0 = E_0/c = 300/3\times10^8 = 10^{-6}$ T.

**Key insight:** $B_0$ is tiny compared to $E_0$ in SI units — the $c$ ratio.

## Common Traps

- $E_0 = cB_0$ — fields are *in phase*, ratio exactly $c$
- $c$ only in vacuum; slower in materials
- Intensity ∝ $E_0^2$, not $E_0$
- Don't confuse with [[04-Waves-Optics/04.6-Electromagnetic-Waves]] (spectrum) — this is the mechanism
