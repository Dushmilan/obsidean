# 5.8 Electrical Measurements

Precision electrical measurements rely on null methods — techniques where you adjust a circuit until a detector reads zero. This eliminates errors from the measuring instrument itself, because at the null point, no current flows through the detector. The potentiometer and Wheatstone bridge are the classic examples.

**The Intuition:** Think of balancing a see-saw. When perfectly balanced, the pivot carries no net torque — you can measure forces without disturbing the system. A null method works the same way: adjust until the detector shows zero, then calculate from the known components. The beauty is that the detector doesn't need to be accurate — it just needs to detect "zero vs not zero."

**The Math:**

Wheatstone bridge balance condition:

$\frac{R_1}{R_2} = \frac{R_3}{R_4}$

Potentiometer principle (uniform wire):

$V \propto l$

Unknown EMF from potentiometer balance:

$EMF = \frac{l_1}{l_2} \times V_{std}$

where $l_1$ is the balance length for the unknown EMF and $l_2$ is the balance length for a standard EMF.

**What does this mean for Physics?**

Measurement techniques connect to [[05-Electricity-Magnetism/05.3-Current-Electricity|circuit analysis]] and [[01-Measurement/01.2-Errors-Uncertainties|error analysis]]. Null methods are used in precision resistance measurement, EMF determination, and sensor calibration. The Wheatstone bridge principle extends to strain gauges, temperature sensors, and pressure transducers.

---

**Setup:** A Wheatstone bridge has $R_1 = 100\ \Omega$, $R_2 = 200\ \Omega$, $R_3 = 150\ \Omega$. Find $R_4$ at balance.

**Solution:**

At balance: $\frac{R_1}{R_2} = \frac{R_3}{R_4}$

$R_4 = \frac{R_2 \times R_3}{R_1} = \frac{200 \times 150}{100} = 300\ \Omega$

**Key insight:** The bridge measures resistance by comparison, not by absolute measurement. This makes it independent of the source voltage and detector calibration — only the ratio matters.
