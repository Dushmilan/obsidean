# Electrical Measurements

## Definition

Precision methods for measuring EMF and resistance using **null methods** — no current flows at balance, so the reading is independent of meter calibration:

- **Potentiometer:** $EMF \propto l$ (balance length). Unknown: $EMF = \frac{l_1}{l_2}V_{std}$.
- **Wheatstone bridge:** at balance $\frac{R_1}{R_2} = \frac{R_3}{R_4}$.

## The Intuition

A bridge measures by *comparison*, not absolute measurement. At balance, the detector carries no current, so the source voltage and detector calibration don't matter — only the resistance ratio. This is why null methods are the gold standard for precision.

## The Physics

| Instrument | Balance condition | Measures |
|------------|-------------------|----------|
| Potentiometer | $V \propto l$ | EMF without drawing current |
| Wheatstone bridge | $\frac{R_1}{R_2} = \frac{R_3}{R_4}$ | resistance |
| Ammeter (in series) | — | current |
| Voltmeter (in parallel) | — | potential difference |

## Derivation

Potentiometer: a uniform wire gives $V = \lambda l$; comparing balance lengths ratios the EMFs. Wheatstone: at balance, no current through the galvanometer implies equal potential drops, giving the ratio equation. [Full derivations: [[Maths/Pure/01-Algebra/04-Polynomials/04-Polynomials]]]

## Method

1. Wheatstone: arrange $R_1/R_2 = R_3/R_4$, solve for the unknown.
2. Potentiometer: measure both balance lengths, ratio against the standard.
3. Meters: ammeter in series (low resistance), voltmeter in parallel (high resistance).

## Worked Examples

**Setup:** Wheatstone with $R_1 = 100\ \Omega$, $R_2 = 200\ \Omega$, $R_3 = 150\ \Omega$. Find $R_4$ at balance.

**Solution:** $R_4 = \frac{R_2R_3}{R_1} = \frac{200\times150}{100} = 300\ \Omega$.

**Key insight:** Only the ratio matters — the bridge is immune to supply fluctuations.

---

**Setup:** A potentiometer balances a standard 1.5 V cell at 60 cm and an unknown cell at 90 cm.

**Solution:** $EMF = \frac{90}{60}\times1.5 = 2.25$ V.

**Key insight:** The potentiometer draws *no* current from the cell — ideal EMF measurement.

## Common Traps

- Ammeter must be in series, voltmeter in parallel — reversed and meters burn out
- Ideal meters: ammeter $R \to 0$, voltmeter $R \to \infty$
- Balance condition assumes a uniform wire (potentiometer)
- Ratio direction in the bridge — keep $R_1/R_2 = R_3/R_4$ consistent
