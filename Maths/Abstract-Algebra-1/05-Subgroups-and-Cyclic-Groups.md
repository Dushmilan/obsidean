# Subgroups & Cyclic Groups

A subgroup is a group living inside a group — a smaller universe of moves that closes on itself. Cyclic groups are the simplest possible groups: everything generated by repeatedly applying a single element. Together they answer the first structural question of algebra: *what does the inside of a group look like?*

**The Intuition:** Think of rotational symmetries of a square sitting inside all symmetries of a square: flip-free rotations compose to give only rotations, include "do nothing", and each rotation undoes another. That's a subgroup — self-sufficient. A cyclic group is a single move iterated: keep turning a key $90°$ and you cycle through exactly four positions before returning home. The element's "reach" is its order, and every element's reach is always a tidy, closed loop.

**The Math:**

- **Subgroup:** $H \subseteq G$ is a subgroup, written $H \le G$, if $H$ is itself a group under the same operation. Necessary checks:
  - $H \neq \emptyset$ (usually: $e \in H$)
  - **Closure under products:** $a, b \in H \Rightarrow ab \in H$
  - **Closure under inverses:** $a \in H \Rightarrow a^{-1} \in H$
- **One-Step Subgroup Test:** a non-empty subset $H \subseteq G$ is a subgroup iff $ab^{-1} \in H$ for all $a, b \in H$. (Packs both closures into one condition.)
- **Cyclic groups:** $G = \langle a \rangle = \{a^k : k \in \mathbb{Z}\}$ for some generator $a$. If $a$ has finite order $n$, then
$$\langle a \rangle = \{e, a, a^2, \dots, a^{n-1}\}$$
and $a^k = e \iff n \mid k$. If no power returns to $e$, the powers are all distinct and $\langle a \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}$.
- **Order facts:** $|a| = |\langle a \rangle|$; in a finite group, $a^{|G|} = e$; the order of any element divides... (in this course: divides the size of the cyclic subgroup it generates).
- **Subgroups of $\mathbb{Z}$:** every subgroup of $(\mathbb{Z}, +)$ is $n\mathbb{Z}$ for some $n \ge 0$.
- **Subgroups of $\mathbb{Z}_n$:** one per divisor $d$ of $n$, namely $\langle [d] \rangle$, with $n/d$ elements.

**Setup:** Prove that every subgroup $H$ of $(\mathbb{Z}, +)$ equals $n\mathbb{Z}$ for some $n$.

**Solution:** If $H = \{0\}$, take $n = 0$. Otherwise pick $h \in H$, $h \neq 0$; since inverses lie in $H$, assume WLOG $h > 0$. By the division algorithm write $h = qn + r$, $0 \le r < n$, where $n$ is the *smallest positive* element of $H$. Then $r = h - qn = h + (-qn) \in H$ by closure, but $r < n$ contradicts minimality unless $r = 0$. So $n \mid h$ for every $h \in H$, i.e. $H = n\mathbb{Z}$.

**Key insight:** The division algorithm + minimality argument is *the* standard weapon for classifying subgroups of cyclic structures — remember the pattern, reuse the pattern.

**Setup:** Find all subgroups of $\mathbb{Z}_{12}$.

**Solution:** One per divisor of $12$: divisors are $1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12$.
$\langle[1]\rangle = \mathbb{Z}_{12}$ (12 elements), $\langle[2]\rangle = \{0,2,4,6,8,10\}$ (6), $\langle[3]\rangle = \{0,3,6,9\}$ (4), $\langle[4]\rangle = \{0,4,8\}$ (3), $\langle[6]\rangle = \{0,6\}$ (2), $\langle[12]\rangle = \langle[0]\rangle = \{0\}$ (1).

**Key insight:** Divisors of $n$ enumerate subgroups of $\mathbb{Z}_n$ completely — no hunting needed, just arithmetic.

**Setup:** In $\mathbb{Z}_{12}$, find all generators and compute the order of $[8]$.

**Solution:** Generators are classes coprime to 12: $[1], [5], [7], [11]$. Order of $[8]$: find least $k$ with $12 \mid 8k$. $\gcd(8,12) = 4$, so $|[8]| = 12/\gcd(8,12) = 3$; indeed $\langle[8]\rangle = \{0, 8, 4\}$. ✓

**Key insight:** In $\mathbb{Z}_n$, the order of $[k]$ is always $n / \gcd(k, n)$ — compute orders without listing anything.

### Additional Notes

Why care about cyclic groups beyond simplicity? Because they are the atoms of element behavior: every element of *any* group generates a cyclic subgroup, so studying cycles inside $\mathbb{Z}_n$ teaches you about every group at once. When you later meet cosets and Lagrange's theorem (the natural next chapter after this course), the payoff is precise: in a finite group, every element's order divides $|G|$ — the statement this note's counting already hints at.
