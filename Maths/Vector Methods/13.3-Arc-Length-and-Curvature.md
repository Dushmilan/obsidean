# Arc Length and Curvature

Arc length measures the total distance along a space curve; curvature measures how sharply the curve bends. Once you know the arc length $s$ along a curve, you can reparameterize by $s$, which simplifies many formulas.

**The Intuition:** Arc length is the odometer reading of a car driving along a curve. Curvature is how much you have to turn the steering wheel — a straight road has $\kappa = 0$, a tight circle has high $\kappa$. The TNB frame is the set of three arrows attached to you: one pointing forward ($\mathbf{T}$), one pointing toward the center of your turn ($\mathbf{N}$), and one pointing "up" perpendicular to both ($\mathbf{B}$).

**The Math:** For a curve $\mathbf{r}(t)$ with $\mathbf{r}'(t) \neq \mathbf{0}$:

- **Arc length:** $L = \int_a^b \|\mathbf{r}'(t)\|\,dt$
- **Arc length function:** $s(t) = \int_a^t \|\mathbf{r}'(u)\|\,du$
- **Curvature (definition):** $\kappa = \left\|\dfrac{d\mathbf{T}}{ds}\right\|$
- **Curvature (chain rule):** $\kappa(t) = \dfrac{\|\mathbf{T}'(t)\|}{\|\mathbf{r}'(t)\|}$
- **Curvature (cross product):** $\kappa = \dfrac{\|\mathbf{r}' \times \mathbf{r}''\|}{\|\mathbf{r}'\|^3}$ — the most practical formula
- **Unit tangent:** $\mathbf{T} = \dfrac{\mathbf{r}'}{\|\mathbf{r}'\|}$
- **Principal normal:** $\mathbf{N} = \dfrac{\mathbf{T}'}{\|\mathbf{T}'\|}$
- **Binormal:** $\mathbf{B} = \mathbf{T} \times \mathbf{N}$



**Setup:** Find the arc length of $\mathbf{r}(t) = \langle \cos t, \sin t, t \rangle$ for $0 \leq t \leq 2\pi$.

**Solution:** $\|\mathbf{r}'(t)\| = \sqrt{\sin^2 t + \cos^2 t + 1} = \sqrt{2}$. Since speed is constant, $L = 2\pi\sqrt{2}$.

**Key insight:** When $\|\mathbf{r}'(t)\|$ is constant, arc length is just speed $\times$ time interval.

**Setup:** Find the curvature of $\mathbf{r}(t) = \langle t, t^2, t^3 \rangle$ at $t = 1$.

**Solution:** $\mathbf{r}' = \langle 1, 2, 3 \rangle$, $\mathbf{r}'' = \langle 0, 2, 6 \rangle$. $\mathbf{r}' \times \mathbf{r}'' = \langle 6, -6, 2 \rangle$, $\|\mathbf{r}' \times \mathbf{r}''\| = 2\sqrt{19}$. $\|\mathbf{r}'\|^3 = 14\sqrt{14}$. $\kappa = \dfrac{\sqrt{19}}{7\sqrt{14}}$.

**Key insight:** Compute $\mathbf{r}'$ and $\mathbf{r}''$ at the point of interest first, then plug into the formula.

**Setup:** Find $\mathbf{T}$, $\mathbf{N}$, $\mathbf{B}$ for $\mathbf{r}(t) = \langle R\cos t, R\sin t, 0 \rangle$.

**Solution:** $\mathbf{T} = \langle -\sin t, \cos t, 0 \rangle$. $\mathbf{N} = \langle -\cos t, -\sin t, 0 \rangle$ (points toward center). $\mathbf{B} = \langle 0, 0, 1 \rangle$ (out of plane).

**Key insight:** For planar curves, $\mathbf{B}$ is always perpendicular to the plane — constant direction.
