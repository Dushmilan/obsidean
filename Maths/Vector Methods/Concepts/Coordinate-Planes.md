# Coordinate Planes

> **Stewart Calculus, Chapter 12, Section 12.1**

## Definition
The three **coordinate planes** are the planes formed by pairs of the coordinate axes. They divide three-dimensional space into octants. The three coordinate planes are:
- **$xy$-plane:** contains the $x$-axis and $y$-axis; equation $z = 0$
- **$yz$-plane:** contains the $y$-axis and $z$-axis; equation $x = 0$
- **$xz$-plane:** contains the $x$-axis and $z$-axis; equation $y = 0$

## Key Properties
- Each coordinate plane contains two of the three coordinate axes and is perpendicular to the third.
- The three planes intersect pairwise along the coordinate axes and meet at the origin.
- A point lies on a coordinate plane if and only if its coordinate corresponding to the missing axis is zero.
- The coordinate planes partition space into eight octants.

## Worked Example
**Find which coordinate plane the point $(4, 0, -3)$ lies on.**

1. The $y$-coordinate is $0$.
2. A point with $y = 0$ lies in the $xz$-plane by definition.
3. Therefore, $(4, 0, -3)$ lies in the **$xz$-plane**.
