# Right-Hand Rule for Coordinate Systems

> **Stewart Calculus, Chapter 12, Section 12.1**

## Definition
The **right-hand rule** is a convention that determines the direction of the positive $z$-axis relative to the positive $x$-axis and positive $y$-axis. A coordinate system is **right-handed** if, when you curl the fingers of your right hand from the positive $x$-axis toward the positive $y$-axis, your thumb points in the direction of the positive $z$-axis.

## Key Properties
- In a right-handed system, if fingers of the right hand curl from $+x$ to $+y$, the thumb points in the $+z$ direction.
- The cross product $\mathbf{i} \times \mathbf{j} = \mathbf{k}$ holds only in a right-handed system.
- Most standard mathematical and physics conventions assume right-handed coordinates.
- A left-handed system reverses the $z$-axis direction; the right-hand rule fails for such systems.

## Worked Example
**Determine if a system where $+x$ points right, $+y$ points up, and $+z$ points out of the page is right-handed.**

1. Point the fingers of your right hand in the $+x$ direction (right).
2. Curl them toward the $+y$ direction (up).
3. Your thumb points out of the page, which is the $+z$ direction.
4. Since the thumb matches the given $+z$ direction, the system is **right-handed**. ✓
