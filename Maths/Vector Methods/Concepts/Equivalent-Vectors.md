# Equivalent Vectors

> **Stewart Calculus, Chapter 12, Section 12.2**

## Definition
Two vectors are **equivalent** (equal) if and only if they have the same components. Position in space does not matter — vectors are **free vectors**.

$$\mathbf{u} = \mathbf{v} \iff \langle u_1, u_2, u_3 \rangle = \langle v_1, v_2, v_3 \rangle$$

## Key Properties
- Equality requires identical magnitude **and** identical direction
- Equivalent vectors may have different initial/terminal points
- All zero vectors are equivalent to each other

## Worked Example
$\mathbf{u} = \overrightarrow{AB}$ where $A(0,0,0)$, $B(1,2,3)$ gives $\mathbf{u} = \langle 1,2,3 \rangle$

$\mathbf{v} = \overrightarrow{CD}$ where $C(2,1,0)$, $D(3,3,3)$ gives $\mathbf{v} = \langle 1,2,3 \rangle$

Since $\mathbf{u} = \langle 1,2,3 \rangle = \mathbf{v}$, the vectors are equivalent despite having different positions.
