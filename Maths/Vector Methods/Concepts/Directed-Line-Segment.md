# Directed Line Segment

> **Stewart Calculus, Chapter 12, Section 12.2**

## Definition
A **directed line segment** is the geometric representation of a vector as an arrow from an **initial point** (tail) to a **terminal point** (tip).

- **Notation:** $\overrightarrow{AB}$ denotes the directed segment from point $A$ to point $B$
- $A$ is the **initial point** (tail), $B$ is the **terminal point** (tip)

## Key Properties
- Two directed line segments represent the **same vector** if they have identical magnitude and direction
- Direction matters: $\overrightarrow{AB} \neq \overrightarrow{BA}$ (they are negatives of each other)
- Length of the arrow encodes the magnitude of the vector

## Worked Example
For points $A(1, 0, 2)$ and $B(3, 4, 0)$:
$$\overrightarrow{AB} = \langle 3-1, 4-0, 0-2 \rangle = \langle 2, 4, -2 \rangle$$
$$\overrightarrow{BA} = \langle 1-3, 0-4, 2-0 \rangle = \langle -2, -4, 2 \rangle = -\overrightarrow{AB}$$
