# Three-Dimensional Rectangular Coordinate System

> **Stewart Calculus, Chapter 12, Section 12.1**

## Definition
A three-dimensional rectangular coordinate system (also called a 3D Cartesian coordinate system) consists of three mutually perpendicular number lines—the $x$-axis, $y$-axis, and $z$-axis—all intersecting at a single point called the **origin** $O(0, 0, 0)$. Every point in $\mathbb{R}^3$ is uniquely represented by an ordered triple $(x, y, z)$, where $x$, $y$, and $z$ are the signed distances from the point to the $yz$-plane, $xz$-plane, and $xy$-plane respectively.

## Key Properties
- The three axes are mutually perpendicular (orthogonal) to each other at the origin.
- The origin $O$ has coordinates $(0, 0, 0)$.
- The positive $x$-axis extends forward, the positive $y$-axis extends rightward, and the positive $z$-axis extends upward in the standard orientation.
- Each point $P(a, b, c)$ corresponds to the unique ordered triple where $a$ is the $x$-coordinate, $b$ is the $y$-coordinate, and $c$ is the $z$-coordinate.

## Worked Example
**Plot the point $P(3, -2, 5)$.**

1. Start at the origin.
2. Move 3 units along the positive $x$-axis.
3. From there, move 2 units in the negative $y$-direction (to the left).
4. From there, move 5 units in the positive $z$-direction (upward).
5. The resulting point is $P(3, -2, 5)$.
