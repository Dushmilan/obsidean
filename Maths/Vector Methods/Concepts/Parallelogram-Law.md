# Parallelogram Law

> **Stewart Calculus, Chapter 12, Section 12.2**

## Definition
The **parallelogram law** is a geometric method for adding two vectors:
1. Place the **initial points** (tails) of both vectors together
2. Complete the parallelogram using the two vectors as adjacent sides
3. The **resultant** is the diagonal from the common initial point

$$\mathbf{u} + \mathbf{v} = \text{diagonal from common tail}$$

## Key Properties
- Equivalent to the [[Triangle-Law]] — both yield the same sum
- Particularly useful when both vectors share a common origin (e.g., forces acting on a point)
- The other diagonal gives $\mathbf{u} - \mathbf{v}$

## Worked Example
Given $\mathbf{u} = \langle 2, 1 \rangle$ and $\mathbf{v} = \langle 1, 3 \rangle$, both starting at the origin:
- The parallelogram has vertices at $(0,0)$, $(2,1)$, $(3,4)$, $(1,3)$
- The diagonal from the origin: $\mathbf{u} + \mathbf{v} = \langle 3, 4 \rangle$
