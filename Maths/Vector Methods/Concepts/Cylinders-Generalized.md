# Cylinders (Generalized)
> Stewart Calculus, Chapter 12, Section 12.6

## Definition
A **cylinder** is a surface generated by a curve in a plane extended along a line (axis). The variable missing from the equation determines the axis of extension — the curve lies in the plane of the two present variables and repeats along the free variable's axis.

Formally: if $f(x, y) = 0$ defines a curve in the $xy$-plane, then $f(x, y) = 0$ in $\mathbb{R}^3$ is a **cylinder along the $z$-axis**.

## Key Properties
- The free variable (absent from the equation) indicates the axis direction.
- Cross-sections perpendicular to the axis are identical copies of the generating curve.
- Cylinders need not be circular — any planar curve generates a cylinder.

## Worked Example
**Parabolic cylinder:** $y = x^2$

- Missing variable: $z$ → extends along the $z$-axis.
- In the $xy$-plane, this is a parabola opening in the $+y$ direction.
- In 3D, every horizontal slice $z = k$ is the same parabola $y = x^2$.

**Circular cylinder:** $x^2 + y^2 = 1$

- Missing variable: $z$ → extends along the $z$-axis.
- In the $xy$-plane, this is a unit circle.
- In 3D, this is the standard tube of radius 1 along $z$.
