# Octants

> **Stewart Calculus, Chapter 12, Section 12.1**

## Definition
The three coordinate planes divide $\mathbb{R}^3$ into eight regions called **octants**. The octant where all three coordinates are positive ($x > 0$, $y > 0$, $z > 0$) is called the **First Octant**. The remaining octants are numbered by analogy with the quadrant numbering in 2D, extended into the third dimension.

## Key Properties
- There are exactly 8 octants in three-dimensional space.
- The First Octant is defined by $x > 0$, $y > 0$, $z > 0$.
- Octants are analogous to quadrants in 2D, but there is no universally agreed-upon numbering convention for octants II through VIII (only the First Octant is standard).
- Points lying on coordinate planes or axes are not in any octant.

## Worked Example
**Determine which octant contains $(-1, 3, 2)$.**

1. $x = -1 < 0$ — the point is on the negative side of the $x$-axis.
2. $y = 3 > 0$ — the point is on the positive side of the $y$-axis.
3. $z = 2 > 0$ — the point is on the positive side of the $z$-axis.
4. In the standard numbering convention, the octant with $(-, +, +)$ is the **Second Octant**.
