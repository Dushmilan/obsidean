# Planes Parallel to Coordinate Planes

> **Stewart Calculus, Chapter 12, Section 12.1**

## Definition
A **plane parallel to a coordinate plane** has an equation of the form $x = k$, $y = k$, or $z = k$, where $k$ is a constant. Each such equation defines a flat plane in $\mathbb{R}^3$ that is parallel to the coordinate plane perpendicular to the axis of the fixed variable.

## Key Properties
- $x = k$ is a plane parallel to the $yz$-plane, at distance $|k|$ from it.
- $y = k$ is a plane parallel to the $xz$-plane, at distance $|k|$ from it.
- $z = k$ is a plane parallel to the $xy$-plane, at distance $|k|$ from it.
- When $k = 0$, the equation gives the coordinate plane itself.
- These planes extend infinitely in the two directions not constrained by the equation.

## Worked Example
**Describe the surface $z = 4$.**

1. The equation $z = 4$ fixes the $z$-coordinate at 4 for all points.
2. The $x$ and $y$ coordinates are free to take any value.
3. This defines a **horizontal plane** parallel to the $xy$-plane.
4. The plane sits 4 units above the $xy$-plane (in the positive $z$-direction).
