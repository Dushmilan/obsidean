# Resultant Force

> **Stewart Calculus, Chapter 12, Section 12.2**

## Definition

The **resultant force** (or net force) is the vector sum of multiple forces acting on a single body or point. If forces $\mathbf{F}_1, \mathbf{F}_2, \ldots, \mathbf{F}_n$ act simultaneously on a point, the resultant is:

$$\mathbf{F}_{\text{net}} = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \mathbf{F}_i = \mathbf{F}_1 + \mathbf{F}_2 + \cdots + \mathbf{F}_n$$

This follows directly from the parallelogram law of vector addition.

## Key Properties

- The resultant force produces the same effect as all individual forces combined
- Forces are added component-wise (as with any vector addition)
- The order of addition does not matter (commutative law)
- If $\mathbf{F}_{\text{net}} = \mathbf{0}$, the body is in equilibrium

## Worked Example

Two forces $\mathbf{F}_1 = \langle 3, 1 \rangle$ and $\mathbf{F}_2 = \langle -1, 4 \rangle$ act on an object. Find the resultant force.

**Solution:**

$$\mathbf{F}_{\text{net}} = \mathbf{F}_1 + \mathbf{F}_2 = \langle 3 + (-1),\; 1 + 4 \rangle = \langle 2, 5 \rangle$$

The resultant force is $\langle 2, 5 \rangle$, with magnitude $\|\mathbf{F}_{\text{net}}\| = \sqrt{4 + 25} = \sqrt{29}$.
