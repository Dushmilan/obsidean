# Newton's Second Law in Vector Form

> **Stewart Calculus, Chapter 13, Section 13.4**

## Definition
Newton's second law relates force and acceleration as vectors:

$$\mathbf{F}(t) = m\mathbf{a}(t)$$

Each component acts independently: $F_x = ma_x$, $F_y = ma_y$, $F_z = ma_z$.

## Key Properties
- Force and acceleration are proportional (mass is the constant of proportionality)
- The law is applied component-wise in Cartesian coordinates
- Fundamental to all classical mechanics, including orbital mechanics
- When combined with $\mathbf{F} = -\frac{GMm}{r^3}\mathbf{r}$, it yields Kepler's laws

## Worked Example
An object of mass $2$ kg has acceleration $\mathbf{a}(t) = \langle 3, -1, 4 \rangle$ m/s². Find the net force.

$$\mathbf{F} = m\mathbf{a} = 2 \langle 3, -1, 4 \rangle = \langle 6, -2, 8 \rangle \text{ N}$$
