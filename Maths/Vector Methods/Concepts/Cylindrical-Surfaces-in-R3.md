# Cylindrical Surfaces in $\mathbb{R}^3$

> **Stewart Calculus, Chapter 12, Section 12.1**

## Definition
A **cylindrical surface** is a 3D surface generated by taking a 2D curve in one coordinate plane and extending it infinitely along the axis of the missing variable. If an equation in $\mathbb{R}^3$ involves only two of the three variables, the third variable is free, and the surface is a cylinder whose rulings (generating lines) are parallel to the axis of the free variable.

## Key Properties
- The equation $f(x, y) = 0$ defines a cylinder along the $z$-axis (since $z$ is free).
- The equation $f(y, z) = 0$ defines a cylinder along the $x$-axis (since $x$ is free).
- The equation $f(x, z) = 0$ defines a cylinder along the $y$-axis (since $y$ is free).
- The cross-section of the cylinder in the plane of the two constrained variables is the generating curve.
- The term "cylinder" in 3D is more general than the usual circular cylinder — any curve swept along an axis qualifies.

## Worked Example
**Describe the surface $y = x^2$ in 3D.**

1. The equation involves only $x$ and $y$; the variable $z$ is absent (free).
2. In the $xy$-plane, $y = x^2$ is a parabola opening in the positive $y$-direction.
3. Since $z$ is free, this parabola is extended infinitely along the $z$-axis.
4. The surface is a **parabolic cylinder** — a sheet curved like a parabola, extending forever up and down.
