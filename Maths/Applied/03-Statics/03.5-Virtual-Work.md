# 3.5 Virtual Work Principle

**Syllabus:** GCE A/L Combined Mathematics (021) — Paper II, Topic 3.5

---

## Principle of Virtual Work

For a system in equilibrium under applied forces, the **total virtual work** done by all forces during any **virtual displacement** consistent with the constraints is zero.

$$
\delta W = \sum \vec{F}_i \cdot \delta\vec{r}_i = 0
$$

Where:
- $\vec{F}_i$ = applied force (external, not constraint forces)
- $\delta\vec{r}_i$ = virtual displacement of point of application

---

## Key Concepts

### Virtual Displacement $\delta\vec{r}$
- **Infinitesimal** change in position
- **Consistent with constraints** (imposed instantaneously, time frozen)
- Not a real displacement (no time elapsed)
- Denoted $\delta$ (not $d$)

### Virtual Work $\delta W$
Work done by force during virtual displacement:
$$
\delta W = \vec{F} \cdot \delta\vec{r} = F \cdot \delta s \cdot \cos\theta
$$

### Constraint Forces (Ideal Constraints)
- Normal reactions (smooth surfaces)
- Tensions in inextensible strings
- Reactions at fixed pins/hinges
- **Do no work** in virtual displacements consistent with constraints

**Reason:** $\vec{R} \perp \delta\vec{r}$ for smooth constraints, or $\delta\vec{r} = 0$ for fixed points.

---

## Applications

### 1. Particle on Smooth Curve/Surface
Constraint: particle moves on curve $f(x,y)=0$ or surface $f(x,y,z)=0$.

Virtual displacement $\delta\vec{r}$ is **tangential** to constraint.
Equilibrium: $\vec{F} \cdot \delta\vec{r} = 0$ for all tangential $\delta\vec{r}$.

**Result:** Component of $\vec{F}$ tangential to constraint = 0.
$$
\vec{F} \cdot \hat{t} = 0
$$

### 2. System of Particles (Rigid Body)
For rigid body, virtual displacements of points related by rigidity constraints.

**Planar rigid body:** 3 degrees of freedom (2 translation + 1 rotation)
$$
\delta\vec{r} = \delta\vec{r}_O + \delta\theta \, \hat{k} \times \vec{r}
$$

Virtual work:
$$
\delta W = \sum \vec{F}_i \cdot (\delta\vec{r}_O + \delta\theta \, \hat{k} \times \vec{r}_i)
= (\sum \vec{F}_i) \cdot \delta\vec{r}_O + \delta\theta \sum (\vec{r}_i \times \vec{F}_i) \cdot \hat{k}
$$

For arbitrary $\delta\vec{r}_O$ and $\delta\theta$:
$$
\sum \vec{F}_i = 0, \quad \sum \vec{r}_i \times \vec{F}_i = 0
$$
→ Recovers standard equilibrium conditions.

### 3. Simple Machines

#### Lever
Effort $E$, load $L$, arms $a, b$. Virtual rotation $\delta\theta$:
$$
\delta W = E(a\delta\theta) - L(b\delta\theta) = 0 \quad \Rightarrow \quad Ea = Lb
$$

#### Inclined Plane
Weight $W$, effort $P$ parallel to plane. Plane angle $\theta$.
Virtual displacement: load moves $\delta s$ up plane.
$$
\delta W = P\delta s - W\sin\theta \, \delta s = 0 \quad \Rightarrow \quad P = W\sin\theta
$$

#### Wedge
Wedge angle $\alpha$, load $W$ on horizontal face. Effort $P$ horizontal.
Virtual displacement: wedge moves $\delta x$ inward.
$$
\delta W = P\delta x - W(\tan\alpha)\delta x = 0 \quad \Rightarrow \quad P = W\tan\alpha
$$

#### Screw Jack
Pitch $p$, handle radius $R$, load $W$, effort $E$ at handle.
One turn: effort moves $2\pi R$, load rises $p$.
$$
\delta W = E(2\pi R) - W p = 0 \quad \Rightarrow \quad E = \frac{Wp}{2\pi R}
$$

## Advantages of Virtual Work

1. **Eliminates constraint forces** — no need to find reactions
2. **Single equation** — for 1 DOF system, one $\delta W = 0$
3. **Handles complex geometry** — work = force × displacement in direction of force
4. **Natural for mechanisms** — directly uses kinematic relationships

---

## Worked Examples

### Example 1: Ladder on Smooth Wall/Floor
Uniform ladder length $2l$, weight $W$, angle $\theta$. Find horizontal force $F$ at bottom to maintain equilibrium.

**Solution:**
Virtual rotation $\delta\theta$ about bottom.
- CM rises: $\delta y = l\cos\theta \, \delta\theta$
- Top moves horizontally: $\delta x = 2l\sin\theta \, \delta\theta$
- Force $F$ does work $F \delta x$

$$
\delta W = F(2l\sin\theta\,\delta\theta) - W(l\cos\theta\,\delta\theta) = 0
$$
$$
F = \frac{W}{2}\cot\theta
$$

---

### Example 2: Beam with Load
Uniform beam length $L$, weight $W$, hinged at one end, supported by cable at angle $\alpha$ at distance $a$ from hinge. Load $P$ at distance $b$ from hinge. Find tension $T$.

**Solution:**
Virtual rotation $\delta\theta$ about hinge.
- Cable attachment moves: $\delta s = a\delta\theta$ perpendicular to beam
- Component of $T$ along displacement: $T\sin\alpha$ (if $\alpha$ from beam)
- Load $P$ moves down: $b\delta\theta$
- Weight $W$ moves down: $(L/2)\delta\theta$

$$
\delta W = T\sin\alpha(a\delta\theta) - P(b\delta\theta) - W(L/2)\delta\theta = 0
$$
$$
T = \frac{Pb + WL/2}{a\sin\alpha}
$$

---

### Example 3: Toggle Joint
Two links length $l$, hinged at ends and centre. Force $P$ applied horizontally at centre. Vertical load $W$ at hinge between links. Angle between links = $2\theta$. Find $P$.

**Solution:**
Virtual displacement: $\theta \to \theta + \delta\theta$.
- Centre moves horizontally: $\delta x = l\sin\theta \, \delta\theta$
- Load $W$ moves vertically: $\delta y = l\cos\theta \, \delta\theta$

$$
\delta W = P(l\sin\theta\,\delta\theta) - W(l\cos\theta\,\delta\theta) = 0
$$
$$
P = W\cot\theta
$$

---

### Example 4: Frame with Multiple Members
Square frame of side $a$, weightless. Diagonal brace. Load $W$ at one corner. Find force in brace.

**Solution:**
Assume brace in compression $C$.
Virtual displacement: square deforms to rhombus, angle $2\theta \to 2\theta + 2\delta\theta$.
- Brace length change: $\delta l = a(\cos\theta - \sin\theta)2\delta\theta$ (approx)
- Load $W$ moves down: $\delta y$

Set up $\delta W = 0$, solve for $C$.

## D'Alembert's Principle (Dynamic Virtual Work)

For accelerating systems, include inertial forces:
$$
\delta W = \sum (\vec{F}_i - m_i\vec{a}_i) \cdot \delta\vec{r}_i = 0
$$

This extends virtual work to dynamics (Lagrange's equations).

---

## Limitations

| Limitation | Explanation |
|------------|-------------|
| Ideal constraints only | No friction in constraints, no deformation |
| Conservative forces | Non-conservative forces (friction) need actual work |
| Finite DOF | Cannot handle continuum (use energy methods) |
| No info on constraint forces | If reactions needed, use standard equilibrium |
