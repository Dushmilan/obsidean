Rigid bodies in equilibrium is the culmination of all statics. Every previous topic feeds into this: forces, friction, centre of mass, and virtual work all appear in the analysis of real structures. The key distinction is between trusses (two-force members, forces only at joints) and frames (multi-force members with bending and shear). The method of joints isolates each joint and applies $\sum F_x = 0$, $\sum F_y = 0$. The method of sections cuts through the truss and applies all three equilibrium equations to one half. For frames, you must disassemble the structure and apply equilibrium to each member separately, using Newton's 3rd Law at connections.

**The Intuition:** A rigid body is like a stiff board. For it to stay put, you need to push it left-right (force balance), up-down (force balance), and stop it spinning (moment balance). In 3D, you also need to prevent it from twisting about each axis. A book on a table: push it left — it slides left. Push it right — it slides right. Push both ways equally — it stays. Twist it — it rotates. For equilibrium, all pushes and twists must cancel.

**The Math:** 3D equilibrium: $\sum \vec{F} = 0$ (3 scalar equations) and $\sum \vec{M}_O = 0$ (3 scalar equations about any point $O$). Total: 6 independent equations. In 2D: $\sum F_x = 0$, $\sum F_y = 0$, $\sum M_O = 0$ (3 equations).

Support types: Roller: 1 reaction. Pin/Hinge: 2 reactions ($R_x, R_y$). Fixed/Built-in: 2 forces + 1 moment. Ball-and-socket (3D): 3 forces.

Trusses: straight members, loads only at joints, two-force members (tension or compression only). Zero-force members: two members at an unloaded joint $\rightarrow$ both zero. Three members at a joint with two collinear $\rightarrow$ the third is zero.

Beams: shear force $\frac{dV}{dx} = -w(x)$, bending moment $\frac{dM}{dx} = V$. Distributed loads replaced by resultants at centroid: UDL: $wL$ at $L/2$. Triangular: $\frac{1}{2}w_0 L$ at $2L/3$ from zero end. Cables with UDL: parabolic shape $y = \frac{w}{2H}x^2$.

**What does this mean for Applied Mechanics?** Identify the structure type first: truss, frame, beam, or cable. For trusses: check for zero-force members, then use joints or sections. For frames: find external reactions first, then disassemble and apply Newton's 3rd Law. For beams: replace distributed loads with resultants, then use $\sum F = 0$ and $\sum M = 0$. Statically indeterminate structures (more unknowns than equations) require additional methods.

**Setup:** Warren truss, span $L$, height $h$, load $P$ at midspan. Find force in members.

**Solution:** Symmetry $\rightarrow$ vertical reactions $P/2$ each. Method of joints at support: 2 unknowns, 2 equations. Or method of sections through midspan: cut 3 members, apply 3 equations.

**Key insight:** Symmetry halves the work — you only need to analyse one half of the truss.

**Setup:** Weight $W$ suspended by 3 cables from points $A, B, C$. Find tensions $T_A, T_B, T_C$.

**Solution:** Unit vectors along cables: $\hat{u}_A, \hat{u}_B, \hat{u}_C$. Equilibrium: $T_A\hat{u}_A + T_B\hat{u}_B + T_C\hat{u}_C + W\hat{k} = 0$. 3 equations (one per component), 3 unknowns.

**Key insight:** In 3D, you always get 3 force equations — exactly right for 3 unknown tensions.
