
## Definition

The full equilibrium framework for real structures:

**3D:** $\sum\vec{F} = 0$ (3 equations) and $\sum\vec{M}_O = 0$ (3 equations) — six independent equations.
**2D:** $\sum F_x = 0$, $\sum F_y = 0$, $\sum M_O = 0$ — three equations.

**Supports:** roller (1 reaction) · pin/hinge (2: $R_x, R_y$) · fixed (2 forces + 1 moment) · ball-and-socket (3 forces).

## The Intuition

A stiff board: to keep it still you must balance left-right pushes, up-down pushes, and spins. A book on a table: push left it slides, push right it slides, push both equally it stays, twist it it rotates. Equilibrium means all pushes and twists cancel. Real structures (trusses, frames, beams) are analysed by applying these balances member by member.

## The Toolkit

| Structure | Method |
|-----------|--------|
| Truss | two-force members; forces only at joints |
| Method of joints | isolate each joint, $\sum F_x = 0$, $\sum F_y = 0$ |
| Method of sections | cut the truss, apply all three equations to one half |
| Frame | disassemble, equilibrium per member, Newton III at connections |
| Beam | $V(x) = \int -w(x)\,dx$, $M(x) = \int V(x)\,dx$ |
| Zero-force member | 2 members at unloaded joint → both zero; 3 with 2 collinear → third zero |

## Derivation

Translational equilibrium is Newton's first law; rotational equilibrium is its moment analogue (from $\sum \vec{r}\times\vec{F} = 0$). The method of sections works because the internal forces at a cut must balance the external loads on either half — the same three equations apply to the half-structure. Beam relations follow from integrating the distributed load: $\frac{dV}{dx} = -w(x)$, $\frac{dM}{dx} = V$. [Full derivations: 03.6-Proofs-Rigid-Bodies]

## Method

1. **Identify the support reactions** — count them (roller 1, pin 2, fixed 3) to check determinacy.
2. **Trusses:** method of joints (two equations per joint, work from joints with ≤ 2 unknowns) or method of sections (cut through ≤ 3 unknown members).
3. **Frames:** separate each member, apply the three equations each, link with Newton's third law at connections.
4. **Distributed loads:** replace with a resultant at the load's centroid (UDL: $wL$ at the midpoint).

## Worked Examples

**Setup:** A simply supported beam of length $L$ carries a central point load $W$. Find the reactions.

**Solution:** By symmetry $R_A = R_B = W/2$. Verify with moments about A: $R_B L = W\frac L2 \Rightarrow R_B = \frac W2$.

**Key insight:** Symmetry halves the work; moments about a support eliminate its reaction.

---

**Setup:** In a truss, joint $J$ has two members meeting at an unloaded joint. What are the member forces?

**Solution:** $\sum F_x = 0$ and $\sum F_y = 0$ at an unloaded two-member joint force both to be zero — **zero-force members**.

**Key insight:** Spotting zero-force members simplifies the whole truss — they carry no load and can be ignored.

## Common Traps

- Truss members are two-force (no bending) — frames are not
- Counting support reactions wrong → indeterminate structures need more work
- Method of sections: cutting more than three unknown members
- Beam sign conventions ($V$, $M$) — keep one consistent set
- Distributed load placed at the wrong point (centroid, not end)
