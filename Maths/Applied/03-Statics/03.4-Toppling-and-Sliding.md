# 3.4 Toppling and Sliding

**Syllabus:** GCE A/L Combined Mathematics (021) — Paper II, Topic 3.4

---

## Stability on an Inclined Plane

Body of width $b$ (base), height $h$, CM at geometric centre (uniform rectangular block). Plane inclined at $\theta$.

### Two Failure Modes

| Mode | Condition | Critical Angle |
|------|-----------|----------------|
| **Sliding** | Friction overcome | $\theta_{\text{slide}} = \tan^{-1}\mu$ |
| **Toppling** | CM line of action outside base | $\theta_{\text{topple}} = \tan^{-1}\frac{b}{2h}$ |

### Which Occurs First?

- If $\mu < \frac{b}{2h}$: **Slides first** ($\theta_{\text{slide}} < \theta_{\text{topple}}$)
- If $\mu > \frac{b}{2h}$: **Topples first** ($\theta_{\text{topple}} < \theta_{\text{slide}}$)
- If $\mu = \frac{b}{2h}$: **Simultaneous**

---

## Body on Horizontal Plane with Applied Force

Block width $b$, height $h$, weight $W$, CM at centre. Force $P$ applied horizontally at height $y$ above base.

### Sliding
$$
F = P, \quad R = W, \quad F \leq \mu R \quad \Rightarrow \quad P \leq \mu W
$$

### Toppling
Take moments about outer edge (point of impending toppling):
$$
P \cdot y = W \cdot \frac{b}{2} \quad \Rightarrow \quad P = \frac{Wb}{2y}
$$

### Comparison
| Force height $y$ | Toppling force $P_{\text{topple}}$ |
|------------------|-----------------------------------|
| $y = h$ (top edge) | $\frac{Wb}{2h}$ |
| $y = h/2$ (mid-height) | $\frac{Wb}{h}$ |
| $y = h/4$ | $\frac{2Wb}{h}$ |

**Lower force application → harder to topple.**

---

## Force Applied at Angle $\alpha$

Force $P$ at angle $\alpha$ to horizontal (upward $\alpha > 0$, downward $\alpha < 0$).

Resolving:
$$
R = W - P\sin\alpha, \quad F = P\cos\alpha
$$

### Sliding
$$
P\cos\alpha = \mu(W - P\sin\alpha) \quad \Rightarrow \quad P_{\text{slide}} = \frac{\mu W}{\cos\alpha + \mu\sin\alpha}
$$

### Toppling
Moments about outer edge:
$$
P\cos\alpha \cdot y + P\sin\alpha \cdot \frac{b}{2} = W\frac{b}{2}
$$
$$
P_{\text{topple}} = \frac{Wb/2}{y\cos\alpha + (b/2)\sin\alpha}
$$

### Special Cases
- **Horizontal force ($\alpha=0$):** $P_{\text{slide}} = \mu W$, $P_{\text{topple}} = \frac{Wb}{2y}$
- **Upward force ($\alpha>0$):** Reduces normal reaction → easier to slide, harder to topple
- **Downward force ($\alpha<0$):** Increases normal reaction → harder to slide, easier to topple

---

## Friction Cone and Toppling

The **friction cone** half-angle $\lambda = \tan^{-1}\mu$.

For no toppling, the line of action of the resultant reaction (normal + friction) must pass within the base.

At limiting friction, resultant makes angle $\lambda$ with normal. Toppling when this line passes through outer edge.

---

## Stability on Horizontal Plane (Small Tilts)

Body tilted by small angle $\phi$ (e.g., by placing on cylinder).

**Restoring moment** = $W \times$ horizontal distance CM moves.

For CM height $h$, base half-width $a = b/2$:
- CM moves horizontally by $h\phi$ (small $\phi$)
- If $h\phi < a$: restoring moment = $W(h\phi)$ → stable
- If $h\phi > a$: overturning → unstable

**Metacentre concept:** For bodies with curved base, stability depends on metacentric height.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1: Cube on Rough Incline
Cube side $a$, mass $m$, $\mu = 0.5$. Incline angle $\theta$.

**Sliding:** $\theta_{\text{slide}} = \tan^{-1}(0.5) \approx 26.6^\circ$

**Toppling:** $\theta_{\text{topple}} = \tan^{-1}(a/2a) = \tan^{-1}(0.5) \approx 26.6^\circ$

**Result:** Simultaneous sliding and toppling.

---

### Example 2: Block with Horizontal Force
Block $b=0.4\text{m}$, $h=0.6\text{m}$, $W=100\text{N}$, $\mu=0.3$. Force $P$ at $y=0.5\text{m}$.

**Slide:** $P_{\text{slide}} = 0.3 \times 100 = 30\text{N}$

**Topple:** $P_{\text{topple}} = \frac{100 \times 0.4}{2 \times 0.5} = 40\text{N}$

**Result:** Slides first (at 30 N).

---

### Example 3: Crate on Incline with Force
Crate mass $M$, base $b$, height $h$, CM at height $h/2$. On incline $\theta$. Force $P$ applied horizontally to prevent motion.

Find range of $P$ for equilibrium.

**Solution:** Three conditions: no slide up, no slide down, no topple. Solve for $P$ in each.
