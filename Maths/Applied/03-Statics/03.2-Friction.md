# 3.2 Friction

**Syllabus:** GCE A/L Combined Mathematics (021) — Paper II, Topic 3.2

---

## Basic Concepts

### Contact Forces
When two surfaces are in contact, the contact force $\vec{R}$ can be resolved into:
- **Normal reaction $R$:** perpendicular to surfaces
- **Friction $F$:** parallel to surfaces, opposes relative motion

### Types of Friction
| Type | Condition | Magnitude |
|------|-----------|-----------|
| **Static** | No relative motion | $F \leq \mu_s R$ (self-adjusting) |
| **Limiting** | Impending motion | $F_{\text{max}} = \mu_s R$ |
| **Kinetic (Dynamic)** | Sliding occurs | $F = \mu_k R$ (constant) |

Usually $\mu_k < \mu_s$. In A/L, often use single $\mu$ for limiting friction.

---

## Laws of Friction (Coulomb/Amontons)

1. **Direction:** Opposes relative motion (or tendency)
2. **Magnitude (limiting):** $F = \mu R$
3. **Independence of area:** Independent of contact area (for rigid bodies)
4. **Independence of velocity:** Kinetic friction ≈ constant

---

## Angle of Friction

When limiting equilibrium is reached:
$$
\tan\lambda = \mu
$$
where $\lambda$ = **angle of friction** — angle between resultant contact force and normal.

**Friction cone:** All possible contact forces lie within a cone of semi-angle $\lambda$ about the normal.

## Equilibrium on Rough Inclined Plane

Particle on plane inclined at $\theta$, coefficient $\mu$.

| Condition | Forces | Friction Direction |
|-----------|--------|-------------------|
| $\theta < \tan^{-1}\mu$ | $F = W\sin\theta$ up plane | Up (prevents sliding down) |
| $\theta = \tan^{-1}\mu$ | $F = \mu R = \mu W\cos\theta$ | Up (limiting) |
| $\theta > \tan^{-1}\mu$ | Slides down, $F = \mu R$ up | Up (kinetic) |

**With applied force $P$ parallel to plane:**
- Up plane: $P = W\sin\theta + \mu W\cos\theta$ (to move up)
- Down plane: $P = W\sin\theta - \mu W\cos\theta$ (to prevent sliding down)
- Equilibrium range: $W(\sin\theta - \mu\cos\theta) \leq P \leq W(\sin\theta + \mu\cos\theta)$

## Ladder Problems

Ladder length $L$, weight $W$ (uniform → at midpoint), against rough wall ($\mu_w$) and rough floor ($\mu_f$).

**Forces:**
- $R_f, F_f = \mu_f R_f$ at floor
- $R_w, F_w = \mu_w R_w$ at wall
- $W$ at centre

**Equilibrium equations:**
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum F_x &= 0: & R_w - F_f &= 0 \\
\sum F_y &= 0: & R_f + F_w - W &= 0 \\
\sum M_{\text{floor}} &= 0: & W\frac{L}{2}\cos\alpha + F_w L\sin\alpha - R_w L\cos\alpha &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

**Key results:**
- $\tan\alpha = \frac{1 - \mu_f\mu_w}{2\mu_f}$ (for limiting equilibrium both ends)
- If floor smooth ($\mu_f=0$): $\tan\alpha = \frac{1}{2\mu_w}$

---

## Wedge Problems

Wedge angle $\alpha$, coefficient $\mu$ on all surfaces.

**Force to push wedge under load $W$:**
$$
P = W \cdot \frac{\sin\alpha + \mu\cos\alpha}{\cos\alpha - \mu\sin\alpha} \quad \text{(if } \mu < \cot\alpha\text{)}
$$

If $\mu \geq \cot\alpha$, wedge **self-locks** (cannot be pushed out).

---

## Rope on Rough Cylinder (Capstan Equation)

Rope wrapped around cylinder with angle of contact $\theta$ (radians), tension $T_1$ on one side, $T_2$ on other ($T_2 > T_1$).

**Capstan equation:**
$$
\frac{T_2}{T_1} = e^{\mu\theta}
$$

**Derivation:** Element $d\theta$ → $dT = \mu T\,d\theta$ → integrate.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1: Block on Rough Plane with Horizontal Force
Block weight $W$ on rough horizontal plane ($\mu$). Horizontal force $P$ applied. Find range of $P$ for equilibrium.

**Solution:**
- $R = W$, $F \leq \mu W$
- $\sum F_x = P - F = 0 \Rightarrow F = P$
- Equilibrium: $|P| \leq \mu W$

### Example 2: Ladder with Man Climbing
Uniform ladder $W$, length $L$, angle $\alpha$, man weight $w$ at distance $x$ from bottom. Floor rough ($\mu$), wall smooth. Find max $x$ before slipping.

**Solution:**
- $R_f = W + w$, $F_f = R_w$
- $\sum M_{\text{bottom}} = W\frac{L}{2}\cos\alpha + wx\cos\alpha - R_w L\sin\alpha = 0$
- $R_w = \frac{W\frac{L}{2} + wx}{L\tan\alpha}$
- Slipping: $F_f = \mu R_f \Rightarrow R_w = \mu(W+w)$
- Solve for $x_{\text{max}}$
