# 3.3 Centre of Mass

**Syllabus:** GCE A/L Combined Mathematics (021) — Paper II, Topic 3.3

---

## Definition

The **centre of mass** (CM) of a body/system is the point where the entire mass can be considered concentrated for translational motion.

For a system of particles:
$$
\vec{r}_{\text{cm}} = \frac{\sum m_i \vec{r}_i}{\sum m_i} = \frac{\sum m_i \vec{r}_i}{M}
$$

For continuous bodies:
$$
\vec{r}_{\text{cm}} = \frac{1}{M}\int \vec{r}\,dm
$$

**Key property:** The body behaves as if all mass is at CM and all external forces act there.

---

## Centre of Mass of Standard Uniform Bodies

### 1. Discrete Systems

| Configuration | Centre of Mass |
|---------------|----------------|
| Two particles $m_1, m_2$ at $\vec{r}_1, \vec{r}_2$ | $\frac{m_1\vec{r}_1 + m_2\vec{r}_2}{m_1+m_2}$ |
| Uniform rod length $L$ | Midpoint ($L/2$ from either end) |
| Uniform rectangular lamina | Intersection of diagonals (centroid) |
| Uniform triangular lamina | Centroid (intersection of medians) |

### 2. Uniform Plane Laminae (2D)

| Shape | Centre of Mass (from reference) |
|-------|--------------------------------|
| Rectangle $a \times b$ | $(a/2, b/2)$ from corner |
| Triangle (vertices $A,B,C$) | $\frac{A+B+C}{3}$ (centroid) |
| Semicircle radius $r$ (diameter on x-axis) | $(0, \frac{4r}{3\pi})$ from centre of diameter |
| Quadrant of circle radius $r$ | $(\frac{4r}{3\pi}, \frac{4r}{3\pi})$ from corner |
| Sector angle $2\alpha$ radius $r$ | $\frac{2r\sin\alpha}{3\alpha}$ from centre on symmetry axis |
| Parabolic segment (base $2a$, height $h$) | $\frac{3h}{5}$ from base on axis |

### 3. Uniform Solids (3D)

| Shape | Centre of Mass |
|-------|----------------|
| Solid hemisphere radius $r$ | $\frac{3r}{8}$ from base on axis |
| Hollow hemisphere radius $r$ | $\frac{r}{2}$ from base on axis |
| Solid cone height $h$ | $\frac{h}{4}$ from base on axis |
| Hollow cone height $h$ | $\frac{h}{3}$ from base on axis |
| Solid cylinder | Midpoint of axis |
| Solid sphere | Centre |
| Tetrahedron (vertices $A,B,C,D$) | $\frac{A+B+C+D}{4}$ |

### 4. Uniform Wires (1D Curves)

| Shape | Centre of Mass |
|-------|----------------|
| Uniform straight wire | Midpoint |
| Semicircular wire radius $r$ | $\frac{2r}{\pi}$ from diameter |
| Quarter circle wire radius $r$ | $(\frac{2r}{\pi}, \frac{2r}{\pi})$ from corner |
| Arc angle $2\alpha$ radius $r$ | $\frac{r\sin\alpha}{\alpha}$ from centre on symmetry axis |

## Methods for Composite Bodies

### 1. Addition Method (Positive Masses)
Break into standard shapes, find CM of each, then combine:
$$
\vec{r}_{\text{cm}} = \frac{\sum m_i \vec{r}_i}{\sum m_i}
$$
where $m_i$ = mass (or area for uniform thickness).

### 2. Subtraction Method (Negative Mass)
For a body with a hole/cutout:
- Treat hole as negative mass
- $m_{\text{total}} = m_{\text{full}} - m_{\text{hole}}$
- $\vec{r}_{\text{cm}} = \frac{m_{\text{full}}\vec{r}_{\text{full}} - m_{\text{hole}}\vec{r}_{\text{hole}}}{m_{\text{total}}}$

### 3. Symmetry Arguments
- Plane of symmetry → CM lies in that plane
- Line of symmetry (intersection of two planes) → CM lies on that line
- Point of symmetry (intersection of three planes) → CM is that point

## Standard Results by Integration

### 1. Uniform Triangular Lamina
Vertices $(0,0), (a,0), (0,b)$:
$$
x_{\text{cm}} = \frac{a}{3}, \quad y_{\text{cm}} = \frac{b}{3}
$$

### 2. Uniform Semicircular Lamina
Radius $r$, diameter on x-axis:
$$
y_{\text{cm}} = \frac{4r}{3\pi}, \quad x_{\text{cm}} = 0
$$

### 3. Uniform Solid Hemisphere
Radius $r$, base on xy-plane:
$$
z_{\text{cm}} = \frac{3r}{8}
$$

### 4. Uniform Solid Cone
Height $h$, base radius $R$, vertex at origin, axis along z:
$$
z_{\text{cm}} = \frac{h}{4}
$$

### 5. Uniform Paraboloid of Revolution
$y = kx^2$, $0 \leq y \leq h$:
$$
y_{\text{cm}} = \frac{2h}{3}
$$

## Centre of Mass and Equilibrium

### Suspension Principle
A body suspended from point $P$ hangs with CM **vertically below** $P$.

**Proof:** For equilibrium, moment of weight about $P$ must be zero → line of action of $W$ passes through $P$.

### Equilibrium on Inclined Plane
A body on a rough plane is in equilibrium if the vertical through CM falls **within the base of contact**.

### Stability
- **Stable:** CM at lowest possible position (returns to equilibrium when displaced)
- **Unstable:** CM at highest position (moves away when displaced)
- **Neutral:** CM height constant (e.g., sphere on plane)

### Toppling Condition (see 3.4)
Body topples when vertical through CM falls **outside** the base.

## Pappus-Guldin Theorems (for surfaces/volumes of revolution)

### First Theorem (Surface Area)
Surface area generated by rotating a plane curve about an external axis in its plane:
$$
A = s \cdot 2\pi \bar{y}
$$
where $s$ = arc length, $\bar{y}$ = distance of curve's centroid from axis.

### Second Theorem (Volume)
Volume generated by rotating a plane area about an external axis in its plane:
$$
V = A \cdot 2\pi \bar{y}
$$
where $A$ = area, $\bar{y}$ = distance of area's centroid from axis.

**Useful for:** Finding CM of curved surfaces/solids by working backwards.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1: L-Shaped Lamina
Uniform lamina: $10\text{cm} \times 10\text{cm}$ square with $4\text{cm} \times 4\text{cm}$ removed from one corner. Find CM from two adjacent sides of original square.

**Solution:**
- Full square: $m_1 = 100\rho$, CM at $(5,5)$
- Removed square: $m_2 = -16\rho$, CM at $(8,8)$ (from original corner)
- Total mass: $84\rho$

$$
x_{\text{cm}} = \frac{100(5) - 16(8)}{84} = \frac{500 - 128}{84} = \frac{372}{84} = \frac{31}{7} \approx 4.43\text{ cm}
$$
$$
y_{\text{cm}} = \frac{31}{7} \text{ cm} \quad \text{(by symmetry)}
$$

### Example 2: Composite Wire
Wire bent into square of side $a$ with one side missing. Find CM from centre of square.

**Solution:**
- Three sides: each mass $m$, total $3m$
- CM of three sides: two vertical sides CM at $(\pm a/2, 0)$, bottom at $(0, -a/2)$
- $x_{\text{cm}} = 0$ (symmetry)
- $y_{\text{cm}} = \frac{m(0) + m(0) + m(-a/2)}{3m} = -\frac{a}{6}$

CM is $a/6$ below centre.

### Example 3: Lamina with Circular Hole
Rectangular lamina $a \times b$ with circular hole radius $r$ at centre. Find CM.

**Solution:** By symmetry, CM remains at centre of rectangle.

If hole at $(x_0, y_0)$ from centre:
- Rectangle: $m_1 = ab\rho$, CM $(0,0)$
- Hole: $m_2 = -\pi r^2\rho$, CM $(x_0, y_0)$
- $x_{\text{cm}} = \frac{-\pi r^2 x_0}{ab - \pi r^2}$
