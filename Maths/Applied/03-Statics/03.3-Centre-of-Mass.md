
## Definition

The centre of mass (CM) is the **weighted average position** of all the mass:

- **Discrete:** $\vec{r}_{\text{cm}} = \dfrac{\sum m_i \vec{r}_i}{M}$
- **Continuous:** $\vec{r}_{\text{cm}} = \dfrac{1}{M}\int \vec{r}\,dm$

Replacing a distributed body with a point mass at the CM makes translational equations identical to particle mechanics. The CM is also the natural point about which rotation occurs.

## The Intuition

The balancing point: support the entire body at one point and the CM is where it balances perfectly — like balancing a plate on your finger. For a uniform triangle, the CM is the centroid (median intersection) — the "average position" of the material. Each piece contributes proportionally to its distance from the origin.

## The Toolkit

| Result | Formula |
|--------|---------|
| Discrete CM | $\vec{r}_{\text{cm}} = \dfrac{\sum m_i\vec{r}_i}{M}$ |
| Continuous CM | $\vec{r}_{\text{cm}} = \dfrac{1}{M}\int\vec{r}\,dm$ |
| Subtraction (holes) | $\vec{r}_{\text{cm}} = \dfrac{m_{\text{full}}\vec{r}_{\text{full}} - m_{\text{hole}}\vec{r}_{\text{hole}}}{m_{\text{total}}}$ |
| Semicircular lamina | $y_{\text{cm}} = \dfrac{4r}{3\pi}$ from the diameter |
| Solid hemisphere | $z_{\text{cm}} = \dfrac{3r}{8}$ from the base |
| Solid cone | $z_{\text{cm}} = \dfrac{h}{4}$ from the base |
| Pappus (surface) | $A = s \cdot 2\pi\bar{y}$ |
| Pappus (volume) | $V = A \cdot 2\pi\bar{y}$ |

## Derivation

The CM formula is the discrete weighted average extended to a continuum by integration. Pappus's theorems come from rotating a lamina/arc about an axis: every point sweeps a circle of radius $\bar{y}$, so the swept surface/volume is the length/area times the circumference at the centroid. [Full derivations: 03.3-Proofs-Centre-of-Mass]

## Method

1. **Check for symmetry first** — one plane of symmetry puts the CM on it; two planes put it on their intersection.
2. Composite bodies: split into standard shapes, use the standard results, take the weighted average.
3. **Holes/cutouts:** subtraction method — treat the removed part as *negative mass*.
4. Remember: the CM need **not** lie inside the body (a ring's CM is its centre).

## Worked Examples

**Setup:** A uniform L-shaped lamina made of two $a \times a$ squares. Find the CM.

**Solution:** Treat as two squares: $m_1 = a^2$ at $(\frac a2, \frac a2)$, $m_2 = a^2$ at $(\frac{3a}{2}, \frac a2)$. $\bar{x} = \frac{a^2\cdot\frac a2 + a^2\cdot\frac{3a}{2}}{2a^2} = a$, $\bar{y} = \frac a2$.

**Key insight:** Split into shapes with known CMs and weight-average — no integration needed.

---

**Setup:** A circular lamina of radius $R$ with a circular hole of radius $r$ centred at distance $d$ from the centre. Find the CM.

**Solution:** $\bar{x} = \frac{(\pi R^2)(0) - (\pi r^2)(d)}{\pi(R^2 - r^2)} = -\frac{r^2 d}{R^2 - r^2}$ — the CM shifts away from the hole.

**Key insight:** The hole is negative mass; the CM moves opposite the missing material.

## Common Traps

- Treating the hole as positive mass instead of subtracting it
- CM outside the body (ring, L-shapes) — that's expected, not an error
- Forgetting to check symmetry before computing
- Weighting by length instead of mass for non-uniform bodies
- Pappus: using the wrong axis or centroid distance
