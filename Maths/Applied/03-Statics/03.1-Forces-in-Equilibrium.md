# 3.1 Forces in Equilibrium

**Syllabus:** GCE A/L Combined Mathematics (021) — Paper II, Topic 3.1

---

## Equilibrium Conditions

A rigid body is in **equilibrium** if:
1. **Translational equilibrium:** $\sum \vec{F} = 0$ (vector sum)
2. **Rotational equilibrium:** $\sum \vec{M} = 0$ (vector sum of moments)

In 2D (component form):
$$
\sum F_x = 0, \quad \sum F_y = 0, \quad \sum M_O = 0 \quad \text{(about any point O)}
$$

---

## Two Forces in Equilibrium

Two forces can only be in equilibrium if they are:
- Equal in magnitude
- Opposite in direction
- Collinear (same line of action)

---

## Three Coplanar Forces in Equilibrium

### Theorem of Triangle of Forces
Three forces in equilibrium can be represented in magnitude and direction by the three sides of a triangle taken in order.

If $\vec{F}_1 + \vec{F}_2 + \vec{F}_3 = 0$, the vectors form a closed triangle.

### Lami's Theorem
For three coplanar forces $F_1, F_2, F_3$ acting at a point in equilibrium:
$$
\frac{F_1}{\sin\alpha_1} = \frac{F_2}{\sin\alpha_2} = \frac{F_3}{\sin\alpha_3}
$$
where $\alpha_1$ is the angle between $F_2$ and $F_3$, etc.

**Derivation:** From sine rule on triangle of forces.

### Converse of Lami's Theorem
If three forces acting at a point satisfy Lami's theorem, they are in equilibrium.

---

## Multiple Coplanar Forces

### Polygon of Forces
Forces $\vec{F}_1, \vec{F}_2, \dots, \vec{F}_n$ in equilibrium → vector polygon closes:
$$
\vec{F}_1 + \vec{F}_2 + \dots + \vec{F}_n = 0
$$

### Resolution Method (Analytical)
Resolve each force into $x$ and $y$ components:
$$
\sum F_x = 0, \quad \sum F_y = 0
$$

### Moment Method
Take moments about any point:
$$
\sum M_O = 0
$$
**Principle of Moments:** Sum of clockwise moments = sum of anticlockwise moments.

---

## Non-Concurrent Forces (Rigid Body Equilibrium)

Forces not meeting at a point require:
1. $\sum F_x = 0$
2. $\sum F_y = 0$
3. $\sum M_O = 0$ about **any** point

Choosing a convenient point (where unknown forces pass through) simplifies equations.

---

## Common Configurations

### 1. Particle on Smooth Inclined Plane
Forces: Weight $W$, Normal $R$, Applied $F$ (if any)
- Perpendicular: $R = W\cos\theta$
- Parallel: $F = W\sin\theta$ (if in equilibrium)

### 2. Particle Suspended by Strings
Resolve at knot: $T_1, T_2, T_3$ tensions, weight $W$
- Horizontal: $\sum T_x = 0$
- Vertical: $\sum T_y = 0$

### 3. Ladder Against Rough Wall/Floor
Forces: Weight $W$ (at CM), Normal $R_f, R_w$, Friction $F_f, F_w$
- 3 equations: $\sum F_x=0, \sum F_y=0, \sum M=0$
- Friction: $F_f \leq \mu_f R_f$, $F_w \leq \mu_w R_w$

### 4. Beam on Supports
- Two supports: determinate (3 equations, 3 unknowns: $R_1, R_2, \text{moment}$)
- Three supports: indeterminate (need deformation/elasticity)

## Worked Examples

### Example 1: Three Forces Using Lami's Theorem
A particle of weight $W$ is held in equilibrium by two strings of lengths $l_1, l_2$ attached to points distance $d$ apart horizontally. Find tensions.

**Solution:** Triangle of forces: sides proportional to $T_1, T_2, W$. Use geometry to find angles, then Lami's theorem.

### Example 2: Beam on Two Supports
Uniform beam length $L$, weight $W$, supported at ends. Load $P$ at distance $x$ from left. Find reactions.

**Solution:**
- $\sum F_y = R_A + R_B - W - P = 0$
- $\sum M_A = R_B L - W(L/2) - Px = 0$
- Solve: $R_B = \frac{W}{2} + \frac{Px}{L}$, $R_A = W + P - R_B$
