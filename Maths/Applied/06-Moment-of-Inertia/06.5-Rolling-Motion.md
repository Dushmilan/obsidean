# 6.5 Rolling Motion

**Syllabus Reference:** GCE A/L Combined Mathematics (021) - Applied Mathematics Paper II, Topic 6.5

---

## 1. Rolling Without Slipping

A rigid body (wheel, sphere, cylinder) rolls on a surface without slipping. The point of contact is instantaneously at rest relative to the surface.

### 1.1 Kinematic Condition
If the centre of mass moves with velocity $v$ and the body rotates with angular velocity $\omega$:

$$v = \omega r$$

where $r$ is the radius of the rolling body.

Similarly for acceleration:
$$a = \alpha r$$

---

## 2. Kinetic Energy of a Rolling Body

Total kinetic energy = translational KE of CM + rotational KE about CM:

$$K = \frac{1}{2} M v^2 + \frac{1}{2} I_{CM} \omega^2$$

Using $\omega = v/r$ and $I_{CM} = M k^2$ (where $k$ is radius of gyration about CM):

$$K = \frac{1}{2} M v^2 + \frac{1}{2} M k^2 \frac{v^2}{r^2} = \frac{1}{2} M v^2 \left(1 + \frac{k^2}{r^2}\right)$$

**Key factor:** $1 + \frac{k^2}{r^2}$

---

## 3. Equations of Motion

### 3.1 On a Horizontal Surface
For a body rolling without slipping under an applied force $F$ at its centre:

**Translation:** $F - f = Ma$

**Rotation about CM:** $f r = I_{CM} \alpha = I_{CM} \frac{a}{r}$

Solving:
$$f = \frac{I_{CM}}{r^2} a = \frac{M k^2}{r^2} a$$
$$F = Ma + f = Ma\left(1 + \frac{k^2}{r^2}\right)$$
$$a = \frac{F}{M\left(1 + \frac{k^2}{r^2}\right)}, \quad f = \frac{F \frac{k^2}{r^2}}{1 + \frac{k^2}{r^2}}$$

### 3.2 On an Inclined Plane
Body rolls down incline of angle $\theta$ without slipping.

Forces along plane: $Mg\sin\theta - f = Ma$
Torque about CM: $f r = I_{CM} \alpha = I_{CM} \frac{a}{r}$

Solving:
$$a = \frac{g\sin\theta}{1 + \frac{k^2}{r^2}}$$
$$f = \frac{Mg\sin\theta \cdot \frac{k^2}{r^2}}{1 + \frac{k^2}{r^2}}$$

**Condition for rolling without slipping:**
$$f \leq \mu_s N = \mu_s Mg\cos\theta$$
$$\mu_s \geq \frac{\tan\theta}{1 + \frac{r^2}{k^2}}$$

---

## 4. Acceleration for Standard Bodies

| Body | $k^2/r^2$ | $a$ (incline) | $v^2$ after distance $s$ |
|------|-----------|---------------|--------------------------|
| Ring / Hollow cylinder | 1 | $\frac{1}{2}g\sin\theta$ | $g s \sin\theta$ |
| Disc / Solid cylinder | $\frac{1}{2}$ | $\frac{2}{3}g\sin\theta$ | $\frac{4}{3}g s \sin\theta$ |
| Solid sphere | $\frac{2}{5}$ | $\frac{5}{7}g\sin\theta$ | $\frac{10}{7}g s \sin\theta$ |
| Hollow sphere | $\frac{2}{3}$ | $\frac{3}{5}g\sin\theta$ | $\frac{6}{5}g s \sin\theta$ |

**Order of arrival (starting from rest):** Solid sphere > Disc > Hollow sphere > Ring

---

## 5. Energy Method

For a body rolling down an incline of height $h$:

Loss in PE = Gain in KE
$$Mgh = \frac{1}{2} M v^2 \left(1 + \frac{k^2}{r^2}\right)$$

$$v^2 = \frac{2gh}{1 + \frac{k^2}{r^2}}$$

This avoids dealing with friction forces!

---

## 6. Rolling with an External Force

### 6.1 Force Applied at Centre
As derived in section 3.1.

### 6.2 Force Applied at Top (Horizontal)
Let horizontal force $F$ be applied at top of a cylinder/sphere of radius $r$.

Translation: $F + f = Ma$ (if friction $f$ acts forward)
Rotation about CM: $Fr - fr = I_{CM} \frac{a}{r}$

Solving gives different $f$ depending on $F$ and geometry.

### 6.3 Force Applied at Centre of Mass at an Angle
Decompose into horizontal and vertical components.

## 7. Rolling with Slipping

If the surface is not rough enough, slipping occurs. Then:

- Friction is kinetic: $f = \mu_k N$
- $v \neq \omega r$ (no fixed relationship)
- Equations:
  - $F \pm f = Ma$
  - $f r = I_{CM} \alpha$ (if friction acts at contact point)
- Solve for $v(t)$ and $\omega(t)$ until $v = \omega r$ (pure rolling begins)

---

## 8. Combined Translation and Rotation (General)

For any rigid body motion:

1. **Translation of CM:** $\sum \mathbf{F} = M \mathbf{a}_{CM}$
2. **Rotation about CM:** $\sum \boldsymbol{\tau}_{CM} = I_{CM} \boldsymbol{\alpha}$

The instantaneous axis of rotation (IAR) is the point with zero velocity. For rolling without slipping, IAR is the contact point.

---

## 9. Rolling on a Moving Surface

If the surface itself moves (e.g., conveyor belt, accelerating plank):

- Let $v_s$ be surface velocity
- No-slip condition: $v - \omega r = v_s$ (sign depends on direction)

---

## 10. Work Done by Friction in Pure Rolling

**Zero!** The point of contact is instantaneously at rest, so friction does no work. This is why energy conservation works perfectly for pure rolling problems.

---

## 11. Common Problem Types

1. **Find acceleration** down an incline
2. **Find minimum $\mu$** for no slipping
3. **Compare arrival times** of different bodies
4. **Find speed after distance** using energy
5. **Rolling with external force** applied at various points
6. **Rolling up an incline** (deceleration)
7. **Transition from slipping to rolling**

## 12. Key Formulae Summary

| Quantity | Formula |
|----------|---------|
| No-slip condition | $v = \omega r$, $a = \alpha r$ |
| Total KE | $K = \frac{1}{2}Mv^2(1 + \frac{k^2}{r^2})$ |
| Acceleration (incline) | $a = \frac{g\sin\theta}{1 + k^2/r^2}$ |
| Friction force (incline) | $f = \frac{Mg\sin\theta \cdot k^2/r^2}{1 + k^2/r^2}$ |
| Min $\mu$ for no slip | $\mu_{min} = \frac{\tan\theta}{1 + r^2/k^2}$ |
| Speed after height $h$ | $v^2 = \frac{2gh}{1 + k^2/r^2}$ |

---

## 13. Exam Tips

1. **Energy method** is fastest for speed/height problems
2. **Force/torque method** needed for friction, acceleration
3. **Direction of friction** can be tricky - assume direction, solve, check sign
4. **Friction does no work** in pure rolling
5. **Compare $k^2/r^2$** to rank bodies: smaller $k^2/r^2$ = faster
6. **Pure rolling condition** must be checked: $f \leq \mu N$
