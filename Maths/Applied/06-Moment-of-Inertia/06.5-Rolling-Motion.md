Rolling without slipping is the constraint $v = \omega r$ (or $a = \alpha r$), meaning the contact point is instantaneously at rest. The total kinetic energy splits into translational KE of the CM and rotational KE about the CM: $K = \frac{1}{2}Mv^2 + \frac{1}{2}I_{CM}\omega^2 = \frac{1}{2}Mv^2(1 + k^2/r^2)$, where $k$ is the radius of gyration. For a body rolling down an incline of angle $\theta$, the acceleration is $a = \frac{g\sin\theta}{1 + k^2/r^2}$, which is always less than $g\sin\theta$ because some energy goes into rotation.

**The Intuition:** A coin rolling on a table — it moves forward without slipping because friction at the contact point provides the torque needed for rotation. The contact point is the instantaneous axis of rotation (IAR). Every point on the body rotates about this point. The CM moves with $v = \omega r$, and the top of the body moves with $v_{\text{top}} = 2\omega r$ (twice the CM speed). Without slipping, the arc length rolled equals the distance travelled: $s = r\theta$. Differentiating gives $v = r\omega$. The constraint links translation and rotation, reducing the problem to one variable.

**The Math:** No-slip condition: $v = \omega r$, $a = \alpha r$. Total KE: $K = \frac{1}{2}Mv^2(1 + k^2/r^2)$.

Acceleration (incline): Forces along plane: $Mg\sin\theta - f = Ma$. Torque about CM: $fr = I_{CM}\alpha = I_{CM}(a/r)$. Eliminate $f$: $Mg\sin\theta - I_{CM}a/r^2 = Ma \implies a = \frac{g\sin\theta}{1 + I_{CM}/(Mr^2)} = \frac{g\sin\theta}{1 + k^2/r^2}$.

Energy method: $Mgh = \frac{1}{2}Mv^2(1 + k^2/r^2) \implies v^2 = \frac{2gh}{1 + k^2/r^2}$.

Friction force (incline): $f = \frac{Mg\sin\theta \cdot k^2/r^2}{1 + k^2/r^2}$. Min $\mu$ for no slip: $\mu_{\min} = \frac{\tan\theta}{1 + r^2/k^2}$.

Order of arrival (from rest): Solid sphere ($k^2/r^2 = 2/5$) > Disc ($1/2$) > Hollow sphere ($2/3$) > Ring ($1$). The smaller $k^2/r^2$, the faster the body rolls.

Friction does no work in pure rolling — the contact point is instantaneously at rest, so the friction force acts through zero displacement. This is why energy conservation works perfectly for rolling problems. The no-slip condition $f \leq \mu N$ must be checked — if $\mu$ is too small, slipping occurs and $v \neq \omega r$.

**What does this mean for Applied Mechanics?** For speed problems, use energy conservation: $Mgh = \frac{1}{2}Mv^2(1 + k^2/r^2)$. For acceleration or friction, use force/torque equations with $a = \alpha r$. Check the no-slip condition: $f \leq \mu N$. To rank bodies, compare $k^2/r^2$: smaller = faster. Remember: friction in pure rolling does no work.

**Setup:** Ring, disc, and solid sphere start from rest at the top of an incline. Which arrives first? By what factor does the winner beat the loser?

**Solution:** $a_{\text{ring}} = \frac{1}{2}g\sin\theta$, $a_{\text{disc}} = \frac{2}{3}g\sin\theta$, $a_{\text{sphere}} = \frac{5}{7}g\sin\theta$. Since $5/7 > 2/3 > 1/2$: sphere wins, ring loses. Time to reach bottom (length $L$): $t = \sqrt{2L/a}$. $t_{\text{sphere}}/t_{\text{ring}} = \sqrt{a_{\text{ring}}/a_{\text{sphere}}} = \sqrt{(1/2)/(5/7)} = \sqrt{7/10} \approx 0.837$.

**Key insight:** The race result depends only on $k^2/r^2$, not on mass or radius.

**Setup:** Solid sphere ($k^2/r^2 = 2/5$) rolls down from height $h = 1$ m. Find the speed at the bottom.

**Solution:** $v^2 = \frac{2gh}{1 + k^2/r^2} = \frac{2 \times 9.8 \times 1}{1 + 2/5} = \frac{19.6}{1.4} = 14$. $v = \sqrt{14} \approx 3.74$ m/s.

**Key insight:** Without rolling (sliding), $v = \sqrt{2gh} \approx 4.43$ m/s. Rolling is slower because some energy goes into rotation.
