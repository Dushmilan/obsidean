
## Definition

**Rolling without slipping:** the no-slip constraint $v = \omega r$ (and $a = \alpha r$) — the contact point is instantaneously at rest. Total KE splits:

$$K = \frac12 Mv^2 + \frac12 I_{CM}\omega^2 = \frac12 Mv^2\left(1 + \frac{k^2}{r^2}\right)$$

**Rolling down an incline** ($\theta$):
$$a = \frac{g\sin\theta}{1 + k^2/r^2} \ < \ g\sin\theta \quad (\text{energy goes into rotation})$$

## The Intuition

A coin rolling on a table: friction at the contact point provides the torque for rotation. The contact point is the **instantaneous axis of rotation** (IAR). The CM moves at $v = \omega r$; the top of the body moves at $2v$. Without slipping, the arc length rolled equals the distance travelled: $s = r\theta$, hence $v = r\omega$ — the constraint reduces the problem to one variable.

## The Toolkit

| Quantity | Formula |
|----------|---------|
| No-slip constraint | $v = \omega r$, $a = \alpha r$ |
| Total KE | $\frac12 Mv^2 + \frac12 I_{CM}\omega^2$ |
| With gyration | $\frac12 Mv^2\left(1 + \dfrac{k^2}{r^2}\right)$ |
| Incline acceleration | $\dfrac{g\sin\theta}{1 + k^2/r^2}$ |
| Disc/cylinder ratio | $k^2/r^2 = \frac12$ |
| Solid sphere ratio | $k^2/r^2 = \frac25$ |
| Hoop ratio | $k^2/r^2 = 1$ |

## Derivation

No-slip: arc $s = r\theta$ → differentiate for $v = r\omega$. KE split: the body translates with the CM and rotates about it (König). Incline: along the plane $Mg\sin\theta - f = Ma$; torque about CM $fr = I_{CM}\alpha = I_{CM}a/r$; eliminate $f$ → $a = g\sin\theta/(1 + I_{CM}/Mr^2) = g\sin\theta/(1 + k^2/r^2)$. [Full derivations: 06.5-Rolling-Motion-Proof]

## Method

1. **Confirm no slip** — then $v = \omega r$ and $a = \alpha r$ link translation and rotation.
2. **Energy problems:** $K = \frac12 Mv^2(1 + k^2/r^2)$; solve for $v$.
3. **Incline dynamics:** use $a = g\sin\theta/(1+k^2/r^2)$ — compare bodies by their $k^2/r^2$ (hoop rolls slowest).
4. If the surface is rough enough, friction is static (does no work); slipping problems change the physics.

## Worked Examples

**Setup:** A solid sphere, disc, and hoop of equal mass and radius roll down the same incline. Which reaches the bottom first?

**Solution:** $a \propto 1/(1 + k^2/r^2)$: sphere $\frac57$, disc $\frac23$, hoop $\frac12$ — **sphere wins**, hoop loses.

**Key insight:** More mass near the centre (smaller $k^2/r^2$) → less rotational energy → faster descent.

---

**Setup:** A disc rolls from rest down a height $h$. Find its CM speed at the bottom.

**Solution:** Energy: $Mgh = \frac12 Mv^2(1+\frac12) = \frac34 Mv^2 \Rightarrow v = \sqrt{\frac43 gh}$.

**Key insight:** Rolling uses some of the PE for rotation — the speed is less than $\sqrt{2gh}$ (slipping).

## Common Traps

- Assuming friction does work in rolling — static friction at the contact point does **zero** work (no slip)
- Using $v = \omega r$ when slipping occurs (then the constraint breaks)
- Forgetting the rotational KE term in rolling energy
- Comparing $a$ values without the $1+k^2/r^2$ denominator
- Top speed is $2v$ (translation + rotation add), not $v$
