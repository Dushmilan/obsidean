This topic derives the moment of inertia for six standard bodies by direct integration, then uses the parallel axes and perpendicular axes theorems to find MIs about other axes. The integration method follows a standard recipe: choose a coordinate system, express $dm$ in terms of density and a differential element, express the perpendicular distance $r$ from the axis, and integrate $I = \int r^2\,dm$. The key results are building blocks — every composite body problem reduces to combining these standard results.

**The Intuition:** A hoop vs a solid disc of the same mass — the hoop has all its mass at distance $R$, so $I = MR^2$. The disc has mass spread from 0 to $R$, so $I = \frac{1}{2}MR^2$ — half as much, because inner mass contributes less. MI weights mass by $r^2$. Mass at the edge contributes maximally; mass at the centre contributes nothing. That is why hollow bodies have higher MI than solid ones of the same mass. A sphere's MI ($\frac{2}{5}MR^2$) is less than a hollow sphere's ($\frac{2}{3}MR^2$) because the solid sphere has mass concentrated nearer the centre.

**The Math:** Disc about central axis: Disc = concentric rings of radius $r$, width $dr$. $dm = \sigma \cdot 2\pi r\,dr = \frac{M}{\pi R^2} \cdot 2\pi r\,dr = \frac{2M}{R^2}r\,dr$. $I = \int_0^R r^2\,dm = \frac{2M}{R^2}\int_0^R r^3\,dr = \frac{2M}{R^2}\cdot\frac{R^4}{4} = \frac{1}{2}MR^2$.

Solid sphere by integration: Slice into discs of thickness $dx$ at distance $x$ from centre. Radius of disc: $y = \sqrt{R^2 - x^2}$. $dm = \rho\pi y^2\,dx = \frac{3M}{4R^3}\pi(R^2-x^2)\,dx$. MI of disc about its diameter: $dI = \frac{1}{4}\,dm\,y^2 = \frac{1}{4}\cdot\frac{3M}{4R^3}(R^2-x^2)^2\,dx$. $I = \frac{3M}{16R^3}\int_{-R}^{R}(R^2-x^2)^2\,dx = \frac{2}{5}MR^2$.

Hollow sphere by integration: Slice into rings of radius $R\sin\theta$, width $R\,d\theta$. $dm = \sigma \cdot 2\pi R\sin\theta \cdot R\,d\theta = \frac{M}{2}\sin\theta\,d\theta$. Distance from diameter: $r = R\sin\theta$. $I = \int_0^\pi r^2\,dm = \frac{MR^2}{2}\int_0^\pi \sin^3\theta\,d\theta = \frac{2}{3}MR^2$.

Ring vs disc: Ring $I = MR^2$ (all mass at $R$). Disc $I = \frac{1}{2}MR^2$ (mass distributed from 0 to $R$). Solid vs hollow sphere: Solid $\frac{2}{5}MR^2 = 0.4MR^2$. Hollow $\frac{2}{3}MR^2 \approx 0.67MR^2$ (about 67% more MI).

**What does this mean for Applied Mechanics?** Memorise the standard MIs — they appear in virtually every rotational dynamics problem. For new axes, use parallel axes: $I = I_{CM} + Md^2$. For laminas, use perpendicular axes: $I_z = I_x + I_y$. For composite bodies, add MIs of each component about the same axis. Always check: is the axis through the CM? If not, shift first.

**Setup:** Uniform solid sphere, radius $R$, mass $M$. Find $I$ about a diameter by integrating by slicing into discs.

**Solution:** Slice into discs of thickness $dx$ at distance $x$ from centre. Radius of disc: $y = \sqrt{R^2 - x^2}$. $dm = \rho\pi y^2\,dx = \frac{3M}{4R^3}\pi(R^2-x^2)\,dx$. MI of disc about its diameter: $dI = \frac{1}{4}\,dm\,y^2 = \frac{1}{4}\cdot\frac{3M}{4R^3}(R^2-x^2)^2\,dx$. $I = \frac{3M}{16R^3}\int_{-R}^{R}(R^2-x^2)^2\,dx = \frac{3M}{16R^3}\cdot\frac{16R^5}{15} = \frac{2}{5}MR^2$.

**Key insight:** The integration over the sphere's volume naturally produces the $\frac{2}{5}$ factor.
