
## Definition

The MI of six standard bodies (by integration), used as building blocks for composites:

| Body | Axis | $I$ |
|------|------|-----|
| Hoop/ring | central, ⊥ plane | $MR^2$ |
| Solid disc/cylinder | central | $\frac{1}{2}MR^2$ |
| Solid sphere | diameter | $\frac{2}{5}MR^2$ |
| Hollow sphere | diameter | $\frac{2}{3}MR^2$ |
| Rod | centre, ⊥ length | $\frac{1}{12}ML^2$ |
| Rod | end | $\frac{1}{3}ML^2$ |

## The Intuition

A hoop vs a solid disc of the same mass: the hoop has *all* its mass at distance $R$ → $I = MR^2$. The disc spreads mass from 0 to $R$ → $I = \frac12 MR^2$. MI weights mass by $r^2$: edge mass contributes maximally, centre mass nothing. That's why hollow bodies have higher MI than solid ones of the same mass.

## The Toolkit

| Result | Formula |
|--------|---------|
| Disc (central) | $I = \frac12 MR^2$ |
| Solid sphere | $I = \frac25 MR^2$ |
| Thin rod (centre) | $I = \frac{1}{12}ML^2$ |
| Rod (end) | $I = \frac13 ML^2$ |
| Any MI about CM axis | shift with $I = I_{CM} + Md^2$ |

## Derivation

**Disc:** concentric rings — $dm = \frac{M}{\pi R^2}2\pi r\,dr = \frac{2M}{R^2}r\,dr$, then $I = \int_0^R r^2 dm = \frac{2M}{R^2}\frac{R^4}{4} = \frac12 MR^2$. **Solid sphere:** slice into discs at distance $x$ (radius $y = \sqrt{R^2-x^2}$) and integrate. **Rod:** $I = \int_{-L/2}^{L/2}x^2\frac{M}{L}dx = \frac{1}{12}ML^2$. [Full derivations: 06.2-MI-Standard-Bodies-Proof]

## Method

1. **Recognise the body + axis** — pick from the standard table.
2. Different axis → parallel-axes theorem ($I = I_{CM} + Md^2$).
3. Composite bodies → combine standard MIs (subtract holes as negative mass).
4. Unknown shapes → integrate $I = \int r^2\,dm$ with the right $dm$.

## Worked Examples

**Setup:** A solid disc of mass $M$, radius $R$ rotates about an axis through its rim, ⊥ the plane. Find $I$.

**Solution:** Central disc $I_{CM} = \frac12 MR^2$; parallel axes with $d = R$: $I = \frac12 MR^2 + MR^2 = \frac32 MR^2$.

**Key insight:** Any off-centre axis = standard value + $Md^2$ — no re-integration.

---

**Setup:** Two discs (radius $R$) at the ends of a light rod of length $L$ rotate about the rod's centre. Find $I$.

**Solution:** $I = 2\left(\frac12 MR^2 + M(L/2)^2\right) = MR^2 + \frac12 ML^2$.

**Key insight:** Each disc contributes its own MI plus the $Md^2$ shift — composite = sum of parts.

## Common Traps

- Hoop vs disc: $MR^2$ vs $\frac12 MR^2$ — the factor depends on the mass distribution
- Solid vs hollow sphere: $\frac25$ vs $\frac23$
- Rod centre vs end: $\frac{1}{12}$ vs $\frac13$
- Forgetting the parallel-axes shift on off-centre axes
- Treating a cylinder as a disc for end-to-end axes
