
## Definition

**Rotational KE** (fixed axis): $K_{\text{rot}} = \frac12 I\omega^2$.
**Total KE** (general motion): $K = \frac12 Mv_{CM}^2 + \frac12 I_{CM}\omega^2$ — translational + rotational.
**Angular momentum:** $L = I\omega$. **Torque law:** $\tau_{\text{ext}} = \frac{dL}{dt}$; **conservation:** $\tau_{\text{ext}} = 0 \Rightarrow L$ constant.

## The Intuition

A spinning ice skater pulling arms in: $I$ decreases, so $\omega$ increases to conserve $L$ — and rotational KE increases (the muscles do work). Each mass element $dm$ at distance $r$ has speed $v = \omega r$ and KE $\frac12 dm\,\omega^2 r^2$; summing gives $\frac12 I\omega^2$. The $r^2$ weighting in $I$ captures how mass distribution affects rotational energy.

## The Toolkit

| Quantity | Formula | Analogue |
|----------|---------|----------|
| Rotational KE | $K_{\text{rot}} = \frac12 I\omega^2$ | $\frac12 mv^2$ |
| Total KE | $\frac12 Mv_{CM}^2 + \frac12 I_{CM}\omega^2$ | — |
| Angular momentum | $L = I\omega$ | $p = mv$ |
| Torque | $\tau = I\alpha = \frac{dL}{dt}$ | $F = ma = \frac{dp}{dt}$ |
| Work (rotation) | $W = \tau\theta$ | $W = Fs$ |
| Conservation | $L = I\omega$ constant if $\tau_{\text{ext}} = 0$ | $p$ conserved |

## Derivation

Every element has KE $\frac12 dm\,(\omega r)^2$; integrating over the body gives $\frac12 \omega^2 \int r^2 dm = \frac12 I\omega^2$. The general-motion split comes from separating the motion into CM translation plus rotation about the CM (König's theorem). Angular momentum: $\vec{L} = \int \vec{r}\times\vec{v}\,dm = I\vec{\omega}$ for a rigid body about a fixed axis. [Full derivations: 06.3-Rotational-KE-Angular-Momentum-Proof]

## Method

1. **Identify the axis** and the right $I$ (standard table + theorems).
2. General motion: split KE into translation of the CM + rotation about the CM.
3. **Conservation:** if no external torque, $I_1\omega_1 = I_2\omega_2$ — solve for the new $\omega$.
4. Work-energy with rotation: $W = \tau\theta = \Delta K_{\text{rot}}$.

## Worked Examples

**Setup:** A disc ($M$, $R$) rolls with CM speed $v$. Find its total KE.

**Solution:** $K = \frac12 Mv^2 + \frac12(\frac12 MR^2)(v/R)^2 = \frac12 Mv^2 + \frac14 Mv^2 = \frac34 Mv^2$.

**Key insight:** Rolling adds exactly half again to the translational KE for a disc ($k^2/r^2 = \frac12$).

---

**Setup:** A skater spins at $\omega_1$ with $I_1$; pulling arms in halves the MI. Find the new $\omega$.

**Solution:** $I_1\omega_1 = I_2\omega_2 = \frac{I_1}{2}\omega_2 \Rightarrow \omega_2 = 2\omega_1$.

**Key insight:** Conservation of $L$: halve the moment of inertia, double the spin.

## Common Traps

- Forgetting the $\frac12 I_{CM}\omega^2$ term in rolling/general motion
- Using $L = I\omega$ for a point mass at radius $r$ as $I = mr^2$ — that's correct but easily mis-assigned
- Ignoring the direction of $\vec{L}$ (a vector)
- $\tau = I\alpha$ needs $I$ about the *same* axis as $\tau$
- Work $W = \tau\theta$ only for constant torque
