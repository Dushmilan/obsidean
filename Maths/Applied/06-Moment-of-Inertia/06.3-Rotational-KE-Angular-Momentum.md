# 6.3 Rotational Kinetic Energy & Angular Momentum

**Syllabus Reference:** GCE A/L Combined Mathematics (021) - Applied Mathematics Paper II, Topic 6.3

---

## 1. Rotational Kinetic Energy

### 1.1 Definition
A rigid body rotating with angular velocity $\omega$ about a fixed axis has rotational kinetic energy:

$$K_{rot} = \frac{1}{2} I \omega^2$$

where $I$ is the moment of inertia about the axis of rotation.

### 1.2 Derivation
For a particle of mass $m_i$ at distance $r_i$ from axis, moving with speed $v_i = \omega r_i$:

$$K_i = \frac{1}{2} m_i v_i^2 = \frac{1}{2} m_i \omega^2 r_i^2$$

Total kinetic energy:
$$K_{rot} = \sum \frac{1}{2} m_i \omega^2 r_i^2 = \frac{1}{2} \omega^2 \sum m_i r_i^2 = \frac{1}{2} I \omega^2$$

### 1.3 Total Kinetic Energy of a Rigid Body
For a body in general motion (translation + rotation):
$$K_{total} = K_{trans} + K_{rot} = \frac{1}{2} M v_{CM}^2 + \frac{1}{2} I_{CM} \omega^2$$

This is the sum of translational KE of the centre of mass and rotational KE about the centre of mass.

---

## 2. Angular Momentum

### 2.1 For a Particle
Angular momentum of a particle about a point $O$:
$$\mathbf{L} = \mathbf{r} \times \mathbf{p} = m (\mathbf{r} \times \mathbf{v})$$

Magnitude: $L = m v r_\perp = m v r \sin\theta$

### 2.2 For a Rigid Body About a Fixed Axis
$$\mathbf{L} = I \boldsymbol{\omega}$$

where $I$ is the moment of inertia about the axis, and $\boldsymbol{\omega}$ is the angular velocity vector.

### 2.3 For a Rigid Body About an Arbitrary Point
$$\mathbf{L} = \mathbf{L}_{CM} + \mathbf{R}_{CM} \times \mathbf{P}_{total}$$
where $\mathbf{L}_{CM}$ is angular momentum about CM, $\mathbf{R}_{CM}$ is position of CM, and $\mathbf{P}_{total}$ is total linear momentum.

---

## 3. Torque and Angular Momentum

### 3.1 Torque on a Particle
$$\boldsymbol{\tau} = \mathbf{r} \times \mathbf{F}$$

### 3.2 Newton's Second Law for Rotation
For a rigid body rotating about a fixed axis:
$$\tau = I \alpha$$

where $\tau$ is the net external torque about the axis, and $\alpha = \frac{d\omega}{dt}$ is the angular acceleration.

### 3.3 Angular Momentum Principle
$$\boldsymbol{\tau}_{ext} = \frac{d\mathbf{L}}{dt}$$

For a fixed axis: $\tau_{ext} = \frac{dL}{dt} = I \alpha$

**Proof:** See [[Proofs/06.3-Rotational-KE-Angular-Momentum-Proof]]

---

## 4. Conservation of Angular Momentum

If the net external torque about an axis is zero:
$$\frac{dL}{dt} = 0 \quad \Rightarrow \quad L = \text{constant}$$

i.e., $I \omega = \text{constant}$

**Applications:**
- Ice skater pulling arms in
- Collapsing star
- Rotating platform with moving mass

---

## 5. Work-Energy Theorem for Rotation

Work done by a constant torque $\tau$ through angular displacement $\theta$:
$$W = \tau \theta$$

Work-energy theorem:
$$W_{net} = \Delta K_{rot} = \frac{1}{2} I \omega_f^2 - \frac{1}{2} I \omega_i^2$$

For variable torque: $W = \int \tau d\theta$

---

## 6. Power in Rotational Motion

Instantaneous power:
$$P = \tau \omega$$

---

## 7. Rolling Motion (Without Slipping)

### 7.1 Kinematic Constraint
$$v_{CM} = \omega R, \quad a_{CM} = \alpha R$$

### 7.2 Total Kinetic Energy
$$K = \frac{1}{2} M v_{CM}^2 + \frac{1}{2} I_{CM} \omega^2 = \frac{1}{2} M v_{CM}^2 \left(1 + \frac{k^2}{R^2}\right)$$

where $k$ is the radius of gyration about CM.

### 7.3 Rolling Down an Inclined Plane
For a body rolling down an incline of angle $\theta$ without slipping:

Acceleration:
$$a = \frac{g \sin\theta}{1 + k^2/R^2}$$

Time to reach bottom (length $L$):
$$t = \sqrt{\frac{2L}{a}} = \sqrt{\frac{2L(1 + k^2/R^2)}{g \sin\theta}}$$

**Note:** Acceleration depends on $k^2/R^2$ (shape), not on mass or radius separately.

| Body | $k^2/R^2$ | $a/g\sin\theta$ |
|------|-----------|-----------------|
| Ring/Hoop | 1 | $1/2$ |
| Disc/Cylinder | $1/2$ | $2/3$ |
| Solid sphere | $2/5$ | $5/7$ |
| Hollow sphere | $2/3$ | $3/5$ |

---

## 8. Equations of Motion for Rolling

### Forces acting:
- Weight $Mg$ down the plane: $Mg \sin\theta$
- Friction $f$ up the plane
- Normal reaction $N = Mg \cos\theta$

### Equations:
1. Translation: $Mg \sin\theta - f = M a$
2. Rotation about CM: $f R = I_{CM} \alpha = I_{CM} \frac{a}{R}$
3. Rolling condition: $a = \alpha R$

Eliminating $f$:
$$Mg \sin\theta - \frac{I_{CM}}{R^2} a = M a \quad \Rightarrow \quad a = \frac{g \sin\theta}{1 + I_{CM}/MR^2} = \frac{g \sin\theta}{1 + k^2/R^2}$$

---

## 9. Key Formulae Summary

| Quantity | Formula |
|----------|---------|
| Rotational KE | $\frac{1}{2} I \omega^2$ |
| Angular momentum (fixed axis) | $L = I \omega$ |
| Torque | $\tau = I \alpha$ |
| Angular momentum principle | $\tau = \frac{dL}{dt}$ |
| Work by torque | $W = \int \tau d\theta$ |
| Power | $P = \tau \omega$ |
| Rolling acceleration | $a = \frac{g \sin\theta}{1 + k^2/R^2}$ |
| Rolling KE | $\frac{1}{2} M v^2 (1 + k^2/R^2)$ |

---

## 10. Key Points for Exams

1. **Distinguish** between rotation about fixed axis and general motion
2. **Use** $K = \frac{1}{2}Mv_{CM}^2 + \frac{1}{2}I_{CM}\omega^2$ for general motion
3. **Conservation of angular momentum** applies when net external torque = 0
4. **For rolling:** always use $v = \omega R$ and $a = \alpha R$
5. **Friction** in rolling does no work (point of contact is instantaneously at rest)
6. **Direction of friction** in rolling: acts to prevent slipping, direction depends on situation
