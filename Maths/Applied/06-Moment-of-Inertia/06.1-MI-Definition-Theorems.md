# 6.1 Moment of Inertia — Definition & Theorems

**Syllabus Reference:** GCE A/L Combined Mathematics (021) - Applied Mathematics Paper II, Topic 6.1

---

## 1. Definition of Moment of Inertia

### 1.1 Discrete Mass Distribution
For a system of particles with masses $m_i$ at perpendicular distances $r_i$ from an axis:

$$I = \sum_{i} m_i r_i^2$$

### 1.2 Continuous Mass Distribution
For a continuous body with mass density $\rho$:

$$I = \int r^2 \, dm$$

where $dm = \rho dV$ (3D), $dm = \sigma dA$ (2D), $dm = \lambda dl$ (1D), and $r$ is the perpendicular distance from the axis.

### 1.3 Radius of Gyration
The radius of gyration $k$ is defined such that:

$$I = Mk^2 \quad \Rightarrow \quad k = \sqrt{\frac{I}{M}}$$

It represents the distance from the axis at which the entire mass could be concentrated to give the same moment of inertia.

---

## 2. Parallel Axes Theorem (Steiner's Theorem)

### 2.1 Statement
The moment of inertia $I$ of a body about any axis is equal to its moment of inertia $I_{CM}$ about a parallel axis through the centre of mass plus the product of the total mass $M$ and the square of the perpendicular distance $d$ between the axes:

$$I = I_{CM} + Md^2$$

### 2.2 Proof
Let axis 1 pass through centre of mass $C$, axis 2 be parallel at distance $d$.

For a mass element $dm$ at position $\mathbf{r}$ relative to $C$, its distance from axis 2 is $|\mathbf{r} + \mathbf{d}|$ where $\mathbf{d}$ is the vector from axis 1 to axis 2 (perpendicular to both).

$$I = \int |\mathbf{r} + \mathbf{d}|^2 dm = \int (r^2 + d^2 + 2\mathbf{r}\cdot\mathbf{d}) dm$$

Since $\mathbf{d}$ is constant and $\int \mathbf{r} dm = 0$ (by definition of CM):

$$I = \int r^2 dm + d^2 \int dm + 2\mathbf{d} \cdot \int \mathbf{r} dm = I_{CM} + Md^2 + 0$$

---

## 3. Perpendicular Axes Theorem

### 3.1 Statement
For a **planar lamina** (thin flat body) lying in the $xy$-plane, the moment of inertia about the $z$-axis (perpendicular to the plane) is the sum of the moments of inertia about the $x$ and $y$ axes (both in the plane):

$$I_z = I_x + I_y$$

### 3.2 Proof
For a mass element $dm$ at $(x, y)$:

$$I_x = \int y^2 dm, \quad I_y = \int x^2 dm, \quad I_z = \int (x^2 + y^2) dm$$

$$I_z = \int x^2 dm + \int y^2 dm = I_x + I_y$$

**Note:** This theorem applies **only to planar bodies** (laminae).

---

## 4. Centre of Mass and Moment of Inertia

### 4.1 Centre of Mass Position Vector
$$\mathbf{R}_{CM} = \frac{1}{M} \int \mathbf{r} \, dm$$

### 4.2 Moment of Inertia About Centre of Mass
The moment of inertia is minimum about an axis through the centre of mass (for a given direction).

---

## 5. Calculation of Moment of Inertia for Common Geometries

### 5.1 Using Integration
1. Choose coordinate system
2. Express $dm$ in terms of density and differential element
3. Express $r$ (perpendicular distance from axis)
4. Integrate: $I = \int r^2 dm$

### 5.2 Using Theorems
- **Parallel axes:** Shift from CM axis to parallel axis
- **Perpendicular axes:** For laminae, find $I_z$ from $I_x, I_y$ or vice versa

## 6. Key Results for Common Symmetric Bodies (About Symmetry Axes)

| Body | Axis | $I$ |
|------|------|-----|
| Thin rod (length $L$) | Centre, $\perp$ to rod | $\frac{1}{12}ML^2$ |
| Thin rod | End, $\perp$ to rod | $\frac{1}{3}ML^2$ |
| Thin ring (radius $R$) | Centre, $\perp$ to plane | $MR^2$ |
| Thin ring | Diameter | $\frac{1}{2}MR^2$ |
| Disc (radius $R$) | Centre, $\perp$ to plane | $\frac{1}{2}MR^2$ |
| Disc | Diameter | $\frac{1}{4}MR^2$ |
| Solid sphere (radius $R$) | Any diameter | $\frac{2}{5}MR^2$ |
| Hollow sphere (radius $R$) | Any diameter | $\frac{2}{3}MR^2$ |
| Rectangular lamina ($a \times b$) | Centre, $\perp$ to plane | $\frac{1}{12}M(a^2+b^2)$ |
| Rectangular lamina | Centre, $\parallel$ to $b$ | $\frac{1}{12}Ma^2$ |

---

## 7. Applications of Theorems

### 7.1 Rod About End
$I_{\text{end}} = I_{\text{centre}} + M(L/2)^2 = \frac{1}{12}ML^2 + \frac{1}{4}ML^2 = \frac{1}{3}ML^2$

### 7.2 Ring About Tangent in Plane
$I_{\text{tangent}} = I_{\text{diam}} + MR^2 = \frac{1}{2}MR^2 + MR^2 = \frac{3}{2}MR^2$

### 7.3 Disc About Tangent Perpendicular to Plane
$I_{\text{tangent}} = I_{\text{centre}} + MR^2 = \frac{1}{2}MR^2 + MR^2 = \frac{3}{2}MR^2$

### 7.4 Rectangular Lamina About Corner
$I_{\text{corner}} = \frac{1}{12}M(a^2+b^2) + M\left(\frac{a}{2}\right)^2 + M\left(\frac{b}{2}\right)^2 = \frac{1}{3}M(a^2+b^2)$

---

## 8. Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Using perpendicular axes for 3D bodies** - only valid for planar laminae
2. **Confusing $d$ in parallel axes** - must be perpendicular distance between axes
3. **Forgetting to use CM as reference** - parallel axes theorem requires $I_{CM}$
4. **Mixing up axes** - clearly define which axis is which

---

## 9. Exam Tips

- **Memorise** standard MIs for rod, ring, disc, sphere
- **Practice** integration for at least one body type
- **Always** state which theorem you are using
- **Check** dimensions: $I$ has dimensions $ML^2$
- **Use** radius of gyration $k$ to compare bodies
