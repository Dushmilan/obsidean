# 6.4 Compound Pendulum

**Syllabus Reference:** GCE A/L Combined Mathematics (021) - Applied Mathematics Paper II, Topic 6.4

---

## 1. Definition

A **compound pendulum** (or physical pendulum) is a rigid body of any shape that oscillates about a horizontal axis not passing through its centre of mass.

---

## 2. Equation of Motion

Let:
- $M$ = total mass
- $h$ = distance from pivot to centre of mass
- $I$ = moment of inertia about the pivot axis
- $\theta$ = angular displacement from equilibrium

For small oscillations:

Torque about pivot: $\tau = -Mgh \sin\theta \approx -Mgh \theta$

Equation of motion: $I \ddot{\theta} = -Mgh \theta$

$$\ddot{\theta} + \frac{Mgh}{I} \theta = 0$$

This is SHM with angular frequency:

$$\omega = \sqrt{\frac{Mgh}{I}}$$

**Period of small oscillations:**
$$T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{I}{Mgh}}$$

---

## 3. Radius of Gyration and Equivalent Simple Pendulum

Let $k$ be the radius of gyration about the **centre of mass** ($I_{CM} = Mk^2$).

By parallel axes theorem:
$$I = I_{CM} + Mh^2 = M(k^2 + h^2)$$

Substitute into period formula:
$$T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{k^2 + h^2}{gh}}$$

Define **equivalent simple pendulum length** $L_{eq}$ such that:
$$T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{L_{eq}}{g}} \quad \Rightarrow \quad L_{eq} = \frac{k^2 + h^2}{h} = \frac{k^2}{h} + h$$

---

## 4. Centre of Oscillation

The point on the line joining the pivot to the centre of mass, at distance $L_{eq}$ from the pivot, is called the **centre of oscillation**.

If the pendulum is suspended from the centre of oscillation, the period is the same!

**Key property:** The periods about two points on the line through the centre of mass are equal if their distances from the CM are $h$ and $h'$ where:
$$h' = \frac{k^2}{h}$$

This means $h$ and $k^2/h$ are **conjugate points** with the same period.

---

## 5. Minimum Period

For a given body (fixed $k$), the period as a function of $h$ is:
$$T(h) = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{k^2/h + h}{g}}$$

To find minimum, set $dT/dh = 0$:
$$\frac{d}{dh}\left(\frac{k^2}{h} + h\right) = -\frac{k^2}{h^2} + 1 = 0 \quad \Rightarrow \quad h = k$$

**Minimum period:**
$$T_{min} = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{2k}{g}}$$

Occurs when the pivot is at distance $k$ (radius of gyration about CM) from the centre of mass.

---

## 6. Experimental Determination of $g$ and $k$

### 6.1 Method
1. Measure period $T$ for several values of $h$
2. Plot $T^2$ vs $h$ or $hT^2$ vs $h^2$

From $T^2 = \frac{4\pi^2}{g}\left(\frac{k^2}{h} + h\right)$:
$$h T^2 = \frac{4\pi^2}{g}(k^2 + h^2)$$

Plot $h T^2$ (y-axis) vs $h^2$ (x-axis):
- Slope = $\frac{4\pi^2}{g}$ ? find $g$
- Intercept = $\frac{4\pi^2}{g} k^2$ ? find $k$

---

## 7. Special Cases

### 7.1 Simple Pendulum
Point mass $m$ at distance $l$ from pivot:
$I = ml^2$, $h = l$, $k = 0$

$$T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{l}{g}}$$

### 7.2 Uniform Rod (Length $L$)
Pivoted at one end: $h = L/2$, $k^2 = L^2/12$

$$I = \frac{1}{3}ML^2, \quad T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{2L}{3g}}$$

Pivoted at distance $x$ from centre: $h = x$, $k^2 = L^2/12$

$$T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{L^2/12 + x^2}{gx}}$$

### 7.3 Circular Disc (Radius $R$)
Pivoted at a point on its rim:
$h = R$, $k^2 = R^2/2$

$$T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{3R}{2g}}$$

### 7.4 Circular Ring (Radius $R$)
Pivoted at a point on its rim:
$h = R$, $k^2 = R^2$

$$T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{2R}{g}}$$

---

## 8. Compound Pendulum vs Simple Pendulum

| Property | Simple Pendulum | Compound Pendulum |
|----------|-----------------|-------------------|
| Mass distribution | Point mass | Extended body |
| Period formula | $2\pi\sqrt{l/g}$ | $2\pi\sqrt{I/Mgh}$ |
| Equivalent length | $l$ | $L_{eq} = I/Mh$ |
| Centre of oscillation | At the bob | At distance $L_{eq}$ from pivot |
| Conjugate points | No | Yes ($h$ and $k^2/h$) |
| Minimum period | Not applicable | $T_{min} = 2\pi\sqrt{2k/g}$ at $h=k$ |

---

## 9. Applications

1. **Kater's Reversible Pendulum** - Used for precise $g$ measurement
2. **Determination of MI** - By measuring $T$ for different $h$
3. **Engineering** - Flywheels, rotating machinery balancing

---

## 10. Key Formulae Summary

| Quantity | Formula |
|----------|---------|
| Period | $T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{I}{Mgh}}$ |
| Parallel axes | $I = M(k^2 + h^2)$ |
| Equivalent length | $L_{eq} = \frac{k^2}{h} + h$ |
| Conjugate distance | $h' = \frac{k^2}{h}$ |
| Minimum period | $T_{min} = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{2k}{g}}$ at $h = k$ |

---

## 11. Exam Tips

1. **Always identify $h$ correctly** - distance from pivot to CM
2. **Use parallel axes** to get $I$ from $I_{CM} = Mk^2$
3. **Conjugate points** give same period - useful for finding $k$
4. **Minimum period** occurs at $h = k$ - memorise $T_{min} = 2\pi\sqrt{2k/g}$
5. **Small angle approximation** is used: $\sin\theta \approx \theta$
6. **Kater's pendulum** - adjust weights to make periods equal from both ends
