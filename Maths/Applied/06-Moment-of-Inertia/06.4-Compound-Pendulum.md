
## Definition

A rigid body oscillating about a horizontal pivot **not through its CM**. Small-angle SHM:

$$\ddot{\theta} + \frac{Mgh}{I_O}\theta = 0, \qquad \omega = \sqrt{\frac{Mgh}{I_O}}, \qquad T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{I_O}{Mgh}}$$

With the parallel-axes theorem, $I_O = I_{CM} + Mh^2 = M(k^2 + h^2)$, so

$$T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{k^2 + h^2}{gh}}$$

**Equivalent simple pendulum length:** $L_{\text{eq}} = \dfrac{k^2 + h^2}{h} = \dfrac{k^2}{h} + h$ — the length of a simple pendulum with the same period.

## The Intuition

A swinging ruler: pivot one end and it oscillates. The period depends on the mass distribution (captured by $I_O$) and how far the CM is from the pivot ($h$). The restoring torque $\tau = -Mgh\sin\theta$ comes from gravity acting at the CM; the resistance to rotation is $I_O$. Larger $I_O$ → slower; larger $h$ → stronger torque → faster. The period balances the two.

## The Toolkit

| Quantity | Formula |
|----------|---------|
| Restoring torque | $\tau = -Mgh\sin\theta \approx -Mgh\theta$ |
| ODE | $\ddot{\theta} + \frac{Mgh}{I_O}\theta = 0$ |
| Angular frequency | $\omega = \sqrt{\dfrac{Mgh}{I_O}}$ |
| Period | $T = 2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{I_O}{Mgh}} = 2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{k^2+h^2}{gh}}$ |
| Equivalent length | $L_{\text{eq}} = \dfrac{k^2+h^2}{h}$ |

## Derivation

Torque about the pivot from gravity acting at the CM: $\tau = -Mgh\sin\theta$. Newton II for rotation: $I_O\ddot{\theta} = \tau$. Small angle: $\sin\theta\approx\theta$ gives SHM with $\omega^2 = Mgh/I_O$. Substitute $I_O = M(k^2+h^2)$ for the gyration form. [Full derivations: 06.4-Compound-Pendulum-Proof]

## Method

1. Find $I_O$ about the pivot (parallel-axes theorem from the CM value).
2. Apply $\omega = \sqrt{Mgh/I_O}$ (or the period formula).
3. For "what pivot gives the shortest period": minimise $T$ over $h$ — the optimum is $h = k$ (the radius of gyration).

## Worked Examples

**Setup:** A uniform rod ($L = 1$ m) pivots at one end. Find the period.

**Solution:** $I_O = \frac13 ML^2$, $h = L/2$. $T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{I_O}{Mgh}} = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{ML^2/3}{MgL/2}} = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{2L}{3g}}$.

**Key insight:** For a rod about an end, the period is $\sqrt{2/3}$ that of a simple pendulum of length $L$.

---

**Setup:** A disc swings about a point on its rim. Find the equivalent simple pendulum length.

**Solution:** $I_O = \frac12 MR^2 + MR^2 = \frac32 MR^2$, $h = R$. $L_{\text{eq}} = \frac{I_O}{Mh} = \frac32 R$.

**Key insight:** The compound pendulum behaves like a simple pendulum of length $L_{\text{eq}}$ — a useful reduction.

## Common Traps

- Using $I_{CM}$ instead of $I_O$ about the pivot
- Forgetting $h$ is the pivot-to-CM distance
- Applying the simple-pendulum formula to a physical pendulum
- Sign of the restoring torque (opposing displacement)
- Small-angle assumption only — large oscillations need elliptic integrals
