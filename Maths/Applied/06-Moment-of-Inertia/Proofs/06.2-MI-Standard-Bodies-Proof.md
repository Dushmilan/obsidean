# 6.2 Moment of Inertia of Standard Bodies: Integration Proofs

**Syllabus Reference:** GCE A/L Combined Mathematics (021) - Applied Mathematics Paper II, Topic 6.2

---

## Proof 1: Thin Uniform Rod (Length $L$, Mass $M$)

### 1.1 Axis Through Centre, Perpendicular to Rod

Let linear density $\lambda = M/L$. Place rod along $x$-axis from $-L/2$ to $L/2$. Axis is $y$-axis.

$$I = \int_{-L/2}^{L/2} x^2 \lambda \, dx = \lambda \left[ \frac{x^3}{3} \right]_{-L/2}^{L/2} = \lambda \cdot \frac{2}{3} \left(\frac{L}{2}\right)^3 = \frac{M}{L} \cdot \frac{L^3}{12} = \frac{1}{12}ML^2$$

### 1.2 Axis Through One End, Perpendicular to Rod

Place rod from $0$ to $L$. Axis is $y$-axis.

$$I = \int_0^L x^2 \lambda \, dx = \lambda \left[ \frac{x^3}{3} \right]_0^L = \frac{M}{L} \cdot \frac{L^3}{3} = \frac{1}{3}ML^2$$

**Alternative:** Using parallel axes: $I = I_{CM} + M(L/2)^2 = \frac{1}{12}ML^2 + \frac{1}{4}ML^2 = \frac{1}{3}ML^2$

## Proof 2: Thin Circular Ring (Radius $R$, Mass $M$)

### 2.1 Axis Through Centre, Perpendicular to Plane

All mass elements at distance $R$:

$$I = \int R^2 dm = R^2 \int dm = MR^2$$

### 2.2 Axis Along a Diameter

Place ring in $xy$-plane, centre at origin. Axis is $x$-axis.
Mass element $dm = \frac{M}{2\pi} d\theta$ at angle $\theta$. Distance from $x$-axis: $|y| = R|\sin\theta|$.

$$I_x = \int_0^{2\pi} (R\sin\theta)^2 \frac{M}{2\pi} d\theta = \frac{MR^2}{2\pi} \int_0^{2\pi} \sin^2\theta \, d\theta = \frac{MR^2}{2\pi} \cdot \pi = \frac{1}{2}MR^2$$

---

## Proof 3: Uniform Circular Disc (Radius $R$, Mass $M$)

### 3.1 Axis Through Centre, Perpendicular to Plane

Surface density $\sigma = M/(\pi R^2)$. Divide into concentric rings of radius $r$, width $dr$.

Mass of ring: $dm = \sigma \cdot 2\pi r dr = \frac{2M}{R^2} r dr$

MI of ring about centre: $dI = r^2 dm = \frac{2M}{R^2} r^3 dr$

$$I = \int_0^R \frac{2M}{R^2} r^3 dr = \frac{2M}{R^2} \left[ \frac{r^4}{4} \right]_0^R = \frac{2M}{R^2} \cdot \frac{R^4}{4} = \frac{1}{2}MR^2$$

### 3.2 Axis Along a Diameter

By perpendicular axes theorem (valid for laminae):

$$I_{\perp} = I_x + I_y = 2I_{diam}$$

$$I_{diam} = \frac{1}{2} I_{\perp} = \frac{1}{4}MR^2$$

## Proof 4: Solid Sphere (Radius $R$, Mass $M$)

### 4.1 Axis Through Centre (Any Diameter)

Volume density $\rho = M / (\frac{4}{3}\pi R^3)$.

Slice sphere into discs perpendicular to $x$-axis (the rotation axis).
Disc at position $x$ has radius $y = \sqrt{R^2 - x^2}$, thickness $dx$.

Mass of disc: $dm = \rho \pi y^2 dx = \rho \pi (R^2 - x^2) dx$

MI of disc about its symmetry axis: $dI = \frac{1}{2} dm y^2 = \frac{1}{2} \rho \pi (R^2 - x^2)^2 dx$

$$I = \frac{1}{2} \rho \pi \int_{-R}^R (R^2 - x^2)^2 dx = \rho \pi \int_0^R (R^4 - 2R^2x^2 + x^4) dx$$

$$= \rho \pi \left[ R^4x - \frac{2}{3}R^2x^3 + \frac{1}{5}x^5 \right]_0^R = \rho \pi R^5 \left(1 - \frac{2}{3} + \frac{1}{5}\right)$$

$$= \rho \pi R^5 \cdot \frac{15 - 10 + 3}{15} = \rho \pi R^5 \cdot \frac{8}{15}$$

Substitute $\rho = \frac{3M}{4\pi R^3}$:

$$I = \frac{3M}{4\pi R^3} \cdot \pi R^5 \cdot \frac{8}{15} = \frac{24}{60}MR^2 = \frac{2}{5}MR^2$$

## Proof 5: Hollow Sphere / Thin Spherical Shell (Radius $R$, Mass $M$)

### 5.1 Axis Through Centre (Any Diameter)

Surface density $\sigma = M/(4\pi R^2)$.

Slice into rings between $\theta$ and $\theta + d\theta$ from vertical axis.
Ring radius: $R\sin\theta$, width: $Rd\theta$.
Ring area: $2\pi R\sin\theta \cdot Rd\theta = 2\pi R^2 \sin\theta d\theta$
Ring mass: $dm = \sigma \cdot 2\pi R^2 \sin\theta d\theta = \frac{M}{2} \sin\theta d\theta$

Distance of ring elements from vertical axis: $R\sin\theta$.

$$I = \int_0^\pi (R\sin\theta)^2 \cdot \frac{M}{2} \sin\theta d\theta = \frac{MR^2}{2} \int_0^\pi \sin^3\theta d\theta$$

$$\int_0^\pi \sin^3\theta d\theta = \int_0^\pi \sin\theta(1-\cos^2\theta) d\theta = \left[-\cos\theta + \frac{\cos^3\theta}{3}\right]_0^\pi = \frac{4}{3}$$

$$I = \frac{MR^2}{2} \cdot \frac{4}{3} = \frac{2}{3}MR^2$$

## Proof 6: Rectangular Lamina (Sides $a$, $b$, Mass $M$)

### 6.1 Axis Through Centre, Perpendicular to Plane

Surface density $\sigma = M/(ab)$. Place lamina in $xy$-plane with centre at origin, extending from $-a/2$ to $a/2$ in $x$, $-b/2$ to $b/2$ in $y$.

$$I = \sigma \int_{-a/2}^{a/2} \int_{-b/2}^{b/2} (x^2 + y^2) dy dx$$

$$= \sigma \left[ \int_{-a/2}^{a/2} x^2 dx \int_{-b/2}^{b/2} dy + \int_{-a/2}^{a/2} dx \int_{-b/2}^{b/2} y^2 dy \right]$$

$$= \sigma \left[ \frac{a^3}{12} \cdot b + a \cdot \frac{b^3}{12} \right] = \frac{M}{ab} \cdot \frac{ab(a^2+b^2)}{12} = \frac{1}{12}M(a^2+b^2)$$

### 6.2 Axis Through Centre, Parallel to Side $b$

$$I = \sigma \int_{-a/2}^{a/2} \int_{-b/2}^{b/2} x^2 dy dx = \sigma \cdot \frac{a^3}{12} \cdot b = \frac{M}{ab} \cdot \frac{a^3 b}{12} = \frac{1}{12}Ma^2$$

---

## Proof 7: Solid Cylinder (Radius $R$, Height $h$, Mass $M$)

### 7.1 Axis Along Cylinder Axis

Same as disc (independent of height): $I = \frac{1}{2}MR^2$

### 7.2 Axis Through Centre, Perpendicular to Cylinder Axis

Slice cylinder into discs of thickness $dx$ at position $x$ from centre.
Disc mass: $dm = \frac{M}{h} dx$
MI of disc about diameter: $dI = \frac{1}{4}dm R^2 + dm x^2$ (perpendicular axes + parallel axes)

$$I = \int_{-h/2}^{h/2} \left( \frac{1}{4}R^2 + x^2 \right) \frac{M}{h} dx = \frac{M}{h} \left[ \frac{1}{4}R^2 x + \frac{x^3}{3} \right]_{-h/2}^{h/2}$$

$$= \frac{M}{h} \left( \frac{1}{4}R^2 h + 2 \cdot \frac{h^3}{24} \right) = M \left( \frac{1}{4}R^2 + \frac{h^2}{12} \right) = \frac{1}{12}M(3R^2 + h^2)$$
