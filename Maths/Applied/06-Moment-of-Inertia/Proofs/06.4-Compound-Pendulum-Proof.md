# 6.4 Compound Pendulum: Proofs

**Syllabus Reference:** GCE A/L Combined Mathematics (021) - Applied Mathematics Paper II, Topic 6.4

---

## Proof 1: Period of Small Oscillations

Consider a rigid body of mass $M$, pivoted at point $O$, with centre of mass $G$ at distance $h$ from $O$. Moment of inertia about $O$ is $I$.

When displaced by small angle $\theta$, the torque about $O$ is:
$$\tau = -Mgh \sin\theta$$

For small angles: $\sin\theta \approx \theta$, so:
$$\tau \approx -Mgh \theta$$

Equation of motion:
$$I \ddot{\theta} = -Mgh \theta$$

$$\ddot{\theta} + \frac{Mgh}{I} \theta = 0$$

This is SHM with angular frequency:
$$\omega = \sqrt{\frac{Mgh}{I}}$$

Period:
$$T = \frac{2\pi}{\omega} = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{I}{Mgh}}$$

---

## Proof 2: Equivalent Simple Pendulum Length

Let $I_G = M k^2$ where $k$ is the radius of gyration about $G$.

By parallel axes theorem:
$$I = I_G + Mh^2 = M(k^2 + h^2)$$

Substitute into period formula:
$$T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{M(k^2 + h^2)}{Mgh}} = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{k^2 + h^2}{gh}}$$

For a simple pendulum of length $L_{eq}$:
$$T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{L_{eq}}{g}}$$

Equating:
$$L_{eq} = \frac{k^2 + h^2}{h} = h + \frac{k^2}{h}$$

This is the **equivalent simple pendulum length**.

---

## Proof 3: Centre of Oscillation

For a given pivot at distance $h$ from $G$, there is another point on the opposite side of $G$ at distance $h'$ that gives the **same period**.

From $T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{h + k^2/h}{g}} = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{h' + k^2/h'}{g}}$, we get:

$$h + \frac{k^2}{h} = h' + \frac{k^2}{h'}$$

$$h - h' = k^2 \left( \frac{1}{h'} - \frac{1}{h} \right) = k^2 \frac{h - h'}{hh'}$$

Assuming $h \neq h'$ (different points):
$$1 = \frac{k^2}{hh'} \quad \Rightarrow \quad hh' = k^2$$

Therefore:
$$h' = \frac{k^2}{h}$$

This is the distance of the **centre of oscillation** from $G$, on the opposite side from the pivot.

---

## Proof 4: Minimum Period

Period squared:
$$T^2 = \frac{4\pi^2}{g} \left( h + \frac{k^2}{h} \right)$$

Minimize $f(h) = h + \frac{k^2}{h}$ for $h > 0$.

$$\frac{df}{dh} = 1 - \frac{k^2}{h^2} = 0 \quad \Rightarrow \quad h^2 = k^2 \quad \Rightarrow \quad h = k$$

(Since $h > 0$)

Second derivative: $\frac{d^2f}{dh^2} = \frac{2k^2}{h^3} > 0$ at $h = k$, confirming minimum.

Minimum value: $f_{min} = k + \frac{k^2}{k} = 2k$

Minimum period:
$$T_{min} = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{2k}{g}}$$

At minimum period, $h = k$, so $h' = \frac{k^2}{h} = \frac{k^2}{k} = k = h$. The two suspension points coincide at distance $k$ from $G$.

## Proof 5: Determining $g$ and $k$ Experimentally

### Method: Two Suspension Points with Equal Period

Suspend from points $P$ and $Q$ on opposite sides of $G$ with distances $h_1$ and $h_2$ from $G$. Measure common period $T$.

From $hh' = k^2$: $h_1 h_2 = k^2$

From period formula:
$$T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{h_1 + h_2}{g}} \quad \text{(since } L_{eq} = h_1 + h_2\text{)}$$

$$g = \frac{4\pi^2 (h_1 + h_2)}{T^2}$$

---

## Proof 6: Symmetry of $T^2$ vs $h$ Curve

Plot $T^2$ against $h$. For each $h$, the period is the same as for $h' = k^2/h$.

The curve is symmetric about $h = k$ in the sense that points $h$ and $k^2/h$ have the same $T$.

For large $h$: $T \approx 2\pi \sqrt{h/g}$ (simple pendulum behaviour)
For small $h$: $T \approx 2\pi \sqrt{k^2/gh}$ (diverges as $h \to 0$)

Minimum at $h = k$.

## Proof 7: Damped Compound Pendulum (Advanced)

With damping torque $-\gamma \dot{\theta}$:

$$I \ddot{\theta} + \gamma \dot{\theta} + Mgh \theta = 0$$

$$\ddot{\theta} + 2\beta \dot{\theta} + \omega_0^2 \theta = 0$$

where $\beta = \frac{\gamma}{2I}$, $\omega_0^2 = \frac{Mgh}{I}$

For light damping ($\beta < \omega_0$):
$$\theta(t) = \theta_0 e^{-\beta t} \cos(\omega t + \phi)$$
$$\omega = \sqrt{\omega_0^2 - \beta^2}$$

Period:
$$T = \frac{2\pi}{\omega} = \frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{\omega_0^2 - \beta^2}} \approx T_0 \left(1 + \frac{\beta^2}{2\omega_0^2}\right)$$

Amplitude decays exponentially with time constant $\tau = 1/\beta$.

## Proof 8: Uniform Rod Example

Rod of length $L$, mass $M$, pivoted at one end.

$I_G = \frac{1}{12}ML^2$, $k^2 = \frac{L^2}{12}$

Pivot at end: $h = L/2$

$I = I_G + Mh^2 = \frac{1}{12}ML^2 + M(L/2)^2 = \frac{1}{3}ML^2$

Period:
$$T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{\frac{1}{3}ML^2}{Mg(L/2)}} = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{2L}{3g}}$$

Equivalent length: $L_{eq} = \frac{2}{3}L$

Centre of oscillation: $h' = \frac{k^2}{h} = \frac{L^2/12}{L/2} = \frac{L}{6}$ from centre = $\frac{L}{3}$ from pivot.
