
## Definition

A vehicle of mass $m$ moves in a horizontal circle of radius $r$ on a track banked at angle $\theta$. Three forces act: weight $mg$, normal $R$ (⊥ surface), friction $F$ (∥ surface, direction depends on speed).

**Design speed** (no friction needed): $v_0 = \sqrt{rg\tan\theta}$ — from $R\cos\theta = mg$, $R\sin\theta = mv^2/r$, giving $\tan\theta = \frac{v^2}{rg}$.

**With friction ($\mu$):**
$$v_{\max} = \sqrt{\frac{rg(\tan\theta + \mu)}{1 - \mu\tan\theta}}, \qquad v_{\min} = \sqrt{\frac{rg(\tan\theta - \mu)}{1 + \mu\tan\theta}}$$

## The Intuition

A tilted tray with a rolling ball: at just the right speed the ball circles without sliding up or down. Too fast → slides up; too slow → slides down. On a flat road, friction alone provides the centripetal force; banking lets the normal reaction contribute a horizontal component. At the design speed, friction is zero. Steeper bank + tighter curve → higher design speed (why exit ramps and race tracks are banked).

## The Toolkit

| Case | Speed |
|------|-------|
| Design (no friction) | $v_0 = \sqrt{rg\tan\theta}$ |
| Maximum (about to slide up) | $\sqrt{\dfrac{rg(\tan\theta + \mu)}{1 - \mu\tan\theta}}$ |
| Minimum (about to slide down) | $\sqrt{\dfrac{rg(\tan\theta - \mu)}{1 + \mu\tan\theta}}$ |

**Friction direction:** above design speed → friction acts *down* the slope; below → *up* the slope. (It opposes the tendency to slide.)

## Derivation

Design speed: resolve $R$ — vertical component balances $mg$, horizontal provides centripetal; divide to eliminate $R$. With friction, add $F = \mu R$ parallel to the surface (up or down depending on which way the car tends to slide), resolve, and solve for $v$. [Full derivations: 04.4-Proofs-Banked-Tracks]

## Method

1. **At design speed:** no friction — just $R\cos\theta = mg$ and $R\sin\theta = mv^2/r$.
2. **Above/below design speed:** include friction in the direction opposing the slide tendency.
3. Solve for $v$; check $\mu\tan\theta < 1$ for the formulas to be finite.

## Worked Examples

**Setup:** A track of radius 100 m is banked at $20^\circ$. Find the design speed.

**Solution:** $v_0 = \sqrt{100 \cdot 10 \cdot \tan 20^\circ} = \sqrt{1000\cdot0.364} \approx 19.1$ m/s.

**Key insight:** The design speed lets cars corner with zero friction — independent of mass.

---

**Setup:** With $\mu = 0.4$, find $v_{\max}$ on the same bank.

**Solution:** $v_{\max} = \sqrt{\frac{1000(0.364 + 0.4)}{1 - 0.4(0.364)}} = \sqrt{\frac{764}{0.854}} \approx 29.9$ m/s.

**Key insight:** Friction widens the allowed speed band around the design speed.

## Common Traps

- Friction direction — depends on speed relative to design speed, not on the car's position
- Applying the no-friction formula when $\mu$ is given
- $1 - \mu\tan\theta$ sign — if it's ≤ 0 the vehicle can't be held by friction at any speed
- Using $mg$ where the resolved normal components belong
- Treating friction as always up or always down the slope
