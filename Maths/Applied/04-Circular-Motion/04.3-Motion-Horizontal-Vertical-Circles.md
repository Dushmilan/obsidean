
## Definition

- **Horizontal circles:** constant speed, straightforward centripetal analysis.
- **Vertical circles:** gravity changes the speed — fastest at the bottom, slowest at the top. Energy conservation links speeds at different heights; radial $F = ma$ gives the tension/normal at each point.

Tension at angle $\theta$ (from the bottom): $T = \dfrac{mu^2}{r} - 2mg + 3mg\cos\theta$ ($u$ = speed at bottom).
Speed at angle $\theta$: $v^2 = u^2 - 2gr(1 - \cos\theta)$.

## The Intuition

Swinging a bucket of water in a vertical circle: at the top, spin fast enough and the water stays in — gravity alone can supply more than the needed centripetal force. At the bottom, tension and gravity act in opposite radial directions, so tension must be large; at the top they act together, so tension can be small or zero. A string's tension must stay non-negative — that sets the minimum-speed condition.

## The Toolkit

| Position | Radial equation | Speed |
|----------|-----------------|-------|
| Bottom ($\theta=0$) | $T - mg = \dfrac{mv^2}{r}$ | $v^2 = u^2$ |
| Side ($\theta=90^\circ$) | $T = \dfrac{mv^2}{r}$ | $v^2 = u^2 - 2gr$ |
| Top ($\theta=180^\circ$) | $T + mg = \dfrac{mv^2}{r}$ | $v^2 = u^2 - 4gr$ |

**Minimum speed at the top (string):** $v_{\min} = \sqrt{gr}$ (tension just zero).
**Minimum speed at the bottom to complete the loop:** $u_{\min} = \sqrt{5gr}$ (from energy + the top condition).

## Derivation

Energy: $\frac12 mv^2 = \frac12 mu^2 - mgr(1-\cos\theta)$. Radial law: $T - mg\cos\theta = \frac{mv^2}{r}$ (careful with the direction of the weight's radial component). Substituting $v^2$ gives the tension formula. Setting $T = 0$ at the top ($\theta = \pi$) yields $\sqrt{gr}$; energy then gives the bottom speed $\sqrt{5gr}$. [Full derivations: 04.3-Proofs-Horizontal-Vertical-Circles]

## Method

1. **Energy first** — link speeds between heights.
2. **Radial $F = ma$ second** — find tension/normal at a position.
3. For "complete the loop," impose $T \ge 0$ at the top and back-solve for the bottom speed.
4. On a **track/bead** (not string), the normal can change sign; loss of contact happens when $R = 0$.

## Worked Examples

**Setup:** A bead on a vertical hoop of radius $r$ is released from the top with negligible speed. Find the speed at the bottom.

**Solution:** Energy: $mg(2r) = \frac12 mv^2 \Rightarrow v = 2\sqrt{gr}$.

**Key insight:** Drop of $2r$ in height converts entirely to kinetic energy.

---

**Setup:** A ball on a string completes a vertical circle with bottom speed $u$. Find the minimum $u$.

**Solution:** Top needs $v_{\text{top}} \ge \sqrt{gr}$; energy $u^2 = v_{\text{top}}^2 + 4gr \ge 5gr$, so $u_{\min} = \sqrt{5gr}$.

**Key insight:** The classic $\sqrt{5gr}$ — memorize the structure: energy adds $4gr$ between bottom and top.

## Common Traps

- Tension direction at the top vs bottom (at the top both $T$ and $mg$ point inward)
- Forgetting energy changes the speed in vertical circles
- Applying the horizontal-circle constant-speed assumption vertically
- String (tension ≥ 0) vs rod/track (can push) — different loss-of-contact conditions
- Using the diameter instead of $2r$ in the energy drop
