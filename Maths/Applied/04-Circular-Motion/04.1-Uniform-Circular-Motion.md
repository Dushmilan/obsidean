# 4.1 Uniform Circular Motion

**Syllabus:** GCE A/L Combined Mathematics (021) — Paper II, Topic 4.1  
**Prerequisites:** Pure Maths Calculus (chain rule), Vectors, Trigonometry (circular functions)  
**Cross-refs:** Physics Mechanics Unit 2, Applied Dynamics (Newton's 2nd Law), Conical Pendulum (4.2)

---

## Core Concepts

### Angular Variables
A particle moves on a circle of radius $r$ with angular position $\theta(t)$.

| Quantity | Symbol | Definition | Units |
|----------|--------|------------|-------|
| Angular displacement | $\theta$ | $\theta = s/r$ | rad |
| Angular velocity | $\omega$ | $\omega = d\theta/dt$ | rad/s |
| Angular acceleration | $\alpha$ | $\alpha = d\omega/dt$ | rad/s² |
| Linear speed | $v$ | $v = r\omega$ | m/s |
| Tangential acceleration | $a_t$ | $a_t = r\alpha = dv/dt$ | m/s² |

### Centripetal (Radial) Acceleration
For **uniform** circular motion ($\omega$ constant, $\alpha = 0$):
$$
a_c = \frac{v^2}{r} = \omega^2 r
$$
**Direction:** Always towards the centre, perpendicular to velocity.

### Centripetal Force
By Newton's 2nd Law ($F = ma$):
$$
F_c = m a_c = \frac{mv^2}{r} = m\omega^2 r
$$
**Not a new force** — it's the net radial component of real forces (tension, normal reaction, friction, gravity).

### Period and Frequency
$$
T = \frac{2\pi}{\omega} = \frac{2\pi r}{v}, \quad f = \frac{1}{T} = \frac{\omega}{2\pi}
$$

---

## Derivation: Centripetal Acceleration (Geometric)

Consider position vectors $\vec{r}(t)$ and $\vec{r}(t+\Delta t)$ of equal magnitude $r$, separated by angle $\Delta\theta$.

Change in velocity: $\Delta\vec{v} = \vec{v}(t+\Delta t) - \vec{v}(t)$.  
Since $\|\vec{v}\| = v$ constant, $\Delta\vec{v}$ is directed towards centre.

For small $\Delta\theta$:
$$
\|\Delta\vec{v}\| \approx v \Delta\theta
$$
Divide by $\Delta t$:
$$
a_c = \lim_{\Delta t \to 0} \frac{\|\Delta\vec{v}\|}{\Delta t} = v \frac{d\theta}{dt} = v\omega = \frac{v^2}{r} = \omega^2 r
$$

---

## Vector Formulation

Position: $\vec{r}(t) = r(\cos\theta\,\hat{i} + \sin\theta\,\hat{j})$

Velocity:
$$
\vec{v} = \frac{d\vec{r}}{dt} = r(-\sin\theta\,\hat{i} + \cos\theta\,\hat{j})\frac{d\theta}{dt} = r\omega(-\sin\theta\,\hat{i} + \cos\theta\,\hat{j})
$$
Magnitude: $\|\vec{v}\| = r\omega = v$. Direction: Tangent to circle.

Acceleration:
$$
\vec{a} = \frac{d\vec{v}}{dt} = r\omega(-\cos\theta\,\hat{i} - \sin\theta\,\hat{j})\omega = -\omega^2\vec{r}
$$
Magnitude: $\|\vec{a}\| = \omega^2 r = v^2/r$. Direction: Radially inward ($-\hat{r}$).

---

## Non-Uniform Circular Motion ($\alpha \neq 0$)

Total acceleration has two perpendicular components:
$$
\vec{a} = \underbrace{a_t}_{\text{tangential}}\,\hat{t} + \underbrace{a_c}_{\text{centripetal}}\,\hat{n}
$$
$$
a_t = r\alpha = \frac{dv}{dt}, \quad a_c = \frac{v^2}{r} = \omega^2 r
$$
Magnitude: $a = \sqrt{a_t^2 + a_c^2}$

Equations of motion (analogous to linear SUVAT):
$$
\begin{aligned}
\omega &= \omega_0 + \alpha t \\
\theta &= \omega_0 t + \frac{1}{2}\alpha t^2 \\
\omega^2 &= \omega_0^2 + 2\alpha\theta
\end{aligned}
$$

---

## Forces Providing Centripetal Force

| Situation | Centripetal Force Provider | Equation |
|-----------|---------------------------|----------|
| Particle on string (horizontal) | Tension $T$ | $T = m\omega^2 r$ |
| Particle on string (vertical) | $T \pm mg\cos\theta$ | Varies with position |
| Car on flat road (cornering) | Friction $F$ | $F = m\omega^2 r \leq \mu R$ |
| Car on banked track (no friction) | Normal component $R\sin\theta$ | $R\sin\theta = m\omega^2 r$ |
| Satellite in orbit | Gravity $GMm/r^2$ | $GMm/r^2 = m\omega^2 r$ |
| Particle inside rotating cylinder | Normal reaction $R$ | $R = m\omega^2 r$ |

---

## Key Results Summary

| Quantity | Formula | Notes |
|----------|---------|-------|
| Angular velocity | $\omega = v/r$ | Constant in UCM |
| Centripetal acceleration | $a_c = v^2/r = \omega^2 r$ | Radially inward |
| Centripetal force | $F_c = mv^2/r = m\omega^2 r$ | Net radial force |
| Period | $T = 2\pi/\omega = 2\pi r/v$ | Time per revolution |
| Frequency | $f = 1/T = \omega/2\pi$ | Revolutions per second |
| Tangential speed | $v = r\omega$ | Linear speed |
| Angular displacement | $\theta = \omega t$ (UCM) | $\theta = \omega_0 t + \frac{1}{2}\alpha t^2$ (non-UCM) |

## Worked Examples

### Example 1: String Breaking Strength
A 0.5 kg mass on a 1.2 m string rotates horizontally. String breaks at 40 N. Find max $\omega$ and $v$.

**Solution:**
$$
T = m\omega^2 r \leq 40 \implies 0.5 \cdot \omega^2 \cdot 1.2 \leq 40 \implies \omega^2 \leq \frac{40}{0.6} = \frac{200}{3}
$$
$$
\omega_{max} = \sqrt{\frac{200}{3}} \approx 8.16 \text{ rad/s}, \quad v_{max} = r\omega_{max} \approx 9.80 \text{ m/s}
$$

### Example 2: Conical Motion Transition
Same mass, now string makes angle $\theta = 30^\circ$ with vertical. Find $\omega$ and tension.

**Solution:**
Vertical: $T\cos\theta = mg \implies T = \frac{mg}{\cos\theta} = \frac{0.5 \cdot 9.8}{\cos 30^\circ} \approx 5.66 \text{ N}$  
Horizontal: $T\sin\theta = m\omega^2 r = m\omega^2 L\sin\theta$  
Cancel $\sin\theta$: $\omega^2 = \frac{T}{mL} = \frac{5.66}{0.5 \cdot 1.2} \approx 9.43 \implies \omega \approx 3.07 \text{ rad/s}$

*(See 4.2 Conical Pendulum for full treatment)*
