
## Definition

A particle of mass $m$ moves on a **smooth sphere** of radius $R$:

- **Outside case:** slides down from the top; the normal reaction decreases and eventually reaches zero — the particle **leaves the surface** at
$$\cos\theta = \frac{2}{3}$$
(independent of $m$, $R$, and $g$).
- **Inside case:** like a bead on a hoop or in a spherical bowl — analysis mirrors vertical circles.

## The Intuition

Sliding down a globe: the surface pushes outward at first. As you descend you speed up, the required centripetal force grows, but the component of gravity providing it shrinks — eventually the surface can't push hard enough and you fly off. Gravity has two jobs: tangential (speeding you up) and radial (centripetal). The normal reaction covers the shortfall until it hits zero.


**Outside sphere:**

| Equation | Formula |
|----------|---------|
| Radial law | $mg\cos\theta - R_N = \dfrac{mv^2}{R}$ |
| Energy (from top) | $v^2 = 2gR(1 - \cos\theta)$ |
| Loss of contact | $R_N = 0 \Rightarrow \cos\theta = \dfrac{2}{3}$ |
| Speed at loss of contact | $v = \sqrt{\dfrac{2gR}{3}}$ |

## Derivation

Substitute the energy result into the radial equation, then set $R_N = 0$: $mg\cos\theta = \frac{mv^2}{R} = \frac{m\cdot2gR(1-\cos\theta)}{R}$, so $\cos\theta = 2(1-\cos\theta)$, giving $\cos\theta = \frac23$. The universal $\frac23$ is a pure geometry result — mass, radius, and gravity all cancel. [Full derivations: 04.5-Proofs-Spherical-Surfaces]

## Method

1. Write the radial equation (weight component minus normal = centripetal).
2. Get $v^2$ from energy (height dropped from the starting point).
3. Loss of contact ⇔ $R_N = 0$.
4. Inside the sphere/bowl: the normal points *toward* the centre — adjust the radial equation sign.

## Worked Examples

**Setup:** A particle slides from rest at the top of a smooth sphere of radius $R$. At what angle does it leave the surface?

**Solution:** Set $R_N = 0$: $\cos\theta = \frac23$, so $\theta \approx 48.2^\circ$.

**Key insight:** The answer is *independent* of $m$, $R$, $g$ — memorise the $\frac23$.

---

**Setup:** What is the speed of the particle when it leaves?

**Solution:** $v^2 = 2gR(1 - \frac23) = \frac{2gR}{3}$, so $v = \sqrt{\frac{2gR}{3}}$.

**Key insight:** Combine the loss-of-contact angle with the energy formula — one substitution.

## Common Traps

- Losing contact at the *top* (it happens at $\cos\theta = 2/3$, not the top)
- Wrong sign for the normal in the radial equation (inward vs outward surface)
- Using $v^2 = 2gR$ — forgetting the $\cos\theta$ term in the height dropped
- Inside-vs-outside normal direction
- Assuming mass cancels only after checking (it does here, but show it)
