
## Definition

A mass $m$ on a string of length $L$ moves in a **horizontal circle** of radius $r = L\sin\theta$, with the string sweeping out a cone at constant angle $\theta$ to the vertical.

Vertical balance: $T\cos\theta = mg$. Horizontal (centripetal): $T\sin\theta = \frac{mv^2}{r} = m\omega^2 r$.

## The Intuition

Swinging a ball above your head: spin faster and it rises (larger $\theta$). The tension has two jobs — $T\cos\theta$ holds the weight up, $T\sin\theta$ pulls inward for the circle. At low $\omega$ the ball hangs nearly vertical; at high $\omega$ it approaches horizontal, but never reaches it (that would need infinite tension).

## The Toolkit

| Quantity | Formula |
|----------|---------|
| Radius | $r = L\sin\theta$ |
| Angular velocity | $\omega^2 = \dfrac{g}{L\cos\theta}$ |
| Tension | $T = \dfrac{mg}{\cos\theta}$ |
| Period | $T = 2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{L\cos\theta}{g}}$ |
| Linear speed | $v = \sqrt{rg\tan\theta}$ |

## Derivation

Divide the two force equations: $\frac{T\sin\theta}{T\cos\theta} = \frac{m\omega^2 r}{mg} \Rightarrow \tan\theta = \frac{\omega^2 r}{g}$, and with $r = L\sin\theta$ this gives $\omega^2 = \frac{g}{L\cos\theta}$. [Full derivations: 04.2-Proofs-Conical-Pendulum]

## Method

1. Resolve tension: vertical component balances weight; horizontal component is centripetal.
2. Write both equations, divide to eliminate $T$.
3. Substitute $r = L\sin\theta$ and solve for the requested quantity.

## Worked Examples

**Setup:** A conical pendulum has $L = 2$ m, $\theta = 30^\circ$. Find $\omega$ and $T$.

**Solution:** $\omega^2 = \frac{g}{L\cos\theta} = \frac{10}{2\cdot\frac{\sqrt3}{2}} = \frac{10}{\sqrt3}$, so $\omega \approx 2.4$ rad/s. $T = \frac{mg}{\cos\theta} = \frac{2mg}{\sqrt3}$.

**Key insight:** $\omega$ depends only on $L\cos\theta$ — the vertical projection of the string — not on $m$.

---

**Setup:** What happens to $\theta$ as $\omega$ increases without limit?

**Solution:** $\cos\theta = \frac{g}{L\omega^2} \to 0$, so $\theta \to 90^\circ$ — but never reaches it.

**Key insight:** A truly horizontal circular motion would require $T\sin\theta \to$ finite with $\cos\theta \to 0$, forcing $T \to \infty$.

## Common Traps

- Using $r = L$ instead of $r = L\sin\theta$
- Forgetting $\omega^2 = g/(L\cos\theta)$ is independent of $m$ and $v$
- Resolving tension in the wrong direction
- $\theta$ measured from the vertical, not the horizontal
- Treating it as a simple pendulum (that's SHM, different motion)
