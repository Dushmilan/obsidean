A particle of mass $m$ is suspended from a fixed point $O$ by a light inextensible string of length $L$. It moves in a horizontal circle of radius $r = L\sin\theta$ at constant angular velocity $\omega$, where $\theta$ is the constant angle the string makes with the vertical. The string sweeps out a cone — hence the name. The physics is a straightforward application of Newton's second law: resolve tension vertically (to balance weight) and horizontally (to provide centripetal force). The two equations yield closed-form expressions for $\omega$, tension, period, and linear speed, all in terms of $\theta$, $L$, $m$, and $g$.

**The Intuition:** Swinging a ball on a string above your head — the faster you spin it, the higher it rises (larger $\theta$), and the string traces a cone. The tension in the string has two jobs: its vertical component $T\cos\theta$ holds the weight up, while its horizontal component $T\sin\theta$ pulls the particle inward to keep it in a circle. As $\omega$ increases, the particle rises (larger $\theta$), increasing the horizontal component and decreasing the vertical component relative to tension. At very low $\omega$, the particle hangs nearly vertically ($\theta \approx 0$). At very high $\omega$, the particle is nearly horizontal ($\theta \to 90°$), but the string can never become truly horizontal because that would require infinite tension and infinite $\omega$.

**The Math:** From Newton's 2nd law: vertical equilibrium gives $T\cos\theta = mg$; horizontal centripetal gives $T\sin\theta = m\omega^2 L\sin\theta$. Dividing eliminates $T$ and yields:

Angular velocity: $\omega = \sqrt{g/(L\cos\theta)}$. Tension: $T = mg/\cos\theta$. Period: $T = 2\pi\sqrt{L\cos\theta/g}$. Radius: $r = L\sin\theta$. Height: $h = L\cos\theta = g/\omega^2$. Linear speed: $v = \omega r = \sqrt{gL\tan\theta\sin\theta}$.

Minimum angular velocity for conical motion: $\omega_{\min} = \sqrt{g/L}$ (at $\theta = 0$). Below this, the particle hangs vertically and does not trace a cone. The period depends only on the vertical height $h = L\cos\theta$ below the pivot, not on the string length or angle individually. Two conical pendulums with the same vertical height below the pivot have the same period.

**What does this mean for Applied Mechanics?** Always resolve forces vertically and horizontally — the vertical equation gives tension, the horizontal gives $\omega$. To find $\theta$ from $\omega$, solve $\cos\theta = g/(L\omega^2)$. Remember the constraint $\omega \geq \sqrt{g/L}$ — if $\omega$ is too small, conical motion is impossible. For string-breaking problems, use $T = mg/\cos\theta \leq T_{\max}$.

**Setup:** $m = 0.8$ kg, $L = 1.5$ m, $\theta = 40°$, $g = 9.8$ m/s². Find the angular velocity, tension, and period.

**Solution:** $T = mg/\cos\theta = (0.8 \times 9.8)/\cos 40° = 7.84/0.766 \approx 10.24$ N. $\omega = \sqrt{g/(L\cos\theta)} = \sqrt{9.8/(1.5 \times 0.766)} = \sqrt{9.8/1.149} \approx 2.92$ rad/s. $T_{\text{period}} = 2\pi/\omega \approx 2.15$ s.

**Key insight:** Tension exceeds weight because it must also provide the centripetal component.

**Setup:** $m = 0.5$ kg, $L = 1$ m, $T_{\max} = 20$ N. Find the maximum angle and the corresponding $\omega$.

**Solution:** $T = mg/\cos\theta \leq 20 \implies \cos\theta \geq (0.5 \times 9.8)/20 = 0.245$. $\theta_{\max} = \cos^{-1}(0.245) \approx 75.8°$. $\omega = \sqrt{g/(L\cos\theta)} = \sqrt{9.8/0.245} \approx 6.32$ rad/s.

**Key insight:** The string breaking strength limits how far the particle can rise.
