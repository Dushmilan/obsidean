
## Definition

1. **First Law (Inertia):** $\sum\vec{F} = 0 \Rightarrow \vec{a} = 0$ — a body keeps constant velocity unless acted on by a net force. Defines inertial frames; mass $m$ measures inertia.
2. **Second Law:** $\sum\vec{F} = \dfrac{d\vec{p}}{dt} = \dfrac{d(m\vec{v})}{dt}$. For constant mass: $\sum\vec{F} = m\vec{a}$, component-wise $\sum F_x = ma_x$, $\sum F_y = ma_y$.
3. **Third Law (Action–Reaction):** $\vec{F}_{AB} = -\vec{F}_{BA}$ — equal, opposite, same type, simultaneous, but on **different bodies** (so they never cancel).

## The Intuition

Pushing a shopping trolley: a heavy trolley accelerates slowly for the same push; a light one shoots forward — that's $F = ma$. A free-body diagram isolates one object and shows every force on it as an arrow; the net arrow points along the acceleration. The first law is the $F = 0$ special case of the second.

## The Toolkit

| Law | Statement | Use |
|-----|-----------|-----|
| 1st | $\sum F = 0 \Rightarrow v$ constant | inertial frames, equilibrium |
| 2nd | $\sum F = ma$ | the workhorse — FBD + resolve |
| 3rd | $F_{AB} = -F_{BA}$ | pairs on *different* bodies |
| Weight | $W = mg$ | near-Earth gravity |
| Normal | $R = mg\cos\theta$ on an incline | never assume $R = mg$ |

## Derivation

The second law defines force as the rate of change of momentum; with constant mass this reduces to $F = ma$. The component form follows from treating $x$ and $y$ axes independently (vector addition). [Full derivations: 02.1-Proofs-Newtons-Laws]

## Method

1. **Draw the free-body diagram** — every force on the chosen body as an arrow.
2. **Choose axes** — along the direction of motion (incline: parallel/perpendicular to the plane).
3. **Resolve** forces into components along the axes.
4. **Apply $F = ma$ per axis**; unknown tension/normal → solve the system.
5. **Check sign:** assume a direction of motion; if $a$ comes out negative, the assumption was wrong.

## Worked Examples

**Setup:** A 5 kg block on a $30^\circ$ frictionless incline. Find acceleration and normal reaction.

**Solution:** Down-slope: $mg\sin30 = ma \Rightarrow a = 5$ m/s². Normal: $R = mg\cos30 = 25\sqrt3$ N.

**Key insight:** $R = mg\cos\theta$, not $mg$ — only the perpendicular component of weight presses into the plane.

---

**Setup:** Two blocks $m_1, m_2$ in contact pushed by force $F$ on a smooth floor. Find the contact force.

**Solution:** System: $a = \frac{F}{m_1+m_2}$. On $m_2$: contact force $= m_2 a = \frac{m_2 F}{m_1+m_2}$.

**Key insight:** Contact forces are internal to the system — isolate one block to expose them.

## Common Traps

- Third-law pairs act on different bodies — they never cancel
- Assuming $R = mg$ (true only on horizontal surfaces with no other vertical forces)
- Static friction opposes the *tendency to move*, not the applied force direction
- Forgetting to resolve weight on inclines ($mg\cos\theta$, $mg\sin\theta$)
- Newton's laws fail at very high speeds / strong gravity (relativity) — stated limits
