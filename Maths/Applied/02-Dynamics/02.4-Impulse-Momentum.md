
## Definition

Momentum: $\vec{p} = m\vec{v}$. Newton's Second Law in momentum form: $\sum\vec{F} = \frac{d\vec{p}}{dt}$.

**Impulse:** $\vec{I} = \int_{t_1}^{t_2}\vec{F}(t)\,dt = \Delta\vec{p} = \vec{p}_f - \vec{p}_i$. For constant force: $\vec{I} = \vec{F}\Delta t = m(\vec{v}_f - \vec{v}_i)$. (Area under the $F$–$t$ graph = impulse.)

**Conservation of momentum:** $\sum\vec{F}_{\text{ext}} = 0 \Rightarrow \vec{P}_{\text{total}}$ constant. For two particles: $m_1\vec{u}_1 + m_2\vec{u}_2 = m_1\vec{v}_1 + m_2\vec{v}_2$.

## The Intuition

A cricket ball hitting a bat: the ball's momentum changes suddenly because the bat exerts a huge force for a tiny time — the impulse equals the change in momentum. Momentum is "motion content": in a collision it's shared between objects but never created or destroyed — total before equals total after. Where work-energy integrates $F = ma$ over *distance*, impulse-momentum integrates over *time* — natural for impacts and short-duration forces.

## The Toolkit

| Result | Formula |
|--------|---------|
| Momentum | $\vec{p} = m\vec{v}$ |
| Impulse | $\vec{I} = \int F\,dt = \Delta\vec{p}$ |
| Constant force | $I = F\Delta t = m(v_f - v_i)$ |
| Conservation | $m_1u_1 + m_2u_2 = m_1v_1 + m_2v_2$ |
| Coefficient of restitution | $e = \dfrac{v_{2n} - v_{1n}}{u_{1n} - u_{2n}}$ |
| Collision equations | momentum + $v_2 - v_1 = e(u_1 - u_2)$ |
| KE loss | $\Delta K = -\dfrac12 \dfrac{m_1m_2}{m_1+m_2}(1-e^2)(u_1-u_2)^2$ |

## Derivation

Impulse-momentum is $\sum F = \frac{dp}{dt}$ integrated over time — $\int F\,dt = \Delta p$. Conservation follows from $\sum F_{\text{ext}} = 0 \Rightarrow \frac{dP}{dt} = 0$. During impact, impulsive forces dwarf weight/friction, so external forces are negligible for the brief collision. [Full derivations: 02.4-Proofs-Impulse-Momentum]

## Method

1. **Decide the system** — momentum is conserved only if net external force is zero (or negligible during impact).
2. **Resolve into directions** — conservation holds in each direction separately.
3. **During impact:** ignore non-impulsive forces (weight, friction); only impulsive forces act.
4. **Oblique collisions:** work along the line of centres (normal) and perpendicular (tangential) separately.

## Worked Examples

**Setup:** A 0.15 kg ball hits a wall at 20 m/s and rebounds at 15 m/s. Find the impulse.

**Solution:** Take the rebound direction positive: $I = m(v_f - v_i) = 0.15(15 - (-20)) = 5.25$ N·s.

**Key insight:** Velocity is signed — rebounding means the initial velocity is negative.

---

**Setup:** A 2 kg object at 3 m/s collides with a 4 kg object at rest and sticks. Find the common velocity.

**Solution:** $2(3) + 4(0) = 6v \Rightarrow v = 1$ m/s.

**Key insight:** Sticking = perfectly inelastic ($e = 0$) — momentum still conserved.

## Common Traps

- Sign of velocities in impulse — direction matters, rebounding flips signs
- Applying conservation when external forces act (need $\sum F_{\text{ext}} = 0$)
- Ignoring that conservation holds **per direction** in oblique collisions
- Counting non-impulsive forces during the impact instant
- Confusing impulse with work (one integrates force over time, the other over distance)
