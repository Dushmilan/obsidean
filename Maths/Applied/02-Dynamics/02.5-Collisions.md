
## Definition

Every collision uses two equations:
1. **Momentum conserved** (net external force zero during impact): $m_1u_1 + m_2u_2 = m_1v_1 + m_2v_2$.
2. **Restitution:** $v_2 - v_1 = e(u_1 - u_2)$, with $e = \dfrac{\text{separation speed}}{\text{approach speed}}$ along the line of centres.

**Classification:** elastic ($e = 1$, KE conserved) · inelastic ($0 < e < 1$, some KE lost) · perfectly inelastic ($e = 0$, objects stick, max KE lost).

## The Intuition

Dropping a steel ball (bouncy, $e \approx 1$) vs a lump of putty (not bouncy, $e \approx 0$): the ball rebounds nearly to drop height; the putty splats. The coefficient measures bounciness. Momentum is always shared; how much kinetic energy survives depends on $e$.

## The Toolkit

| Result | Formula |
|--------|---------|
| Solution for $v_1$ | $\dfrac{m_1 - em_2}{m_1+m_2}u_1 + \dfrac{m_2(1+e)}{m_1+m_2}u_2$ |
| Solution for $v_2$ | $\dfrac{m_1(1+e)}{m_1+m_2}u_1 + \dfrac{m_2 - em_1}{m_1+m_2}u_2$ |
| KE loss | $\Delta K = -\dfrac12\dfrac{m_1m_2}{m_1+m_2}(1-e^2)(u_1-u_2)^2$ |
| Wall (smooth, angle $\theta$) | $v_n = -eu\cos\theta$, $v_t = u\sin\theta$ |
| Rebound angle | $\tan\theta' = \dfrac{\tan\theta}{e}$ |
| Speed after wall hit | $v = u\sqrt{\sin^2\theta + e^2\cos^2\theta}$ |
| Impulse on wall | $I = mu\cos\theta\,(1+e)$ |
| Ballistic pendulum | $v = \dfrac{M+m}{m}\sqrt{2gh}$ |

## Derivation

Solve the momentum + restitution pair simultaneously for $v_1, v_2$. The wall case: only the normal component reverses (scaled by $e$); the tangential component is unchanged for smooth surfaces. The KE loss formula comes from substituting the solutions into $\Delta K = \frac12 m_1v_1^2 + \frac12 m_2v_2^2 - \frac12 m_1u_1^2 - \frac12 m_2u_2^2$. [Full derivations: 02.5-Proofs-Collisions]

## Method

1. **Choose positive direction** along the line of centres.
2. Write momentum + restitution equations.
3. Solve for the unknowns; check signs.
4. **Check for a second collision** — the trailing particle must be faster than the leading one.
5. **Oblique:** resolve into normal (restitution applies) and tangential (unchanged for smooth surfaces).

## Worked Examples

**Setup:** $m_1 = 2$ kg at 4 m/s collides with $m_2 = 3$ kg at rest, $e = 0.5$. Find the velocities.

**Solution:** Momentum: $2(4) = 2v_1 + 3v_2$. Restitution: $v_2 - v_1 = 0.5(4)$. Solving: $v_1 = 0.4$ m/s, $v_2 = 2.4$ m/s.

**Key insight:** Two equations, two unknowns — always solvable; check the signs make physical sense.

---

**Setup:** A ball hits a smooth wall at $30^\circ$ with $e = \frac12$. Find the rebound angle.

**Solution:** $\tan\theta' = \frac{\tan 30^\circ}{0.5} = \frac{1/\sqrt3}{1/2} = \frac{2}{\sqrt3} \Rightarrow \theta' \approx 49.1^\circ$.

**Key insight:** The tangential component is untouched; only the normal shrinks — so the ball bounces *steeper* off the wall.

## Common Traps

- Restitution applies only **along the line of centres** — not to tangential motion
- Forgetting the sign in $v_2 - v_1 = e(u_1 - u_2)$
- Perfectly inelastic ≠ momentum not conserved (it is — they stick)
- Not checking whether a second collision occurs after the first
- Ballistic pendulum: the pendulum swing uses energy *after* the inelastic join
