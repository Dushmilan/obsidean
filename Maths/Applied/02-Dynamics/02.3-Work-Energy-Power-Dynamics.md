
## Definition

**Work** (constant force): $W = \vec{F}\cdot\vec{s} = Fs\cos\theta$. Variable force: $W = \int \vec{F}\cdot d\vec{r}$.

**Work-Energy Theorem:** $W_{\text{net}} = \Delta K = \frac12 mv^2 - \frac12 mu^2$.

**Kinetic energy:** $K = \frac12 mv^2 = \frac{p^2}{2m}$. **Gravitational PE:** $U_g = mgh$. **Elastic PE:** $U_s = \frac12 kx^2$.

**Conservation of mechanical energy** (conservative forces only): $K + U = \text{constant}$. With non-conservative forces: $K_f + U_f = K_i + U_i + W_{\text{nc}}$.

**Power:** $P = \frac{dW}{dt} = \vec{F}\cdot\vec{v}$; constant force/velocity: $P = Fv\cos\theta$.

## The Intuition

Pushing a box: more force and more distance → more energy transferred → that's work. If the box speeds up, the work went to kinetic energy; if friction slows it, it went to heat. Energy is a bank account — work by external forces is a deposit/withdrawal, conservative forces (gravity, springs) move money between savings (potential) and checking (kinetic), and friction is a fee that leaves the system.

## The Toolkit

| Quantity | Formula | Notes |
|----------|---------|-------|
| Work (const.) | $W = Fs\cos\theta$ | $\theta$ between force and displacement |
| Kinetic energy | $K = \frac12 mv^2$ | always non-negative |
| Gravitational PE | $U = mgh$ | reference level matters |
| Elastic PE | $U = \frac12 kx^2$ | spring |
| Work-energy | $W_{\text{net}} = \Delta K$ | net work = change in KE |
| Energy conservation | $K_f + U_f = K_i + U_i + W_{\text{nc}}$ | friction ⇒ negative $W_{\text{nc}}$ |
| Power | $P = Fv\cos\theta$ | — |

**Zero-work forces:** normal reactions (⟂ displacement), tensions in inextensible strings.

## Derivation

Work-energy follows from integrating $F = ma$ over displacement: $\int F\,ds = \int m\frac{dv}{dt}\,ds = \int mv\,dv = \frac12 mv^2$. The $W_{\text{nc}}$ term accounts for the energy dissipated by friction ($W = -\mu N s$). [Full derivations: 02.3-Proofs-Work-Energy-Power]

## Method

1. **Identify the forces** doing work — skip zero-work forces (normal, string tension).
2. **Choose the method:** speed-change over a distance (no time) → energy; time or forces per point → $F = ma$.
3. Write $K_i + U_i + W_{\text{nc}} = K_f + U_f$; substitute, solve for the unknown.
4. Friction always does **negative** work $-\mu N s$.

## Worked Examples

**Setup:** A 2 kg block slides down a $30^\circ$ incline of height 10 m, with friction coefficient $0.2$. Find speed at the bottom.

**Solution:** Energy: $mgh = \frac12 mv^2 + \mu mg\cos30 \cdot \frac{h}{\sin30}$. So $10g = \frac12 v^2 + 0.2g\cos30\cdot20$, giving $v \approx 12.1$ m/s.

**Key insight:** Work-energy skips the acceleration entirely — compare states, not the journey.

---

**Setup:** A spring of constant $k$ is compressed $x$ from rest; find the launch speed of mass $m$.

**Solution:** $\frac12 kx^2 = \frac12 mv^2 \Rightarrow v = x\sqrt{k/m}$.

**Key insight:** Elastic PE converts fully to KE — energy conservation is one line.

## Common Traps

- Normal reaction and string tension do **zero** work — don't count them
- $W = Fs\cos\theta$ — the angle is between force and displacement, not the incline angle
- Friction work is negative
- Gravitational PE needs a consistent reference level
- $K = \frac12 mv^2$ is never negative
