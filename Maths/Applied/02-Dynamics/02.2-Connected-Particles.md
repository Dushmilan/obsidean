
## Definition

Newton's second law applied to **systems** of particles linked by strings/pulleys. Never treat the system as one blob: draw a separate free-body diagram per particle, write $F = ma$ for each, then link them with **constraints**:

- **Inextensible string** → equal acceleration magnitude on both sides.
- **Smooth pulley** → equal tension on both sides.
- **Movable pulley** → mechanical advantage 2 ($2T = W$ statically).

## The Intuition

A tug-of-war: the rope transmits force; if it doesn't stretch, both teams move at the same speed — that's the constraint. Each particle lives in its own world with its own FBD; the string is the only link, pulling both with equal magnitude.

## The Toolkit

**Atwood machine** ($m_1$ horizontal, $m_2$ hanging):
$$a = \frac{m_2g}{m_1+m_2}, \qquad T = \frac{m_1m_2g}{m_1+m_2}$$

**Two inclined planes** ($m_1$ assumed moving up):
$$a = \frac{m_2\sin\theta_2 - m_1\sin\theta_1}{m_1+m_2}\,g$$

**Movable pulley:** $2T = W$ static; $2T - W = \frac{W}{g}a$ dynamic.

## Derivation

Write $F = ma$ along each particle's direction of motion, then combine with the constraint $a_1 = a_2$ (inextensible string) and $T_1 = T_2$ (smooth pulley). Two equations, two unknowns — solve. For multi-pulley systems, differentiate the string-length geometry twice for the acceleration constraint. [Full derivations: 02.2-Proofs-Connected-Particles]

## Method

1. **Draw a separate FBD** for each particle.
2. **Assume a direction of motion** (solve; a negative $a$ flips it).
3. Write $F = ma$ for each particle **along its motion**.
4. Add the constraints ($a$ equal, $T$ equal).
5. Solve the system.

## Worked Examples

**Setup:** $m_1 = 2$ kg (horizontal, smooth) and $m_2 = 5$ kg (hanging) over a smooth pulley. Find $a$ and $T$.

**Solution:** $a = \frac{m_2g}{m_1+m_2} = \frac{5g}{7}$; $T = \frac{m_1m_2g}{m_1+m_2} = \frac{10g}{7}$ N.

**Key insight:** The hanging mass alone doesn't set the acceleration — the whole system's mass resists it.

---

**Setup:** A 4 kg mass and a 6 kg mass hang over a pulley. Find the tension.

**Solution:** $a = \frac{(6-4)g}{10} = 2$ m/s². For the 4 kg: $T - 40 = 4(2) \Rightarrow T = 48$ N.

**Key insight:** Tension is *between* $m_1g$ and $m_2g$ — compute it from either particle's equation after finding $a$.

## Common Traps

- Applying $F = ma$ to the whole system when internal tensions matter
- Assuming the tension equals the hanging weight ($T = mg$ is only true at rest)
- Ignoring the sign of $a$ — a wrong initial assumption still gives the right magnitude
- Rough pulleys / massive strings — tension differs (less common at A-level)
- Forgetting to differentiate geometry twice for movable-pulley constraints
