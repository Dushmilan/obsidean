
## Definition

When mass changes, Newton's second law must use its fundamental momentum form:

$$M\frac{dv}{dt} = v_{\text{rel}}\frac{dM}{dt} + \sum F_{\text{ext}}$$

where $v_{\text{rel}}$ is the velocity of the added/removed mass relative to the system, and $v_{\text{rel}}\frac{dM}{dt}$ is the **thrust** term ($dM/dt > 0$ for mass gain, $< 0$ for mass loss).

## The Intuition

A garden hose on a skateboard: spray water backward and the hose moves forward — the more water, and the faster it's ejected, the more forward force. A rocket is constantly throwing mass backward; each ejected bit carries momentum backward, so the rocket gains momentum forward. Rocket + fuel momentum is conserved when no external forces act.

## The Toolkit

| Scenario | Result |
|----------|--------|
| Rocket (free space) | $v = v_0 + u\ln\dfrac{M_0}{M}$ |
| Rocket with gravity | $v = u\ln\dfrac{M_0}{M} - gt$ |
| Rocket (Tsiolkovsky) | $\Delta v = u\ln\dfrac{M_0}{M_f}$ |
| Chain onto a scale | $F = 3\lambda gx$ |
| Chain sliding off table | $v^2 = \dfrac{g}{L}(x^2 - x_0^2)$ |
| Chain lifted at constant $v$ | $F = \lambda gx + \lambda v^2$ |
| Raindrop accretion | $\dfrac{d}{dt}(mv) = mg$ |

## Derivation

Start from $\sum F = \frac{d}{dt}(Mv) = M\frac{dv}{dt} + v\frac{dM}{dt}$ and rearrange with the relative-velocity sign convention. For the rocket, $\frac{dM}{dt} = -\alpha$ (fuel burn rate) and the thrust term becomes $u\alpha$ opposite the exhaust direction. Chain problems come from accounting the rate of momentum change of the moving and accumulating parts. [Full derivations: 02.6-Proofs-Variable-Mass]

## Method

1. **Identify the system** whose mass changes.
2. Determine $v_{\text{rel}}$ (velocity of added/removed mass relative to the system) and the sign of $dM/dt$.
3. Write the variable-mass equation, including external forces (gravity, etc.).
4. Separate variables / integrate with the given initial conditions.

## Worked Examples

**Setup:** A rocket with initial mass $M_0$ exhausts at speed $u$, burn rate $\alpha$, no gravity. Find the velocity after mass $M$ remains.

**Solution:** $v = u\ln\frac{M_0}{M}$.

**Key insight:** The mass ratio, not the burn rate, sets $\Delta v$ — more fuel efficiency means more $\ln(M_0/M)$.

---

**Setup:** A chain of linear density $\lambda$ falls onto a scale from a height, with an accumulating pile of length $x$. Find the scale reading.

**Solution:** The scale supports the pile's weight $\lambda gx$ plus the rate of momentum change of the arriving links: total $F = \lambda gx + \lambda v^2 = 3\lambda gx$ (using $v^2 = 2gx$).

**Key insight:** The "impact force" doubles the static weight — momentum change of incoming mass adds a $\lambda v^2$ term.

## Common Traps

- Using $F = ma$ where $m$ changes — must go back to $\frac{d}{dt}(mv)$
- Sign of $v_{\text{rel}}$ — direction relative to the system matters
- Confusing the thrust term with an external force (it's internal to rocket+fuel)
- Chain problems: the scale reading has *two* contributions (weight + momentum rate)
- Not applying initial conditions after integrating
