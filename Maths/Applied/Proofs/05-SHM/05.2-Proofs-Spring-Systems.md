# Proofs: Spring Systems

**Syllabus:** GCE A/L Combined Mathematics (021) — Paper II, Topic 5.2

---

## Proof 1: Horizontal Spring-Mass System is SHM

**Statement:** Mass $m$ on frictionless horizontal surface, spring constant $k$. Equation: $\ddot{x} + \frac{k}{m}x = 0$.

**Proof:**
Forces on mass at displacement $x$ from equilibrium (unstretched position):
- Spring force: $F = -kx$ (Hooke's law)

Newton's 2nd Law:
$$
m\ddot{x} = -kx \implies \ddot{x} + \frac{k}{m}x = 0
$$

This is the SHM equation with $\omega^2 = k/m$, so $\omega = \sqrt{k/m}$.

Period: $T = 2\pi/\omega = 2\pi\sqrt{m/k}$.
∎

## Proof 2: Vertical Spring-Mass System has Same Period

**Statement:** Mass $m$ hanging vertically from spring $k$. Oscillations about equilibrium have $\omega = \sqrt{k/m}$, same as horizontal.

**Proof:**
At equilibrium, spring extension $x_0$ satisfies:
$$
kx_0 = mg \implies x_0 = \frac{mg}{k}
$$

Let $y$ be displacement **downward from equilibrium**. Total extension = $x_0 + y$.

Forces (downward positive):
- Weight: $mg$
- Spring force: $-k(x_0 + y)$

Net force:
$$
F_{net} = mg - k(x_0 + y) = mg - kx_0 - ky
$$

But $mg = kx_0$ (equilibrium condition), so:
$$
F_{net} = -ky
$$

Equation of motion:
$$
m\ddot{y} = -ky \implies \ddot{y} + \frac{k}{m}y = 0
$$

Same as horizontal case with $\omega = \sqrt{k/m}$, $T = 2\pi\sqrt{m/k}$.

**Key insight:** Gravity shifts equilibrium but does not affect the effective spring constant or frequency.
∎

## Proof 3: Springs in Series

**Statement:** Two springs $k_1, k_2$ in series have effective constant $k_{eq} = \frac{k_1 k_2}{k_1 + k_2}$.

**Proof:**
Force $F$ is the **same** through both springs.

Extensions:
$$
x_1 = \frac{F}{k_1}, \quad x_2 = \frac{F}{k_2}
$$

Total extension:
$$
x = x_1 + x_2 = F\left(\frac{1}{k_1} + \frac{1}{k_2}\right)
$$

By definition of equivalent spring: $F = k_{eq} x$, so:
$$
\frac{1}{k_{eq}} = \frac{1}{k_1} + \frac{1}{k_2} \implies k_{eq} = \frac{k_1 k_2}{k_1 + k_2}
$$

**Generalisation ($n$ springs in series):**
$$
\frac{1}{k_{eq}} = \sum_{i=1}^n \frac{1}{k_i}
$$
∎

## Proof 4: Springs in Parallel

**Statement:** Two springs $k_1, k_2$ in parallel have effective constant $k_{eq} = k_1 + k_2$.

**Proof:**
Displacement $x$ is the **same** for both springs.

Forces:
$$
F_1 = k_1 x, \quad F_2 = k_2 x
$$

Total force:
$$
F = F_1 + F_2 = (k_1 + k_2)x
$$

By definition: $F = k_{eq} x$, so:
$$
k_{eq} = k_1 + k_2
$$

**Generalisation ($n$ springs in parallel):**
$$
k_{eq} = \sum_{i=1}^n k_i
$$
∎

## Proof 5: Mass Between Two Fixed Springs (Fixed Ends)

**Statement:** Mass $m$ between springs $k_1$ (left) and $k_2$ (right), both fixed at far ends. Effective $k_{eq} = k_1 + k_2$.

**Proof:**
Displace mass rightward by $x$.

Left spring: extension $x$ → force leftward $= -k_1 x$

Right spring: compression $x$ → force leftward $= -k_2 x$

Total restoring force:
$$
F = -k_1 x - k_2 x = -(k_1 + k_2)x
$$

Equation: $m\ddot{x} = -(k_1 + k_2)x$

Effective constant: $k_{eq} = k_1 + k_2$ (parallel combination).

Angular frequency: $\omega = \sqrt{(k_1 + k_2)/m}$.
∎

## Proof 6: Vertical Spring with Two Springs

### Case A: Series (one above other, mass at bottom)
**Statement:** $k_{eq} = \frac{k_1 k_2}{k_1 + k_2}$, equilibrium extension $x_0 = \frac{mg}{k_{eq}}$, $T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{m}{k_{eq}}}$.

**Proof:**
Same as horizontal series. Gravity adds equilibrium shift but doesn't change $k_{eq}$.
$$
x_0 = \frac{mg}{k_{eq}} = mg\frac{k_1 + k_2}{k_1 k_2}
$$
Period: $T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{m}{k_{eq}}}$.
∎

### Case B: Parallel (side by side, mass attached to both)
**Statement:** $k_{eq} = k_1 + k_2$, $x_0 = \frac{mg}{k_1 + k_2}$, $T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{m}{k_1 + k_2}}$.

**Proof:**
Same as horizontal parallel. Each spring shares the load.
∎

## Proof 7: Spring Cut into Equal Pieces

**Statement:** A spring of stiffness $k$ and length $L$, cut into $n$ equal pieces. Each piece has stiffness $nk$.

**Proof:**
Original spring = $n$ identical pieces in series.

For series: $\frac{1}{k} = \sum_{i=1}^n \frac{1}{k_{piece}} = \frac{n}{k_{piece}}$

Thus: $k_{piece} = nk$.

**Alternative derivation (material property):**
Spring constant $k = \frac{EA}{L}$ (Young's modulus $E$, cross-section $A$, length $L$).

Piece length $L' = L/n$, so $k' = \frac{EA}{L/n} = n\frac{EA}{L} = nk$.
∎

## Proof 8: Effective Mass of a Massive Spring

**Statement:** Uniform spring mass $m_s$, load mass $m$. Effective oscillating mass $m_{eff} = m + \frac{1}{3}m_s$. Corrected period $T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{m + m_s/3}{k}}$.

**Proof:**
Consider spring element at distance $x$ from fixed end ($0 \le x \le L$).

Mass of element: $dm = \frac{m_s}{L}dx$

Velocity of element: $v(x) = \frac{x}{L}v_{end}$ (linear velocity profile, assuming spring coils don't bunch)

Kinetic energy of element:
$$
dK = \frac{1}{2}dm\,v(x)^2 = \frac{1}{2}\frac{m_s}{L}dx\left(\frac{x}{L}v_{end}\right)^2 = \frac{m_s}{2L^3}v_{end}^2 x^2 dx
$$

Total spring KE:
$$
K_{spring} = \int_0^L \frac{m_s}{2L^3}v_{end}^2 x^2 dx = \frac{m_s v_{end}^2}{2L^3}\left[\frac{x^3}{3}\right]_0^L = \frac{1}{6}m_s v_{end}^2
$$

This equals $\frac{1}{2}m_{eff,spring}v_{end}^2$ with $m_{eff,spring} = \frac{1}{3}m_s$.

Total effective mass: $m_{total} = m_{load} + \frac{1}{3}m_{spring}$.

Corrected period:
$$
T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{m + m_s/3}{k}}
$$
∎

## Proof 9: Two Masses on a Spring (Free-Free)

**Statement:** Masses $m_1, m_2$ connected by spring $k$, no external forces. Reduced mass $\mu = \frac{m_1 m_2}{m_1 + m_2}$, $\omega = \sqrt{k/\mu}$.

**Proof:**
Let $x_1, x_2$ be displacements of $m_1, m_2$ from their equilibrium positions. Extension of spring: $x = x_2 - x_1 - L_0$.

Forces:
- On $m_1$: $F_1 = +kx$ (rightward if spring stretched)
- On $m_2$: $F_2 = -kx$

Equations:
$$
m_1\ddot{x}_1 = kx, \quad m_2\ddot{x}_2 = -kx
$$

Subtract: $\ddot{x}_2 - \ddot{x}_1 = -kx\left(\frac{1}{m_2} + \frac{1}{m_1}\right)$

Since $x = x_2 - x_1 - L_0$, $\ddot{x} = \ddot{x}_2 - \ddot{x}_1$:
$$
\ddot{x} = -k\left(\frac{m_1 + m_2}{m_1 m_2}\right)x = -\frac{k}{\mu}x
$$
where $\mu = \frac{m_1 m_2}{m_1 + m_2}$ (reduced mass).

Thus $\omega = \sqrt{k/\mu}$, $T = 2\pi\sqrt{\mu/k}$.
∎

## Proof 10: Energy in Vertical Spring System

**Statement:** Total potential energy $U_{total} = \frac{1}{2}ky^2 + \text{constant}$, where $y$ is displacement from equilibrium.

**Proof:**
Extension from natural length: $x = x_0 + y$, with $x_0 = mg/k$.

Spring potential energy:
$$
U_{spring} = \frac{1}{2}kx^2 = \frac{1}{2}k(x_0 + y)^2 = \frac{1}{2}kx_0^2 + kx_0y + \frac{1}{2}ky^2
$$

Gravitational potential (reference at natural length, $y$ positive down):
$$
U_{grav} = -mgx = -mg(x_0 + y) = -kx_0(x_0 + y) = -kx_0^2 - kx_0y
$$

Total potential:
$$
U_{total} = U_{spring} + U_{grav} = \left(\frac{1}{2}kx_0^2 + kx_0y + \frac{1}{2}ky^2\right) + \left(-kx_0^2 - kx_0y\right)
$$
$$
= \frac{1}{2}ky^2 - \frac{1}{2}kx_0^2
$$

The constant $-\frac{1}{2}kx_0^2$ doesn't affect dynamics. Near equilibrium, $U \approx \frac{1}{2}ky^2$.
∎
