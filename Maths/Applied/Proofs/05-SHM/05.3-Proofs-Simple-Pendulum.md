# Proofs: Simple Pendulum

**Syllabus:** GCE A/L Combined Mathematics (021) — Paper II, Topic 5.3

---

## Proof 1: Equation of Motion (Exact)

**Statement:** For simple pendulum (point mass $m$, length $L$, small angle $\theta$), $\ddot{\theta} + \frac{g}{L}\sin\theta = 0$.

**Proof:**
Forces on bob: tension $T$ along string, weight $mg$ down.

Tangential component of weight: $-mg\sin\theta$ (restoring).

Tangential acceleration: $a_t = L\ddot{\theta}$.

Newton's 2nd law (tangential):
$$
mL\ddot{\theta} = -mg\sin\theta
$$

Divide by $mL$:
$$
\ddot{\theta} + \frac{g}{L}\sin\theta = 0
$$
∎

---

## Proof 2: Small Angle Approximation → SHM

**Statement:** For $\theta \ll 1$, $\sin\theta \approx \theta$, giving $\ddot{\theta} + \frac{g}{L}\theta = 0$ with $\omega = \sqrt{g/L}$.

**Proof:**
From Proof 1: $\ddot{\theta} + \frac{g}{L}\sin\theta = 0$.

Taylor expansion: $\sin\theta = \theta - \frac{\theta^3}{6} + \frac{\theta^5}{120} - \cdots$

For $|\theta| \ll 1$ rad: $\sin\theta \approx \theta$.

Substitute:
$$
\ddot{\theta} + \frac{g}{L}\theta = 0
$$

Standard SHM form $\ddot{\theta} + \omega^2\theta = 0$ with:
$$
\omega^2 = \frac{g}{L} \implies \omega = \sqrt{\frac{g}{L}}
$$

**Period:**
$$
T = \frac{2\pi}{\omega} = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{L}{g}}
$$

**Error estimate:** Next term $\theta^3/6$. For $\theta_0 = 10^\circ = 0.175$ rad, error $\approx 0.175^3/6 \approx 0.0009$ (0.09%).
∎

---

## Proof 3: Energy Conservation Derivation

**Statement:** Using energy conservation, $\omega = \sqrt{g/L}$ for small oscillations.

**Proof:**
Potential energy (zero at bottom):
$$
U = mgL(1 - \cos\theta)
$$

Kinetic energy:
$$
K = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 = \frac{1}{2}m(L\dot{\theta})^2
$$

Total energy $E = K + U$ is conserved:
$$
\frac{1}{2}mL^2\dot{\theta}^2 + mgL(1 - \cos\theta) = E
$$

At maximum displacement $\theta = \theta_0$, $\dot{\theta} = 0$:
$$
E = mgL(1 - \cos\theta_0)
$$

Substitute back:
$$
\frac{1}{2}mL^2\dot{\theta}^2 = mgL(\cos\theta - \cos\theta_0)
$$

For small $\theta$: $\cos\theta \approx 1 - \frac{\theta^2}{2}$, $\cos\theta_0 \approx 1 - \frac{\theta_0^2}{2}$:
$$
\frac{1}{2}mL^2\dot{\theta}^2 \approx mgL\frac{\theta_0^2 - \theta^2}{2}
$$
$$
\dot{\theta}^2 = \frac{g}{L}(\theta_0^2 - \theta^2)
$$

Differentiate with respect to time:
$$
2\dot{\theta}\ddot{\theta} = -\frac{2g}{L}\theta\dot{\theta}
$$

For $\dot{\theta} \neq 0$:
$$
\ddot{\theta} + \frac{g}{L}\theta = 0 \implies \omega = \sqrt{\frac{g}{L}}
$$
∎

## Proof 4: Exact Period via Elliptic Integral

**Statement:** Exact period for amplitude $\theta_0$:
$$
T = 4\sqrt{\frac{L}{g}} \int_0^{\theta_0} \frac{d\theta}{\sqrt{2(\cos\theta - \cos\theta_0)}} = 4\sqrt{\frac{L}{g}} K(k), \quad k = \sin(\theta_0/2)
$$

**Proof:**
From energy conservation (Proof 3):
$$
\frac{1}{2}mL^2\dot{\theta}^2 = mgL(\cos\theta - \cos\theta_0)
$$
$$
\dot{\theta} = \sqrt{\frac{2g}{L}(\cos\theta - \cos\theta_0)}
$$

Period $T$ = 4 × time from $\theta = 0$ to $\theta = \theta_0$:
$$
T = 4\int_0^{\theta_0} \frac{d\theta}{\dot{\theta}} = 4\sqrt{\frac{L}{2g}} \int_0^{\theta_0} \frac{d\theta}{\sqrt{\cos\theta - \cos\theta_0}}
$$

Use identity: $\cos\theta - \cos\theta_0 = 2(\sin^2\frac{\theta_0}{2} - \sin^2\frac{\theta}{2})$.

Let $k = \sin(\theta_0/2)$, and substitute $\sin(\theta/2) = k\sin\phi$:
- When $\theta = 0$, $\phi = 0$
- When $\theta = \theta_0$, $\phi = \pi/2$
- $d\theta = \frac{2k\cos\phi}{\sqrt{1 - k^2\sin^2\phi}}d\phi$

Then:
$$
\cos\theta = 1 - 2\sin^2(\theta/2) = 1 - 2k^2\sin^2\phi
$$
$$
\cos\theta - \cos\theta_0 = 2k^2(1 - \sin^2\phi) = 2k^2\cos^2\phi
$$
$$
\sqrt{\cos\theta - \cos\theta_0} = \sqrt{2}k\cos\phi
$$

Substitute:
$$
T = 4\sqrt{\frac{L}{2g}} \int_0^{\pi/2} \frac{2k\cos\phi\,d\phi/\sqrt{1 - k^2\sin^2\phi}}{\sqrt{2}k\cos\phi}
$$
$$
= 4\sqrt{\frac{L}{g}} \int_0^{\pi/2} \frac{d\phi}{\sqrt{1 - k^2\sin^2\phi}} = 4\sqrt{\frac{L}{g}} K(k)
$$
where $K(k)$ is the complete elliptic integral of the first kind.
∎

## Proof 5: Series Expansion of Exact Period

**Statement:** $T = T_0\left[1 + \frac{1}{4}\sin^2\frac{\theta_0}{2} + \frac{9}{64}\sin^4\frac{\theta_0}{2} + \frac{25}{256}\sin^6\frac{\theta_0}{2} + \cdots\right]$, $T_0 = 2\pi\sqrt{L/g}$.

**Proof:**
The complete elliptic integral $K(k)$ has series:
$$
K(k) = \frac{\pi}{2}\left[1 + \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^2 k^2 + \left(\frac{1\cdot 3}{2\cdot 4}\right)^2 k^4 + \left(\frac{1\cdot 3\cdot 5}{2\cdot 4\cdot 6}\right)^2 k^6 + \cdots\right]
$$

General term: $\left[\frac{(2n-1)!!}{(2n)!!}\right]^2 k^{2n}$.

Using $T = 4\sqrt{L/g} K(k)$ and $T_0 = 2\pi\sqrt{L/g}$:
$$
\frac{T}{T_0} = \frac{4}{\pi} K(k) = 1 + \frac{1}{4}k^2 + \frac{9}{64}k^4 + \frac{25}{256}k^6 + \cdots
$$

Since $k = \sin(\theta_0/2)$:
$$
T = T_0\left[1 + \frac{1}{4}\sin^2\frac{\theta_0}{2} + \frac{9}{64}\sin^4\frac{\theta_0}{2} + \frac{25}{256}\sin^6\frac{\theta_0}{2} + \cdots\right]
$$

**Alternative form (in radians):**
Using $\sin^2(\theta_0/2) \approx \theta_0^2/4$:
$$
T \approx T_0\left(1 + \frac{\theta_0^2}{16} + \frac{11\theta_0^4}{3072} + \cdots\right)
$$
∎

## Proof 6: Physical Pendulum Period

**Statement:** For rigid body pivoted at $O$, distance $d$ from CM, moment of inertia $I_O$ about $O$:
$$
T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{I_O}{mgd}} = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{I_G + md^2}{mgd}}
$$
where $I_G$ is moment of inertia about CM.

**Proof:**
Torque about pivot $O$: $\tau = -mgd\sin\theta$ (restoring).

Rotational equation: $I_O\ddot{\theta} = \tau = -mgd\sin\theta$.

For small $\theta$: $\sin\theta \approx \theta$:
$$
\ddot{\theta} + \frac{mgd}{I_O}\theta = 0
$$

Angular frequency:
$$
\omega = \sqrt{\frac{mgd}{I_O}}
$$

Period:
$$
T = \frac{2\pi}{\omega} = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{I_O}{mgd}}
$$

**Parallel axis theorem:** $I_O = I_G + md^2$ where $d$ is distance from $O$ to CM.

Thus:
$$
T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{I_G + md^2}{mgd}}
$$
∎

## Proof 7: Equivalent Simple Pendulum Length

**Statement:** Equivalent length $L_{eq} = I_O/(md)$ gives same period as simple pendulum.

**Proof:**
From Proof 6, physical pendulum period:
$$
T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{I_O}{mgd}}
$$

Simple pendulum period:
$$
T_0 = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{L}{g}}
$$

Equate $T = T_0$:
$$
2\pi\sqrt{\frac{I_O}{mgd}} = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{L_{eq}}{g}} \implies \frac{I_O}{md} = L_{eq}
$$

**Centre of oscillation:** Point at distance $L_{eq}$ from pivot along line through CM. If suspended from this point, period is unchanged.

**Centre of percussion:** Point where an impulse produces no reaction at pivot. Same as centre of oscillation.
∎

## Proof 8: Uniform Rod Pivoted at End

**Statement:** Uniform rod length $L$, mass $m$, pivoted at one end. $T = 2\pi\sqrt{2L/3g}$.

**Proof:**
For rod about end: $I_O = \frac{1}{3}mL^2$ (or parallel axis: $I_G = \frac{1}{12}mL^2$, $d = L/2$, $I_O = \frac{1}{12}mL^2 + m(L/2)^2 = \frac{1}{3}mL^2$).

Distance to CM: $d = L/2$.

Period:
$$
T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{\frac{1}{3}mL^2}{mg(L/2)}} = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{2L}{3g}}
$$

Equivalent simple pendulum length: $L_{eq} = \frac{2L}{3}$.
∎

## Proof 9: Conical Pendulum

**Statement:** Conical pendulum (string length $L$, angle $\theta$ with vertical) has period $T = 2\pi\sqrt{L\cos\theta/g}$.

**Proof:**
Forces on bob:
- Tension $T$ along string
- Weight $mg$ down

Vertical equilibrium: $T\cos\theta = mg \implies T = mg/\cos\theta$

Horizontal (centripetal): $T\sin\theta = m\omega^2 r$, where $r = L\sin\theta$ is radius.

Substitute $T$:
$$
\frac{mg}{\cos\theta}\sin\theta = m\omega^2 L\sin\theta
$$

Cancel $m\sin\theta$ (assuming $\theta \neq 0$):
$$
\frac{g}{\cos\theta} = \omega^2 L \implies \omega^2 = \frac{g}{L\cos\theta}
$$

Period:
$$
T = \frac{2\pi}{\omega} = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{L\cos\theta}{g}}
$$

**Note:** As $\theta \to 0$, $T \to 2\pi\sqrt{L/g}$ (simple pendulum limit).
∎

---

## Proof 10: Torsional Pendulum

**Statement:** Disc moment of inertia $I$ suspended by wire with torsion constant $c$. Period $T = 2\pi\sqrt{I/c}$.

**Proof:**
Restoring torque: $\tau = -c\theta$.

Equation of motion: $I\ddot{\theta} = -c\theta \implies \ddot{\theta} + \frac{c}{I}\theta = 0$.

SHM with $\omega = \sqrt{c/I}$, $T = 2\pi\sqrt{I/c}$.

**Application:** Measure $I$ of irregular body by timing oscillations.
∎
