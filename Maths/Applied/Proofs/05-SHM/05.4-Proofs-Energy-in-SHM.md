# Proofs: Energy in Simple Harmonic Motion

**Syllabus:** GCE A/L Combined Mathematics (021) — Paper II, Topic 5.4

---

## Proof 1: Total Energy is Constant in Ideal SHM

**Statement:** For $x = A\cos(\omega t + \phi)$, total energy $E = \frac{1}{2}kA^2 = \text{constant}$.

**Proof:**
Displacement: $x = A\cos(\omega t + \phi)$

Velocity: $v = \dot{x} = -A\omega\sin(\omega t + \phi)$

Kinetic energy:
$$
K = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 = \frac{1}{2}mA^2\omega^2\sin^2(\omega t + \phi)
$$

Potential energy (spring):
$$
U = \frac{1}{2}kx^2 = \frac{1}{2}kA^2\cos^2(\omega t + \phi)
$$

Using $\omega^2 = k/m$:
$$
K = \frac{1}{2}kA^2\sin^2(\omega t + \phi)
$$

Total energy:
$$
E = K + U = \frac{1}{2}kA^2[\sin^2(\omega t + \phi) + \cos^2(\omega t + \phi)] = \frac{1}{2}kA^2
$$

Independent of time. $E = \frac{1}{2}kA^2 = \frac{1}{2}m\omega^2 A^2$.
∎

## Proof 2: Energy as Function of Displacement

**Statement:** $K = \frac{1}{2}k(A^2 - x^2)$, $U = \frac{1}{2}kx^2$, $E = \frac{1}{2}kA^2$.

**Proof:**
From energy conservation: $E = K + U = \frac{1}{2}kA^2$.

Potential energy at $x$: $U = \frac{1}{2}kx^2$.

Kinetic energy:
$$
K = E - U = \frac{1}{2}kA^2 - \frac{1}{2}kx^2 = \frac{1}{2}k(A^2 - x^2)
$$

**Alternative derivation:** Using $v^2 = \omega^2(A^2 - x^2)$:
$$
K = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 = \frac{1}{2}m\omega^2(A^2 - x^2) = \frac{1}{2}k(A^2 - x^2)
$$

**Velocity at $x$:**
$$
v = \pm\sqrt{\frac{k}{m}(A^2 - x^2)} = \pm\omega\sqrt{A^2 - x^2}
$$
∎

## Proof 3: Energy-Time Graphs ($K$, $U$ oscillate at $2\omega$)

**Statement:** $K = \frac{1}{4}kA^2(1 - \cos(2\omega t + 2\phi))$, $U = \frac{1}{4}kA^2(1 + \cos(2\omega t + 2\phi))$.

**Proof:**
From Proof 1:
$$
K = \frac{1}{2}kA^2\sin^2(\omega t + \phi)
$$
$$
U = \frac{1}{2}kA^2\cos^2(\omega t + \phi)
$$

Using $\sin^2\theta = \frac{1}{2}(1 - \cos 2\theta)$, $\cos^2\theta = \frac{1}{2}(1 + \cos 2\theta)$:

$$
K = \frac{1}{4}kA^2(1 - \cos(2\omega t + 2\phi))
$$
$$
U = \frac{1}{4}kA^2(1 + \cos(2\omega t + 2\phi))
$$

Both have:
- DC component: $\frac{1}{4}kA^2 = E/2$
- AC component: frequency $2\omega$ (angular), i.e., $2f$

$K$ and $U$ are $\pi$ out of phase: when $K$ is max, $U$ is min, and vice versa.
∎

## Proof 4: Virial Theorem for SHM

**Statement:** Time-averaged kinetic energy equals time-averaged potential energy: $\langle K \rangle = \langle U \rangle = E/2$.

**Proof:**
For any periodic function with period $T = 2\pi/\omega$:
$$
\langle f \rangle = \frac{1}{T}\int_0^T f(t)\,dt
$$

From Proof 3:
$$
K(t) = \frac{E}{2}(1 - \cos(2\omega t + 2\phi))
$$
$$
U(t) = \frac{E}{2}(1 + \cos(2\omega t + 2\phi))
$$

Average over one period:
$$
\langle K \rangle = \frac{E}{2}\left(1 - \frac{1}{T}\int_0^T \cos(2\omega t + 2\phi)\,dt\right) = \frac{E}{2}(1 - 0) = \frac{E}{2}
$$

Similarly $\langle U \rangle = E/2$.

**General virial theorem:** For $U \propto x^n$, $\langle K \rangle = \frac{n}{2}\langle U \rangle$. For SHM, $U \propto x^2$ ($n=2$), so $\langle K \rangle = \langle U \rangle$.
∎

## Proof 5: Energy Method for Finding $\omega$

**Statement:** For conservative 1D system with potential $U(x)$ near stable equilibrium $x_0$, $\omega = \sqrt{U''(x_0)/m}$.

**Proof:**
Expand $U(x)$ about equilibrium $x_0$ (where $U'(x_0) = 0$):
$$
U(x) = U(x_0) + \frac{1}{2}U''(x_0)(x - x_0)^2 + O((x-x_0)^3)
$$

Let $y = x - x_0$ (small displacement). Effective spring constant:
$$
k_{eff} = U''(x_0)
$$

Total energy (conserved):
$$
E = \frac{1}{2}m\dot{y}^2 + \frac{1}{2}k_{eff}y^2
$$

Differentiate with respect to time:
$$
0 = m\dot{y}\ddot{y} + k_{eff}y\dot{y}
$$

For $\dot{y} \neq 0$:
$$
m\ddot{y} + k_{eff}y = 0 \implies \ddot{y} + \frac{U''(x_0)}{m}y = 0
$$

Thus:
$$
\omega = \sqrt{\frac{U''(x_0)}{m}}
$$

**Example (simple pendulum):**
$$
U = mgL(1 - \cos\theta) \approx \frac{1}{2}mgL\theta^2 \quad (U''(0) = mgL)
$$
$$
\omega = \sqrt{\frac{mgL}{mL^2}} = \sqrt{\frac{g}{L}}
$$
∎

## Proof 6: Two Masses on a Spring (Reduced Mass Energy)

**Statement:** Masses $m_1, m_2$ connected by spring $k$. Total energy in COM frame: $E = \frac{1}{2}\mu\dot{x}^2 + \frac{1}{2}kx^2$, $\mu = \frac{m_1 m_2}{m_1 + m_2}$.

**Proof:**
Let $x_1, x_2$ be positions, $x = x_2 - x_1 - L_0$ extension.

Centre of mass:
$$
X_{CM} = \frac{m_1x_1 + m_2x_2}{M}, \quad M = m_1 + m_2
$$

Relative coordinate: $x = x_2 - x_1 - L_0$.

Velocities:
$$
\dot{x}_1 = \dot{X}_{CM} - \frac{m_2}{M}\dot{x}, \quad \dot{x}_2 = \dot{X}_{CM} + \frac{m_1}{M}\dot{x}
$$

Kinetic energy:
$$
K = \frac{1}{2}m_1\dot{x}_1^2 + \frac{1}{2}m_2\dot{x}_2^2
$$
$$
= \frac{1}{2}m_1\left(\dot{X}_{CM} - \frac{m_2}{M}\dot{x}\right)^2 + \frac{1}{2}m_2\left(\dot{X}_{CM} + \frac{m_1}{M}\dot{x}\right)^2
$$
$$
= \frac{1}{2}M\dot{X}_{CM}^2 + \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{m_1 m_2^2 + m_2 m_1^2}{M^2}\right)\dot{x}^2
$$

Cross terms cancel. Simplify coefficient:
$$
\frac{m_1 m_2(m_1 + m_2)}{(m_1 + m_2)^2} = \frac{m_1 m_2}{M} = \mu
$$

Thus:
$$
K = \frac{1}{2}M\dot{X}_{CM}^2 + \frac{1}{2}\mu\dot{x}^2
$$

Potential energy: $U = \frac{1}{2}kx^2$.

In COM frame ($\dot{X}_{CM} = 0$):
$$
E = \frac{1}{2}\mu\dot{x}^2 + \frac{1}{2}kx^2 = \frac{1}{2}kA^2
$$

Angular frequency: $\omega = \sqrt{k/\mu}$.
∎

## Proof 7: Energy in Vertical Spring System

**Statement:** For vertical spring-mass, total potential $U_{total} = \frac{1}{2}ky^2 + \text{const}$ where $y$ is from equilibrium.

**Proof:**
Extension from natural length: $x = x_0 + y$, $x_0 = mg/k$.

Spring potential:
$$
U_{spring} = \frac{1}{2}kx^2 = \frac{1}{2}k(x_0 + y)^2 = \frac{1}{2}kx_0^2 + kx_0y + \frac{1}{2}ky^2
$$

Gravitational potential (reference at natural length, $y$ positive down):
$$
U_{grav} = -mgx = -mg(x_0 + y) = -kx_0(x_0 + y) = -kx_0^2 - kx_0y
$$

Total potential:
$$
U_{total} = U_{spring} + U_{grav} = \left(\frac{1}{2}kx_0^2 + kx_0y + \frac{1}{2}ky^2\right) + \left(-kx_0^2 - kx_0y\right)
$$
$$
= \frac{1}{2}ky^2 - \frac{1}{2}kx_0^2
$$

Variable part: $\frac{1}{2}ky^2$ (same as horizontal spring). Constant $-\frac{1}{2}kx_0^2$ doesn't affect dynamics.
∎

## Proof 8: Work Done by Spring Force

**Statement:** Work done by spring from $x_1$ to $x_2$: $W = \frac{1}{2}k(x_1^2 - x_2^2)$.

**Proof:**
Spring force: $F = -kx$.

Work done by spring:
$$
W = \int_{x_1}^{x_2} F\,dx = \int_{x_1}^{x_2} (-kx)\,dx = -k\left[\frac{x^2}{2}\right]_{x_1}^{x_2} = \frac{1}{2}k(x_1^2 - x_2^2)
$$

**Work-energy theorem:** $W = \Delta K$.

If mass moves from $x_1$ to $x_2$:
$$
\Delta K = \frac{1}{2}mv_2^2 - \frac{1}{2}mv_1^2 = \frac{1}{2}k(x_1^2 - x_2^2)
$$

Rearrange: $\frac{1}{2}mv_1^2 + \frac{1}{2}kx_1^2 = \frac{1}{2}mv_2^2 + \frac{1}{2}kx_2^2$ — energy conservation.
∎

## Proof 9: Energy in Coupled Oscillators (Normal Modes)

**Statement:** Two identical masses $m$ with springs $k$ (wall—$k$—$m$—$k$—$m$—$k$—wall). Normal mode energies are independent.

**Proof:**
Coordinates: $x_1, x_2$ displacements from equilibrium.

Potential energy:
$$
U = \frac{1}{2}kx_1^2 + \frac{1}{2}k(x_2 - x_1)^2 + \frac{1}{2}kx_2^2 = \frac{1}{2}k(2x_1^2 - 2x_1x_2 + 2x_2^2)
$$

Normal coordinates:
$$
q_1 = x_1 + x_2 \quad \text{(symmetric)}, \quad q_2 = x_1 - x_2 \quad \text{(antisymmetric)}
$$

Inverse:
$$
x_1 = \frac{q_1 + q_2}{2}, \quad x_2 = \frac{q_1 - q_2}{2}
$$

Substitute into $U$:
$$
U = \frac{1}{2}k\left[2\left(\frac{q_1+q_2}{2}\right)^2 - 2\left(\frac{q_1+q_2}{2}\right)\left(\frac{q_1-q_2}{2}\right) + 2\left(\frac{q_1-q_2}{2}\right)^2\right]
$$
$$
= \frac{1}{2}k\left[\frac{1}{2}(q_1^2 + 2q_1q_2 + q_2^2) - \frac{1}{2}(q_1^2 - q_2^2) + \frac{1}{2}(q_1^2 - 2q_1q_2 + q_2^2)\right]
$$
$$
= \frac{1}{2}k(q_1^2 + q_2^2)
$$

Kinetic energy:
$$
K = \frac{1}{2}m(\dot{x}_1^2 + \dot{x}_2^2) = \frac{1}{2}m\left(\frac{\dot{q}_1^2 + \dot{q}_2^2}{2}\right) = \frac{1}{4}m(\dot{q}_1^2 + \dot{q}_2^2)
$$

Total energy:
$$
E = \frac{1}{4}m(\dot{q}_1^2 + \dot{q}_2^2) + \frac{1}{2}k(q_1^2 + q_2^2)
$$

Normal mode frequencies: $\omega_1 = \sqrt{k/m}$ (symmetric), $\omega_2 = \sqrt{3k/m}$ (antisymmetric).

Energies in normal modes are separately conserved.
∎

## Proof 10: Damped Oscillator Energy Decay

**Statement:** For $\ddot{x} + 2\gamma\dot{x} + \omega_0^2 x = 0$, average energy $\langle E \rangle \approx E_0 e^{-2\gamma t}$.

**Proof:**
Multiply equation by $\dot{x}$:
$$
\dot{x}\ddot{x} + 2\gamma\dot{x}^2 + \omega_0^2 x\dot{x} = 0
$$

Recognize:
$$
\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{1}{2}\dot{x}^2\right) + 2\gamma\dot{x}^2 + \frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{1}{2}\omega_0^2 x^2\right) = 0
$$

Total energy: $E = \frac{1}{2}\dot{x}^2 + \frac{1}{2}\omega_0^2 x^2$ (per unit mass).

$$
\frac{dE}{dt} = -2\gamma\dot{x}^2
$$

For light damping ($\gamma \ll \omega_0$): $x \approx Ae^{-\gamma t}\cos\omega_0 t$, $\dot{x} \approx -A\omega_0 e^{-\gamma t}\sin\omega_0 t$.

Average over cycle:
$$
\langle \dot{x}^2 \rangle = \frac{1}{2}A^2\omega_0^2 e^{-2\gamma t}
$$

Average energy:
$$
\langle E \rangle = \frac{1}{2}\omega_0^2 A^2 e^{-2\gamma t} = E_0 e^{-2\gamma t}
$$

Exact solution of $dE/dt = -2\gamma\dot{x}^2$ gives $E(t) = E_0 e^{-2\gamma t}$ for light damping.
∎
