# Proofs: Damped & Forced Oscillations

**Syllabus:** GCE A/L Combined Mathematics (021) — Paper II, Topic 5.5

---

## Proof 1: Damped Oscillator Characteristic Equation

**Statement:** For $m\ddot{x} + b\dot{x} + kx = 0$, characteristic equation is $r^2 + 2\gamma r + \omega_0^2 = 0$ with $\gamma = b/2m$, $\omega_0 = \sqrt{k/m}$.

**Proof:**
Divide by $m$:
$$
\ddot{x} + \frac{b}{m}\dot{x} + \frac{k}{m}x = 0
$$

Define $\gamma = \frac{b}{2m}$, $\omega_0^2 = \frac{k}{m}$:
$$
\ddot{x} + 2\gamma\dot{x} + \omega_0^2 x = 0
$$

Assume $x = e^{rt}$:
$$
r^2 e^{rt} + 2\gamma r e^{rt} + \omega_0^2 e^{rt} = 0 \implies r^2 + 2\gamma r + \omega_0^2 = 0
$$

Roots:
$$
r = -\gamma \pm \sqrt{\gamma^2 - \omega_0^2}
$$

Three regimes:
- $\gamma < \omega_0$: complex roots (underdamped)
- $\gamma = \omega_0$: repeated real root (critical)
- $\gamma > \omega_0$: two real roots (overdamped)
∎

## Proof 2: Underdamped Solution ($\gamma < \omega_0$)

**Statement:** $x(t) = Ae^{-\gamma t}\cos(\omega_d t + \phi)$ with $\omega_d = \sqrt{\omega_0^2 - \gamma^2}$.

**Proof:**
Roots: $r = -\gamma \pm i\omega_d$ where $\omega_d = \sqrt{\omega_0^2 - \gamma^2}$.

General solution:
$$
x(t) = e^{-\gamma t}(C_1 e^{i\omega_d t} + C_2 e^{-i\omega_d t})
$$

Using Euler: $e^{\pm i\omega_d t} = \cos\omega_d t \pm i\sin\omega_d t$:
$$
x(t) = e^{-\gamma t}(A_1\cos\omega_d t + A_2\sin\omega_d t)
$$

Combine: $A = \sqrt{A_1^2 + A_2^2}$, $\phi = \arctan(-A_2/A_1)$:
$$
x(t) = Ae^{-\gamma t}\cos(\omega_d t + \phi)
$$

**Period:** $T_d = \frac{2\pi}{\omega_d} = \frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{\omega_0^2 - \gamma^2}} \approx T_0\left(1 + \frac{\gamma^2}{2\omega_0^2}\right)$
∎

---

## Proof 3: Critical Damping Solution ($\gamma = \omega_0$)

**Statement:** $x(t) = (A + Bt)e^{-\gamma t}$.

**Proof:**
Repeated root: $r = -\gamma$.

Two independent solutions: $e^{-\gamma t}$ and $te^{-\gamma t}$.

General solution:
$$
x(t) = (A + Bt)e^{-\gamma t}
$$

**Fastest non-oscillatory return:** For $x(0) = x_0$, $\dot{x}(0) = 0$:
$A = x_0$, $B = \gamma x_0$, so $x(t) = x_0(1 + \gamma t)e^{-\gamma t}$.
∎

---

## Proof 4: Overdamped Solution ($\gamma > \omega_0$)

**Statement:** $x(t) = Ae^{r_1 t} + Be^{r_2 t}$ where $r_1 = -\gamma + \sqrt{\gamma^2 - \omega_0^2}$, $r_2 = -\gamma - \sqrt{\gamma^2 - \omega_0^2}$.

**Proof:**
Real distinct roots: $r_1 = -\gamma + \beta$, $r_2 = -\gamma - \beta$ where $\beta = \sqrt{\gamma^2 - \omega_0^2}$.

General solution:
$$
x(t) = Ae^{(-\gamma + \beta)t} + Be^{(-\gamma - \beta)t}
$$

Both terms decay exponentially. No oscillation.
∎

## Proof 5: Quality Factor $Q = \omega_0/2\gamma$

**Statement:** $Q = \frac{\omega_0}{2\gamma} = \frac{\text{energy stored}}{\text{energy lost per radian}}$.

**Proof:**
**Energy stored** (maximum potential energy):
$$
E = \frac{1}{2}kA^2 = \frac{1}{2}m\omega_0^2 A^2
$$

**Energy lost per cycle:** Work by damping force $F_d = -b\dot{x}$ over period $T \approx 2\pi/\omega_0$:
$$
\Delta E = \int_0^T b\dot{x}^2 dt
$$

For $x = Ae^{-\gamma t}\cos\omega_d t \approx A\cos\omega_0 t$ (light damping):
$$
\dot{x} \approx -A\omega_0\sin\omega_0 t
$$
$$
\dot{x}^2 \approx A^2\omega_0^2\sin^2\omega_0 t
$$
$$
\Delta E \approx b A^2\omega_0^2 \int_0^T \sin^2\omega_0 t\, dt = b A^2\omega_0^2 \cdot \frac{T}{2} = b A^2\omega_0^2 \cdot \frac{\pi}{\omega_0} = \pi b A^2 \omega_0
$$

**Energy lost per radian:**
$$
\frac{\Delta E}{2\pi} = \frac{1}{2}b A^2 \omega_0
$$

**Quality factor:**
$$
Q = \frac{E}{\Delta E/2\pi} = \frac{\frac{1}{2}m\omega_0^2 A^2}{\frac{1}{2}b A^2 \omega_0} = \frac{m\omega_0}{b} = \frac{\omega_0}{2\gamma}
$$
∎

## Proof 6: Logarithmic Decrement $\delta = \pi/Q$

**Statement:** $\delta = \ln(A_n/A_{n+1}) = \frac{2\pi\gamma}{\omega_d} = \frac{\pi}{Q}$.

**Proof:**
Amplitude envelope: $A(t) = A_0 e^{-\gamma t}$.

At $n$-th peak: $t_n = n T_d = n\frac{2\pi}{\omega_d}$.

Amplitude at peak $n$:
$$
A_n = A_0 e^{-\gamma n T_d}
$$

At peak $n+1$:
$$
A_{n+1} = A_0 e^{-\gamma (n+1) T_d}
$$

Ratio:
$$
\frac{A_n}{A_{n+1}} = e^{\gamma T_d} = e^{\gamma \cdot 2\pi/\omega_d}
$$

Logarithmic decrement:
$$
\delta = \ln\left(\frac{A_n}{A_{n+1}}\right) = \frac{2\pi\gamma}{\omega_d}
$$

For light damping: $\omega_d \approx \omega_0$, $\delta \approx \frac{2\pi\gamma}{\omega_0} = \frac{\pi}{Q}$.
∎

## Proof 7: Energy Decay in Light Damping

**Statement:** Average energy $\langle E \rangle \approx E_0 e^{-2\gamma t}$.

**Proof:**
Equation: $\ddot{x} + 2\gamma\dot{x} + \omega_0^2 x = 0$.

Multiply by $\dot{x}$:
$$
\dot{x}\ddot{x} + 2\gamma\dot{x}^2 + \omega_0^2 x\dot{x} = 0
$$

Recognize:
$$
\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{1}{2}\dot{x}^2\right) + 2\gamma\dot{x}^2 + \frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{1}{2}\omega_0^2 x^2\right) = 0
$$

Total energy (per unit mass): $E = \frac{1}{2}\dot{x}^2 + \frac{1}{2}\omega_0^2 x^2$:
$$
\frac{dE}{dt} = -2\gamma\dot{x}^2
$$

For light damping: $x \approx Ae^{-\gamma t}\cos\omega_0 t$, $\dot{x} \approx -A\omega_0 e^{-\gamma t}\sin\omega_0 t$.

Average $\dot{x}^2$ over cycle:
$$
\langle \dot{x}^2 \rangle = \frac{1}{2}A^2\omega_0^2 e^{-2\gamma t}
$$

Average energy:
$$
\langle E \rangle = \frac{1}{2}\omega_0^2 A^2 e^{-2\gamma t} = E_0 e^{-2\gamma t}
$$

Amplitude: $A(t) = A_0 e^{-\gamma t}$.
∎

## Proof 8: Forced Oscillator Steady-State Solution

**Statement:** For $\ddot{x} + 2\gamma\dot{x} + \omega_0^2 x = \frac{F_0}{m}\cos\omega t$, steady state $x(t) = A\cos(\omega t - \phi)$ with
$$
A = \frac{F_0/m}{\sqrt{(\omega_0^2 - \omega^2)^2 + 4\gamma^2\omega^2}}, \quad \tan\phi = \frac{2\gamma\omega}{\omega_0^2 - \omega^2}
$$

**Proof:**
Assume $x_{PI} = A\cos(\omega t - \phi)$.

Substitute into LHS:
$$
\ddot{x} = -A\omega^2\cos(\omega t - \phi)
$$
$$
\dot{x} = -A\omega\sin(\omega t - \phi)
$$
$$
2\gamma\dot{x} = -2\gamma A\omega\sin(\omega t - \phi)
$$
$$
\omega_0^2 x = \omega_0^2 A\cos(\omega t - \phi)
$$

Sum LHS:
$$
A[(\omega_0^2 - \omega^2)\cos(\omega t - \phi) - 2\gamma\omega\sin(\omega t - \phi)]
$$

Expand using $\cos(\omega t - \phi) = \cos\omega t\cos\phi + \sin\omega t\sin\phi$, $\sin(\omega t - \phi) = \sin\omega t\cos\phi - \cos\omega t\sin\phi$:

LHS = $A\{[\omega_0^2 - \omega^2]\cos\phi + 2\gamma\omega\sin\phi\}\cos\omega t$
$+ A\{[\omega_0^2 - \omega^2]\sin\phi - 2\gamma\omega\cos\phi\}\sin\omega t$

Equate to RHS $= \frac{F_0}{m}\cos\omega t$:

$\cos\omega t$ coefficient:
$$
A[(\omega_0^2 - \omega^2)\cos\phi + 2\gamma\omega\sin\phi] = \frac{F_0}{m} \quad (1)
$$

$\sin\omega t$ coefficient:
$$
A[(\omega_0^2 - \omega^2)\sin\phi - 2\gamma\omega\cos\phi] = 0 \quad (2)
$$

From (2):
$$
(\omega_0^2 - \omega^2)\sin\phi = 2\gamma\omega\cos\phi \implies \tan\phi = \frac{2\gamma\omega}{\omega_0^2 - \omega^2}
$$

Square (1) and (2) and add:
$$
A^2[(\omega_0^2 - \omega^2)^2(\cos^2\phi + \sin^2\phi) + 4\gamma^2\omega^2(\sin^2\phi + \cos^2\phi)] = \left(\frac{F_0}{m}\right)^2
$$
$$
A^2[(\omega_0^2 - \omega^2)^2 + 4\gamma^2\omega^2] = \left(\frac{F_0}{m}\right)^2
$$

Thus:
$$
A = \frac{F_0/m}{\sqrt{(\omega_0^2 - \omega^2)^2 + 4\gamma^2\omega^2}}
$$
∎

## Proof 9: Resonance Frequency $\omega_r = \sqrt{\omega_0^2 - 2\gamma^2}$

**Statement:** Amplitude maximum occurs at $\omega_r = \sqrt{\omega_0^2 - 2\gamma^2} \approx \omega_0$ (light damping).

**Proof:**
Amplitude:
$$
A(\omega) = \frac{F_0/m}{\sqrt{(\omega_0^2 - \omega^2)^2 + 4\gamma^2\omega^2}}
$$

Maximize $A$ $\Leftrightarrow$ minimize denominator $D(\omega) = (\omega_0^2 - \omega^2)^2 + 4\gamma^2\omega^2$.

$$
\frac{dD}{d\omega} = 2(\omega_0^2 - \omega^2)(-2\omega) + 8\gamma^2\omega = 0
$$
$$
-4\omega(\omega_0^2 - \omega^2) + 8\gamma^2\omega = 0
$$
$$
\omega[-\omega_0^2 + \omega^2 + 2\gamma^2] = 0
$$

Non-zero solution:
$$
\omega_r^2 = \omega_0^2 - 2\gamma^2 \implies \omega_r = \sqrt{\omega_0^2 - 2\gamma^2}
$$

For $\gamma \ll \omega_0$: $\omega_r \approx \omega_0$.

**Note:** Power resonance (max $\langle P \rangle$) occurs at $\omega = \omega_0$ exactly.
∎

## Proof 10: Maximum Amplitude at Resonance

**Statement:** $A_{max} = \frac{F_0/m}{2\gamma\sqrt{\omega_0^2 - \gamma^2}} \approx \frac{F_0}{2m\gamma\omega_0} = \frac{Q F_0}{m\omega_0^2}$.

**Proof:**
Substitute $\omega_r^2 = \omega_0^2 - 2\gamma^2$ into $A(\omega)$:

$(\omega_0^2 - \omega_r^2)^2 = (2\gamma^2)^2 = 4\gamma^4$

$4\gamma^2\omega_r^2 = 4\gamma^2(\omega_0^2 - 2\gamma^2) = 4\gamma^2\omega_0^2 - 8\gamma^4$

Denominator:
$$
\sqrt{4\gamma^4 + 4\gamma^2\omega_0^2 - 8\gamma^4} = \sqrt{4\gamma^2\omega_0^2 - 4\gamma^4} = 2\gamma\sqrt{\omega_0^2 - \gamma^2}
$$

Maximum amplitude:
$$
A_{max} = \frac{F_0/m}{2\gamma\sqrt{\omega_0^2 - \gamma^2}}
$$

For light damping ($\gamma \ll \omega_0$):
$$
\sqrt{\omega_0^2 - \gamma^2} \approx \omega_0 \implies A_{max} \approx \frac{F_0/m}{2\gamma\omega_0} = \frac{F_0}{2m\gamma\omega_0}
$$

Using $Q = \omega_0/2\gamma \implies 1/2\gamma = Q/\omega_0$:
$$
A_{max} \approx \frac{F_0}{m} \cdot \frac{Q}{\omega_0} \cdot \frac{1}{\omega_0} = \frac{Q F_0}{m\omega_0^2}
$$
∎

## Proof 11: Phase at Resonance is $\pi/2$

**Statement:** At $\omega = \omega_0$, $\phi = \pi/2$.

**Proof:**
From Proof 8: $\tan\phi = \frac{2\gamma\omega}{\omega_0^2 - \omega^2}$.

At $\omega = \omega_0$: denominator $= 0$, numerator $> 0$.

$\tan\phi \to +\infty \implies \phi = \pi/2$ (or $90^\circ$).

**Physical meaning:** Velocity $\dot{x} = -A\omega\sin(\omega t - \pi/2) = A\omega\cos\omega t$ is in phase with driving force $F_0\cos\omega t$. Maximum power transfer.
∎

## Proof 12: Average Power Absorption

**Statement:** $\langle P_{in} \rangle = \frac{F_0^2}{2m} \frac{\gamma\omega^2}{(\omega_0^2 - \omega^2)^2 + 4\gamma^2\omega^2}$. Maximum at $\omega = \omega_0$.

**Proof:**
Instantaneous power: $P = F(t)\dot{x}(t)$.

Steady state: $x = A\cos(\omega t - \phi)$, $\dot{x} = -A\omega\sin(\omega t - \phi)$.
Driving force: $F = F_0\cos\omega t$.

$$
P = F_0\cos\omega t \cdot (-A\omega\sin(\omega t - \phi))
$$
$$
= -F_0 A\omega \cos\omega t \sin(\omega t - \phi)
$$

Use identity: $\cos A \sin B = \frac{1}{2}[\sin(A+B) - \sin(A-B)]$:
$$
P = -\frac{F_0 A\omega}{2}[\sin(2\omega t - \phi) - \sin\phi]
$$

Average over period $T = 2\pi/\omega$:
$$
\langle P \rangle = \frac{F_0 A\omega}{2}\sin\phi
$$

From Proof 8: $A\sin\phi = \frac{F_0/m \cdot 2\gamma\omega}{(\omega_0^2 - \omega^2)^2 + 4\gamma^2\omega^2}$ (derived from equating coefficients).

Substitute:
$$
\langle P \rangle = \frac{F_0 \omega}{2} \cdot \frac{F_0/m \cdot 2\gamma\omega}{(\omega_0^2 - \omega^2)^2 + 4\gamma^2\omega^2} = \frac{F_0^2}{2m} \frac{\gamma\omega^2}{(\omega_0^2 - \omega^2)^2 + 4\gamma^2\omega^2}
$$

**Power resonance:** Maximize $\langle P \rangle$ w.r.t. $\omega$. Derivative gives $\omega = \omega_0$ exactly.
∎

## Proof 13: Resonance Curve Width (FWHM)

**Statement:** Full width at half maximum $\Delta\omega \approx 2\gamma = \omega_0/Q$ for $Q \gg 1$.

**Proof:**
Half maximum power occurs when $\langle P \rangle = \frac{1}{2}\langle P \rangle_{max}$.

At $\omega = \omega_0$: $\langle P \rangle_{max} = \frac{F_0^2}{8m\gamma}$.

Set $\langle P \rangle = \frac{1}{2}\langle P \rangle_{max}$:
$$
\frac{\gamma\omega^2}{(\omega_0^2 - \omega^2)^2 + 4\gamma^2\omega^2} = \frac{1}{4\gamma\omega_0^2}
$$

For light damping, let $\omega = \omega_0 + \delta$ with $\delta \ll \omega_0$:
$\omega_0^2 - \omega^2 \approx -2\omega_0\delta$, $\omega^2 \approx \omega_0^2$.

$$
\frac{\gamma\omega_0^2}{4\omega_0^2\delta^2 + 4\gamma^2\omega_0^2} = \frac{1}{4\gamma\omega_0^2}
$$
$$
\frac{\gamma}{\delta^2 + \gamma^2} = \frac{1}{\gamma} \implies \delta^2 + \gamma^2 = \gamma^2 \implies \delta = \pm\gamma
$$

Width: $\Delta\omega = 2\delta = 2\gamma = \omega_0/Q$.
∎

## Proof 14: Critical Damping Coefficient

**Statement:** Critical damping $b_c = 2\sqrt{mk}$.

**Proof:**
Critical damping condition: $\gamma = \omega_0$.

$\gamma = b/2m$, $\omega_0 = \sqrt{k/m}$:
$$
\frac{b_c}{2m} = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}} \implies b_c = 2m\sqrt{\frac{k}{m}} = 2\sqrt{mk}
$$

**Alternative:** $b_c = 2m\omega_0 = 2\sqrt{km}$.
∎

## Proof 15: Quality Factor and Critical Damping

**Statement:** $Q = 1/2$ for critical damping.

**Proof:**
Critical damping: $\gamma = \omega_0$.

$Q = \frac{\omega_0}{2\gamma} = \frac{\omega_0}{2\omega_0} = \frac{1}{2}$.

**Overdamped:** $\gamma > \omega_0 \implies Q < 1/2$.

**Underdamped:** $\gamma < \omega_0 \implies Q > 1/2$.
∎
