# Proofs: Work, Energy, Power

**Syllabus:** GCE A/L Combined Mathematics (021) — Paper II, Topic 2.3

---

## Proof 1: Work-Energy Theorem (from Newton's 2nd Law)

**Statement:** $W_{\text{net}} = \Delta K = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 - \frac{1}{2}mu^2$

**Proof:**
Newton's 2nd law: $\vec{F}_{\text{net}} = m\vec{a} = m\frac{d\vec{v}}{dt}$

Work done by net force:
$$
W_{\text{net}} = \int \vec{F}_{\text{net}} \cdot d\vec{r} = \int m\frac{d\vec{v}}{dt} \cdot \vec{v}\,dt = \int m\vec{v} \cdot d\vec{v}
$$
$$
= \frac{1}{2}m\int d(v^2) = \frac{1}{2}m(v^2 - u^2) = \Delta K
$$
∎

---

## Proof 2: Work Done by Gravity (Path Independence)

**Statement:** $W_g = -mg\Delta h$, independent of path.

**Proof:**
$\vec{F}_g = -mg\hat{j}$. Work along path $C$:
$$
W_g = \int_C (-mg\hat{j}) \cdot (dx\hat{i} + dy\hat{j} + dz\hat{k}) = -mg\int_C dy = -mg(y_f - y_i) = -mg\Delta h
$$
Since integral depends only on endpoints, gravity is conservative. ∎

---

## Proof 3: Work Done by Spring Force

**Statement:** $W_s = \frac{1}{2}kx_i^2 - \frac{1}{2}kx_f^2$

**Proof:**
Spring force: $F = -kx$. Work from $x_i$ to $x_f$:
$$
W = \int_{x_i}^{x_f} (-kx)\,dx = -\frac{1}{2}k[x^2]_{x_i}^{x_f} = \frac{1}{2}kx_i^2 - \frac{1}{2}kx_f^2
$$
Define $U(x) = \frac{1}{2}kx^2$, then $W = U_i - U_f = -\Delta U$. ∎

---

## Proof 4: Potential Energy Definition for Conservative Forces

**Statement:** For conservative $\vec{F}$, $\vec{F} = -\nabla U$, $U(\vec{r}) = -\int_{\vec{r}_0}^{\vec{r}} \vec{F} \cdot d\vec{r}'$

**Proof:**
Conservative $\implies \oint \vec{F} \cdot d\vec{r} = 0 \implies$ work path-independent.
Define $U(\vec{r}) = -\int_{\vec{r}_0}^{\vec{r}} \vec{F} \cdot d\vec{r}'$.

Then $dU = \frac{\partial U}{\partial x}dx + \frac{\partial U}{\partial y}dy + \frac{\partial U}{\partial z}dz = -\vec{F} \cdot d\vec{r}$.
So $F_x = -\frac{\partial U}{\partial x}$, etc. $\implies \vec{F} = -\nabla U$. ∎

---

## Proof 5: Conservation of Mechanical Energy (Conservative Forces Only)

**Statement:** $E = K + U = \text{constant}$ when only conservative forces act.

**Proof:**
$W_{\text{net}} = \Delta K$. For conservative forces, $W_{\text{cons}} = -\Delta U$.
If all forces are conservative: $\Delta K = -\Delta U \implies \Delta(K + U) = 0 \implies K + U = \text{constant}$. ∎

---

## Proof 6: Work-Energy with Non-Conservative Forces

**Statement:** $K_f + U_f = K_i + U_i + W_{\text{nc}}$

**Proof:**
Total work = conservative + non-conservative:
$W_{\text{tot}} = W_{\text{cons}} + W_{\text{nc}}$.

But $W_{\text{cons}} = -\Delta U$ and $W_{\text{tot}} = \Delta K$.
So $\Delta K = -\Delta U + W_{\text{nc}} \implies K_f - K_i = -(U_f - U_i) + W_{\text{nc}}$
Rearrange: $K_f + U_f = K_i + U_i + W_{\text{nc}}$. ∎

---

## Proof 7: Kinetic Energy in Terms of Momentum

**Statement:** $K = \frac{p^2}{2m}$

**Proof:**
$p = mv \implies v = p/m$. Then $K = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 = \frac{1}{2}m(p^2/m^2) = \frac{p^2}{2m}$. ∎

---

## Proof 8: Power as Rate of Work

**Statement:** $P = \frac{dW}{dt} = \vec{F} \cdot \vec{v}$

**Proof:**
$dW = \vec{F} \cdot d\vec{r}$. Divide by $dt$:
$$
\frac{dW}{dt} = \vec{F} \cdot \frac{d\vec{r}}{dt} = \vec{F} \cdot \vec{v}
$$
Instantaneous power. Average power: $P_{\text{avg}} = \frac{W}{\Delta t}$. ∎

---

## Proof 9: Power for Constant Force

**Statement:** $P = Fv\cos\theta$

**Proof:**
From Proof 8: $P = \vec{F} \cdot \vec{v} = Fv\cos\theta$. ∎

---

## Proof 10: Power to Lift Mass at Constant Speed

**Statement:** $P = mgv$

**Proof:**
Force needed = weight = $mg$ (upward). Velocity = $v$ upward.
$P = \vec{F} \cdot \vec{v} = (mg)(v)\cos 0 = mgv$. ∎

---

## Proof 11: Work Done by Variable Force (1D)

**Statement:** $W = \int_{x_1}^{x_2} F(x)\,dx$

**Proof:**
Divide path into small segments $\Delta x$. Work $\Delta W \approx F(x_i)\Delta x$.
Sum and take limit: $W = \lim \sum F(x_i)\Delta x = \int F(x)\,dx$. ∎

---

## Proof 12: Work-Energy Theorem for Rotation (Analogy)

**Statement:** $W = \Delta K_{\text{rot}} = \frac{1}{2}I\omega^2 - \frac{1}{2}I\omega_0^2$

**Proof:**
Torque $\tau = I\alpha = I\frac{d\omega}{dt}$.
Work: $W = \int \tau\,d\theta = \int I\frac{d\omega}{dt} \omega\,dt = \int I\omega\,d\omega = \frac{1}{2}I(\omega^2 - \omega_0^2)$. ∎

---

## Proof 13: Work Done by Friction (Non-Conservative)

**Statement:** $W_f = -\mu N s$ (path-dependent, always negative)

**Proof:**
$f = \mu N$ opposes motion. $dW = -f\,ds = -\mu N\,ds$.
Total work: $W = -\mu N \int ds = -\mu N s$, where $s$ is total path length.
Depends on $s$, not just endpoints $\implies$ non-conservative. ∎

---

## Proof 14: Elastic Potential Energy

**Statement:** $U_{\text{spring}} = \frac{1}{2}kx^2$

**Proof:**
From Proof 3, $W_s = U_i - U_f$. Define $U(0) = 0$.
Then $U(x) = -W_{0\to x} = -(-\frac{1}{2}kx^2 + 0) = \frac{1}{2}kx^2$. ∎

---

## Proof 15: Gravitational Potential Energy (Near Earth)

**Statement:** $U = mgh$ (with $U=0$ at $h=0$)

**Proof:**
From Proof 2, $W_g = -mg\Delta h$. $U(h) = -W_{0\to h} = -(-mgh) = mgh$. ∎

---

## Proof 16: Work Done by Tension in Inextensible String (Internal)

**Statement:** Total work by internal tensions in connected system = 0.

**Proof:**
For each particle $i$, tension force $\vec{T}_i$. String length constant $\implies \sum \vec{T}_i \cdot \Delta \vec{r}_i = 0$.
Sum of works: $\sum W_i = \sum \vec{T}_i \cdot \Delta \vec{r}_i = 0$.
(More formally: constraint forces do no net work.) ∎

---

## Proof 17: Average Power

**Statement:** $P_{\text{avg}} = \frac{W}{t}$

**Proof:**
By definition of average value over interval $[0,t]$:
$P_{\text{avg}} = \frac{1}{t}\int_0^t P(t')\,dt' = \frac{1}{t}\int_0^t \frac{dW}{dt'}\,dt' = \frac{W(t) - W(0)}{t} = \frac{W}{t}$. ∎

---

## Proof 18: Power Delivered by Rocket Engine

**Statement:** $P = \frac{1}{2}\frac{dM}{dt}v_{\text{ex}}^2 + F_{\text{ext}}v$ (for variable mass)

**Proof:**
Kinetic energy of rocket + exhaust. For mass ejection rate $\dot{m} = -dM/dt$:
$\frac{dK}{dt} = \frac{1}{2}\dot{m}v_{\text{ex}}^2 + \vec{F}_{\text{ext}} \cdot \vec{v}$.
Power from engine = rate of KE increase of exhaust relative to rocket. ∎

---

## Proof 19: Work-Energy for Centre of Mass Motion

**Statement:** $W_{\text{ext}} = \Delta K_{\text{cm}}$

**Proof:**
$\vec{F}_{\text{ext}} = M\vec{a}_{\text{cm}}$. Work: $\int \vec{F}_{\text{ext}} \cdot d\vec{R}_{\text{cm}} = \int M\vec{a}_{\text{cm}} \cdot \vec{v}_{\text{cm}}\,dt = \frac{1}{2}M(v_{\text{cm}}^2 - u_{\text{cm}}^2) = \Delta K_{\text{cm}}$. ∎

---

## Proof 20: König's Theorem (Total KE = KE_cm + KE_rel)

**Statement:** $K = \frac{1}{2}Mv_{\text{cm}}^2 + \sum \frac{1}{2}m_i v_i'^2$

**Proof:**
$\vec{v}_i = \vec{v}_{\text{cm}} + \vec{v}_i'$.
$K = \sum \frac{1}{2}m_i(\vec{v}_{\text{cm}} + \vec{v}_i')^2 = \sum \frac{1}{2}m_i(v_{\text{cm}}^2 + 2\vec{v}_{\text{cm}}\cdot\vec{v}_i' + v_i'^2)$.
Since $\sum m_i\vec{v}_i' = 0$ (definition of CM frame), cross term vanishes.
$K = \frac{1}{2}Mv_{\text{cm}}^2 + \sum \frac{1}{2}m_i v_i'^2$. ∎
