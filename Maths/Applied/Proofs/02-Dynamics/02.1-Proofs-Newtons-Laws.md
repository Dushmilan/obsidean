# Proofs: Newton's Laws of Motion

**Syllabus:** GCE A/L Combined Mathematics (021) — Paper II, Topic 2.1

## Proof 1: Equivalence of $F = ma$ and $F = \frac{dp}{dt}$ for Constant Mass

**Statement:** For a body of constant mass $m$, $\sum \vec{F} = m\vec{a} \iff \sum \vec{F} = \frac{d\vec{p}}{dt}$ where $\vec{p} = m\vec{v}$.

**Proof:**
$$
\vec{p} = m\vec{v} \implies \frac{d\vec{p}}{dt} = \frac{d}{dt}(m\vec{v}) = m\frac{d\vec{v}}{dt} + \vec{v}\frac{dm}{dt}
$$
For constant mass, $\frac{dm}{dt} = 0$, so:
$$
\frac{d\vec{p}}{dt} = m\frac{d\vec{v}}{dt} = m\vec{a}
$$
Thus $\sum \vec{F} = m\vec{a} = \frac{d\vec{p}}{dt}$. ∎

## Proof 2: Newton's Third Law Implies Momentum Conservation for Isolated System

**Statement:** For an isolated system of particles interacting via Newton's 3rd law pairs, total momentum is conserved.

**Proof:**
Consider $N$ particles with masses $m_i$, velocities $\vec{v}_i$. Total momentum:
$$
\vec{P} = \sum_{i=1}^N m_i\vec{v}_i
$$
Time derivative:
$$
\frac{d\vec{P}}{dt} = \sum_{i=1}^N m_i\frac{d\vec{v}_i}{dt} = \sum_{i=1}^N \vec{F}_i
$$
where $\vec{F}_i$ is net force on particle $i$. By Newton's 3rd law, internal forces cancel in pairs:
$$
\vec{F}_i = \sum_{j \neq i} \vec{F}_{ij} + \vec{F}_{i}^{\text{ext}}
$$
where $\vec{F}_{ij} = -\vec{F}_{ji}$. Summing over all $i$:
$$
\sum_i \vec{F}_i = \sum_i \sum_{j \neq i} \vec{F}_{ij} + \sum_i \vec{F}_{i}^{\text{ext}}
$$
The double sum of internal forces vanishes:
$$
\sum_i \sum_{j \neq i} \vec{F}_{ij} = \sum_{i<j} (\vec{F}_{ij} + \vec{F}_{ji}) = \sum_{i<j} (\vec{F}_{ij} - \vec{F}_{ij}) = 0
$$
For isolated system, $\vec{F}_{i}^{\text{ext}} = 0$. Hence:
$$
\frac{d\vec{P}}{dt} = 0 \implies \vec{P} = \text{constant}
$$
∎

## Proof 3: Equations of Motion on Inclined Plane

**Statement:** For a particle of mass $m$ on a smooth plane inclined at angle $\theta$ to horizontal, the acceleration down the plane is $a = g\sin\theta$.

**Proof:**
Resolve weight $\vec{W} = m\vec{g}$ into components:
- Parallel to plane: $mg\sin\theta$ (down the plane)
- Perpendicular to plane: $mg\cos\theta$ (into plane)

Perpendicular: $N - mg\cos\theta = 0 \implies N = mg\cos\theta$ (no acceleration perpendicular)
Parallel: $mg\sin\theta = ma \implies a = g\sin\theta$. ∎

## Proof 4: Atwood Machine Acceleration and Tension

**Statement:** For masses $m_1$, $m_2$ ($m_2 > m_1$) hanging over a smooth, light pulley:
$$
a = \frac{m_2 - m_1}{m_1 + m_2}g, \quad T = \frac{2m_1m_2}{m_1 + m_2}g
$$

**Proof:**
FBDs:
- $m_1$: $T - m_1g = m_1a$ (up positive)
- $m_2$: $m_2g - T = m_2a$ (down positive)

Add equations:
$$
m_2g - m_1g = (m_1 + m_2)a \implies a = \frac{m_2 - m_1}{m_1 + m_2}g
$$

Substitute back:
$$
T = m_1(g + a) = m_1g\left(1 + \frac{m_2 - m_1}{m_1 + m_2}\right) = m_1g\left(\frac{2m_2}{m_1 + m_2}\right) = \frac{2m_1m_2}{m_1 + m_2}g
$$
∎

## Proof 5: Motion on Rough Inclined Plane

**Statement:** For a particle on a rough incline ($\theta$, coefficient $\mu$), accelerating down:
$$
a = g(\sin\theta - \mu\cos\theta)
$$

**Proof:**
FBD:
- Weight: $mg$ down
- Normal: $N = mg\cos\theta$ (perpendicular equilibrium)
- Friction: $F = \mu N = \mu mg\cos\theta$ (up the plane, opposing motion)

Parallel to plane (down positive):
$$
mg\sin\theta - \mu mg\cos\theta = ma
$$
$$
a = g(\sin\theta - \mu\cos\theta)
$$
**Condition for motion:** $a > 0 \iff \tan\theta > \mu$. ∎

## Proof 6: Apparent Weight in Accelerating Lift

**Statement:** Apparent weight (normal reaction $R$) of mass $m$ in lift accelerating vertically with acceleration $a$:
- Upward acceleration: $R = m(g + a)$
- Downward acceleration: $R = m(g - a)$
- Free fall ($a = g$): $R = 0$ (weightlessness)

**Proof:**
FBD of person in lift (upward positive):
$$
R - mg = ma \implies R = m(g + a)
$$
If lift accelerates downward with $a$ (magnitude):
$$
R - mg = -ma \implies R = m(g - a)
$$
If $a = g$ (free fall): $R = 0$. ∎

## Proof 7: Variable Mass System — Rocket Equation

**Statement:** For a rocket ejecting mass at rate $\frac{dM}{dt} = -k$ (negative) with exhaust velocity $u$ relative to rocket, in absence of external forces:
$$
M\frac{dv}{dt} = -u\frac{dM}{dt} \implies \Delta v = u\ln\left(\frac{M_0}{M}\right)
$$

**Proof:**
Consider rocket + fuel as system. At time $t$, mass $M$, velocity $v$. At $t+dt$, mass $M+dM$ (where $dM < 0$), velocity $v+dv$. Ejected mass $-dM$ has velocity $v - u$ (relative to ground).

Momentum conservation (no external forces):
$$
Mv = (M+dM)(v+dv) + (-dM)(v-u)
$$
Expand:
$$
Mv = Mv + M\,dv + v\,dM + dM\,dv - v\,dM + u\,dM
$$
Neglect $dM\,dv$ (second order):
$$
0 = M\,dv + u\,dM \implies M\frac{dv}{dt} = -u\frac{dM}{dt}
$$
Integrate from $M_0$ to $M$:
$$
\int_0^{\Delta v} dv = -u\int_{M_0}^M \frac{dM}{M} \implies \Delta v = u\ln\left(\frac{M_0}{M}\right)
$$
∎

## Proof 8: Fictitious Force in Non-Inertial Frame

**Statement:** In a frame accelerating with $\vec{a}_0$ relative to an inertial frame, a fictitious force $\vec{F}_{\text{fict}} = -m\vec{a}_0$ must be added to Newton's 2nd law.

**Proof:**
Let $\vec{r}$\vec{r}$ be position in inertial frame $S$, $\vec{r}'$ in accelerating frame $S'$.
$$
\vec{r} = \vec{r}' + \vec{r}_0(t)
$$
where $\vec{r}_0(t)$ is origin of $S'$ in $S$, with $\ddot{\vec{r}}_0 = \vec{a}_0$.

Velocity:
$$
\vec{v} = \vec{v}' + \vec{v}_0
$$
Acceleration:
$$
\vec{a} = \vec{a}' + \vec{a}_0
$$
Newton's 2nd law in $S$:
$$
\vec{F} = m\vec{a} = m(\vec{a}' + \vec{a}_0)
$$
Rearrange for equation in $S'$:
$$
m\vec{a}' = \vec{F} - m\vec{a}_0
$$
Define fictitious force $\vec{F}_{\text{fict}} = -m\vec{a}_0$. Then:
$$
m\vec{a}' = \vec{F} + \vec{F}_{\text{fict}}
$$
∎

## Proof 9: Connected Particles on Double Incline

**Statement:** For masses $m_1$, $m_2$ on smooth planes $\theta_1$, $\theta_2$ connected over pulley:
$$
a = \frac{m_2\sin\theta_2 - m_1\sin\theta_1}{m_1 + m_2}g
$$

**Proof:**
Equations:
- $m_1$: $T - m_1g\sin\theta_1 = m_1a$
- $m_2$: $m_2g\sin\theta_2 - T = m_2a$

Add:
$$
g(m_2\sin\theta_2 - m_1\sin\theta_1) = (m_1 + m_2)a
$$
$$
a = \frac{m_2\sin\theta_2 - m_1\sin\theta_1}{m_1 + m_2}g
$$
∎

## Proof 10: Constraint Equation for Inverting Pulley

**Statement:** For a particle on wedge (angle $\theta$) connected to fixed point via string over pulley on wedge top, if wedge moves horizontally by $x$, particle displacement along plane $y$ satisfies:
$$
a_{\text{wedge}} + a_{\text{particle}}\cos\theta = 0
$$

**Proof:**
String length $L = x + y + \text{constants}$.
Differentiate twice:
$$
0 = \ddot{x} + \ddot{y}\cos\theta \implies a_w + a_p\cos\theta = 0
$$
(The $\cos\theta$ comes from horizontal component of particle's displacement along string.) ∎
