# Proofs: Impulse and Momentum

**Syllabus:** GCE A/L Combined Mathematics (021) — Paper II, Topic 2.4

---

## Proof 1: Impulse-Momentum Theorem (from Newton's 2nd Law)

**Statement:** $\vec{I} = \int \vec{F}\,dt = \Delta \vec{p} = \vec{p}_f - \vec{p}_i$

**Proof:**
Newton's 2nd law: $\vec{F} = \frac{d\vec{p}}{dt}$.

Integrate from $t_1$ to $t_2$:
$$
\int_{t_1}^{t_2} \vec{F}\,dt = \int_{t_1}^{t_2} \frac{d\vec{p}}{dt}\,dt = \int_{\vec{p}_i}^{\vec{p}_f} d\vec{p} = \vec{p}_f - \vec{p}_i
$$
Define impulse $\vec{I} = \int \vec{F}\,dt$. Then $\vec{I} = \Delta \vec{p}$. ∎

---

## Proof 2: Impulse for Constant Force

**Statement:** If $\vec{F}$ is constant, $\vec{I} = \vec{F}\Delta t = \Delta \vec{p}$.

**Proof:**
$\vec{I} = \int_{t_1}^{t_2} \vec{F}\,dt = \vec{F}\int_{t_1}^{t_2} dt = \vec{F}(t_2 - t_1) = \vec{F}\Delta t$.

By impulse-momentum theorem: $\vec{F}\Delta t = \Delta \vec{p}$. ∎

---

## Proof 3: Average Force

**Statement:** $\vec{F}_{\text{avg}} = \frac{\vec{I}}{\Delta t} = \frac{\Delta \vec{p}}{\Delta t}$

**Proof:**
By definition of average value of a function:
$$
\vec{F}_{\text{avg}} = \frac{1}{\Delta t}\int_{t_1}^{t_2} \vec{F}\,dt = \frac{\vec{I}}{\Delta t}
$$
Using impulse-momentum: $\vec{F}_{\text{avg}} = \frac{\Delta \vec{p}}{\Delta t}$. ∎

---

## Proof 4: Conservation of Momentum (No External Impulse)

**Statement:** If $\vec{I}_{\text{ext}} = 0$, then $\vec{p}_{\text{total}} = \text{constant}$.

**Proof:**
For a system of particles, $\frac{d\vec{P}_{\text{total}}}{dt} = \vec{F}_{\text{ext}}$.
Integrate: $\Delta \vec{P}_{\text{total}} = \vec{I}_{\text{ext}}$.
If $\vec{I}_{\text{ext}} = 0$, then $\Delta \vec{P}_{\text{total}} = 0 \implies \vec{P}_{\text{total}} = \text{constant}$. ∎

## Proof 5: Coefficient of Restitution from Impulse

**Statement:** For direct collision of masses $m_1$, $m_2$:
$$
e = \frac{v_2 - v_1}{u_1 - u_2} = \frac{\text{speed of separation}}{\text{speed of approach}}
$$

**Proof:**
Impulse on $m_1$: $I = m_1(v_1 - u_1)$ (positive if $v_1 > u_1$)
Impulse on $m_2$: $I = m_2(v_2 - u_2)$ (same magnitude, opposite direction on $m_2$)

From Newton's experimental law: $v_2 - v_1 = e(u_1 - u_2)$.

Also $m_1(v_1 - u_1) = -m_2(v_2 - u_2) = I$.

Solve for $v_1, v_2$:
$$
v_1 = \frac{m_1u_1 + m_2u_2 + m_2e(u_2 - u_1)}{m_1 + m_2}
$$
$$
v_2 = \frac{m_1u_1 + m_2u_2 + m_1e(u_1 - u_2)}{m_1 + m_2}
$$

Then speed of separation:
$$
v_2 - v_1 = \frac{m_1e(u_1-u_2) + m_2e(u_2-u_1)}{m_1+m_2} = e(u_1 - u_2)
$$
Thus $e = \frac{v_2 - v_1}{u_1 - u_2}$. ∎

## Proof 6: Velocity After Direct Collision

**Statement:**
$$
v_1 = \frac{m_1 - em_2}{m_1 + m_2}u_1 + \frac{m_2(1+e)}{m_1 + m_2}u_2
$$
$$
v_2 = \frac{m_1(1+e)}{m_1 + m_2}u_1 + \frac{m_2 - em_1}{m_1 + m_2}u_2
$$

**Proof:**
From momentum: $m_1u_1 + m_2u_2 = m_1v_1 + m_2v_2$  (1)
From restitution: $v_2 - v_1 = e(u_1 - u_2)$  (2)

From (2): $v_2 = v_1 + e(u_1 - u_2)$.

Substitute into (1):
$$
m_1u_1 + m_2u_2 = m_1v_1 + m_2[v_1 + e(u_1 - u_2)]
= (m_1 + m_2)v_1 + m_2e(u_1 - u_2)
$$
$$
(m_1 + m_2)v_1 = m_1u_1 + m_2u_2 - m_2e(u_1 - u_2)
= (m_1 - em_2)u_1 + m_2(1+e)u_2
$$
$$
v_1 = \frac{m_1 - em_2}{m_1 + m_2}u_1 + \frac{m_2(1+e)}{m_1 + m_2}u_2
$$

Then $v_2 = v_1 + e(u_1 - u_2)$ gives the second formula. ∎

## Proof 7: Kinetic Energy Loss in Direct Collision

**Statement:**
$$
\Delta K = -\frac{1}{2}\frac{m_1m_2}{m_1+m_2}(1-e^2)(u_1 - u_2)^2 \leq 0
$$

**Proof:**
Initial KE: $K_i = \frac{1}{2}m_1u_1^2 + \frac{1}{2}m_2u_2^2$
Final KE: $K_f = \frac{1}{2}m_1v_1^2 + \frac{1}{2}m_2v_2^2$

Using $v_1, v_2$ from Proof 6, compute $K_f - K_i$.

Alternative: Use reduced mass $\mu = \frac{m_1m_2}{m_1+m_2}$ and relative velocity.

Let $u = u_1 - u_2$, $v = v_2 - v_1 = eu$.

Centre of mass velocity: $V = \frac{m_1u_1 + m_2u_2}{m_1+m_2}$ (unchanged).

$K_i = \frac{1}{2}(m_1+m_2)V^2 + \frac{1}{2}\mu u^2$
$K_f = \frac{1}{2}(m_1+m_2)V^2 + \frac{1}{2}\mu (eu)^2$

$\Delta K = K_f - K_i = \frac{1}{2}\mu(e^2 - 1)u^2 = -\frac{1}{2}\frac{m_1m_2}{m_1+m_2}(1-e^2)(u_1-u_2)^2$

Since $e \leq 1$, $\Delta K \leq 0$. Maximum loss when $e=0$. ∎

## Proof 8: Impulse in Direct Collision

**Statement:** $I = \frac{m_1m_2(1+e)}{m_1+m_2}(u_1 - u_2)$

**Proof:**
Impulse on $m_1$: $I = m_1(v_1 - u_1)$.

Substitute $v_1$:
$$
I = m_1\left[\frac{m_1 - em_2}{m_1+m_2}u_1 + \frac{m_2(1+e)}{m_1+m_2}u_2 - u_1\right]
= m_1\left[\frac{m_1 - em_2 - m_1 - m_2}{m_1+m_2}u_1 + \frac{m_2(1+e)}{m_1+m_2}u_2\right]
= \frac{m_1m_2(1+e)}{m_1+m_2}(u_2 - u_1)
$$
Magnitude: $|I| = \frac{m_1m_2(1+e)}{m_1+m_2}|u_1 - u_2|$. ∎

## Proof 9: Oblique Collision — Tangential Velocity Unchanged

**Statement:** For smooth spheres, $v_{1t} = u_{1t}$, $v_{2t} = u_{2t}$.

**Proof:**
No friction $\implies$ no tangential impulse.
Impulse is purely along line of centres (normal direction).
$\Delta p_t = I_t = 0 \implies m_1(v_{1t} - u_{1t}) = 0 \implies v_{1t} = u_{1t}$.
Similarly $v_{2t} = u_{2t}$. ∎

---

## Proof 10: Oblique Collision — Normal Velocity Equations

**Statement:** For oblique collision with smooth spheres:
$$
v_{1n} = \frac{m_1 - em_2}{m_1+m_2}u_{1n} + \frac{m_2(1+e)}{m_1+m_2}u_{2n}
$$
$$
v_{2n} = \frac{m_1(1+e)}{m_1+m_2}u_{1n} + \frac{m_2 - em_1}{m_1+m_2}u_{2n}
$$
where $n$ = normal (line of centres) components.

**Proof:**
Momentum and restitution apply only to normal components (tangential unchanged).
The normal components satisfy exactly the same equations as direct collision.
Thus the direct collision formulas apply directly to $u_{1n}, u_{2n}, v_{1n}, v_{2n}$. ∎

## Proof 11: Collision with Fixed Plane

**Statement:** For smooth plane, $v_n = -eu_n$, $v_t = u_t$.

**Proof:**
Plane has infinite mass, so $V = 0$ before and after.
Restitution: $v_n - V = e(U - u_n) \implies v_n = -eu_n$ (since $V=U=0$).
No friction: $v_t = u_t$.

Rebound angle $\theta'$:
$\tan\theta' = \frac{v_t}{|v_n|} = \frac{u_t}{eu_n} = \frac{1}{e}\tan\theta_i$. ∎

## Proof 12: Ballistic Pendulum

**Statement:** $v_{\text{bullet}} = \frac{M+m}{m}\sqrt{2gh}$

**Proof:**
Stage 1: Inelastic collision. Momentum conserved:
$mv = (M+m)V$

Stage 2: Swing to height $h$. Energy conserved:
$\frac{1}{2}(M+m)V^2 = (M+m)gh \implies V = \sqrt{2gh}$

Substitute: $mv = (M+m)\sqrt{2gh} \implies v = \frac{M+m}{m}\sqrt{2gh}$. ∎

## Proof 13: Bouncing Ball — Total Time and Distance

**Statement:** For ball dropped from height $H$, restitution $e < 1$:
Total time: $T = \frac{2u}{g}\frac{1}{1-e}$ where $u = \sqrt{2gH}$
Total distance: $S = H\frac{1+e^2}{1-e^2}$

**Proof:**
First fall time: $t_1 = \sqrt{2H/g} = u/g$.

After 1st bounce: $v_1 = eu$. Time to max height: $t_2 = v_1/g = eu/g$.
Time for round trip (up+down): $2eu/g$.

After 2nd bounce: $v_2 = e^2u$. Round trip time: $2e^2u/g$.

Total time:
$$
T = \frac{u}{g} + \frac{2eu}{g} + \frac{2e^2u}{g} + \dots
= \frac{u}{g}\left(1 + 2\sum_{n=1}^\infty e^n\right)
= \frac{u}{g}\left(1 + \frac{2e}{1-e}\right)
= \frac{u}{g}\frac{1+e}{1-e}
= \frac{2u}{g}\frac{1}{1-e} \quad (\text{since } u = \sqrt{2gH})
$$

Distances:
First fall: $H$.
1st bounce height: $h_1 = \frac{(eu)^2}{2g} = e^2H$.
Each round trip: $2e^2H, 2e^4H, \dots$

Total distance:
$$
S = H + 2e^2H + 2e^4H + \dots = H\left(1 + 2\sum_{n=1}^\infty e^{2n}\right)
= H\left(1 + \frac{2e^2}{1-e^2}\right) = H\frac{1+e^2}{1-e^2}
$$
∎

## Proof 14: Impulse on Wall from Ball Collision

**Statement:** $I = m(1+e)u\cos\theta$ for ball mass $m$, speed $u$, angle $\theta$ to normal.

**Proof:**
Normal component: $u_n = u\cos\theta$.
Wall is fixed $\implies$ momentum change normal: $\Delta p_n = m(v_n - u_n) = m(-eu_n - u_n) = -m(1+e)u_n$.
Impulse on ball: $I = -m(1+e)u\cos\theta$ (direction away from wall).
Impulse on wall: equal and opposite: $m(1+e)u\cos\theta$. ∎

## Proof 15: Explosion (Superelastic Collision)

**Statement:** For body mass $M$ at rest exploding into $m_1, m_2$ with energy release $Q$:
$$
\frac{1}{2}m_1v_1^2 + \frac{1}{2}m_2v_2^2 = Q
$$

**Proof:**
Initial momentum = 0 $\implies m_1v_1 + m_2v_2 = 0 \implies v_2 = -\frac{m_1}{m_2}v_1$.

Final KE: $\frac{1}{2}m_1v_1^2 + \frac{1}{2}m_2v_2^2 = \frac{1}{2}m_1v_1^2 + \frac{1}{2}m_2\frac{m_1^2}{m_2^2}v_1^2 = \frac{1}{2}m_1v_1^2\left(1 + \frac{m_1}{m_2}\right) = \frac{1}{2}\frac{m_1m_2}{m_1+m_2}(v_1 - v_2)^2$.

By energy conservation: Initial KE + $Q$ = Final KE. Initial KE = 0.
So $Q = \frac{1}{2}\frac{m_1m_2}{m_1+m_2}(v_1 - v_2)^2$. ∎

## Proof 16: Impulse from Force-Time Graph

**Statement:** $I = \text{area under } F\text{-}t \text{ graph}$.

**Proof:**
$I = \int F\,dt$. By definition of definite integral, this is the signed area between the graph and the time axis. ∎
