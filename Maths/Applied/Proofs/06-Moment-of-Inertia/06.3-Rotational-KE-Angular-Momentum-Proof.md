# 6.3 Rotational Kinetic Energy & Angular Momentum: Proofs

**Syllabus Reference:** GCE A/L Combined Mathematics (021) - Applied Mathematics Paper II, Topic 6.3

---

## Proof 1: Rotational Kinetic Energy Formula

### 1.1 For a Rigid Body Rotating About a Fixed Axis

Consider a rigid body composed of particles of mass $m_i$ at distances $r_i$ from the axis, rotating with angular velocity $\omega$.

Speed of particle $i$: $v_i = \omega r_i$

Kinetic energy of particle $i$:
$$K_i = \frac{1}{2} m_i v_i^2 = \frac{1}{2} m_i \omega^2 r_i^2$$

Total kinetic energy:
$$K = \sum_i K_i = \sum_i \frac{1}{2} m_i \omega^2 r_i^2 = \frac{1}{2} \omega^2 \sum_i m_i r_i^2 = \frac{1}{2} I \omega^2$$

where $I = \sum m_i r_i^2$ is the moment of inertia about the axis.

For continuous body:
$$K = \int \frac{1}{2} \omega^2 r^2 dm = \frac{1}{2} I \omega^2$$

---

### 1.2 For General Motion (Translation + Rotation)

Let $\mathbf{v}_{CM}$ be the velocity of the centre of mass, and $\boldsymbol{\omega}$ the angular velocity about CM.

Velocity of a particle: $\mathbf{v} = \mathbf{v}_{CM} + \boldsymbol{\omega} \times \mathbf{r}'$ where $\mathbf{r}'$ is position relative to CM.

Kinetic energy:
$$K = \frac{1}{2} \int v^2 dm = \frac{1}{2} \int (\mathbf{v}_{CM} + \boldsymbol{\omega} \times \mathbf{r}')^2 dm$$

$$= \frac{1}{2} \int v_{CM}^2 dm + \int \mathbf{v}_{CM} \cdot (\boldsymbol{\omega} \times \mathbf{r}') dm + \frac{1}{2} \int (\boldsymbol{\omega} \times \mathbf{r}')^2 dm$$

First term: $\frac{1}{2} M v_{CM}^2$

Second term: $\mathbf{v}_{CM} \cdot (\boldsymbol{\omega} \times \int \mathbf{r}' dm) = 0$ since $\int \mathbf{r}' dm = 0$ (by definition of CM)

Third term: $\frac{1}{2} \int (\boldsymbol{\omega} \times \mathbf{r}')^2 dm = \frac{1}{2} I_{CM} \omega^2$

Therefore:
$$K = \frac{1}{2} M v_{CM}^2 + \frac{1}{2} I_{CM} \omega^2$$

## Proof 2: Angular Momentum

### 2.1 Angular Momentum of a Particle

For a particle of mass $m$ at position $\mathbf{r}$ with velocity $\mathbf{v}$:
$$\mathbf{L} = \mathbf{r} \times \mathbf{p} = m (\mathbf{r} \times \mathbf{v})$$

### 2.2 Angular Momentum of a Rigid Body About a Fixed Axis

For a body rotating about the $z$-axis with angular velocity $\omega$:
$$\mathbf{L} = \sum_i \mathbf{r}_i \times (m_i \mathbf{v}_i) = \sum_i m_i \mathbf{r}_i \times (\boldsymbol{\omega} \times \mathbf{r}_i)$$

Using vector identity $\mathbf{a} \times (\mathbf{b} \times \mathbf{a}) = a^2 \mathbf{b} - (\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b})\mathbf{a}$:
$$\mathbf{L} = \sum_i m_i (r_i^2 \boldsymbol{\omega} - (\mathbf{r}_i \cdot \boldsymbol{\omega})\mathbf{r}_i)$$

For rotation about $z$-axis: $\boldsymbol{\omega} = \omega \hat{z}$, $\mathbf{r}_i = (x_i, y_i, z_i)$:
$$\mathbf{r}_i \cdot \boldsymbol{\omega} = \omega z_i$$

$z$-component of $\mathbf{L}$:
$$L_z = \sum_i m_i (x_i^2 + y_i^2 + z_i^2) \omega - \sum_i m_i \omega z_i^2 = \omega \sum_i m_i (x_i^2 + y_i^2) = I \omega$$

$x$ and $y$ components:
$$L_x = -\omega \sum m_i x_i z_i, \quad L_y = -\omega \sum m_i y_i z_i$$

These are the **products of inertia**. For a symmetric body about the axis, they vanish, and $\mathbf{L} = I \omega \hat{z}$.

---

### 2.3 Angular Momentum About Centre of Mass

For general motion:
$$\mathbf{L} = \int \mathbf{r} \times \mathbf{v} dm$$

Write $\mathbf{r} = \mathbf{R}_{CM} + \mathbf{r}'$, $\mathbf{v} = \mathbf{v}_{CM} + \mathbf{v}'$:

$$\mathbf{L} = \int (\mathbf{R}_{CM} + \mathbf{r}') \times (\mathbf{v}_{CM} + \mathbf{v}') dm$$

$$= \mathbf{R}_{CM} \times \int \mathbf{v}_{CM} dm + \mathbf{R}_{CM} \times \int \mathbf{v}' dm + \int \mathbf{r}' \times \mathbf{v}_{CM} dm + \int \mathbf{r}' \times \mathbf{v}' dm$$

- $\int \mathbf{v}_{CM} dm = M \mathbf{v}_{CM}$
- $\int \mathbf{v}' dm = 0$ (by definition of CM)
- $\int \mathbf{r}' dm = 0$ (by definition of CM)
- $\int \mathbf{r}' \times \mathbf{v}' dm = \mathbf{L}_{CM}$ (angular momentum about CM)

$$\mathbf{L} = \mathbf{R}_{CM} \times M \mathbf{v}_{CM} + \mathbf{L}_{CM}$$

## Proof 3: Torque and Angular Momentum

### 3.1 Torque on a Particle
$$\boldsymbol{\tau} = \mathbf{r} \times \mathbf{F}$$

### 3.2 Angular Momentum Principle

For a system of particles:
$$\frac{d\mathbf{L}}{dt} = \sum_i \frac{d}{dt}(\mathbf{r}_i \times \mathbf{p}_i) = \sum_i (\dot{\mathbf{r}}_i \times \mathbf{p}_i + \mathbf{r}_i \times \dot{\mathbf{p}}_i)$$

Since $\dot{\mathbf{r}}_i = \mathbf{v}_i$ and $\mathbf{p}_i = m_i \mathbf{v}_i$, the first term is zero ($\mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{v} = 0$).

Second term: $\mathbf{r}_i \times \mathbf{F}_i = \boldsymbol{\tau}_i$

$$\frac{d\mathbf{L}}{dt} = \sum_i \boldsymbol{\tau}_i = \boldsymbol{\tau}_{ext}$$

Internal torques cancel by Newton's third law (equal and opposite forces give equal and opposite torques about any point).

---

### 3.3 Torque-Angular Acceleration Relation for Fixed Axis

For a rigid body rotating about a fixed axis:
$$\tau = I \alpha$$

From angular momentum: $\tau = \frac{dL}{dt} = \frac{d}{dt}(I \omega) = I \alpha$ (since $I$ is constant for fixed axis).

---

## Proof 4: Work-Energy Theorem for Rotation

Work done by a constant torque $\tau$ through angular displacement $\theta$:
$$W = \tau \theta$$

Work-energy theorem:
$$W_{net} = \Delta K_{rot} = \frac{1}{2} I \omega_f^2 - \frac{1}{2} I \omega_i^2$$

For variable torque: $W = \int \tau d\theta$

---

## Proof 5: Power in Rotational Motion

Instantaneous power:
$$P = \frac{dW}{dt} = \tau \frac{d\theta}{dt} = \tau \omega$$

---

## Proof 6: Rolling Motion (Without Slipping)

### 6.1 Kinematic Constraint
$$v_{CM} = \omega r, \quad a_{CM} = \alpha r$$

### 6.2 Total Kinetic Energy
$$K = \frac{1}{2} M v_{CM}^2 + \frac{1}{2} I_{CM} \omega^2 = \frac{1}{2} M v_{CM}^2 \left(1 + \frac{k^2}{r^2}\right)$$

where $k$ is the radius of gyration about CM.

### 6.3 Rolling Down an Inclined Plane

For a body rolling down an incline of angle $\theta$ without slipping:

Forces along plane: $Mg\sin\theta - f = M a$
Torque about CM: $f r = I_{CM} \alpha = I_{CM} \frac{a}{r}$

Solving:
$$a = \frac{g\sin\theta}{1 + I_{CM}/Mr^2} = \frac{g\sin\theta}{1 + k^2/r^2}$$

$$f = \frac{Mg\sin\theta \cdot k^2/r^2}{1 + k^2/r^2}$$

**Condition for no slipping:** $f \leq \mu_s N = \mu_s Mg\cos\theta$

$$\mu_s \geq \frac{\tan\theta}{1 + r^2/k^2}$$

### 6.4 Energy Method (Alternative)

Loss in PE = Gain in KE:
$$Mgh = \frac{1}{2} M v^2 \left(1 + \frac{k^2}{r^2}\right)$$

$$v^2 = \frac{2gh}{1 + k^2/r^2}$$

Since $v^2 = 2as$ and $h = s\sin\theta$:
$$a = \frac{g\sin\theta}{1 + k^2/r^2}$$

---

## Proof 7: Equations of Motion for Rolling (General)

For a rigid body with forces and torques:

1. **Translation of CM:** $\sum \mathbf{F} = M \mathbf{a}_{CM}$
2. **Rotation about CM:** $\sum \boldsymbol{\tau}_{CM} = I_{CM} \boldsymbol{\alpha}$

The instantaneous axis of rotation (IAR) is the point with zero velocity. For rolling without slipping, IAR is the contact point.

---

## Proof 8: Instantaneous Axis of Rotation Method

For pure rolling, contact point $P$ has zero velocity. Body rotates instantaneously about $P$.

Moment of inertia about $P$ (parallel axes):
$$I_P = I_{CM} + M r^2 = M(k^2 + r^2)$$

Angular momentum about $P$:
$$L_P = I_P \omega = M(k^2 + r^2) \frac{v}{r}$$

Rate of change of $L_P$ = torque about $P$.

For rolling down incline:
Torque about $P$ = $Mg r \sin\theta$

$$M(k^2 + r^2) \frac{a}{r} = Mg r \sin\theta$$

$$a = \frac{g r^2 \sin\theta}{k^2 + r^2} = \frac{g\sin\theta}{1 + k^2/r^2}$$

**Same result, often simpler!**

---

## Proof 9: Work Done by Friction in Pure Rolling

**Zero!** The point of contact is instantaneously at rest, so friction does no work. This is why energy conservation works perfectly for pure rolling problems.

---

## Proof 10: Rolling with an External Force

### Force Applied at Centre of Mass (Horizontal Surface)
$$F - f = Ma, \quad f r = I_{CM} \frac{a}{r}$$

$$a = \frac{F}{M(1 + k^2/r^2)}, \quad f = F \frac{k^2/r^2}{1 + k^2/r^2}$$

### Force Applied at Top of Wheel (Radius $r$)
Translation: $F + f = Ma$
Rotation: $F r - f r = I_{CM} \frac{a}{r} = M k^2 \frac{a}{r}$

$$a = \frac{2F}{M(1 + k^2/r^2)}$$
