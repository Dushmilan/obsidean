## Proof 1: Parallel Axes Theorem (Steiner's Theorem)

### Statement
The moment of inertia $I$ of a body about any axis is equal to its moment of inertia $I_{CM}$ about a parallel axis through the centre of mass plus the product of the total mass $M$ and the square of the perpendicular distance $d$ between the axes:

$$I = I_{CM} + Md^2$$

### Proof

Let:
- Axis 1 pass through the centre of mass $C$
- Axis 2 be parallel to axis 1 at perpendicular distance $d$
- $\mathbf{r}$ be the position vector of a mass element $dm$ relative to $C$
- $\mathbf{d}$ be the vector from axis 1 to axis 2 (perpendicular to both axes)

The distance of $dm$ from axis 1 is $|\mathbf{r}_\perp|$ (perpendicular component of $\mathbf{r}$).
The distance of $dm$ from axis 2 is $|(\mathbf{r} + \mathbf{d})_\perp|$.

Since $\mathbf{d}$ is perpendicular to the axes, the perpendicular component is:
$$|(\mathbf{r} + \mathbf{d})_\perp|^2 = |\mathbf{r}_\perp + \mathbf{d}|^2 = r_\perp^2 + d^2 + 2\mathbf{r}_\perp \cdot \mathbf{d}$$

Now:
$$I = \int |(\mathbf{r} + \mathbf{d})_\perp|^2 dm = \int (r_\perp^2 + d^2 + 2\mathbf{r}_\perp \cdot \mathbf{d}) dm$$

$$I = \int r_\perp^2 dm + d^2 \int dm + 2\mathbf{d} \cdot \int \mathbf{r}_\perp dm$$

The first term is $I_{CM}$.
The second term is $d^2 M = Md^2$.
The third term: $\int \mathbf{r}_\perp dm = 0$ because the integral of position vector over the whole body relative to the centre of mass is zero (definition of centre of mass).

Therefore:
$$I = I_{CM} + Md^2$$

## Proof 2: Perpendicular Axes Theorem

### Statement
For a planar lamina (thin flat body) lying in the $xy$-plane, the moment of inertia about the $z$-axis (perpendicular to the plane) equals the sum of the moments of inertia about the $x$ and $y$ axes (both in the plane):

$$I_z = I_x + I_y$$

### Proof

Consider a mass element $dm$ at coordinates $(x, y)$ in the lamina.

- Distance from $x$-axis: $|y|$
- Distance from $y$-axis: $|x|$
- Distance from $z$-axis: $\sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$

By definition:
$$I_x = \int y^2 dm$$
$$I_y = \int x^2 dm$$
$$I_z = \int (x^2 + y^2) dm = \int x^2 dm + \int y^2 dm = I_x + I_y$$

**Note:** This theorem applies **only to planar laminae** (2D bodies). For 3D bodies, the analogous relation involves products of inertia.

## Proof 3: Minimum Moment of Inertia Through Centre of Mass

### Statement
The moment of inertia of a body about an axis is minimum when the axis passes through the centre of mass (for a given direction).

### Proof

From parallel axes theorem: $I = I_{CM} + Md^2$

Since $Md^2 \geq 0$ for all $d$, with equality only when $d = 0$, it follows that:
$$I \geq I_{CM}$$

Thus $I_{CM}$ is the minimum moment of inertia for axes of that direction.

## Proof 4: Rotational Kinetic Energy Formula

### Statement
The rotational kinetic energy of a rigid body rotating with angular velocity $\omega$ about a fixed axis is $K = \frac{1}{2} I \omega^2$.

### Proof

Consider a rigid body rotating about a fixed axis. A mass element $dm$ at perpendicular distance $r$ from the axis has speed $v = \omega r$.

Its kinetic energy:
$$dK = \frac{1}{2} dm \cdot v^2 = \frac{1}{2} dm \cdot \omega^2 r^2$$

Total kinetic energy:
$$K = \int dK = \frac{1}{2} \omega^2 \int r^2 dm = \frac{1}{2} I \omega^2$$

## Proof 5: Angular Momentum for Fixed Axis Rotation

### Statement
For a rigid body rotating about a fixed axis with angular velocity $\omega$, the angular momentum about that axis is $L = I \omega$.

### Proof

Angular momentum of mass element $dm$ about the axis:
$$dL = r \times dm \mathbf{v} = r \times dm (\boldsymbol{\omega} \times \mathbf{r})$$

For fixed axis rotation, $\boldsymbol{\omega}$ is along the axis. Taking component along the axis:
$$dL_{axis} = dm \cdot r^2 \omega$$

Integrating:
$$L_{axis} = \omega \int r^2 dm = I \omega$$

## Proof 6: Torque-Angular Acceleration Relation

### Statement
For a rigid body rotating about a fixed axis: $\tau = I \alpha$

### Proof

From Newton's second law for a particle: $d\mathbf{F} = dm \mathbf{a}$

Torque of $d\mathbf{F}$ about the axis:
$$d\tau = \mathbf{r} \times d\mathbf{F} = \mathbf{r} \times dm \mathbf{a}$$

For fixed axis rotation: $\mathbf{a} = \boldsymbol{\alpha} \times \mathbf{r} + \boldsymbol{\omega} \times (\boldsymbol{\omega} \times \mathbf{r})$

The centripetal term $\boldsymbol{\omega} \times (\boldsymbol{\omega} \times \mathbf{r})$ is parallel to $\mathbf{r}$, so its cross product with $\mathbf{r}$ is zero.

Thus:
$$d\tau_{axis} = dm \cdot r^2 \alpha$$

Integrating:
$$\tau_{axis} = \alpha \int r^2 dm = I \alpha$$
