# 6.5 Rolling Motion: Proofs

**Syllabus Reference:** GCE A/L Combined Mathematics (021) - Applied Mathematics Paper II, Topic 6.5

---

## Proof 1: Kinematic Condition for Rolling Without Slipping

Consider a rigid body of radius $r$ rolling on a horizontal surface. Let the centre of mass move with velocity $v_{CM}$ and the body rotate with angular velocity $\omega$ about the CM.

The velocity of the contact point $P$ relative to the ground is:
$$\mathbf{v}_P = \mathbf{v}_{CM} + \boldsymbol{\omega} \times \mathbf{r}_{P/CM}$$

For a wheel rolling to the right: $\mathbf{v}_{CM} = v \hat{\mathbf{i}}$, $\boldsymbol{\omega} = -\omega \hat{\mathbf{k}}$ (clockwise)
$$\mathbf{r}_{P/CM} = -r \hat{\mathbf{j}}$$

$$\mathbf{v}_P = v \hat{\mathbf{i}} + (-\omega \hat{\mathbf{k}}) \times (-r \hat{\mathbf{j}}) = v \hat{\mathbf{i}} + \omega r (-\hat{\mathbf{i}}) = (v - \omega r) \hat{\mathbf{i}}$$

**No-slip condition:** $\mathbf{v}_P = 0 \Rightarrow v = \omega r$

Similarly for acceleration:
$$\mathbf{a}_P = \mathbf{a}_{CM} + \boldsymbol{\alpha} \times \mathbf{r}_{P/CM} + \boldsymbol{\omega} \times (\boldsymbol{\omega} \times \mathbf{r}_{P/CM})$$

For the tangential component: $a_P = a_{CM} - \alpha r = 0 \Rightarrow a = \alpha r$

## Proof 2: Total Kinetic Energy of a Rolling Body

For a rigid body in general motion:
$$K = K_{trans} + K_{rot} = \frac{1}{2} M v_{CM}^2 + \frac{1}{2} I_{CM} \omega^2$$

For pure rolling: $\omega = v/r$ and $I_{CM} = M k^2$ (where $k$ is radius of gyration about CM).

$$K = \frac{1}{2} M v^2 + \frac{1}{2} M k^2 \frac{v^2}{r^2} = \frac{1}{2} M v^2 \left( 1 + \frac{k^2}{r^2} \right)$$

**Note:** Friction does no work in pure rolling since the contact point is instantaneously at rest.

## Proof 3: Rolling Down an Inclined Plane (Force/Torque Method)

Body of mass $M$, radius $r$, radius of gyration $k$ rolls down incline of angle $\theta$.

### Forces:
- Weight component along plane: $Mg\sin\theta$ (down)
- Friction $f$ (up the plane)
- Normal reaction $N = Mg\cos\theta$

**Translation along plane:**
$$Mg\sin\theta - f = Ma \quad \text{(1)}$$

**Rotation about CM (torque = $fr$):**
$$f r = I_{CM} \alpha = M k^2 \alpha \quad \text{(2)}$$

**Rolling condition:**
$$a = \alpha r \quad \text{(3)}$$

### Solving:
From (2) and (3): $f r = M k^2 \frac{a}{r} \Rightarrow f = \frac{M k^2}{r^2} a$

Substitute into (1):
$$Mg\sin\theta - \frac{M k^2}{r^2} a = Ma$$

$$a \left( 1 + \frac{k^2}{r^2} \right) = g\sin\theta$$

$$\boxed{a = \frac{g\sin\theta}{1 + \frac{k^2}{r^2}}}$$

Friction force:
$$f = Mg\sin\theta \cdot \frac{k^2/r^2}{1 + k^2/r^2}$$

### No-Slip Condition:
Static friction must satisfy $f \leq \mu_s N$:
$$\mu_s Mg\cos\theta \geq Mg\sin\theta \cdot \frac{k^2/r^2}{1 + k^2/r^2}$$

$$\mu_s \geq \frac{\tan\theta}{1 + \frac{r^2}{k^2}}$$

## Proof 4: Rolling Down an Incline (Energy Method)

Body rolls from rest down incline of height $h$ (or distance $s$ along plane).

Loss in PE: $Mgh = Mgs\sin\theta$

Gain in KE: $\frac{1}{2} M v^2 \left( 1 + \frac{k^2}{r^2} \right)$

$$Mgs\sin\theta = \frac{1}{2} M v^2 \left( 1 + \frac{k^2}{r^2} \right)$$

$$v^2 = \frac{2gs\sin\theta}{1 + k^2/r^2}$$

Using $v^2 = 2as$: $a = \frac{g\sin\theta}{1 + k^2/r^2}$

**Same result, avoids dealing with friction!**

---

## Proof 5: Acceleration for Standard Bodies

| Body | $k^2/r^2$ | $a$ (incline) | $v^2$ after distance $s$ |
|------|-----------|---------------|--------------------------|
| Ring / Hollow cylinder | 1 | $\frac{1}{2}g\sin\theta$ | $g s \sin\theta$ |
| Disc / Solid cylinder | $\frac{1}{2}$ | $\frac{2}{3}g\sin\theta$ | $\frac{4}{3}g s \sin\theta$ |
| Solid sphere | $\frac{2}{5}$ | $\frac{5}{7}g\sin\theta$ | $\frac{10}{7}g s \sin\theta$ |
| Hollow sphere | $\frac{2}{3}$ | $\frac{3}{5}g\sin\theta$ | $\frac{6}{5}g s \sin\theta$ |

**Order of arrival (starting from rest):** Solid sphere > Disc > Hollow sphere > Ring

## Proof 6: Instantaneous Axis of Rotation Method

For pure rolling, contact point $P$ has zero velocity. Body rotates instantaneously about $P$.

Moment of inertia about $P$ (parallel axes):
$$I_P = I_{CM} + M r^2 = M(k^2 + r^2)$$

Angular momentum about $P$:
$$L_P = I_P \omega = M(k^2 + r^2) \frac{v}{r}$$

Rate of change of $L_P$ = torque about $P$.

For rolling down incline:
Torque about $P$ = $Mg r \sin\theta$

$$M(k^2 + r^2) \frac{a}{r} = Mg r \sin\theta$$

$$a = \frac{g r^2 \sin\theta}{k^2 + r^2} = \frac{g\sin\theta}{1 + k^2/r^2}$$

**Same result, often simpler!**

---

## Proof 7: Work Done by Friction in Pure Rolling

**Zero!** The point of contact is instantaneously at rest, so friction does no work. This is why energy conservation works perfectly for pure rolling problems.

---


### 8.1 Force Applied at Centre of Mass (Horizontal Surface)
$$F - f = Ma, \quad f r = I_{CM} \frac{a}{r}$$

$$a = \frac{F}{M(1 + k^2/r^2)}, \quad f = F \frac{k^2/r^2}{1 + k^2/r^2}$$

### 8.2 Force Applied at Top of Wheel (Radius $r$)
Translation: $F + f = Ma$
Rotation: $F r - f r = I_{CM} \frac{a}{r} = M k^2 \frac{a}{r}$

$$a = \frac{2F}{M(1 + k^2/r^2)}$$

### 8.3 Force Applied at Height $h$ Above Ground
Translation: $F \pm f = Ma$
Rotation: $F(r - h) \pm f r = I_{CM} \frac{a}{r}$

Sign of friction depends on direction of $F$ relative to rotation.

## Proof 9: Rolling on a Moving Surface

If the surface itself moves with velocity $v_s$ (e.g., conveyor belt, accelerating plank):

No-slip condition: $v_{CM} - \omega r = v_s$

Equations:
$$F \pm f = Ma_{CM}$$
$$f r = I_{CM} \frac{a_{CM} - a_s}{r}$$

Solve for $a_{CM}$ and $f$.

---

## Proof 10: Rolling with Slipping (Transition to Pure Rolling)

If surface is not rough enough, slipping occurs initially. Then:

- Friction is kinetic: $f = \mu_k N$
- No fixed relationship between $v$ and $\omega$

Equations:
$$F \pm \mu_k N = M a$$
$$\mu_k N r = I_{CM} \alpha$$

Solve for $v(t)$ and $\omega(t)$. Pure rolling begins when $v = \omega r$.

Time to start pure rolling:
$$t = \frac{v_0 - \omega_0 r}{\alpha r \mp a}$$

(depends on initial conditions and directions)

---

## Proof 11: Rolling Up an Incline

Body rolls up an incline with initial velocity $v_0$.

Forces along plane: $-Mg\sin\theta - f = Ma$
Torque: $-f r = I_{CM} \alpha = I_{CM} \frac{a}{r}$

Since friction acts down the plane (opposing relative motion at contact):
$$a = \frac{-g\sin\theta}{1 + k^2/r^2}$$

Distance travelled before stopping:
$$v^2 = v_0^2 + 2as \quad \Rightarrow \quad s = \frac{v_0^2 (1 + k^2/r^2)}{2g\sin\theta}$$

Height gained: $h = s\sin\theta = \frac{v_0^2 (1 + k^2/r^2)}{2g}$

Energy check: $\frac{1}{2} M v_0^2 (1 + k^2/r^2) = Mgh$ ?

## Proof 12: Power in Rolling Motion

Instantaneous power: $P = \frac{dW}{dt} = \tau \frac{d\theta}{dt} = \tau \omega$

From work-energy: $\frac{d}{dt}(\frac{1}{2}I\omega^2) = I\omega \dot{\omega} = (I\alpha)\omega = \tau \omega$

---

## Proof 13: Combined Translation and Rotation (General)

For any rigid body motion:

1. **Translation of CM:** $\sum \mathbf{F} = M \mathbf{a}_{CM}$
2. **Rotation about CM:** $\sum \boldsymbol{\tau}_{CM} = I_{CM} \boldsymbol{\alpha}$

The instantaneous axis of rotation (IAR) is the point with zero velocity. For rolling without slipping, IAR is the contact point.

---

## Proof 14: Friction Direction in Rolling

**Key principle:** Friction acts to prevent relative motion at the contact point.

- **Rolling down incline:** Without friction, wheel would slip down faster than it rotates. Friction acts **up** the plane.
- **Rolling up incline:** Friction acts **down** the plane.
- **Force at centre (horizontal):** If $F$ pulls forward, bottom tends to slip backward ? friction acts **forward**.
- **Force at top (horizontal):** If $F$ pulls forward at top, bottom tends to slip forward ? friction acts **backward**.
