# Proofs: Uniform Circular Motion

**Syllabus:** GCE A/L Combined Mathematics (021) — Paper II, Topic 4.1

---

## Proof 1: Centripetal Acceleration from Geometry (Standard Derivation)

**Statement:** For uniform circular motion of radius $r$ and speed $v$, the centripetal acceleration is $a_c = v^2/r$.

**Proof:**
Consider a particle at points $P$ and $Q$ on a circle of radius $r$, separated by small angle $\Delta\theta$ in time $\Delta t$.

Position vectors: $\vec{r}_P$ and $\vec{r}_Q$, both of magnitude $r$.
Velocity vectors: $\vec{v}_P$ and $\vec{v}_Q$, both of magnitude $v$, perpendicular to radii.

The change in velocity: $\Delta\vec{v} = \vec{v}_Q - \vec{v}_P$.

Since the velocity vectors have rotated by $\Delta\theta$, the magnitude of $\Delta\vec{v}$ is:
$$
\|\Delta\vec{v}\| = 2v\sin(\Delta\theta/2) \approx v\Delta\theta \quad \text{for small } \Delta\theta
$$

Direction of $\Delta\vec{v}$ is perpendicular to the bisector of the angle between $\vec{v}_P$ and $\vec{v}_Q$, i.e., towards the centre.

Average acceleration: $\vec{a}_{avg} = \Delta\vec{v}/\Delta t$.

Instantaneous acceleration:
$$
a_c = \lim_{\Delta t \to 0} \frac{\|\Delta\vec{v}\|}{\Delta t} = \lim_{\Delta t \to 0} \frac{v\Delta\theta}{\Delta t} = v\frac{d\theta}{dt} = v\omega
$$
Since $v = r\omega$:
$$
\boxed{a_c = v\omega = \frac{v^2}{r} = \omega^2 r}
$$
∎

## Proof 2: Centripetal Acceleration from Vector Calculus

**Statement:** $\vec{a} = -\omega^2\vec{r}$ for uniform circular motion.

**Proof:**
Position vector in plane:
$$
\vec{r}(t) = r\cos(\omega t)\,\hat{i} + r\sin(\omega t)\,\hat{j}
$$

Velocity:
$$
\vec{v}(t) = \frac{d\vec{r}}{dt} = -r\omega\sin(\omega t)\,\hat{i} + r\omega\cos(\omega t)\,\hat{j}
$$
$$
\|\vec{v}\| = r\omega = v
$$

Acceleration:
$$
\vec{a}(t) = \frac{d\vec{v}}{dt} = -r\omega^2\cos(\omega t)\,\hat{i} - r\omega^2\sin(\omega t)\,\hat{j} = -\omega^2\vec{r}(t)
$$

Magnitude:
$$
\|\vec{a}\| = \omega^2 r = \frac{v^2}{r}
$$
Direction: opposite to $\vec{r}$, i.e., radially inward.
∎

## Proof 3: Centripetal Force from Newton's Second Law

**Statement:** The net force required for uniform circular motion is $F_c = mv^2/r = m\omega^2 r$ directed towards the centre.

**Proof:**
By Newton's Second Law: $\vec{F}_{net} = m\vec{a}$.
From Proof 2: $\vec{a} = -\omega^2\vec{r}$.
Thus:
$$
\vec{F}_{net} = -m\omega^2\vec{r} = -\frac{mv^2}{r}\hat{r}
$$
Magnitude: $F_c = m\omega^2 r = mv^2/r$. Direction: radially inward ($-\hat{r}$).
∎

## Proof 4: Period and Frequency Relations

**Statement:** $T = 2\pi/\omega = 2\pi r/v$, $f = 1/T = \omega/2\pi$.

**Proof:**
Angular velocity: $\omega = d\theta/dt$. For one complete revolution: $\Delta\theta = 2\pi$.
Time for one revolution: $T = \Delta\theta/\omega = 2\pi/\omega$.

Linear speed: $v = r\omega \implies \omega = v/r$.
Substitute: $T = 2\pi/(v/r) = 2\pi r/v$.

Frequency: $f = 1/T = \omega/2\pi = v/(2\pi r)$.
∎

## Proof 5: Tangential and Normal Components of Acceleration (General)

**Statement:** For any planar motion, $\vec{a} = a_t\hat{t} + a_n\hat{n}$ where $a_t = dv/dt$ and $a_n = v^2/\rho$ ($\rho$ = radius of curvature).

**Proof:**
Let $\hat{t} = \vec{v}/v$ be unit tangent vector.
Velocity: $\vec{v} = v\hat{t}$.
Acceleration:
$$
\vec{a} = \frac{d\vec{v}}{dt} = \frac{dv}{dt}\hat{t} + v\frac{d\hat{t}}{dt}
$$

Since $\|\hat{t}\| = 1$, $d\hat{t}/dt$ is perpendicular to $\hat{t}$ (derivative of unit vector).
Let $d\theta$ be angle change of $\hat{t}$ in time $dt$.
Then $\|d\hat{t}\| = d\theta$.
Radius of curvature: $\rho = ds/d\theta = v\,dt/d\theta \implies d\theta = v\,dt/\rho$.
Thus:
$$
\frac{d\hat{t}}{dt} = \frac{d\theta}{dt}\hat{n} = \frac{v}{\rho}\hat{n}
$$
where $\hat{n}$ is unit normal.

Substitute:
$$
\vec{a} = \frac{dv}{dt}\hat{t} + \frac{v^2}{\rho}\hat{n}
$$
For circular motion: $\rho = r$, $a_n = v^2/r = a_c$.
∎

## Proof 6: Non-Uniform Circular Motion Equations

**Statement:** $\omega = \omega_0 + \alpha t$, $\theta = \omega_0 t + \frac{1}{2}\alpha t^2$, $\omega^2 = \omega_0^2 + 2\alpha\theta$ for constant $\alpha$.

**Proof:**
By definition: $\alpha = d\omega/dt = \text{constant}$.
Integrate: $\omega = \omega_0 + \alpha t$.

$\theta = \int \omega\,dt = \int (\omega_0 + \alpha t)\,dt = \omega_0 t + \frac{1}{2}\alpha t^2$.

Eliminate $t$: $t = (\omega - \omega_0)/\alpha$.
Substitute into $\theta$:
$$
\theta = \omega_0\left(\frac{\omega - \omega_0}{\alpha}\right) + \frac{1}{2}\alpha\left(\frac{\omega - \omega_0}{\alpha}\right)^2
$$
$$
= \frac{\omega_0\omega - \omega_0^2}{\alpha} + \frac{\omega^2 - 2\omega_0\omega + \omega_0^2}{2\alpha}
$$
$$
= \frac{2\omega_0\omega - 2\omega_0^2 + \omega^2 - 2\omega_0\omega + \omega_0^2}{2\alpha}
$$
$$
= \frac{\omega^2 - \omega_0^2}{2\alpha}
$$
$$
\implies \omega^2 = \omega_0^2 + 2\alpha\theta
$$
∎

## Proof 7: Relation Between Linear and Angular Quantities

**Statement:** $v = r\omega$, $a_t = r\alpha$, $a_c = r\omega^2 = v^2/r$.

**Proof:**
Arc length: $s = r\theta$.
Linear speed: $v = ds/dt = r\,d\theta/dt = r\omega$.
Tangential acceleration: $a_t = dv/dt = r\,d\omega/dt = r\alpha$.
Centripetal acceleration: $a_c = v^2/r = (r\omega)^2/r = r\omega^2$.
∎

## Proof 8: Acceleration Vector in Polar Coordinates

**Statement:** In polar coordinates $(r, \theta)$, $\vec{a} = (\ddot{r} - r\dot{\theta}^2)\hat{r} + (r\ddot{\theta} + 2\dot{r}\dot{\theta})\hat{\theta}$.

**Proof:**
Position: $\vec{r} = r\hat{r}$.
Velocity: $\vec{v} = \dot{r}\hat{r} + r\dot{\hat{r}}$.
Since $\dot{\hat{r}} = \dot{\theta}\hat{\theta}$:
$$
\vec{v} = \dot{r}\hat{r} + r\dot{\theta}\hat{\theta}
$$

Acceleration:
$$
\vec{a} = \frac{d\vec{v}}{dt} = \ddot{r}\hat{r} + \dot{r}\dot{\hat{r}} + \dot{r}\dot{\theta}\hat{\theta} + r\ddot{\theta}\hat{\theta} + r\dot{\theta}\dot{\hat{\theta}}
$$
Using $\dot{\hat{r}} = \dot{\theta}\hat{\theta}$, $\dot{\hat{\theta}} = -\dot{\theta}\hat{r}$:
$$
\vec{a} = \ddot{r}\hat{r} + \dot{r}\dot{\theta}\hat{\theta} + \dot{r}\dot{\theta}\hat{\theta} + r\ddot{\theta}\hat{\theta} - r\dot{\theta}^2\hat{r}
$$
$$
\vec{a} = (\ddot{r} - r\dot{\theta}^2)\hat{r} + (r\ddot{\theta} + 2\dot{r}\dot{\theta})\hat{\theta}
$$

For circular motion: $\dot{r} = 0$, $\ddot{r} = 0$, $\dot{\theta} = \omega$, $\ddot{\theta} = \alpha$:
$$
\vec{a} = -r\omega^2\hat{r} + r\alpha\hat{\theta} = -a_c\hat{r} + a_t\hat{\theta}
$$
∎

## Proof 9: Work Done by Centripetal Force is Zero

**Statement:** In uniform circular motion, the work done by the centripetal force over any displacement is zero.

**Proof:**
Centripetal force: $\vec{F}_c = F_c(-\hat{r})$ (radially inward).
Displacement in time $dt$: $d\vec{s} = \vec{v}\,dt = v\hat{t}\,dt$ (tangential).
Work done: $dW = \vec{F}_c \cdot d\vec{s} = F_c(-\hat{r}) \cdot v\hat{t}\,dt = 0$ since $\hat{r} \perp \hat{t}$.

Over any path: $W = \int \vec{F}_c \cdot d\vec{s} = 0$.
This is consistent with kinetic energy being constant in UCM.
∎

## Proof 10: Angular Momentum Conservation in Central Force Motion

**Statement:** For motion under a central force ($\vec{F} = F(r)\hat{r}$), angular momentum $\vec{L} = \vec{r} \times \vec{p}$ is conserved.

**Proof:**
Torque: $\vec{\tau} = \vec{r} \times \vec{F} = \vec{r} \times F(r)\hat{r} = 0$ (parallel vectors).
Rate of change of angular momentum: $d\vec{L}/dt = \vec{\tau} = 0$.
Thus $\vec{L} = \text{constant}$.

Magnitude: $L = mvr = mr^2\omega = \text{constant}$.
For circular motion with constant $r$: $v = \text{constant}$, $\omega = \text{constant}$.
∎
