# Proofs: Virtual Work

**Syllabus:** GCE A/L Combined Mathematics (021) — Paper II, Topic 3.5

---

## Proof 1: Principle of Virtual Work for a Particle

**Statement:** A particle in equilibrium under forces $\vec{F}_1, \vec{F}_2, \dots$ satisfies $\sum \vec{F}_i \cdot \delta\vec{r} = 0$ for any virtual displacement $\delta\vec{r}$.

**Proof:**
For a particle in equilibrium, $\sum \vec{F}_i = 0$ (Newton's 2nd law, $\vec{a}=0$).
For any virtual displacement $\delta\vec{r}$, multiply both sides by $\delta\vec{r}$:
$(\sum \vec{F}_i) \cdot \delta\vec{r} = 0 \cdot \delta\vec{r} = 0$.
$\sum (\vec{F}_i \cdot \delta\vec{r}) = 0$.
This is the virtual work equation.
∎

## Proof 2: Virtual Work for a Rigid Body in 2D

**Statement:** A rigid body in 2D equilibrium satisfies $\sum \vec{F}_i \cdot \delta\vec{r}_i + \sum M_i \delta\theta = 0$ for virtual translation $\delta\vec{r}$ and rotation $\delta\theta$.

**Proof:**
Virtual displacement of point $i$: $\delta\vec{r}_i = \delta\vec{r}_{\text{CM}} + \delta\vec{\theta} \times \vec{r}_i$.
Virtual work: $\delta W = \sum \vec{F}_i \cdot (\delta\vec{r}_{\text{CM}} + \delta\vec{\theta} \times \vec{r}_i)$
$= (\sum \vec{F}_i) \cdot \delta\vec{r}_{\text{CM}} + \sum \vec{F}_i \cdot (\delta\vec{\theta} \times \vec{r}_i)$
$= \vec{R} \cdot \delta\vec{r}_{\text{CM}} + \delta\vec{\theta} \cdot \sum (\vec{r}_i \times \vec{F}_i)$
$= \vec{R} \cdot \delta\vec{r}_{\text{CM}} + \vec{M} \cdot \delta\vec{\theta}$.
For equilibrium: $\vec{R} = 0$ and $\vec{M} = 0$, so $\delta W = 0$ for any $\delta\vec{r}_{\text{CM}}, \delta\vec{\theta}$.
Conversely, if $\delta W = 0$ for all virtual displacements, then $\vec{R} = 0$ and $\vec{M} = 0$.
∎

## Proof 3: Constraint Forces Do No Work

**Statement:** Forces of reaction at smooth constraints do no work in any virtual displacement consistent with the constraint.

**Proof:**
Smooth constraint (surface, pin, roller): reaction force $\vec{R}$ is normal to the allowed motion.
Virtual displacement $\delta\vec{r}$ is tangent to the constraint surface (consistent with constraint).
Therefore $\vec{R} \cdot \delta\vec{r} = 0$.
Work done by reaction = 0.
∎

## Proof 4: Virtual Work for a Particle on a Smooth Curve

**Statement:** A particle on a smooth curve $f(x,y)=0$ is in equilibrium under force $\vec{F}$ iff the tangential component of $\vec{F}$ is zero.

**Proof:**
Virtual displacement $\delta\vec{r}$ is tangent to curve.
Virtual work: $\delta W = \vec{F} \cdot \delta\vec{r}$.
Equilibrium $\Leftrightarrow \delta W = 0$ for all tangential $\delta\vec{r}$.
$\Leftrightarrow \vec{F} \cdot \hat{t} = 0$ (tangential component zero).
Normal component is balanced by reaction (does no work).
∎

## Proof 5: Virtual Work for a Simple Lever

**Statement:** For a lever with arms $a, b$ and forces $F_1, F_2$, equilibrium condition is $F_1 a = F_2 b$.

**Proof:**
Virtual displacement: small rotation $\delta\theta$.
Displacement of $F_1$: $\delta x_1 = a\delta\theta$ (perpendicular to arm).
Displacement of $F_2$: $\delta x_2 = b\delta\theta$.
Virtual work: $F_1 a\delta\theta - F_2 b\delta\theta = 0$ (opposite directions).
$F_1 a = F_2 b$.
∎

## Proof 6: Virtual Work for a Wedge

**Statement:** For a smooth wedge of angle $\alpha$ with load $W$ and applied force $P$, equilibrium: $P = W\tan\alpha$.

**Proof:**
Virtual displacements: wedge moves horizontally $\delta x$, load moves vertically $\delta y$.
Constraint: $\delta y = \delta x \tan\alpha$ (geometry).
Virtual work: $P \delta x - W \delta y = 0$.
$P \delta x - W(\delta x \tan\alpha) = 0$.
$P = W \tan\alpha$.
∎

## Proof 7: Virtual Work for a Rope and Pulley System

**Statement:** For an ideal (massless, frictionless) pulley system with $n$ strands supporting load $W$, effort $P = W/n$.

**Proof:**
Virtual displacement: effort moves distance $\delta x$, load moves $\delta y$.
Constraint (inextensible string): $\delta x = n \delta y$.
Virtual work: $P \delta x - W \delta y = 0$.
$P(n \delta y) - W \delta y = 0 \Rightarrow P = W/n$.
∎

## Proof 8: Virtual Work for a Frame (Method of Sections)

**Statement:** To find force in a specific truss member, cut the member and apply virtual work to one part.

**Proof:**
Cut member $i$ with force $F_i$ (tension).
Apply virtual displacement consistent with remaining structure (e.g., remove one support to create a mechanism, give it $\delta\theta$).
Virtual work equation involves only external forces and $F_i$ (internal forces in uncut members do no work as they remain rigid).
Solve for $F_i$.
This is equivalent to the method of sections but uses work instead of moments.
∎

## Proof 9: D'Alembert's Principle (Extension to Dynamics)

**Statement:** For a system with accelerations, $\sum (\vec{F}_i - m_i\vec{a}_i) \cdot \delta\vec{r}_i = 0$.

**Proof:**
Newton's 2nd law for particle $i$: $\vec{F}_i + \vec{R}_i = m_i\vec{a}_i$, where $\vec{R}_i$ are constraint forces.
$\vec{F}_i - m_i\vec{a}_i = -\vec{R}_i$.
Virtual work of constraint forces: $\sum \vec{R}_i \cdot \delta\vec{r}_i = 0$ (ideal constraints).
Thus $\sum (\vec{F}_i - m_i\vec{a}_i) \cdot \delta\vec{r}_i = 0$.
This converts dynamics problem to a virtual work problem with "inertial forces" $-m_i\vec{a}_i$.
∎

## Proof 10: Minimum Potential Energy Principle

**Statement:** For a conservative system in equilibrium, the potential energy $V$ is stationary: $\delta V = 0$.

**Proof:**
Forces from potential: $\vec{F}_i = -\nabla_i V$.
Virtual work: $\delta W = \sum \vec{F}_i \cdot \delta\vec{r}_i = \sum (-\nabla_i V) \cdot \delta\vec{r}_i = -\delta V$.
Equilibrium: $\delta W = 0 \Rightarrow -\delta V = 0 \Rightarrow \delta V = 0$.
Stable equilibrium $\Leftrightarrow \delta^2 V > 0$ (minimum).
Unstable $\Leftrightarrow \delta^2 V < 0$ (maximum).
Neutral $\Leftrightarrow \delta^2 V = 0$.
∎
