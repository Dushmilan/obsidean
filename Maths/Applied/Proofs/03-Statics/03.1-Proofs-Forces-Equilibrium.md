# Proofs: Forces in Equilibrium

**Syllabus:** GCE A/L Combined Mathematics (021) — Paper II, Topic 3.1

---

## Proof 1: Lami's Theorem

**Statement:** For three coplanar forces $F_1, F_2, F_3$ acting at a point in equilibrium:
$$
\frac{F_1}{\sin\alpha_1} = \frac{F_2}{\sin\alpha_2} = \frac{F_3}{\sin\alpha_3}
$$
where $\alpha_1$ is angle between $F_2$ and $F_3$, etc.

**Proof:**
From triangle of forces: $\vec{F}_1 + \vec{F}_2 + \vec{F}_3 = 0$.
These three vectors form a closed triangle.
Let angles of triangle be $\beta_1, \beta_2, \beta_3$ (opposite sides $F_1, F_2, F_3$).

By sine rule in triangle:
$$
\frac{F_1}{\sin\beta_1} = \frac{F_2}{\sin\beta_2} = \frac{F_3}{\sin\beta_3}
$$

Angle $\beta_1$ is supplement of $\alpha_1$ (external angle of triangle):
$\beta_1 = 180^\circ - \alpha_1 \Rightarrow \sin\beta_1 = \sin\alpha_1$.

Thus:
$$
\frac{F_1}{\sin\alpha_1} = \frac{F_2}{\sin\alpha_2} = \frac{F_3}{\sin\alpha_3}
$$
∎

## Proof 2: Principle of Moments (Varignon's Theorem)

**Statement:** The moment of the resultant of several forces about any point equals the sum of moments of the individual forces about the same point.

**Proof:**
Let forces $\vec{F}_1, \vec{F}_2, \dots, \vec{F}_n$ act at points with position vectors $\vec{r}_1, \dots, \vec{r}_n$ relative to point $O$.
Resultant: $\vec{R} = \sum \vec{F}_i$.
Moment of resultant: $\vec{M} = \sum \vec{r}_i \times \vec{F}_i$.

Sum of individual moments:
$$
\sum (\vec{r}_i \times \vec{F}_i) = \sum \vec{r}_i \times \vec{F}_i
$$

Since moment is distributive over addition, this is exactly the moment of the resultant.
∎

## Proof 3: Equilibrium of Three Forces Meeting at a Point

**Statement:** Three non-parallel forces acting at a point are in equilibrium iff they can be represented in magnitude and direction by the three sides of a triangle taken in order.

**Proof:**
($\Rightarrow$) If in equilibrium: $\vec{F}_1 + \vec{F}_2 + \vec{F}_3 = 0$.
Place vectors head-to-tail: $\vec{F}_1 + \vec{F}_2 = -\vec{F}_3$.
This forms a closed triangle.

($\Leftarrow$) If forces form a triangle: $\vec{F}_1 + \vec{F}_2 + \vec{F}_3 = 0$ by vector addition around closed triangle. Hence equilibrium.
∎

## Proof 4: Conditions for Equilibrium of a Rigid Body in 2D

**Statement:** A rigid body in a plane is in equilibrium iff:
1. $\sum F_x = 0$
2. $\sum F_y = 0$
3. $\sum M_O = 0$ for any point $O$

**Proof:**
($\Rightarrow$) If in equilibrium, no translation $\Rightarrow \sum \vec{F} = 0 \Rightarrow \sum F_x = \sum F_y = 0$.
No rotation $\Rightarrow \sum \vec{M} = 0$ about any point.

($\Leftarrow$) Assume $\sum F_x = \sum F_y = \sum M_O = 0$.
- No net force $\Rightarrow$ no translational acceleration (Newton's 2nd law).
- $\sum M_O = 0$ about one point $\Rightarrow \sum M_P = 0$ for any point $P$ (since $\sum M_P = \sum M_O + \vec{OP} \times \sum \vec{F} = 0$).
- No net moment about any point $\Rightarrow$ no rotational acceleration.
∎

## Proof 5: Equivalent Force Systems

**Statement:** Two force systems are equivalent iff they have the same resultant force and the same resultant moment about any point.

**Proof:**
Let system 1 have forces $\vec{F}_i$ at $\vec{r}_i$, system 2 have $\vec{G}_j$ at $\vec{s}_j$.
Resultant force: $\vec{R}_1 = \sum \vec{F}_i$, $\vec{R}_2 = \sum \vec{G}_j$.
Resultant moment about $O$: $\vec{M}_1 = \sum \vec{r}_i \times \vec{F}_i$, $\vec{M}_2 = \sum \vec{s}_j \times \vec{G}_j$.

If equivalent: replacing one by the other has no effect on any rigid body.
$\Rightarrow$ Same effect on translation: $\vec{R}_1 = \vec{R}_2$.
$\Rightarrow$ Same effect on rotation about any point: $\vec{M}_1 = \vec{M}_2$.

Conversely, if $\vec{R}_1 = \vec{R}_2$ and $\vec{M}_1 = \vec{M}_2$, then for any rigid body, the equations of motion ($\sum \vec{F} = m\vec{a}$, $\sum \vec{M} = I\alpha$) are identical. Hence equivalent.
∎

## Proof 6: Resultant of Parallel Forces

**Statement:** System of parallel forces has a single resultant $R = \sum F_i$ acting at point $\bar{x}$ such that $R\bar{x} = \sum F_i x_i$.

**Proof:**
Let forces $F_1, F_2, \dots, F_n$ act at $x_1, x_2, \dots, x_n$ along a line.
Take moments about origin:
$$
M = \sum F_i x_i
$$
If replaced by single force $R$ at $\bar{x}$, moment is $R\bar{x}$.
Equating: $R\bar{x} = \sum F_i x_i$.
Also total force: $R = \sum F_i$.
Thus $\bar{x} = \frac{\sum F_i x_i}{\sum F_i}$ (centre of parallel forces).
∎

## Proof 7: Couple Moment is Independent of Reference Point

**Statement:** The moment of a couple (two equal and opposite parallel forces separated by distance $d$) is the same about any point.

**Proof:**
Let forces $+F$ and $-F$ act at points $A$ and $B$, with $\vec{AB} = \vec{d}$.
Moment about $O$:
$$
M = \vec{OA} \times \vec{F} + \vec{OB} \times (-\vec{F})
= (\vec{OA} - \vec{OB}) \times \vec{F}
= \vec{BA} \times \vec{F} = -\vec{d} \times \vec{F}
$$
This does not depend on $O$. Hence constant everywhere.
Magnitude: $|M| = Fd$.
∎

## Proof 8: Reduction of Coplanar Force System

**Statement:** Any system of coplanar forces reduces to either:
- A single force (if resultant $\vec{R} \neq 0$)
- A single couple (if $\vec{R} = 0$ but net moment $\neq 0$)
- Nothing (if $\vec{R} = 0$ and net moment $= 0$)

**Proof:**
Let resultant force $\vec{R} = \sum \vec{F}_i$, resultant moment $M = \sum M_i$.
Case 1: $\vec{R} \neq 0$. Place single force $\vec{R}$ at distance $d = M/R$ from reference such that its moment equals $M$. This single force has same $\vec{R}$ and $M$.
Case 2: $\vec{R} = 0, M \neq 0$. Cannot be replaced by single force (would need infinite distance). Remains as pure couple $M$.
Case 3: $\vec{R} = 0, M = 0$. System is in equilibrium.
∎
