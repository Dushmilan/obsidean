# Proofs: Rigid Bodies in Equilibrium

**Syllabus:** GCE A/L Combined Mathematics (021) — Paper II, Topic 3.6

---

## Proof 1: 2D Equilibrium Equations

**Statement:** A rigid body in a plane is in equilibrium iff $\sum F_x = 0$, $\sum F_y = 0$, $\sum M_O = 0$ for any point $O$.

**Proof:**
($\Rightarrow$) Equilibrium $\Rightarrow$ no translation $\Rightarrow \sum \vec{F} = 0 \Rightarrow \sum F_x = \sum F_y = 0$.
No rotation $\Rightarrow$ no angular acceleration $\Rightarrow \sum \vec{M}_O = 0$ for any $O$.

($\Leftarrow$) If $\sum F_x = \sum F_y = 0$, then $\sum \vec{F} = 0 \Rightarrow$ no translational acceleration.
If $\sum M_O = 0$ for one point, then for any other point $P$:
$\sum M_P = \sum M_O + \vec{OP} \times \sum \vec{F} = 0 + 0 = 0$.
Thus no moment about any point $\Rightarrow$ no rotational acceleration.
∎

## Proof 2: Moment About Any Point for Parallel Forces

**Statement:** For a system of parallel forces, the resultant moment about any point is the same.

**Proof:**
Let parallel forces $F_i$ act at points with position vectors $\vec{r}_i$.
Moment about $O$: $\sum M_O = \sum (\vec{r}_i \times \vec{F}_i)$.
Since all $\vec{F}_i$ are parallel, $\vec{F}_i = F_i \hat{u}$.
$M_O = (\sum F_i \vec{r}_i) \times \hat{u} = (\sum F_i) \bar{\vec{r}} \times \hat{u}$, where $\bar{\vec{r}} = \frac{\sum F_i \vec{r}_i}{\sum F_i}$.
This depends only on the resultant force $\sum F_i \hat{u}$ and its line of action (through $\bar{\vec{r}}$), not on the choice of $O$.
∎

## Proof 3: Method of Joints for Trusses

**Statement:** In a plane truss with pin joints, each joint is in equilibrium under the member forces and external loads.

**Proof:**
Assumptions:
1. Members are two-force members (only axial forces).
2. Joints are frictionless pins.
3. Loads applied only at joints.

Consider a joint as a free body. Forces acting are:
- Member forces (tension/compression along member directions)
- External loads/reactions at that joint

Since the joint is a particle in equilibrium: $\sum F_x = 0$, $\sum F_y = 0$.
Solve sequentially starting from joints with $\leq 2$ unknowns.
∎

## Proof 4: Method of Sections for Trusses

**Statement:** To find forces in specific truss members, cut through them (max 3) and apply equilibrium to one part.

**Proof:**
Cut truss into two parts. Consider one part as a free body.
Forces on this part: external loads, reactions, and cut member forces.
The part is a rigid body in equilibrium: $\sum F_x = 0$, $\sum F_y = 0$, $\sum M = 0$.
Three equations can solve for up to three unknown member forces.
∎

## Proof 5: Zero-Force Members

**Statement:** 
1. Two non-collinear members at a joint with no external load $\Rightarrow$ both zero force.
2. Three members, two collinear, no external load $\Rightarrow$ third member zero force.

**Proof:**
(1) Joint with two members at angle $\theta \neq 0, \pi$. No external force.
Equilibrium: $F_1 + F_2 = 0$ (vectors).
Since not collinear, only solution is $F_1 = F_2 = 0$.

(2) Three members: two collinear ($A$ and $B$), third $C$ at angle. No external load.
Resolve along $C$: $F_C = 0$.
Resolve along $A/B$: $F_A + F_B = 0$ (they are collinear).
$F_A$ and $F_B$ can be non-zero (tension/compression pair).
∎

## Proof 6: Distributed Load Resultant

**Statement:** A distributed load $w(x)$ on a beam is equivalent to a single force $R = \int w(x)dx$ acting at the centroid $\bar{x} = \frac{\int x w(x)dx}{\int w(x)dx}$.

**Proof:**
Moment of distributed load about origin: $\int x w(x)dx$.
Moment of resultant $R$ at $\bar{x}$: $R \bar{x}$.
Equate: $R \bar{x} = \int x w(x)dx \Rightarrow \bar{x} = \frac{\int x w(x)dx}{R}$.
$R = \int w(x)dx$ by force equivalence.
∎

## Proof 7: Common Distributed Load Resultants

**Statement:** 
- UDL $w$ over $L$: $R = wL$, at $L/2$.
- Triangular $0$ to $w_0$ over $L$: $R = w_0L/2$, at $2L/3$ from zero end.

**Proof:**
UDL: $R = \int_0^L w dx = wL$.
$\bar{x} = \frac{\int_0^L x w dx}{wL} = \frac{w L^2/2}{wL} = L/2$.

Triangular: $w(x) = \frac{w_0}{L}x$.
$R = \int_0^L \frac{w_0}{L}x dx = \frac{w_0}{L} \frac{L^2}{2} = \frac{w_0L}{2}$.
$\bar{x} = \frac{\int_0^L x \cdot \frac{w_0}{L}x dx}{w_0L/2} = \frac{w_0 L^3/3L}{w_0L/2} = \frac{2L}{3}$.
∎

## Proof 8: Three-Hinged Arch — Horizontal Thrust

**Statement:** For a three-hinged arch with span $L$, rise $h$, horizontal thrust $H$ at supports: $H = \frac{M_{\text{beam}}}{h}$ where $M_{\text{beam}}$ is bending moment at crown of equivalent simply supported beam.

**Proof:**
Three-hinged arch: hinges at two supports and crown.
Take moments about crown hinge for left half.
Moment from reactions = 0 (hinge has no moment).
Let $V_A, H_A$ be reactions at left support.
Moment about crown: $V_A \cdot \frac{L}{2} - H_A \cdot h = 0$.
For symmetric arch: $V_A = \frac{\text{total vertical load}}{2}$.
But $V_A \cdot \frac{L}{2}$ is the moment of simply supported beam at midspan (crown).
Thus $H_A = \frac{V_A \cdot L/2}{h} = \frac{M_{\text{beam}}}{h}$.
∎

## Proof 9: Cable with Uniform Load — Parabolic Shape

**Statement:** A flexible cable with uniform horizontal load $w$ (self-weight negligible) takes a parabolic shape $y = \frac{w}{2H}x^2$ where $H$ is horizontal tension.

**Proof:**
Consider element $dx$. Load $w dx$.
Tension $T$, angle $\theta$: $H = T\cos\theta$ (constant, no horizontal load).
Vertical equilibrium: $V = \int w dx = wx$ (for symmetric cable with lowest point at $x=0$).
Slope: $\tan\theta = \frac{V}{H} = \frac{wx}{H}$.
$\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{wx}{H} \Rightarrow y = \frac{w}{2H}x^2$.
∎

## Proof 10: Equilibrium in 3D

**Statement:** A rigid body in 3D is in equilibrium iff $\sum \vec{F} = 0$ and $\sum \vec{M}_O = 0$ for some point $O$.

**Proof:**
($\Rightarrow$) Equilibrium $\Rightarrow$ no translation, no rotation $\Rightarrow \sum \vec{F} = 0$, $\sum \vec{M} = 0$.

($\Leftarrow$) $\sum \vec{F} = 0 \Rightarrow$ no translational acceleration.
For moments: $\sum \vec{M}_P = \sum \vec{M}_O + \vec{OP} \times \sum \vec{F} = 0 + 0 = 0$ for any $P$.
Thus no moment about any point $\Rightarrow$ no rotational acceleration.
∎

## Proof 11: Wrench (Screw) Reduction of 3D Force System

**Statement:** Any 3D force system with $\vec{R} \neq 0$ reduces to a wrench: force $\vec{R}$ along a line + moment $\vec{M}_\parallel$ parallel to $\vec{R}$.

**Proof:**
Given resultant force $\vec{R}$ and resultant moment $\vec{M}_O$ about $O$.
Decompose $\vec{M}_O = \vec{M}_\parallel + \vec{M}_\perp$, where $\vec{M}_\parallel$ is parallel to $\vec{R}$, $\vec{M}_\perp$ is perpendicular.
Let $\vec{M}_\parallel = p \vec{R}$ (pitch $p = \frac{\vec{R} \cdot \vec{M}_O}{|\vec{R}|^2}$).
$\vec{M}_\perp = \vec{r} \times \vec{R}$ for some $\vec{r}$ perpendicular to $\vec{R}$ (solution: $\vec{r} = \frac{\vec{R} \times \vec{M}_\perp}{|\vec{R}|^2}$).
Move $\vec{R}$ to line through $O + \vec{r}$. Moment about $O$ becomes $(\vec{r} \times \vec{R}) + p\vec{R} = \vec{M}_\perp + \vec{M}_\parallel = \vec{M}_O$.
Thus system = force $\vec{R}$ at line + couple $p\vec{R}$ (wrench).
∎

## Proof 12: Mechanical Advantage from Virtual Work

**Statement:** For an ideal machine (no friction, rigid bodies), $\text{MA} = \text{VR} = \frac{\delta x_{\text{in}}}{\delta x_{\text{out}}}$.

**Proof:**
Virtual work: $F_{\text{in}} \delta x_{\text{in}} = F_{\text{out}} \delta x_{\text{out}}$.
Mechanical Advantage: $\text{MA} = \frac{F_{\text{out}}}{F_{\text{in}}}$.
Velocity Ratio: $\text{VR} = \frac{\delta x_{\text{in}}}{\delta x_{\text{out}}}$.
From virtual work: $F_{\text{in}} \delta x_{\text{in}} = F_{\text{out}} \delta x_{\text{out}} \Rightarrow \frac{F_{\text{out}}}{F_{\text{in}}} = \frac{\delta x_{\text{in}}}{\delta x_{\text{out}}}$.
Thus $\text{MA} = \text{VR}$.
∎
