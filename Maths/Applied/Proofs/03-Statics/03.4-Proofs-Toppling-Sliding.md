# Proofs: Toppling and Sliding

**Syllabus:** GCE A/L Combined Mathematics (021) — Paper II, Topic 3.4

---

## Proof 1: Sliding Condition on Inclined Plane

**Statement:** A block on a rough inclined plane (angle $\theta$, coefficient $\mu$) slides when $\tan\theta > \mu$.

**Proof:**
Forces: $W$ vertically down, $R$ normal to plane, $F$ up the plane.
Resolve perpendicular: $R = W\cos\theta$.
Resolve parallel: $F = W\sin\theta$.
Limiting friction: $F_{\text{max}} = \mu R = \mu W\cos\theta$.
Equilibrium requires $F \leq F_{\text{max}}$:
$W\sin\theta \leq \mu W\cos\theta \Rightarrow \tan\theta \leq \mu$.
Sliding occurs when $\tan\theta > \mu$.
∎

---

## Proof 2: Toppling Condition on Inclined Plane

**Statement:** A uniform rectangular block (base width $b$, height $h$, CM at centre) on an inclined plane topples when $\tan\theta > \frac{b}{h}$.

**Proof:**
Take moments about the lower edge (point of toppling).
Weight $W$ acts at CM. Perpendicular distance from CM to lower edge = $\frac{b}{2}\cos\theta - \frac{h}{2}\sin\theta$.
Normal $R$ acts at lower edge (moment = 0).
Friction $F$ acts at lower edge (moment = 0).
For no toppling, moment of $W$ must be restoring (tending to rotate back):
$W \cdot (\text{perpendicular distance}) \geq 0$.
$\frac{b}{2}\cos\theta - \frac{h}{2}\sin\theta \geq 0 \Rightarrow b\cos\theta \geq h\sin\theta \Rightarrow \tan\theta \leq \frac{b}{h}$.
Toppling occurs when $\tan\theta > \frac{b}{h}$.
∎

## Proof 3: Comparison of Sliding and Toppling Angles

**Statement:** For a uniform block on an inclined plane:
- Slides first if $\mu < \frac{b}{h}$
- Topples first if $\mu > \frac{b}{h}$
- Simultaneous if $\mu = \frac{b}{h}$

**Proof:**
Sliding angle: $\theta_{\text{slide}} = \tan^{-1}\mu$.
Toppling angle: $\theta_{\text{topple}} = \tan^{-1}(b/h)$.
Compare $\mu$ and $b/h$:
- $\mu < b/h \Rightarrow \theta_{\text{slide}} < \theta_{\text{topple}} \Rightarrow$ slides first.
- $\mu > b/h \Rightarrow \theta_{\text{slide}} > \theta_{\text{topple}} \Rightarrow$ topples first.
- $\mu = b/h \Rightarrow$ simultaneous.
∎

## Proof 4: Block on Horizontal Plane with Horizontal Force

**Statement:** For a block (width $b$, height $h$, CM at centre) on rough horizontal plane ($\mu$), with horizontal force $P$ at height $y$:
- Slides when $P = \mu W$
- Topples when $P = \frac{Wb}{2y}$

**Proof:**
Sliding: $F = P$, $R = W$. Limiting: $P = \mu W$.
Toppling: Take moments about outer edge. $P \cdot y = W \cdot \frac{b}{2} \Rightarrow P = \frac{Wb}{2y}$.
∎

---

## Proof 5: Force Applied at an Angle

**Statement:** For force $P$ at angle $\alpha$ to horizontal:
- Slide: $P_{\text{slide}} = \frac{\mu W}{\cos\alpha + \mu\sin\alpha}$
- Topple: $P_{\text{topple}} = \frac{Wb/2}{y\cos\alpha + (b/2)\sin\alpha}$

**Proof:**
Resolving:
$R = W - P\sin\alpha$, $F = P\cos\alpha$.
Slide (limiting): $P\cos\alpha = \mu(W - P\sin\alpha) \Rightarrow P = \frac{\mu W}{\cos\alpha + \mu\sin\alpha}$.

Toppling (moments about outer edge):
$P\cos\alpha \cdot y + P\sin\alpha \cdot \frac{b}{2} = W \cdot \frac{b}{2}$
$P = \frac{Wb/2}{y\cos\alpha + (b/2)\sin\alpha}$.
∎

## Proof 6: Friction Cone and Toppling

**Statement:** The resultant contact force must lie within the friction cone (angle $\lambda = \tan^{-1}\mu$) and its line of action must intersect the base.

**Proof:**
Resultant contact force $\vec{S} = \vec{R} + \vec{F}$.
Angle with normal: $\tan\theta = \frac{F}{R} \leq \mu = \tan\lambda \Rightarrow \theta \leq \lambda$.
Line of action of $\vec{S}$ must pass through the base for no toppling (otherwise moment about CM causes rotation).
Both conditions must hold simultaneously.
∎

## Proof 7: Stability on Horizontal Plane (Small Tilts)

**Statement:** A body with CM at height $h$ and base half-width $a$ is stable against small tilts iff $h < a/\tan\phi$ where $\phi$ is tilt angle, but for infinitesimal tilts, always stable if CM is above base.

**Proof:**
Body tilted by small angle $\phi$. CM moves horizontally by $h\phi$.
Restoring moment = $W \cdot (a - h\phi)$.
Stable if $a - h\phi > 0 \Rightarrow \phi < \frac{a}{h}$.
For infinitesimal $\phi$, always stable if $a > 0$ (CM above base).
Metacentric height $GM = \frac{a}{h}$ for rectangular block.
∎

## Proof 8: Cube on Inclined Plane — Simultaneous Sliding and Toppling

**Statement:** A cube of side $a$ on a rough plane ($\mu$) slides and topples simultaneously when $\mu = 1$.

**Proof:**
Cube: $b = a$, $h = a$. CM at centre.
$\theta_{\text{slide}} = \tan^{-1}\mu$, $\theta_{\text{topple}} = \tan^{-1}(b/h) = \tan^{-1}(1) = 45^\circ$.
Simultaneous when $\mu = 1$.
If $\mu < 1$: slides first.
If $\mu > 1$: topples first.
∎

## Proof 9: Equilibrium Range on Inclined Plane with Applied Force

**Statement:** For a block on rough incline ($\theta$, $\mu$) with force $P$ up the plane, equilibrium range:
$$
W(\sin\theta - \mu\cos\theta) \leq P \leq W(\sin\theta + \mu\cos\theta)
$$

**Proof:**
Friction $F$ can act up or down.
Case 1 (preventing slide down): $P + F = W\sin\theta$, $F \leq \mu W\cos\theta$.
$\Rightarrow P \geq W\sin\theta - \mu W\cos\theta$.

Case 2 (preventing slide up): $P = W\sin\theta + F \leq W\sin\theta + \mu W\cos\theta$.
Combining gives the range.
∎

## Proof 10: Ladder Against Rough Floor, Smooth Wall

**Statement:** Ladder length $2l$, weight $W$, angle $\theta$, rough floor ($\mu$), smooth wall. Minimum $\theta$ for equilibrium: $\tan\theta = \frac{1}{2\mu}$.

**Proof:**
Forces: $W$ at centre, $R_w$ at wall, $R_f, F_f = \mu R_f$ at floor.
$\sum F_x = 0: R_w = F_f = \mu R_f$.
$\sum F_y = 0: R_f = W$.
$\sum M_{\text{floor}} = 0: W(l\cos\theta) = R_w(2l\sin\theta) = \mu W(2l\sin\theta)$.
$\cos\theta = 2\mu\sin\theta \Rightarrow \cot\theta = 2\mu \Rightarrow \tan\theta = \frac{1}{2\mu}$.
∎

## Proof 11: Ladder with Man Climbing

**Statement:** Uniform ladder $W$, length $L$, angle $\theta$, rough floor ($\mu$), smooth wall. Man weight $w$ at distance $x$ from bottom. Max $x$ before slip: $x = \frac{L}{2}\left(\frac{W + 2w}{w} - \frac{W}{w}\cot\theta \cdot 2\mu\right)$? Let me re-derive.

**Proof:**
Forces: $R_w = F_f = \mu R_f$, $R_f = W + w$.
$\sum M_{\text{floor}} = 0: W\frac{L}{2}\cos\theta + wx\cos\theta = R_w L\sin\theta = \mu(W+w)L\sin\theta$.
$wx\cos\theta = \mu(W+w)L\sin\theta - \frac{WL}{2}\cos\theta$.
$x = \frac{L}{w}(\mu(W+w)\tan\theta - \frac{W}{2})$.
At max $\mu$ before slip: use limiting $\mu$.
If ladder alone just slips: $\mu = \frac{\cot\theta}{2}$.
Substitute: $x = \frac{L}{w}((W+w)\frac{\cot\theta}{2}\tan\theta - \frac{W}{2}) = \frac{L}{w}(\frac{W+w}{2} - \frac{W}{2}) = \frac{L}{2}$.
Makes sense: man at top just causes slip.
∎

## Proof 12: Wedge Self-Locking Condition

**Statement:** Wedge angle $\alpha$, coefficient $\mu$ on all faces. Self-locks (cannot be pushed out) when $\mu \geq \cot\alpha$.

**Proof:**
Force $P$ to push wedge under load $W$.
Vertical on load: $W = R_1\cos\alpha - F_1\sin\alpha = R_1(\cos\alpha - \mu\sin\alpha)$.
Horizontal on wedge: $P = R_1\sin\alpha + F_1\cos\alpha + F_2 = R_1\sin\alpha + \mu R_1\cos\alpha + \mu R_2$.
Vertical on wedge: $R_2 = R_1\cos\alpha + F_1\sin\alpha = R_1(\cos\alpha + \mu\sin\alpha)$.

$P = R_1\sin\alpha + \mu R_1\cos\alpha + \mu R_1(\cos\alpha + \mu\sin\alpha)$
$= R_1[\sin\alpha + \mu\cos\alpha + \mu\cos\alpha + \mu^2\sin\alpha]$
$= R_1[(1+\mu^2)\sin\alpha + 2\mu\cos\alpha]$.

From $W = R_1(\cos\alpha - \mu\sin\alpha)$:
$P = W \frac{(1+\mu^2)\sin\alpha + 2\mu\cos\alpha}{\cos\alpha - \mu\sin\alpha}$.

Denominator $\cos\alpha - \mu\sin\alpha \leq 0 \Rightarrow \mu \geq \cot\alpha \Rightarrow$ self-locking.
∎
