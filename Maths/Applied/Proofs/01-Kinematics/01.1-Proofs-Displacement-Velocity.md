# Proofs: Displacement, Velocity, Acceleration

**Syllabus:** GCE A/L Combined Mathematics (021) — Paper II, Topic 1.1

---

## Proof 1: Instantaneous Velocity as Derivative of Position

**Statement:** $v = \frac{ds}{dt} = \lim_{\Delta t \to 0} \frac{\Delta s}{\Delta t}$

**Proof:**
Let particle position at time $t$ be $s(t)$, at $t+\Delta t$ be $s(t+\Delta t)$.
Displacement in $\Delta t$: $\Delta s = s(t+\Delta t) - s(t)$.
Average velocity over $\Delta t$: $\bar{v} = \frac{\Delta s}{\Delta t}$.
Instantaneous velocity is limit as $\Delta t \to 0$:
$$
v = \lim_{\Delta t \to 0} \bar{v} = \lim_{\Delta t \to 0} \frac{s(t+\Delta t) - s(t)}{\Delta t} = \frac{ds}{dt}
$$
∎

---

## Proof 2: Instantaneous Acceleration as Derivative of Velocity

**Statement:** $a = \frac{dv}{dt} = \lim_{\Delta t \to 0} \frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t}$

**Proof:**
Let velocity at time $t$ be $v(t)$, at $t+\Delta t$ be $v(t+\Delta t)$.
Change in velocity: $\Delta v = v(t+\Delta t) - v(t)$.
Average acceleration: $\bar{a} = \frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t}$.
Instantaneous acceleration:
$$
a = \lim_{\Delta t \to 0} \bar{a} = \lim_{\Delta t \to 0} \frac{v(t+\Delta t) - v(t)}{\Delta t} = \frac{dv}{dt}
$$
∎

---

## Proof 3: Acceleration in Terms of Displacement ($a = v \frac{dv}{ds}$)

**Statement:** $a = \frac{dv}{dt} = v\frac{dv}{ds}$

**Proof:**
By chain rule:
$$
a = \frac{dv}{dt} = \frac{dv}{ds} \cdot \frac{ds}{dt} = \frac{dv}{ds} \cdot v = v\frac{dv}{ds}
$$
∎

## Proof 4: Distance in $n$th Second ($s_n = u + a(n - \frac{1}{2})$)

**Statement:** Distance travelled during the $n$th second ($t \in [n-1, n]$) under constant acceleration $a$ with initial velocity $u$:
$$
s_n = u + a\left(n - \frac{1}{2}\right)
$$

**Proof:**
Total displacement after $n$ seconds:
$$
s(n) = un + \frac{1}{2}an^2
$$
Total displacement after $n-1$ seconds:
$$
s(n-1) = u(n-1) + \frac{1}{2}a(n-1)^2
$$
Distance in $n$th second:
$$
s_n = s(n) - s(n-1) = \left[un + \frac{1}{2}an^2\right] - \left[u(n-1) + \frac{1}{2}a(n^2 - 2n + 1)\right]
$$
$$
= u + \frac{1}{2}a(2n - 1) = u + a\left(n - \frac{1}{2}\right)
$$
∎

## Proof 5: Relationship Between Speed and Velocity

**Statement:** Speed $v = |\vec{v}|$ where $\vec{v} = \frac{d\vec{r}}{dt}$

**Proof:**
Position vector: $\vec{r}(t)$.
Velocity vector: $\vec{v} = \frac{d\vec{r}}{dt}$.
Speed is magnitude of velocity vector:
$$
v = \|\vec{v}\| = \left\|\frac{d\vec{r}}{dt}\right\|
$$
For rectilinear motion along $x$-axis: $\vec{r} = x\hat{i}$, $\vec{v} = \dot{x}\hat{i}$, $v = |\dot{x}|$.
∎

## Proof 6: Average Velocity vs Average Speed

**Statement:** For any motion, $|\text{average velocity}| \leq \text{average speed}$, with equality iff motion is unidirectional.

**Proof:**
Let time interval $[0, T]$.
Average velocity: $\bar{\vec{v}} = \frac{1}{T}\int_0^T \vec{v}\,dt = \frac{\vec{r}(T) - \vec{r}(0)}{T}$
Average speed: $\bar{v} = \frac{1}{T}\int_0^T \|\vec{v}\|\,dt$

By triangle inequality for integrals:
$$
\left\|\int_0^T \vec{v}\,dt\right\| \leq \int_0^T \|\vec{v}\|\,dt
$$
$$
\implies \|\bar{\vec{v}}\| \leq \bar{v}
$$
Equality holds when $\vec{v}$ does not change direction (all $\vec{v}$ parallel).
∎

## Proof 7: Tangential and Normal Components of Acceleration

**Statement:** For any curved path, $\vec{a} = \frac{dv}{dt}\hat{t} + \frac{v^2}{\rho}\hat{n}$ where $\rho$ = radius of curvature.

**Proof:**
Let $\hat{t} = \frac{\vec{v}}{v}$ be unit tangent vector.
Velocity: $\vec{v} = v\hat{t}$.
Acceleration:
$$
\vec{a} = \frac{d\vec{v}}{dt} = \frac{dv}{dt}\hat{t} + v\frac{d\hat{t}}{dt}
$$
The change in $\hat{t}$ is perpendicular to $\hat{t}$ (since $\|\hat{t}\|=1$).
For a small angle $d\theta$ in time $dt$: $\|d\hat{t}\| = d\theta$.
Radius of curvature $\rho = \frac{ds}{d\theta}$, so $d\theta = \frac{ds}{\rho} = \frac{v\,dt}{\rho}$.
Thus $\frac{d\hat{t}}{dt} = \frac{v}{\rho}\hat{n}$ where $\hat{n}$ is unit normal.
Substituting:
$$
\vec{a} = \frac{dv}{dt}\hat{t} + \frac{v^2}{\rho}\hat{n}
$$
∎

## Proof 8: Distance vs Displacement for Variable Velocity

**Statement:** Distance $D = \int_0^T |v|\,dt$, Displacement $s = \int_0^T v\,dt$, and $D \geq |s|$.

**Proof:**
By triangle inequality for integrals:
$$
|s| = \left|\int_0^T v\,dt\right| \leq \int_0^T |v|\,dt = D
$$
Equality when $v$ does not change sign (unidirectional motion).
If $v$ changes sign at $t_1, t_2, \dots$:
$$
D = \sum \left|\int_{t_i}^{t_{i+1}} v\,dt\right| \geq \left|\sum \int_{t_i}^{t_{i+1}} v\,dt\right| = |s|
$$
∎
