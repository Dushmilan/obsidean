# Proofs: Variable Acceleration

**Syllabus:** GCE A/L Combined Mathematics (021) — Paper II, Topic 1.5

---

## Proof 1: $a = v\frac{dv}{ds}$ (Chain Rule)

**Statement:** $a = \frac{dv}{dt} = v\frac{dv}{ds}$

**Proof:**
By chain rule:
$$
a = \frac{dv}{dt} = \frac{dv}{ds} \cdot \frac{ds}{dt} = \frac{dv}{ds} \cdot v = v\frac{dv}{ds}
$$
∎

---

## Proof 2: Velocity from $a(s)$ — Energy Method

**Statement:** $v^2 = u^2 + 2\int_0^s a(s')\,ds'$

**Proof:**
Using $a = v\frac{dv}{ds}$:
$$
a(s) = v\frac{dv}{ds} \implies v\,dv = a(s)\,ds
$$
Integrate from $s=0$ (where $v=u$) to $s$:
$$
\int_u^v v'\,dv' = \int_0^s a(s')\,ds' \implies \frac{1}{2}(v^2 - u^2) = \int_0^s a(s')\,ds'
$$
$$
v^2 = u^2 + 2\int_0^s a(s')\,ds'
$$
∎

---

## Proof 3: Time from $a(s)$

**Statement:** $t = \int_0^s \frac{ds'}{\sqrt{u^2 + 2\int_0^{s'} a(s'')\,ds''}}$

**Proof:**
From Proof 2: $v(s') = \sqrt{u^2 + 2\int_0^{s'} a(s'')\,ds''}$
Since $v = \frac{ds}{dt}$, $dt = \frac{ds}{v(s)}$
$$
t = \int_0^s \frac{ds'}{v(s')} = \int_0^s \frac{ds'}{\sqrt{u^2 + 2\int_0^{s'} a(s'')\,ds''}}
$$
∎

---

## Proof 4: Time from $a(v)$

**Statement:** $t = \int_u^v \frac{dv'}{a(v')}$

**Proof:**
$a(v) = \frac{dv}{dt} \implies dt = \frac{dv}{a(v)}$
Integrate from $v=u$ at $t=0$ to $v$:
$$
t = \int_0^t dt' = \int_u^v \frac{dv'}{a(v')}
$$
∎

---

## Proof 5: Displacement from $a(v)$

**Statement:** $s = \int_u^v \frac{v'\,dv'}{a(v')}$

**Proof:**
Using $a = v\frac{dv}{ds}$:
$$
a(v) = v\frac{dv}{ds} \implies ds = \frac{v\,dv}{a(v)}
$$
Integrate from $v=u$ at $s=0$ to $v$:
$$
s = \int_0^s ds' = \int_u^v \frac{v'\,dv'}{a(v')}
$$
∎

## Proof 6: Linear Drag ($a = -kv$) — Velocity and Position

**Statement:** For $a = -kv$, $u$ at $t=0$:
- $v = u e^{-kt}$
- $x = \frac{u}{k}(1 - e^{-kt})$

**Proof:**
$a = \frac{dv}{dt} = -kv \implies \frac{dv}{v} = -k\,dt$
Integrate: $\ln v = -kt + C$, at $t=0$, $v=u \implies C = \ln u$
$$
\ln\frac{v}{u} = -kt \implies v = u e^{-kt}
$$
Position: $v = \frac{dx}{dt} = u e^{-kt}$
$$
x = \int_0^t u e^{-k\tau}\,d\tau = \frac{u}{k}(1 - e^{-kt})
$$
∎

---

## Proof 7: Quadratic Drag ($a = -kv^2$) — Velocity and Position

**Statement:** For $a = -kv^2$, $u$ at $t=0$:
- $v = \frac{u}{1 + ukt}$
- $x = \frac{1}{k}\ln(1 + ukt)$

**Proof:**
$\frac{dv}{dt} = -kv^2 \implies \frac{dv}{v^2} = -k\,dt$
Integrate: $-\frac{1}{v} = -kt + C$, at $t=0$, $v=u \implies C = -\frac{1}{u}$
$$
\frac{1}{v} = kt + \frac{1}{u} = \frac{1 + ukt}{u} \implies v = \frac{u}{1 + ukt}
$$
Position: $\frac{dx}{dt} = \frac{u}{1 + ukt}$
$$
x = \int_0^t \frac{u}{1 + uk\tau}\,d\tau = \frac{1}{k}\ln(1 + ukt)
$$
∎

## Proof 8: Vertical Motion with Linear Drag (Upward)

**Statement:** For $\frac{dv}{dt} = -g - \frac{k}{m}v$, $u$ upward at $t=0$:
- $v = \left(u + \frac{mg}{k}\right)e^{-kt/m} - \frac{mg}{k}$
- Max height at $t = \frac{m}{k}\ln\left(1 + \frac{ku}{mg}\right)$

**Proof:**
Let $v_{\text{term}} = -\frac{mg}{k}$ (terminal velocity downward).
$\frac{dv}{dt} = -g - \frac{k}{m}v = -\frac{k}{m}(v - v_{\text{term}})$
$\frac{dv}{v - v_{\text{term}}} = -\frac{k}{m}dt$
Integrate: $\ln|v - v_{\text{term}}| = -\frac{k}{m}t + C$
At $t=0$, $v = u \implies C = \ln|u - v_{\text{term}}| = \ln(u + \frac{mg}{k})$
$$
v - v_{\text{term}} = (u - v_{\text{term}})e^{-kt/m} = \left(u + \frac{mg}{k}\right)e^{-kt/m}
$$
$$
v = \left(u + \frac{mg}{k}\right)e^{-kt/m} - \frac{mg}{k}
$$
Max height when $v=0$:
$$
0 = \left(u + \frac{mg}{k}\right)e^{-kt/m} - \frac{mg}{k} \implies e^{kt/m} = 1 + \frac{ku}{mg}
$$
$$
t = \frac{m}{k}\ln\left(1 + \frac{ku}{mg}\right)
$$
∎

## Proof 9: Simple Harmonic Motion ($a = -\omega^2 x$)

**Statement:** For $a = -\omega^2 x$, $x=0$, $v=u$ at $t=0$:
- $v^2 = u^2 - \omega^2 x^2$
- $x = \frac{u}{\omega}\sin(\omega t)$
- Period $T = \frac{2\pi}{\omega}$

**Proof:**
Using $a = v\frac{dv}{dx} = -\omega^2 x$:
$$
v\,dv = -\omega^2 x\,dx \implies \frac{1}{2}v^2 = -\frac{1}{2}\omega^2 x^2 + C
$$
At $x=0$, $v=u \implies C = \frac{1}{2}u^2$
$$
v^2 = u^2 - \omega^2 x^2
$$
Since $v = \frac{dx}{dt} = \sqrt{u^2 - \omega^2 x^2}$:
$$
\frac{dx}{\sqrt{u^2 - \omega^2 x^2}} = dt \implies \frac{1}{\omega}\sin^{-1}\left(\frac{\omega x}{u}\right) = t
$$
$$
x = \frac{u}{\omega}\sin(\omega t)
$$
Period: $\omega T = 2\pi \implies T = \frac{2\pi}{\omega}$
∎

## Proof 10: Rocket Equation (Tsiolkovsky)

**Statement:** Rocket mass $m(t)$, exhaust speed $u$ relative to rocket, no external forces. Velocity:
$$
v = u\ln\left(\frac{m_0}{m}\right)
$$

**Proof:**
At time $t$, mass $m$, velocity $v$. In time $dt$, eject mass $dm$ ($dm < 0$) at speed $u$ relative to rocket.
Momentum conservation:
$$
mv = (m+dm)(v+dv) + (-dm)(v-u)
$$
$$
mv = mv + m\,dv + v\,dm + dm\,dv - v\,dm + u\,dm
$$
Neglect $dm\,dv$: $0 = m\,dv + u\,dm \implies m\,dv = -u\,dm$
$$
\int_0^v dv = -u\int_{m_0}^m \frac{dm}{m} \implies v = u\ln\left(\frac{m_0}{m}\right)
$$
∎

## Proof 11: Falling Body with Quadratic Drag — Terminal Velocity

**Statement:** For $\frac{dv}{dt} = g - \frac{k}{m}v^2$ (downward positive), terminal velocity $v_{\text{term}} = \sqrt{\frac{mg}{k}}$.

**Proof:**
Terminal velocity when $\frac{dv}{dt} = 0$:
$$
g - \frac{k}{m}v_{\text{term}}^2 = 0 \implies v_{\text{term}} = \sqrt{\frac{mg}{k}}
$$

Velocity as function of time:
$\frac{dv}{dt} = \frac{k}{m}(v_{\text{term}}^2 - v^2)$
$\int_0^v \frac{dv}{v_{\text{term}}^2 - v^2} = \frac{k}{m}t$
$\frac{1}{2v_{\text{term}}}\ln\left(\frac{v_{\text{term}}+v}{v_{\text{term}}-v}\right) = \frac{k}{m}t$
$v = v_{\text{term}}\tanh\left(\frac{v_{\text{term}}k}{m}t\right) = \sqrt{\frac{mg}{k}}\tanh\left(\sqrt{\frac{gk}{m}}t\right)$
∎

## Proof 12: Inverse Square Acceleration ($a = -\frac{k}{x^2}$)

**Statement:** For $a = -\frac{k}{x^2}$ (attractive force), starting from rest at $x = R$:
$$
v^2 = 2k\left(\frac{1}{x} - \frac{1}{R}\right)
$$

**Proof:**
$a = v\frac{dv}{dx} = -\frac{k}{x^2}$
$v\,dv = -k x^{-2}\,dx$
Integrate from $x=R$ (where $v=0$) to $x$:
$$
\int_0^v v'\,dv' = -k\int_R^x x'^{-2}\,dx'
$$
$$
\frac{1}{2}v^2 = k\left(\frac{1}{x} - \frac{1}{R}\right)
$$
$$
v^2 = 2k\left(\frac{1}{x} - \frac{1}{R}\right)
$$
∎

## Proof 13: Time to Fall from Rest under Inverse Square Law

**Statement:** Time to fall from $x=R$ to $x=0$ under $a = -\frac{k}{x^2}$:
$$
t = \frac{\pi}{2\sqrt{2}}\frac{R^{3/2}}{\sqrt{k}}
$$

**Proof:**
From Proof 12: $v = \frac{dx}{dt} = -\sqrt{2k\left(\frac{1}{x} - \frac{1}{R}\right)}$ (negative since $x$ decreases)
$dt = -\frac{dx}{\sqrt{2k}\sqrt{\frac{1}{x} - \frac{1}{R}}} = -\frac{dx}{\sqrt{2k}}\sqrt{\frac{xR}{R-x}}$
Let $x = R\sin^2\theta$, $dx = 2R\sin\theta\cos\theta\,d\theta$:
$t = \int_{\pi/2}^0 -\frac{2R\sin\theta\cos\theta\,d\theta}{\sqrt{2k}}\sqrt{\frac{R\sin^2\theta}{R\cos^2\theta}} = \int_0^{\pi/2} \frac{2R\sin^2\theta\,d\theta}{\sqrt{2k}}$
$= \frac{2R}{\sqrt{2k}}\int_0^{\pi/2} \sin^2\theta\,d\theta = \frac{2R}{\sqrt{2k}}\cdot\frac{\pi}{4} = \frac{\pi R}{2\sqrt{2k}} = \frac{\pi}{2\sqrt{2}}\frac{R^{3/2}}{\sqrt{k}}$
(using $R$ in place of $\sqrt{k}$... wait, dimensions: $k = GM$, so $k$ has units $L^3/T^2$, $\frac{R^{3/2}}{\sqrt{k}}$ has units $T$. Correct.)
∎

## Proof 14: Cycloid as Tautochrone (Brachistochrone)

**Statement:** The curve of fastest descent under gravity is a cycloid.

**Proof Sketch:**
Using variational calculus (Fermat's principle / Euler-Lagrange):
Time functional: $T = \int \frac{ds}{\sqrt{2gy}} = \int \frac{\sqrt{1+y'^2}}{\sqrt{2gy}}\,dx$
Euler-Lagrange equation for $f(y, y') = \frac{\sqrt{1+y'^2}}{\sqrt{y}}$:
Since $f$ independent of $x$: $f - y'\frac{\partial f}{\partial y'} = \text{constant}$
$\frac{\sqrt{1+y'^2}}{\sqrt{y}} - y'\frac{y'}{\sqrt{y}\sqrt{1+y'^2}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{y}\sqrt{1+y'^2}} = \frac{1}{C}$
$y(1+y'^2) = C^2$
Solution: $x = C^2(\theta - \sin\theta)$, $y = C^2(1 - \cos\theta)$ — a cycloid.
∎
