# Proofs: Relative Motion

**Syllabus:** GCE A/L Combined Mathematics (021) — Paper II, Topic 1.4

---

## Proof 1: Relative Velocity Definition

**Statement:** $\vec{v}_{A/B} = \vec{v}_A - \vec{v}_B$

**Proof:**
Position of A: $\vec{r}_A$, position of B: $\vec{r}_B$.
Relative position of A with respect to B: $\vec{r}_{A/B} = \vec{r}_A - \vec{r}_B$.
Differentiate with respect to time:
$$
\vec{v}_{A/B} = \frac{d\vec{r}_{A/B}}{dt} = \frac{d\vec{r}_A}{dt} - \frac{d\vec{r}_B}{dt} = \vec{v}_A - \vec{v}_B
$$
∎

---

## Proof 2: Relative Acceleration

**Statement:** $\vec{a}_{A/B} = \vec{a}_A - \vec{a}_B$

**Proof:**
Differentiate relative velocity:
$$
\vec{a}_{A/B} = \frac{d\vec{v}_{A/B}}{dt} = \frac{d\vec{v}_A}{dt} - \frac{d\vec{v}_B}{dt} = \vec{a}_A - \vec{a}_B
$$
∎

---

## Proof 3: Magnitude of Relative Velocity

**Statement:** $|\vec{v}_{A/B}|^2 = v_A^2 + v_B^2 - 2v_A v_B \cos\theta$

**Proof:**
$$
|\vec{v}_{A/B}|^2 = (\vec{v}_A - \vec{v}_B)\cdot(\vec{v}_A - \vec{v}_B) = v_A^2 + v_B^2 - 2\vec{v}_A\cdot\vec{v}_B
$$
Since $\vec{v}_A\cdot\vec{v}_B = v_A v_B \cos\theta$:
$$
|\vec{v}_{A/B}|^2 = v_A^2 + v_B^2 - 2v_A v_B \cos\theta
$$
∎

## Proof 4: Closest Approach (Constant Velocities)

**Statement:** Two particles with constant velocities $\vec{v}_A$, $\vec{v}_B$ and initial separation $\vec{r}_0 = \vec{r}_A(0) - \vec{r}_B(0)$. Time of closest approach:
$$
t_{\text{min}} = -\frac{\vec{r}_0 \cdot \vec{v}_{\text{rel}}}{|\vec{v}_{\text{rel}}|^2}
$$
Minimum distance:
$$
d_{\text{min}} = \frac{|\vec{r}_0 \times \vec{v}_{\text{rel}}|}{|\vec{v}_{\text{rel}}|}
$$

**Proof:**
Relative position at time $t$:
$$
\vec{r}_{\text{rel}}(t) = \vec{r}_0 + \vec{v}_{\text{rel}} t
$$
Distance squared:
$$
d^2(t) = |\vec{r}_{\text{rel}}(t)|^2 = |\vec{r}_0 + \vec{v}_{\text{rel}} t|^2 = r_0^2 + 2(\vec{r}_0\cdot\vec{v}_{\text{rel}})t + v_{\text{rel}}^2 t^2
$$
Minimize: $\frac{d}{dt}d^2 = 0$
$$
2\vec{r}_0\cdot\vec{v}_{\text{rel}} + 2v_{\text{rel}}^2 t = 0 \implies t_{\text{min}} = -\frac{\vec{r}_0\cdot\vec{v}_{\text{rel}}}{v_{\text{rel}}^2}
$$
Substitute into $d^2(t)$:
$$
d_{\text{min}}^2 = r_0^2 - \frac{(\vec{r}_0\cdot\vec{v}_{\text{rel}})^2}{v_{\text{rel}}^2} = \frac{r_0^2 v_{\text{rel}}^2 - (\vec{r}_0\cdot\vec{v}_{\text{rel}})^2}{v_{\text{rel}}^2}
$$
Numerator = $|\vec{r}_0 \times \vec{v}_{\text{rel}}|^2$ (Lagrange's identity).
Thus $d_{\text{min}} = \frac{|\vec{r}_0 \times \vec{v}_{\text{rel}}|}{|\vec{v}_{\text{rel}}|}$.
∎

## Proof 5: Interception Condition

**Statement:** Particle A at $\vec{r}_A$ with velocity $\vec{v}_A$. Particle B at $\vec{r}_B$ with speed $v_B$ (direction controllable). Interception possible iff:
$$
|\vec{r}_A - \vec{r}_B| \leq v_B \cdot \frac{|\vec{r}_A - \vec{r}_B| \cdot |\vec{v}_A| \cos\phi + \sqrt{(\vec{r}_A - \vec{r}_B)^2 v_B^2 - |\vec{r}_A - \vec{r}_B|^2 v_A^2 \sin^2\phi}}{v_B^2 - v_A^2}
$$
(Solutions exist when $v_B > v_A$ for head-on approach)

**Proof:**
Collision requires $\vec{r}_A + \vec{v}_A t = \vec{r}_B + \vec{v}_B t$ for some $t > 0$.
$\vec{v}_B t = \vec{r}_A - \vec{r}_B + \vec{v}_A t$
Square both sides:
$$
v_B^2 t^2 = |\vec{r}_A - \vec{r}_B|^2 + v_A^2 t^2 + 2(\vec{r}_A - \vec{r}_B)\cdot\vec{v}_A t
$$
$$
(v_B^2 - v_A^2)t^2 - 2(\vec{r}_A - \vec{r}_B)\cdot\vec{v}_A t - |\vec{r}_A - \vec{r}_B|^2 = 0
$$
Quadratic in $t$. Real positive solution exists iff discriminant $\geq 0$ and roots positive.
∎

## Proof 6: Rain/Wind Apparent Velocity

**Statement:** Rain falling vertically at speed $v_r$. Observer moving horizontally at speed $v_p$. Rain appears at angle $\theta$ to vertical where $\tan\theta = \frac{v_p}{v_r}$.

**Proof:**
Rain velocity (ground frame): $\vec{v}_r = -v_r \hat{j}$
Observer velocity: $\vec{v}_p = v_p \hat{i}$
Relative velocity of rain wrt observer:
$$
\vec{v}_{r/p} = \vec{v}_r - \vec{v}_p = -v_p \hat{i} - v_r \hat{j}
$$
Angle $\theta$ to vertical (downward):
$$
\tan\theta = \frac{\text{horizontal component}}{\text{vertical component}} = \frac{v_p}{v_r}
$$
∎

## Proof 7: River Crossing — Shortest Path

**Statement:** River width $d$, current speed $v_r$, boat speed in still water $v_b$. To cross perpendicularly:
- Angle upstream: $\sin\theta = \frac{v_r}{v_b}$
- Time: $t = \frac{d}{v_b\cos\theta} = \frac{d}{\sqrt{v_b^2 - v_r^2}}$

**Proof:**
Boat velocity relative to water: $\vec{v}_{b/w} = v_b\cos\theta \hat{j} + v_b\sin\theta \hat{i}$ (across + upstream)
Water velocity: $\vec{v}_w = v_r \hat{i}$
Ground velocity: $\vec{v}_g = \vec{v}_{b/w} + \vec{v}_w = (v_b\sin\theta + v_r)\hat{i} + v_b\cos\theta \hat{j}$
For perpendicular crossing (no downstream drift): $v_b\sin\theta + v_r = 0 \implies \sin\theta = -\frac{v_r}{v_b}$ (upstream)
Time to cross: $t = \frac{d}{v_b\cos\theta} = \frac{d}{v_b\sqrt{1 - v_r^2/v_b^2}} = \frac{d}{\sqrt{v_b^2 - v_r^2}}$
∎

---

## Proof 8: River Crossing — Shortest Time

**Statement:** Shortest time crossing: point straight across. Time $t = \frac{d}{v_b}$, drift $D = \frac{v_r d}{v_b}$.

**Proof:**
Point straight across: $\vec{v}_{b/w} = v_b \hat{j}$
Ground velocity: $\vec{v}_g = v_r \hat{i} + v_b \hat{j}$
Time to cross: $t = \frac{d}{v_b}$
Drift: $D = v_r t = \frac{v_r d}{v_b}$
This minimizes $t$ since $v_b\cos\theta \leq v_b$.
∎

## Proof 9: Aircraft Navigation — Course Correction

**Statement:** Airspeed $v_a$, wind $\vec{v}_w$. Desired ground track $\vec{v}_g$ (magnitude $v_g$). Course to steer $\theta$ satisfies:
$$
v_a^2 = v_g^2 + v_w^2 - 2v_g v_w \cos\alpha
$$
where $\alpha$ = angle between desired track and wind.

**Proof:**
$\vec{v}_g = \vec{v}_a + \vec{v}_w \implies \vec{v}_a = \vec{v}_g - \vec{v}_w$
$v_a^2 = |\vec{v}_g - \vec{v}_w|^2 = v_g^2 + v_w^2 - 2\vec{v}_g\cdot\vec{v}_w = v_g^2 + v_w^2 - 2v_g v_w \cos\alpha$
Sine rule for direction:
$$
\frac{\sin\theta}{v_w} = \frac{\sin\alpha}{v_a} \implies \sin\theta = \frac{v_w}{v_a}\sin\alpha
$$
∎

## Proof 10: Relative Motion with Constant Acceleration

**Statement:** If $\vec{a}_A$, $\vec{a}_B$ constant, all SUVAT equations apply to relative quantities.

**Proof:**
$\vec{a}_{\text{rel}} = \vec{a}_A - \vec{a}_B = \text{constant}$.
$\vec{v}_{\text{rel}}(t) = \vec{v}_{\text{rel}}(0) + \vec{a}_{\text{rel}} t$
$\vec{r}_{\text{rel}}(t) = \vec{r}_{\text{rel}}(0) + \vec{v}_{\text{rel}}(0)t + \frac{1}{2}\vec{a}_{\text{rel}}t^2$
$v_{\text{rel}}^2 = u_{\text{rel}}^2 + 2\vec{a}_{\text{rel}}\cdot\vec{s}_{\text{rel}}$
Since $\vec{a}_{\text{rel}}$ is constant, the derivation of SUVAT equations is identical to 1D case, just with vectors.
∎

## Proof 11: Constraint — Inextensible String

**Statement:** For two particles connected by inextensible string over fixed pulley: $a_A = -a_B$.

**Proof:**
Let $s_A$, $s_B$ be displacements from pulley. String length $L = s_A + s_B + \text{constant}$.
Differentiate twice: $\frac{d^2L}{dt^2} = 0 = a_A + a_B \implies a_A = -a_B$.
∎

## Proof 12: Constraint — Movable Pulley

**Statement:** String fixed at one end, passes under movable pulley (mass $m_2$), over fixed pulley, attached to mass $m_1$. If $a_1$ = acceleration of $m_1$, $a_2$ = acceleration of movable pulley: $a_1 = -2a_2$.

**Proof:**
Let $x_1$ = displacement of $m_1$ downward. Let $x_2$ = displacement of movable pulley upward.
String length: $L = x_1 + (x_1 - x_2) + (x_1 - x_2) + \text{constant} = 3x_1 - 2x_2 + C$
Differentiate twice: $0 = 3a_1 - 2(-a_2)$ (since $x_2$ upward, acceleration downward is $-a_2$)
Wait — careful: if $x_1$ down +ve, $x_2$ up +ve, then length = $x_1 + (x_1 - x_2) + (x_1 - x_2) = 3x_1 - 2x_2$.
$a_1 = \ddot{x}_1$ (down), $a_2 = \ddot{x}_2$ (up).
$0 = 3a_1 - 2a_2 \implies a_2 = \frac{3}{2}a_1$... that's not right.

Let me re-derive:
Fixed end → down to movable pulley → up to fixed pulley → down to $m_1$.
Length = (fixed to movable) + (movable to fixed) + (fixed to $m_1$)
$= y_2 + y_2 + x_1 = 2y_2 + x_1$ where $y_2$ = distance from fixed to movable pulley (down +ve), $x_1$ = distance from fixed to $m_1$ (down +ve).
$L = 2y_2 + x_1 = \text{constant}$.
$\ddot{L} = 2\ddot{y}_2 + \ddot{x}_1 = 0 \implies 2a_2 + a_1 = 0$ where $a_2 = \ddot{y}_2$ (downward acceleration of movable pulley).
So $a_1 = -2a_2$. If $m_1$ goes down (+ve), movable pulley goes up (-ve $a_2$).
∎

## Proof 13: Centre of Mass Velocity

**Statement:** $\vec{v}_{CM} = \frac{\sum m_i \vec{v}_i}{M}$ where $M = \sum m_i$.

**Proof:**
Position of CM: $\vec{r}_{CM} = \frac{\sum m_i \vec{r}_i}{M}$
Velocity: $\vec{v}_{CM} = \frac{d\vec{r}_{CM}}{dt} = \frac{\sum m_i \frac{d\vec{r}_i}{dt}}{M} = \frac{\sum m_i \vec{v}_i}{M}$
∎

---

## Proof 14: Momentum in CM Frame

**Statement:** Total momentum in CM frame is zero: $\sum m_i \vec{v}_i' = 0$.

**Proof:**
In CM frame: $\vec{v}_i' = \vec{v}_i - \vec{v}_{CM}$
Total momentum: $\sum m_i \vec{v}_i' = \sum m_i \vec{v}_i - \sum m_i \vec{v}_{CM} = M\vec{v}_{CM} - M\vec{v}_{CM} = 0$
∎
