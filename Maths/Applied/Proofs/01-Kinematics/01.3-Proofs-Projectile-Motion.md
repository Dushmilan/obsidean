# Proofs: Projectile Motion

**Syllabus:** GCE A/L Combined Mathematics (021) — Paper II, Topic 1.3

---

## Proof 1: Equation of Trajectory (Cartesian Form)

**Statement:** $y = x\tan\theta - \frac{gx^2}{2u^2\cos^2\theta}$

**Proof:**
From parametric equations:
$$
x = (u\cos\theta)t \implies t = \frac{x}{u\cos\theta}
$$
$$
y = (u\sin\theta)t - \frac{1}{2}gt^2
$$
Substitute $t$:
$$
y = (u\sin\theta)\left(\frac{x}{u\cos\theta}\right) - \frac{1}{2}g\left(\frac{x}{u\cos\theta}\right)^2
$$
$$
y = x\tan\theta - \frac{gx^2}{2u^2\cos^2\theta}
$$
Using $\sec^2\theta = 1/\cos^2\theta$:
$$
y = x\tan\theta - \frac{gx^2}{2u^2}\sec^2\theta
$$
∎

---

## Proof 2: Time of Flight $T = \frac{2u\sin\theta}{g}$

**Proof:**
At $t = T$, $y = 0$:
$$
(u\sin\theta)T - \frac{1}{2}gT^2 = 0 \implies T(u\sin\theta - \frac{1}{2}gT) = 0
$$
Non-zero root: $T = \frac{2u\sin\theta}{g}$
∎

---

## Proof 3: Maximum Height $H = \frac{u^2\sin^2\theta}{2g}$

**Proof:**
At max height, $v_y = 0$:
$$
v_y = u\sin\theta - gt = 0 \implies t = \frac{u\sin\theta}{g}
$$
Substitute into $y$:
$$
H = (u\sin\theta)\left(\frac{u\sin\theta}{g}\right) - \frac{1}{2}g\left(\frac{u\sin\theta}{g}\right)^2
= \frac{u^2\sin^2\theta}{g} - \frac{u^2\sin^2\theta}{2g}
= \frac{u^2\sin^2\theta}{2g}
$$
∎

---

## Proof 4: Range $R = \frac{u^2\sin2\theta}{g}$

**Proof:**
At $t = T = \frac{2u\sin\theta}{g}$:
$$
R = x(T) = (u\cos\theta)T = u\cos\theta \cdot \frac{2u\sin\theta}{g} = \frac{2u^2\sin\theta\cos\theta}{g}
$$
Using $\sin2\theta = 2\sin\theta\cos\theta$:
$$
R = \frac{u^2\sin2\theta}{g}
$$
∎

---

## Proof 5: Two Angles for Same Range

**Statement:** For $R < \frac{u^2}{g}$, two angles $\theta_1, \theta_2$ give same range with $\theta_1 + \theta_2 = 90^\circ$.

**Proof:**
Range equation: $R = \frac{u^2\sin2\theta}{g}$
For fixed $u, R$: $\sin2\theta = \frac{gR}{u^2} = k$ (constant $< 1$)
Two solutions in $(0^\circ, 180^\circ)$: $2\theta = \alpha$ or $2\theta = 180^\circ - \alpha$
Thus $\theta_1 = \frac{\alpha}{2}$, $\theta_2 = 90^\circ - \frac{\alpha}{2}$
Sum: $\theta_1 + \theta_2 = 90^\circ$
∎

## Proof 6: Speed at Any Height

**Statement:** $v^2 = u^2 - 2gy$ (speed depends only on height)

**Proof:**
Energy conservation: $K + U = \text{constant}$
Initial: $K_0 = \frac{1}{2}mu^2$, $U_0 = 0$
At height $y$: $K = \frac{1}{2}mv^2$, $U = mgy$
$$
\frac{1}{2}mv^2 + mgy = \frac{1}{2}mu^2 \implies v^2 = u^2 - 2gy
$$
Alternatively from components:
$$
v_x^2 = u^2\cos^2\theta, \quad v_y^2 = u^2\sin^2\theta - 2gy
$$
$$
v^2 = v_x^2 + v_y^2 = u^2(\cos^2\theta + \sin^2\theta) - 2gy = u^2 - 2gy
$$
∎

## Proof 7: Maximum Range Up an Inclined Plane

**Statement:** $R_{\text{max, up}} = \frac{u^2}{g(1+\sin\alpha)}$ at $\theta = 45^\circ + \frac{\alpha}{2}$

**Proof:**
Range up plane (angle $\alpha$):
$$
R = \frac{2u^2\cos(\theta-\alpha)\sin(\theta-\alpha)}{g\cos\alpha} = \frac{u^2\sin2(\theta-\alpha)}{g\cos\alpha}
$$
Maximum when $\sin2(\theta-\alpha) = 1 \implies 2(\theta-\alpha) = 90^\circ \implies \theta = 45^\circ + \frac{\alpha}{2}$
Then:
$$
R_{\text{max}} = \frac{u^2}{g\cos\alpha}
$$
Wait — need to check. Actually:
For up plane: $R = \frac{u^2}{g\cos\alpha}[\sin2\theta\cos\alpha + \cos2\theta\sin\alpha] = \frac{u^2}{g}[\sin2\theta + \cos2\theta\tan\alpha]$
Differentiate wrt $\theta$: $2u^2\cos2\theta - 2u^2\sin2\theta\tan\alpha = 0$
$\tan2\theta = \cot\alpha = \tan(90^\circ - \alpha) \implies 2\theta = 90^\circ - \alpha \implies \theta = 45^\circ - \alpha/2$
Hmm, let me re-derive carefully.

**Correct derivation:**
Coordinate along plane ($x'$) and perpendicular ($y'$):
$a_{x'} = -g\sin\alpha$, $a_{y'} = -g\cos\alpha$
$u_{x'} = u\cos(\theta-\alpha)$, $u_{y'} = u\sin(\theta-\alpha)$
Time of flight: $y' = 0 \implies T = \frac{2u\sin(\theta-\alpha)}{g\cos\alpha}$
Range: $R = u_{x'}T + \frac{1}{2}a_{x'}T^2$
Substitute:
$$
R = \frac{2u^2\cos(\theta-\alpha)\sin(\theta-\alpha)}{g\cos\alpha} - \frac{g\sin\alpha}{2}\left(\frac{2u\sin(\theta-\alpha)}{g\cos\alpha}\right)^2
$$
Simplify:
$$
R = \frac{u^2}{g\cos^2\alpha}\left[2\cos(\theta-\alpha)\sin(\theta-\alpha)\cos\alpha - 2\sin^2(\theta-\alpha)\sin\alpha\right]
$$
Using $2\cos A\sin A = \sin2A$ and $\sin^2 A = \frac{1-\cos2A}{2}$:
$$
R = \frac{u^2}{g\cos^2\alpha}[\sin2(\theta-\alpha)\cos\alpha - (1-\cos2(\theta-\alpha))\sin\alpha]
$$
$$
= \frac{u^2}{g\cos^2\alpha}[\sin(2\theta-2\alpha+\alpha) - \sin\alpha] = \frac{u^2}{g\cos^2\alpha}[\sin(2\theta-\alpha) - \sin\alpha]
$$
Maximum when $\sin(2\theta-\alpha) = 1 \implies 2\theta-\alpha = 90^\circ \implies \theta = 45^\circ + \alpha/2$
Then $R_{\text{max}} = \frac{u^2}{g\cos^2\alpha}(1 - \sin\alpha) = \frac{u^2(1-\sin\alpha)}{g(1-\sin^2\alpha)} = \frac{u^2}{g(1+\sin\alpha)}$
∎

## Proof 8: Range Down an Inclined Plane

**Statement:** $R_{\text{down}} = \frac{u^2}{g\cos^2\alpha}[\sin(2\theta+\alpha) + \sin\alpha]$

**Proof:**
Similar to above but $y'$ axis upward normal, plane slopes downward.
$u_{x'} = u\cos(\theta+\alpha)$, $u_{y'} = u\sin(\theta+\alpha)$
$a_{x'} = g\sin\alpha$, $a_{y'} = -g\cos\alpha$
Time of flight: $T = \frac{2u\sin(\theta+\alpha)}{g\cos\alpha}$
Range: $R = u_{x'}T + \frac{1}{2}a_{x'}T^2$
Substitute and simplify:
$$
R = \frac{u^2}{g\cos^2\alpha}[\sin(2\theta+\alpha) + \sin\alpha]
$$
Maximum when $\sin(2\theta+\alpha) = 1 \implies 2\theta+\alpha = 90^\circ \implies \theta = 45^\circ - \alpha/2$
$R_{\text{max, down}} = \frac{u^2}{g\cos^2\alpha}(1 + \sin\alpha) = \frac{u^2}{g(1-\sin\alpha)}$
∎

## Proof 9: Envelope of Trajectories (Safety Parabola)

**Statement:** Boundary of all points reachable by projectiles of speed $u$: $y = \frac{u^2}{2g} - \frac{gx^2}{2u^2}$

**Proof:**
Family of trajectories (parameter $\theta$):
$$
y = x\tan\theta - \frac{gx^2}{2u^2}\sec^2\theta = x\tan\theta - \frac{gx^2}{2u^2}(1 + \tan^2\theta)
$$
Let $t = \tan\theta$:
$$
y = tx - \frac{gx^2}{2u^2}(1 + t^2)
$$
For fixed $(x,y)$, this is quadratic in $t$:
$$
\frac{gx^2}{2u^2}t^2 - xt + \left(y + \frac{gx^2}{2u^2}\right) = 0
$$
Real $t$ exists (point is reachable) iff discriminant $\geq 0$:
$$
x^2 - 4\frac{gx^2}{2u^2}\left(y + \frac{gx^2}{2u^2}\right) \geq 0
$$
$$
1 - \frac{2g}{u^2}\left(y + \frac{gx^2}{2u^2}\right) \geq 0 \implies y \leq \frac{u^2}{2g} - \frac{gx^2}{2u^2}
$$
Boundary (envelope) when discriminant = 0:
$$
y = \frac{u^2}{2g} - \frac{gx^2}{2u^2}
$$
∎

## Proof 10: Velocity at Impact (Same Level)

**Statement:** Speed on landing equals projection speed; angle of impact = $-\theta$.

**Proof:**
At landing ($y=0$), from energy: $v = u$.
Components: $v_x = u\cos\theta$, $v_y = -u\sin\theta$ (since $v_y^2 = u^2\sin^2\theta$)
$\tan\phi = \frac{v_y}{v_x} = -\tan\theta \implies \phi = -\theta$
∎

## Proof 11: Collision of Two Projectiles

**Condition:** Two particles projected from same point with $(u_1, \theta_1)$ and $(u_2, \theta_2)$ collide iff their position vectors coincide at same $t > 0$.

**Proof:**
$\vec{r}_1(t) = (u_1\cos\theta_1\,t)\hat{i} + (u_1\sin\theta_1\,t - \frac{1}{2}gt^2)\hat{j}$
$\vec{r}_2(t) = (u_2\cos\theta_2\,t)\hat{i} + (u_2\sin\theta_2\,t - \frac{1}{2}gt^2)\hat{j}$
Collision requires:
$$
u_1\cos\theta_1 = u_2\cos\theta_2 \quad \text{(horizontal velocities equal)}
$$
$$
u_1\sin\theta_1 = u_2\sin\theta_2 \quad \text{(vertical velocities equal)}
$$
Thus $\vec{u}_1 = \vec{u}_2$ — they must have identical initial velocities.
If projected from different points, subtract position vectors and equate.
∎

## Proof 12: Bouncing Projectile (Coefficient of Restitution $e$)

**Statement:** After bounce, vertical speed $v_y' = e v_y$, horizontal speed unchanged.

**Proof:**
Impulse during impact is vertical (smooth surface).
Horizontal momentum conserved: $v_x' = v_x$.
Coefficient of restitution: $e = \frac{\text{speed of separation}}{\text{speed of approach}} = \frac{v_y'}{-v_y}$ (since $v_y < 0$ before impact, $v_y' > 0$ after)
Thus $v_y' = -e v_y$.
New angle: $\tan\theta' = \frac{v_y'}{v_x'} = \frac{e v_y}{v_x} = e\tan\theta$
New speed: $v' = \sqrt{v_x^2 + (ev_y)^2} = v\sqrt{\cos^2\theta + e^2\sin^2\theta}$
∎
