# Proofs: Graphical Methods (Motion Graphs)

**Syllabus:** GCE A/L Combined Mathematics (021) — Paper II, Topic 1.6

---

## Proof 1: Gradient of $x$–$t$ Graph = Velocity

**Statement:** $v = \frac{dx}{dt} = \text{gradient of } x\text{–}t \text{ graph}$

**Proof:**
Consider two points on $x$–$t$ graph: $(t, x)$ and $(t+\Delta t, x+\Delta x)$.
Gradient of chord: $\frac{\Delta x}{\Delta t}$.
As $\Delta t \to 0$, chord becomes tangent.
$$
\lim_{\Delta t \to 0} \frac{\Delta x}{\Delta t} = \frac{dx}{dt} = v
$$
∎

---

## Proof 2: Area Under $v$–$t$ Graph = Displacement

**Statement:** $s = \int_{t_1}^{t_2} v\,dt = \text{signed area under } v\text{–}t \text{ graph}$

**Proof:**
$v = \frac{dx}{dt} \implies dx = v\,dt$
Integrate from $t_1$ to $t_2$:
$$
\int_{x_1}^{x_2} dx = \int_{t_1}^{t_2} v\,dt \implies x_2 - x_1 = \int_{t_1}^{t_2} v\,dt
$$
The integral $\int_{t_1}^{t_2} v\,dt$ is the area under the $v$–$t$ curve.
Sign convention: $v > 0$ gives positive area (forward displacement), $v < 0$ gives negative area (backward displacement).
∎

---

## Proof 3: Gradient of $v$–$t$ Graph = Acceleration

**Statement:** $a = \frac{dv}{dt} = \text{gradient of } v\text{–}t \text{ graph}$

**Proof:**
Same as Proof 1 with $v$ in place of $x$.
$$
\lim_{\Delta t \to 0} \frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t} = \frac{dv}{dt} = a
$$
∎

---

## Proof 4: Area Under $a$–$t$ Graph = Change in Velocity

**Statement:** $\Delta v = \int_{t_1}^{t_2} a\,dt = \text{area under } a\text{–}t \text{ graph}$

**Proof:**
$a = \frac{dv}{dt} \implies dv = a\,dt$
Integrate:
$$
\int_{v_1}^{v_2} dv = \int_{t_1}^{t_2} a\,dt \implies v_2 - v_1 = \int_{t_1}^{t_2} a\,dt
$$
∎

---

## Proof 5: Distance Travelled = Total Area Under $|v|$–$t$ Graph

**Statement:** Distance $D = \int_{t_1}^{t_2} |v|\,dt$

**Proof:**
Distance = sum of absolute displacements in each direction.
Split interval $[t_1, t_2]$ at points where $v=0$: $t_1 < \tau_1 < \tau_2 < \dots < t_2$.
$$
D = \sum_i \left|\int_{\tau_i}^{\tau_{i+1}} v\,dt\right| = \int_{t_1}^{t_2} |v|\,dt
$$
Graphically: reflect negative parts of $v$–$t$ graph above $t$-axis, then total area.
∎

## Proof 6: SUVAT from $v$–$t$ Graph (Constant Acceleration)

**Statement:** For constant $a$, $v$–$t$ graph is a straight line with gradient $a$, area = trapezium = $s$.

**Proof:**
$v$–$t$ line: passes through $(0, u)$ and $(t, v)$, slope $a = \frac{v-u}{t}$.
Area = area of trapezium = $\frac{1}{2}(u+v)t = s$.
This is Equation 4: $s = \frac{1}{2}(u+v)t$.
Also $v = u + at$ (from slope).
From area formula: $s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2$ (substitute $v = u+at$).
From $v = u+at$ and $s = \frac{1}{2}(u+v)t$: eliminate $t \to v^2 = u^2 + 2as$.
∎

---

## Proof 7: Displacement from $x$–$t$ Parabola (Constant $a$)

**Statement:** For constant $a$, $x$–$t$ graph is parabola $x = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2$.

**Proof:**
$v = u + at$, $x = \int v\,dt = \int_0^t (u+at')\,dt' = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2$.
This is a parabola opening upward if $a>0$, downward if $a<0$.
Vertex at $t = -\frac{u}{a}$ (if $u$ and $a$ opposite signs).
At vertex, $v = u + a(-\frac{u}{a}) = 0$ — momentary rest.
∎

## Proof 8: Average Velocity from $x$–$t$ Graph

**Statement:** Average velocity $\bar{v} = \frac{x(t_2) - x(t_1)}{t_2 - t_1} = \text{slope of chord on } x\text{–}t \text{ graph}$

**Proof:**
By definition: $\bar{v} = \frac{\Delta x}{\Delta t}$.
On $x$–$t$ graph, points $(t_1, x_1)$ and $(t_2, x_2)$.
Slope of chord = $\frac{x_2 - x_1}{t_2 - t_1} = \bar{v}$.
∎

---

## Proof 9: Mean Value Theorem for $v$–$t$ Graph

**Statement:** If $v$ is continuous on $[t_1, t_2]$, there exists $\tau \in (t_1, t_2)$ such that $v(\tau) = \bar{v} = \frac{1}{t_2-t_1}\int_{t_1}^{t_2} v\,dt$.

**Proof:**
By Mean Value Theorem for integrals (or Fundamental Theorem of Calculus):
Let $F(t) = \int_{t_1}^t v(\tau)\,d\tau$. Then $F(t_2) = \int_{t_1}^{t_2} v\,dt = s$.
$F'(t) = v(t)$.
By MVT for derivatives: $\exists \tau \in (t_1, t_2)$ such that $F'(\tau) = \frac{F(t_2) - F(t_1)}{t_2 - t_1} = \frac{s}{t_2 - t_1} = \bar{v}$.
Thus $v(\tau) = \bar{v}$.
Graphically: horizontal line at height $\bar{v}$ has same area as $v$–$t$ curve on $[t_1, t_2]$.
∎

## Proof 10: Maximum Displacement from $v$–$t$ Graph

**Statement:** Displacement $x(t)$ is maximum when $v(t)$ changes from $+$ to $-$ (crosses $t$-axis from above).

**Proof:**
$x(t) = x(0) + \int_0^t v(\tau)\,d\tau$.
$\frac{dx}{dt} = v(t)$.
At maximum $x$: $\frac{dx}{dt} = 0 \implies v = 0$, and $\frac{d^2x}{dt^2} = a < 0 \implies v$ decreasing through zero.
Thus $v$ crosses $t$-axis from positive to negative.
∎

## Proof 11: Acceleration Sign from $x$–$t$ Concavity

**Statement:** $x$–$t$ concave up $\iff a > 0$; concave down $\iff a < 0$.

**Proof:**
$x''(t) = a(t)$.
Concave up $\iff x''(t) > 0 \iff a > 0$.
Concave down $\iff x''(t) < 0 \iff a < 0$.
∎

---

## Proof 12: Velocity Sign from $x$–$t$ Gradient

**Statement:** $x$–$t$ increasing $\iff v > 0$; decreasing $\iff v < 0$; horizontal $\iff v = 0$.

**Proof:**
$v = \frac{dx}{dt}$.
Increasing $\iff$ derivative $> 0 \iff v > 0$.
Decreasing $\iff$ derivative $< 0 \iff v < 0$.
Horizontal $\iff$ derivative $= 0 \iff v = 0$.
∎

## Proof 13: Area of Parabolic Segment (Variable Acceleration)

**Statement:** For $v = kt^2$ (from $t=0$ to $T$), area = $\frac{1}{3}kT^3$.

**Proof:**
$\int_0^T kt^2\,dt = \frac{1}{3}kT^3$.
Geometric interpretation: parabolic segment area = $\frac{2}{3}$ of circumscribing rectangle (Archimedes).
Rectangle base $T$, height $kT^2$, area $kT^3$.
Parabolic area = $\frac{2}{3}kT^3$? Wait:
Actually $\int_0^T kt^2\,dt = \frac{1}{3}kT^3$.
Rectangle area = $kT^2 \cdot T = kT^3$.
Ratio = $\frac{1}{3}$, not $\frac{2}{3}$.
The $\frac{2}{3}$ is for parabola $y = kx^2$ from $x=0$ to $x=a$, area under curve = $\frac{1}{3}ka^3$, circumscribing rectangle = $ka^2 \cdot a = ka^3$, ratio $\frac{1}{3}$.
The $\frac{2}{3}$ is for the area of the parabolic segment bounded by the parabola and a chord (not the $x$-axis).
∎

## Proof 14: Trapezoidal Rule for $v$–$t$ Graph

**Statement:** For discrete data $(t_i, v_i)$, displacement $\approx \sum \frac{v_i + v_{i+1}}{2}(t_{i+1} - t_i)$.

**Proof:**
Approximate $v(t)$ by straight line segments between data points.
Area of each trapezoid: $\frac{v_i + v_{i+1}}{2} \Delta t_i$.
Sum over all intervals: $\sum \frac{v_i + v_{i+1}}{2} \Delta t_i$.
As $\max \Delta t_i \to 0$, this converges to $\int v\,dt$.
∎

## Proof 15: Simpson's Rule for $v$–$t$ Graph

**Statement:** For equally spaced data $t_0, t_1, t_2$ with $\Delta t = h$: $\int_{t_0}^{t_2} v\,dt \approx \frac{h}{3}(v_0 + 4v_1 + v_2)$.

**Proof:**
Fit parabola $v(t) = At^2 + Bt + C$ through $(t_0, v_0)$, $(t_1, v_1)$, $(t_2, v_2)$.
Integrate exact parabola: $\int_{t_0}^{t_2} (At^2+Bt+C)\,dt = \frac{h}{3}(v_0 + 4v_1 + v_2)$.
Error $\propto v^{(4)}(\xi)$.
∎

## Proof 16: Distance from $x$–$t$ Graph with Reversals

**Statement:** Distance $D = \sum |x(t_{i+1}) - x(t_i)|$ where $t_i$ are successive turning points (including endpoints).

**Proof:**
Between turning points, $v$ does not change sign.
Displacement on each segment: $\Delta x_i = x(t_{i+1}) - x(t_i)$.
Distance on segment: $|\Delta x_i|$.
Total distance = sum of absolute segment displacements = total length of path on $x$–$t$ graph.
∎

---

## Proof 17: Relative Motion from Graphs

**Statement:** If $v_A(t)$ and $v_B(t)$ are velocities of A and B, relative velocity $v_{A/B}(t) = v_A(t) - v_B(t)$. Relative displacement = area under $v_{A/B}$–$t$ graph.

**Proof:**
$\vec{v}_{A/B} = \vec{v}_A - \vec{v}_B$ (1D: signed scalars).
$x_{A/B}(t) = x_A(t) - x_B(t)$.
$v_{A/B} = \frac{d}{dt}x_{A/B} = \frac{dx_A}{dt} - \frac{dx_B}{dt} = v_A - v_B$.
Relative displacement: $\int v_{A/B}\,dt = \int (v_A - v_B)\,dt = x_A - x_B$.
Graphically: subtract $v_B$–$t$ graph from $v_A$–$t$ graph pointwise; area of difference = relative displacement.
∎

## Proof 18: Closest Approach from $x_{rel}$–$t$ Graph

**Statement:** Minimum separation occurs when $v_{rel} = 0$ with sign change $+$ to $-$ (if moving apart then together).

**Proof:**
Separation $d = |x_{rel}|$. Minimum $d$ occurs at minimum $|x_{rel}|$.
$\frac{d}{dt}(x_{rel}^2) = 2x_{rel}v_{rel} = 0 \implies x_{rel}=0$ or $v_{rel}=0$.
If $x_{rel} \neq 0$ (not collision), then $v_{rel} = 0$.
For minimum: $\frac{d^2}{dt^2}(x_{rel}^2) = 2(v_{rel}^2 + x_{rel}a_{rel}) > 0$.
Since $v_{rel}=0$, need $x_{rel}a_{rel} > 0$ (restoring acceleration).
Graphically: $v_{rel}$ crosses $t$-axis with sign indicating approach/separation.
∎
