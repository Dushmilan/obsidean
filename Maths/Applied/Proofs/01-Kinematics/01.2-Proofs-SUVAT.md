# Proofs: Equations of Motion (SUVAT)

**Syllabus:** GCE A/L Combined Mathematics (021) — Paper II, Topic 1.2

---

## Proof 1: $v = u + at$ (Equation 1)

**Derivation from definition of constant acceleration:**

Given: $a = \text{constant}$
By definition: $a = \frac{dv}{dt}$
$$
\int_u^v dv = \int_0^t a\,dt \implies v - u = at \implies v = u + at
$$
∎

---

## Proof 2: $s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2$ (Equation 2)

**Method 1: Integrate velocity**

From Eq 1: $v = u + at = \frac{ds}{dt}$
$$
\int_0^s ds = \int_0^t (u + at)\,dt \implies s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2
$$
∎

**Method 2: Average velocity**

Under constant $a$, velocity increases linearly from $u$ to $v$.
Average velocity: $\bar{v} = \frac{u+v}{2} = \frac{u+(u+at)}{2} = u + \frac{1}{2}at$
Displacement: $s = \bar{v}t = \left(u + \frac{1}{2}at\right)t = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2$
∎

---

## Proof 3: $v^2 = u^2 + 2as$ (Equation 3)

**Derivation using $a = v\frac{dv}{ds}$:**

Given constant $a$:
$$
a = v\frac{dv}{ds} \implies \int_u^v v\,dv = \int_0^s a\,ds
$$
$$
\frac{1}{2}(v^2 - u^2) = as \implies v^2 = u^2 + 2as
$$
∎

**Alternative: Eliminate $t$ from Eq 1 and 2**

From Eq 1: $t = \frac{v-u}{a}$
Substitute into Eq 2:
$$
s = u\left(\frac{v-u}{a}\right) + \frac{1}{2}a\left(\frac{v-u}{a}\right)^2
= \frac{uv - u^2}{a} + \frac{1}{2}\frac{v^2 - 2uv + u^2}{a}
= \frac{2uv - 2u^2 + v^2 - 2uv + u^2}{2a}
= \frac{v^2 - u^2}{2a}
$$
Rearranging: $v^2 = u^2 + 2as$
∎

---

## Proof 4: $s = \frac{1}{2}(u+v)t$ (Equation 4)

**Method 1: Average velocity**

For constant acceleration, velocity changes linearly.
Average velocity = arithmetic mean of initial and final:
$$
\bar{v} = \frac{u+v}{2}
$$
Displacement = average velocity × time:
$$
s = \bar{v}t = \frac{u+v}{2}t
$$
∎

**Method 2: From Eq 2 and Eq 1**

$v = u + at \implies at = v - u$
Substitute into Eq 2:
$$
s = ut + \frac{1}{2}(v-u)t = ut + \frac{1}{2}vt - \frac{1}{2}ut = \frac{1}{2}ut + \frac{1}{2}vt = \frac{1}{2}(u+v)t
$$
∎

---

## Proof 5: $s = vt - \frac{1}{2}at^2$ (Equation 5)

**Derivation by symmetry:**

Substitute $u = v - at$ (from Eq 1) into Eq 2:
$$
s = (v-at)t + \frac{1}{2}at^2 = vt - at^2 + \frac{1}{2}at^2 = vt - \frac{1}{2}at^2
$$
∎

**Physical interpretation:** If we reverse time direction ($t \to -t$, $v \to -v$, $u \to -u$, $a \to -a$), Eq 2 becomes Eq 5.

## Proof 6: Maximum Height in Vertical Motion

**Statement:** For vertical projection with speed $u$ upward, max height $H = \frac{u^2}{2g}$.

**Proof:**
At maximum height, $v = 0$, acceleration $a = -g$.
Using $v^2 = u^2 + 2as$:
$$
0 = u^2 - 2gH \implies H = \frac{u^2}{2g}
$$
∎

---

## Proof 7: Time of Flight for Vertical Projection

**Statement:** Time to return to projection level: $T = \frac{2u}{g}$.

**Proof:**
Displacement $s = 0$ at $t = T$, $u$ given, $a = -g$.
Using $s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2$:
$$
0 = uT - \frac{1}{2}gT^2 \implies T(u - \frac{1}{2}gT) = 0
$$
Non-zero solution: $T = \frac{2u}{g}$
∎

---

## Proof 8: Range of Projectile on Horizontal Plane

**Statement:** Range $R = \frac{u^2\sin2\theta}{g}$ for projection speed $u$, angle $\theta$.

**Proof:**
Horizontal: $x = (u\cos\theta)t$
Vertical: $y = (u\sin\theta)t - \frac{1}{2}gt^2$
At ground impact ($t = T > 0$), $y = 0$:
$$
(u\sin\theta)T - \frac{1}{2}gT^2 = 0 \implies T = \frac{2u\sin\theta}{g}
$$
Range: $R = x(T) = (u\cos\theta)\cdot\frac{2u\sin\theta}{g} = \frac{2u^2\sin\theta\cos\theta}{g} = \frac{u^2\sin2\theta}{g}$
∎

---

## Proof 9: Distance in $n$th Second

**Statement:** $s_n = u + a\left(n - \frac{1}{2}\right)$

**Proof:**
Displacement after $n$ seconds: $s_n^{\text{total}} = un + \frac{1}{2}an^2$
Displacement after $n-1$ seconds: $s_{n-1}^{\text{total}} = u(n-1) + \frac{1}{2}a(n-1)^2$
Distance in $n$th second:
$$
s_n = s_n^{\text{total}} - s_{n-1}^{\text{total}} = u + \frac{1}{2}a(2n-1) = u + a\left(n - \frac{1}{2}\right)
$$
∎

## Proof 10: Velocity at Midpoint of Displacement

**Statement:** For constant $a$, velocity at midpoint of displacement $\frac{s}{2}$ is $v_{\text{mid}} = \sqrt{\frac{u^2+v^2}{2}}$.

**Proof:**
Using $v^2 = u^2 + 2as$ for full displacement $s$:
$$
v^2 = u^2 + 2as \implies as = \frac{v^2 - u^2}{2}
$$
For half displacement $s/2$:
$$
v_{\text{mid}}^2 = u^2 + 2a(s/2) = u^2 + as = u^2 + \frac{v^2 - u^2}{2} = \frac{u^2 + v^2}{2}
$$
$$
\implies v_{\text{mid}} = \sqrt{\frac{u^2+v^2}{2}}
$$
∎

## Proof 11: Symmetry of Projectile Trajectory

**Statement:** For projectile launched and landing at same height, trajectory is symmetric about the apex. Time to apex = half total time. Speed at equal heights is equal.

**Proof:**
Trajectory: $y = x\tan\theta - \frac{gx^2}{2u^2\cos^2\theta}$
This is a parabola symmetric about $x = R/2$.
At $t = T/2 = \frac{u\sin\theta}{g}$, $v_y = 0$ (apex).
For height $y = h$, solve $h = (u\sin\theta)t - \frac{1}{2}gt^2$:
$$
t = \frac{u\sin\theta \pm \sqrt{u^2\sin^2\theta - 2gh}}{g}
$$
Two roots $t_1, t_2$ symmetric about $T/2$.
Speed: $v^2 = u^2\cos^2\theta + (u\sin\theta - gt)^2$.
At $t_1$ and $t_2$, $(u\sin\theta - gt)$ has same magnitude, opposite sign.
Thus $v$ is same at equal heights.
∎

---

## Proof 12: Maximum Range on Horizontal Plane

**Statement:** Maximum range $R_{\text{max}} = \frac{u^2}{g}$ at $\theta = 45^\circ$.

**Proof:**
$R = \frac{u^2\sin2\theta}{g}$.
Maximum when $\sin2\theta = 1 \implies 2\theta = 90^\circ \implies \theta = 45^\circ$.
$R_{\text{max}} = \frac{u^2}{g}$.
∎
