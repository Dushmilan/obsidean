
## Definition

With resistive force $F_d = -b\dot{x}$, the equation becomes

$$m\ddot{x} + b\dot{x} + kx = 0 \quad\Rightarrow\quad \ddot{x} + 2\gamma\dot{x} + \omega_0^2 x = 0$$

with damping constant $\gamma = \frac{b}{2m}$ and natural frequency $\omega_0 = \sqrt{k/m}$.

**Regimes:** underdamped ($\gamma < \omega_0$, decaying oscillations) · critically damped ($\gamma = \omega_0$, fastest return without oscillating) · overdamped ($\gamma > \omega_0$, slow exponential decay). Quality factor: $Q = \frac{\omega_0}{2\gamma}$.

**Forced oscillations** add a driving term; **resonance** peaks slightly below $\omega_0$.

## The Intuition

A swing in air: air resistance gradually reduces the amplitude. Damping is friction stealing energy each cycle — amplitude decays $A(t) = A_0e^{-\gamma t}$, energy $E(t) = E_0e^{-2\gamma t}$. Pushing at the right frequency (forcing) builds the swing to large amplitude — resonance, where energy input matches dissipation. At resonance the driving force is in phase with *velocity* (not displacement), maximising power transfer.

## The Toolkit

| Regime | Condition | Solution shape |
|--------|-----------|----------------|
| Underdamped | $\gamma < \omega_0$ | $x = Ae^{-\gamma t}\cos(\omega_d t + \phi)$, $\omega_d = \sqrt{\omega_0^2 - \gamma^2}$ |
| Critically damped | $\gamma = \omega_0$ | $x = (A + Bt)e^{-\gamma t}$ |
| Overdamped | $\gamma > \omega_0$ | $x = Ae^{r_1 t} + Be^{r_2 t}$, $r_1, r_2 < 0$ |
| Quality factor | $Q = \dfrac{\omega_0}{2\gamma}$ | higher $Q$ = less damping |
| Amplitude decay | $A(t) = A_0 e^{-\gamma t}$ | underdamped |
| Energy decay | $E(t) = E_0 e^{-2\gamma t}$ | — |

## Derivation

Solve the characteristic equation $r^2 + 2\gamma r + \omega_0^2 = 0$. Discriminant sign gives the three regimes: complex roots → underdamped (oscillating), repeated → critical, real → overdamped. The resonance peak for a driven oscillator: amplitude maximised near $\omega = \sqrt{\omega_0^2 - 2\gamma^2}$, slightly below $\omega_0$. [Full derivations: 05.5-Proofs-Damped-Forced-Oscillations]

## Method

1. Compare $\gamma$ with $\omega_0$ to pick the regime.
2. Underdamped: write $A e^{-\gamma t}\cos(\omega_d t + \phi)$; $\omega_d < \omega_0$.
3. Critical damping is the design choice for fastest settling (shock absorbers, doors).
4. For resonance problems: driving frequency near $\omega_0$ → large amplitude; $Q$ measures sharpness.

## Worked Examples

**Setup:** A system has $\omega_0 = 10$ rad/s, $\gamma = 2$ s⁻¹. Classify and find $\omega_d$.

**Solution:** $\gamma < \omega_0$ → underdamped. $\omega_d = \sqrt{100 - 4} = 4\sqrt6 \approx 9.8$ rad/s.

**Key insight:** Damping lowers the oscillation frequency slightly below the natural value.

---

**Setup:** After 10 cycles, the amplitude halves. Estimate $Q$.

**Solution:** One cycle $T = 2\pi/\omega_d \approx 2\pi/10$ s; after $10T$, $e^{-\gamma(2\pi)} \approx \frac12 \Rightarrow \gamma \approx 0.11$, so $Q = \omega_0/(2\gamma) \approx 45$.

**Key insight:** $Q$ counts how many cycles the oscillator survives — high $Q$, narrow resonance.

## Common Traps

- Classifying without comparing $\gamma$ to $\omega_0$
- Using $\omega_0$ where $\omega_d$ belongs (underdamped frequency is lower)
- Critical damping is *fastest return*, not fastest oscillation
- Resonance at exactly $\omega_0$ (the true peak is slightly below with damping)
- Energy decays at $2\gamma$, amplitude at $\gamma$ — factor of two
