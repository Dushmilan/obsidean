# 5.5 Damped & Forced Oscillations

**Syllabus:** GCE A/L Combined Mathematics (021) — Paper II, Topic 5.5  
**Prerequisites:** SHM (5.1), Spring Systems (5.2), Energy (5.4), Differential Equations (Pure Maths 4.6)

---

## Damped Oscillations

**Damping force:** Proportional to velocity, opposes motion.
$$
F_d = -b\dot{x} \quad (b > 0 \text{ is damping coefficient})
$$

**Equation of motion:**
$$
m\ddot{x} + b\dot{x} + kx = 0 \quad \text{or} \quad \ddot{x} + 2\gamma\dot{x} + \omega_0^2 x = 0
$$

where:
- $\omega_0 = \sqrt{k/m}$ = **natural frequency** (undamped)
- $\gamma = \frac{b}{2m}$ = **damping constant** (decay rate)

---

## Three Damping Regimes

Characteristic equation: $r^2 + 2\gamma r + \omega_0^2 = 0$

Roots: $r = -\gamma \pm \sqrt{\gamma^2 - \omega_0^2}$

### 1. Light (Underdamped) Damping: $\gamma < \omega_0$

**Complex roots:** $r = -\gamma \pm i\omega_d$

**Damped frequency:** $\omega_d = \sqrt{\omega_0^2 - \gamma^2}$

**Solution:**
$$
x(t) = Ae^{-\gamma t}\cos(\omega_d t + \phi)
$$

**Properties:**
- Oscillates with decaying amplitude
- Envelope: $\pm Ae^{-\gamma t}$
- Period: $T_d = \frac{2\pi}{\omega_d} \approx T_0\left(1 + \frac{\gamma^2}{2\omega_0^2}\right)$ (slightly longer than $T_0$)
- Amplitude halves in time $t_{1/2} = \frac{\ln 2}{\gamma}$

---

### 2. Critical Damping: $\gamma = \omega_0$

**Repeated real root:** $r = -\gamma$

**Solution:**
$$
x(t) = (A + Bt)e^{-\gamma t}
$$

**Properties:**
- Fastest return to equilibrium **without oscillation**
- No overshoot
- Used in: galvanometers, door closers, shock absorbers

---

### 3. Heavy (Overdamped) Damping: $\gamma > \omega_0$

**Two distinct real roots:** $r_1 = -\gamma + \sqrt{\gamma^2 - \omega_0^2}$, $r_2 = -\gamma - \sqrt{\gamma^2 - \omega_0^2}$

**Solution:**
$$
x(t) = Ae^{r_1 t} + Be^{r_2 t}
$$

**Properties:**
- No oscillation
- Slower return than critical damping
- Two exponential decay modes

---

## Quality Factor (Q-Factor)

**Definition:**
$$
Q = \frac{\omega_0}{2\gamma} = \frac{\text{energy stored}}{\text{energy lost per radian}}
$$

**Physical significance:**
- $Q \gg 1$: Light damping, sharp resonance
- $Q \sim 1$: Critical/heavy damping
- Number of oscillations for amplitude to decay by $e^{-\pi} \approx 1/23$: $N \approx Q/\pi$

**Logarithmic decrement:**
$$
\delta = \ln\left(\frac{A_n}{A_{n+1}}\right) = \frac{2\pi\gamma}{\omega_d} = \frac{\pi}{Q}
$$

---

## Energy in Damped Oscillator

**Total energy:** $E = \frac{1}{2}m\dot{x}^2 + \frac{1}{2}kx^2$

**Rate of energy loss:**
$$
\frac{dE}{dt} = -b\dot{x}^2 \quad (\text{power dissipated by damping force})
$$

**Light damping approximation:** Energy decays exponentially:
$$
E(t) \approx E_0 e^{-2\gamma t}
$$

**Amplitude decay:** $A(t) = A_0 e^{-\gamma t}$

---

## Forced (Driven) Oscillations

**Driving force:** $F(t) = F_0\cos(\omega t)$ (frequency $\omega$, amplitude $F_0$)

**Equation of motion:**
$$
m\ddot{x} + b\dot{x} + kx = F_0\cos(\omega t)
$$
or
$$
\ddot{x} + 2\gamma\dot{x} + \omega_0^2 x = \frac{F_0}{m}\cos(\omega t)
$$

---

## General Solution

**Complete solution = Complementary function + Particular integral**

$$
x(t) = x_{CF}(t) + x_{PI}(t)
$$

- **CF (transient):** Solution to homogeneous equation. Dies out as $e^{-\gamma t}$.
- **PI (steady state):** Particular solution at driving frequency $\omega$. Persists indefinitely.

---

## Steady-State Solution

Assume $x_{PI}(t) = A\cos(\omega t - \phi)$ (phase lag $\phi$)

Substitute into equation and equate coefficients:

**Amplitude:**
$$
A(\omega) = \frac{F_0/m}{\sqrt{(\omega_0^2 - \omega^2)^2 + 4\gamma^2\omega^2}}
$$

**Phase lag:**
$$
\tan\phi = \frac{2\gamma\omega}{\omega_0^2 - \omega^2}
$$

---

## Resonance

**Resonance frequency** (amplitude maximum):
$$
\frac{dA}{d\omega} = 0 \quad \Rightarrow \quad \omega_r = \sqrt{\omega_0^2 - 2\gamma^2}
$$

**For light damping ($\gamma \ll \omega_0$):** $\omega_r \approx \omega_0$

**Maximum amplitude at resonance:**
$$
A_{max} = \frac{F_0/m}{2\gamma\sqrt{\omega_0^2 - \gamma^2}} \approx \frac{F_0}{2m\gamma\omega_0} = \frac{Q F_0}{m\omega_0^2}
$$

**Phase at resonance:** $\phi = \pi/2$ (velocity in phase with driving force)

---

## Resonance Curve (Amplitude vs Frequency)

```
      A
      ↑
      |    /
      |   / \
      |  /   \
      | /     \
      |/       \______
      +----------------→ ω
         ω_r  ω_0
```

**Key features:**
- Peak at $\omega_r = \sqrt{\omega_0^2 - 2\gamma^2}$
- Height $\propto Q$
- Width (FWHM): $\Delta\omega \approx 2\gamma = \omega_0/Q$
- Sharp resonance when $Q \gg 1$

---

## Phase Response

```
      φ
      ↑
   π  |                    /
      |                  /
  π/2 |----------------/--------  (at ω = ω_0)
      |              /
      |            /
   0  |__________/______________→ ω
         ω_0
```

- $\omega \ll \omega_0$: $\phi \approx 0$ (in phase)
- $\omega = \omega_0$: $\phi = \pi/2$ (quadrature)
- $\omega \gg \omega_0$: $\phi \approx \pi$ (out of phase)

---

## Power Absorption & Dissipation

**Instantaneous power input:** $P_{in} = F(t)\dot{x}(t)$

**Average power over one cycle:**
$$
\langle P_{in} \rangle = \frac{1}{2}F_0 A\omega \sin\phi = \frac{F_0^2}{2m} \frac{\gamma\omega^2}{(\omega_0^2 - \omega^2)^2 + 4\gamma^2\omega^2}
$$

**Power resonance:** Maximum at $\omega = \omega_0$ (exactly), not $\omega_r$.

**Average power dissipated by damping:**
$$
\langle P_{diss} \rangle = \frac{1}{2}b A^2\omega^2 = \langle P_{in} \rangle \quad \text{(steady state)}
$$

## Worked Examples

### Example 1: Damping Classification
Mass 0.5 kg on spring $k = 200$ N/m. Damping coefficient $b = 2$ N·s/m. Classify and find $\omega_d$.

**Solution:**
$$
\omega_0 = \sqrt{200/0.5} = 20 \text{ rad/s}, \quad \gamma = \frac{2}{2 \times 0.5} = 2 \text{ s}^{-1}
$$
$\gamma = 2 < \omega_0 = 20$ → **Light damping**
$$
\omega_d = \sqrt{20^2 - 2^2} = \sqrt{396} \approx 19.9 \text{ rad/s}
$$
$Q = \omega_0/2\gamma = 20/4 = 5$.

---

### Example 2: Forced Oscillator
Mass 1 kg on spring $k = 100$ N/m, damping $b = 4$ N·s/m. Driving force $F = 10\cos(\omega t)$. Find $A$ at $\omega = 10$ rad/s and at resonance.

**Solution:**
$$
\omega_0 = 10 \text{ rad/s}, \quad \gamma = 2 \text{ s}^{-1}, \quad Q = 2.5
$$

At $\omega = 10$:
$$
A = \frac{10/1}{\sqrt{(100-100)^2 + 4(2)^2(10)^2}} = \frac{10}{40} = 0.25 \text{ m}
$$

Resonance frequency:
$$
\omega_r = \sqrt{100 - 8} = \sqrt{92} \approx 9.59 \text{ rad/s}
$$
$$
A_{max} = \frac{10}{4\sqrt{100-4}} = \frac{10}{4\sqrt{96}} \approx 0.256 \text{ m}
$$

---

### Example 3: Critical Damping
Find $b$ for critical damping of $m = 2$ kg, $k = 50$ N/m.

**Solution:**
$$
\gamma = \omega_0 \implies \frac{b}{2m} = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}} \implies b = 2\sqrt{mk} = 2\sqrt{2 \times 50} = 20 \text{ N·s/m}
$$

## Common GCE A/L Patterns

| Pattern | Key Formula / Approach |
|---------|------------------------|
| "Classify damping" | Compare $\gamma = b/2m$ with $\omega_0 = \sqrt{k/m}$ |
| "Find damped frequency" | $\omega_d = \sqrt{\omega_0^2 - \gamma^2}$ (light damping only) |
| "Find critical damping coefficient" | $b_c = 2\sqrt{mk}$ |
| "Steady-state amplitude at frequency $\omega$" | $A = \frac{F_0/m}{\sqrt{(\omega_0^2-\omega^2)^2 + 4\gamma^2\omega^2}}$ |
| "Resonance frequency" | $\omega_r = \sqrt{\omega_0^2 - 2\gamma^2} \approx \omega_0$ |
| "Phase difference at resonance" | $\phi = \pi/2$ |
| "Quality factor $Q$" | $Q = \omega_0/2\gamma = \omega_0 m/b$ |
| "Amplitude at resonance" | $A_{max} = \frac{F_0}{b\omega_0} = \frac{Q F_0}{m\omega_0^2}$ |
| "Energy loss per cycle" | $\Delta E = \pi b \omega A^2$ |
| "Logarithmic decrement" | $\delta = \ln(A_n/A_{n+1}) = \pi/Q$ |
