
## Definition

Total mechanical energy is **conserved** and equals the maximum of either energy form:

$$E = \frac{1}{2}kA^2 = \frac{1}{2}mv_{\max}^2$$

**Kinetic:** $K = \frac12 k(A^2 - x^2)$ · **Potential:** $U = \frac12 kx^2$ — at $x = \pm A$ all energy is potential; at $x = 0$ all is kinetic. $K$ and $U$ oscillate at **twice** the displacement frequency ($\omega_K = 2\omega$) and are $\pi$ out of phase.

## The Intuition

A ball rolling in a bowl: at the rim it has max potential, zero kinetic; at the bottom, max kinetic, min potential. Total energy is constant (no friction). In $(x, v)$ phase space the trajectory is an ellipse whose area is proportional to the energy: $E = \frac12 kA^2 = \frac12 m(\omega A)^2$ — the two forms are equal because $\omega^2 = k/m$.

## The Toolkit

| Quantity | Formula | At |
|----------|---------|-----|
| Total energy | $E = \frac12 kA^2 = \frac12 m\omega^2 A^2$ | constant |
| Kinetic | $K = \frac12 k(A^2 - x^2)$ | max at $x = 0$ |
| Potential | $U = \frac12 kx^2$ | max at $x = \pm A$ |
| Speed at position | $v = \omega\sqrt{A^2 - x^2}$ | from energy |
| Oscillation frequency of $K, U$ | $\omega_K = 2\omega$ | — |

## Derivation

$U = \frac12 kx^2$ integrated from the spring force. Substitute $x = A\cos\omega t$ into $K + U$; the $\cos^2 + \sin^2 = 1$ identity collapses it to the constant $\frac12 kA^2$. The doubled frequency comes from $\cos^2\omega t = \frac{1+\cos 2\omega t}{2}$. [Full derivations: 05.4-Proofs-Energy-in-SHM]

## Method

1. **Total energy first:** $E = \frac12 kA^2$ (or $\frac12 m\omega^2 A^2$).
2. **Energy at a position:** $K = E - U = \frac12 k(A^2 - x^2)$.
3. **Speed at a position:** $v = \omega\sqrt{A^2 - x^2}$ — from $K = \frac12 mv^2$.
4. Whenever the problem gives no explicit time, **energy is the tool**.

## Worked Examples

**Setup:** A mass oscillates with $A = 0.5$ m, $\omega = 4$ rad/s, $m = 2$ kg. Find the energy and the speed at $x = 0.3$ m.

**Solution:** $E = \frac12 m\omega^2A^2 = \frac12(2)(16)(0.25) = 4$ J. $v = \omega\sqrt{A^2 - x^2} = 4\sqrt{0.25 - 0.09} = 4(0.4) = 1.6$ m/s.

**Key insight:** The energy formula needs $\omega^2$, not $\omega$.

---

**Setup:** Show the mean kinetic energy equals the mean potential energy over a cycle.

**Solution:** $\overline{K} = \frac14 kA^2 = \overline{U}$ — each is half the total on average.

**Key insight:** The time averages of $K$ and $U$ are equal (both $\frac12 E$), though they oscillate at $2\omega$.

## Common Traps

- $K = \frac12 kA^2$ at equilibrium (that's $E$; $K$ there equals $E$, correct — but at $x \neq 0$ subtract $U$)
- Using $v = \omega A$ away from equilibrium (that's $v_{\max}$ only)
- Forgetting $K + U = E$ constant
- Confusing $\omega$ (displacement) with $2\omega$ (energy oscillation)
- Measuring $U$ from the natural length instead of equilibrium
