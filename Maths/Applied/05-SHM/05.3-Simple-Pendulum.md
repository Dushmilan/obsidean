
## Definition

A point mass $m$ on a light inextensible string of length $L$:

- **Exact (nonlinear):** $\ddot{\theta} + \dfrac{g}{L}\sin\theta = 0$
- **Small angles ($\theta \lesssim 10^\circ$):** $\sin\theta \approx \theta \Rightarrow \ddot{\theta} + \dfrac{g}{L}\theta = 0$ — SHM with
$$\omega = \sqrt{\frac{g}{L}}, \qquad T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{L}{g}}$$

**Isochronous:** the period is independent of mass $m$ and (small-angle) amplitude $\theta_0$.

## The Intuition

A child on a swing: the period depends on chain length, not weight. At the bottom all energy is kinetic; at the extremes all is potential — the exchange drives the oscillation with gravity supplying the restoring torque. A longer string sweeps a longer arc for the same angle (longer period); stronger gravity means a stronger restoring torque (shorter period): $T \propto \sqrt{L/g}$.

## The Toolkit

| Quantity | Formula |
|----------|---------|
| Restoring torque | $\tau = -mgL\sin\theta$ |
| Moment of inertia | $I = mL^2$ |
| Exact ODE | $\ddot{\theta} + \frac{g}{L}\sin\theta = 0$ |
| Small-angle ODE | $\ddot{\theta} + \frac{g}{L}\theta = 0$ |
| Angular frequency | $\omega = \sqrt{g/L}$ |
| Period | $T = 2\pi\sqrt{L/g}$ |

## Derivation

Torque about the pivot: $\tau = -mgL\sin\theta$. Newton II for rotation: $I\ddot{\theta} = \tau$, with $I = mL^2$, gives the exact ODE. Linearise with $\sin\theta \approx \theta$ for small angles → SHM. [Full derivations: 05.3-Proofs-Simple-Pendulum]

## Method

1. Small-angle problem → $T = 2\pi\sqrt{L/g}$ directly.
2. **Nonlinear problems:** keep $\sin\theta$ — no closed SHM form; use energy or numerical methods.
3. For "gravity on another planet" problems, solve $T \propto 1/\sqrt{g}$.

## Worked Examples

**Setup:** A pendulum has $L = 0.25$ m. Find its period ($g = 10$).

**Solution:** $T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{0.25}{10}} = 2\pi\sqrt{0.025} \approx 1.0$ s.

**Key insight:** The mass doesn't appear — any bob on the same string has the same period.

---

**Setup:** A pendulum beats seconds ($T = 2$ s) on Earth. Find $L$.

**Solution:** $L = \frac{gT^2}{4\pi^2} = \frac{10\cdot4}{4\pi^2} = \frac{10}{\pi^2} \approx 1.0$ m.

**Key insight:** The classic "seconds pendulum" is about 1 m — a useful sanity check.

## Common Traps

- Using the exact equation's SHM formula at large angles (breaks down)
- Confusing $L$ and $r$ (pendulum vs conical pendulum)
- Forgetting $T$ is independent of $m$
- Applying the pendulum formula to a *physical* pendulum (needs $I$ about the pivot)
- Using $g$ instead of $\sqrt{g/L}$ for frequency
