# 5.3 Simple Pendulum

**Syllabus:** GCE A/L Combined Mathematics (021) — Paper II, Topic 5.3  
**Prerequisites:** SHM Definition (5.1), Circular Motion, Energy Conservation, Series Expansions (Pure Maths)

---

## Simple Pendulum Model

**Assumptions (Ideal):**
- Light, inextensible string of length $L$
- Point mass $m$ (bob)
- Frictionless pivot
- No air resistance
- Small oscillations (for SHM)

**Forces on bob:** Tension $T$ along string, weight $mg$ vertically down.

---

## Equation of Motion

Let $\theta$ be angular displacement from vertical (positive to the right).

**Tangential component of weight:** $-mg\sin\theta$ (restoring force)

**Tangential acceleration:** $L\ddot{\theta}$

**Newton's 2nd Law (tangential):**
$$
mL\ddot{\theta} = -mg\sin\theta \quad \Rightarrow \quad \ddot{\theta} + \frac{g}{L}\sin\theta = 0
$$

This is the **exact nonlinear equation** of a simple pendulum.

---

## Small Angle Approximation (SHM)

For $\theta \ll 1$ radian ($\lesssim 10^\circ$): $\sin\theta \approx \theta$

$$
\ddot{\theta} + \frac{g}{L}\theta = 0 \quad \text{(SHM)}
$$

**Angular frequency & period:**
$$
\omega = \sqrt{\frac{g}{L}}, \quad T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{L}{g}}, \quad f = \frac{1}{2\pi}\sqrt{\frac{g}{L}}
$$

**Key properties:**
- $T$ independent of mass $m$
- $T$ independent of amplitude $\theta_0$ (isochronous) — **only for small angles**
- $T \propto \sqrt{L}$, $T \propto 1/\sqrt{g}$

**Solution:**
$$
\theta(t) = \theta_0\cos(\omega t + \phi)
$$
where $\theta_0$ = angular amplitude.

---

## Large Angle Oscillations (Exact Period)

For finite amplitude $\theta_0$ (max angular displacement), motion is **periodic but not SHM**.

**Exact period (using energy conservation):**

$$
T = 4\sqrt{\frac{L}{g}} \int_0^{\theta_0} \frac{d\theta}{\sqrt{2(\cos\theta - \cos\theta_0)}} = 4\sqrt{\frac{L}{g}} K(k)
$$

where $K(k)$ is the **complete elliptic integral of the first kind** with modulus $k = \sin(\theta_0/2)$.

**Series expansion (for practical calculation):**

$$
T = T_0 \left[ 1 + \frac{1}{4}\sin^2\frac{\theta_0}{2} + \frac{9}{64}\sin^4\frac{\theta_0}{2} + \frac{25}{256}\sin^6\frac{\theta_0}{2} + \cdots \right]
$$

where $T_0 = 2\pi\sqrt{L/g}$ is the small-angle period.

**Alternative form (in $\theta_0$ radians):**

$$
T \approx T_0 \left( 1 + \frac{\theta_0^2}{16} + \frac{11\theta_0^4}{3072} + \cdots \right)
$$

**Amplitude corrections:**
| $\theta_0$ | $T/T_0$ (approx) | % increase |
|------------|-------------------|------------|
| $5^\circ$ (0.087 rad) | 1.00047 | 0.047% |
| $10^\circ$ (0.175 rad) | 1.0019 | 0.19% |
| $20^\circ$ (0.349 rad) | 1.0077 | 0.77% |
| $30^\circ$ (0.524 rad) | 1.0174 | 1.74% |
| $45^\circ$ (0.785 rad) | 1.0398 | 3.98% |
| $60^\circ$ (1.047 rad) | 1.0732 | 7.32% |
| $90^\circ$ (1.571 rad) | 1.1803 | 18.03% |

**Rule of thumb:** Small angle approximation good to <1% for $\theta_0 \lesssim 20^\circ$.

---

## Energy in Pendulum Motion

**Potential energy** (reference at lowest point):
$$
U = mgL(1 - \cos\theta)
$$

For small $\theta$: $U \approx \frac{1}{2}mgL\theta^2 = \frac{1}{2}m\omega^2(L\theta)^2$ (since $x = L\theta$)

**Kinetic energy:**
$$
K = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 = \frac{1}{2}m(L\dot{\theta})^2
$$

**Total energy:**
$$
E = \frac{1}{2}mL^2\dot{\theta}^2 + mgL(1 - \cos\theta) = mgL(1 - \cos\theta_0) = \text{constant}
$$

**Speed at angle $\theta$:**
$$
v = \sqrt{2gL(\cos\theta - \cos\theta_0)}
$$

## Physical Pendulum (Compound Pendulum)

**General rigid body** pivoted at point $O$, distance $d$ from centre of mass $G$.

**Moment of inertia about $O$:** $I_O = I_G + md^2$ (parallel axis theorem)

**Equation of motion:**
$$
I_O\ddot{\theta} = -mgd\sin\theta
$$

**Small angle approximation:**
$$
\ddot{\theta} + \frac{mgd}{I_O}\theta = 0
$$

**Angular frequency & period:**
$$
\omega = \sqrt{\frac{mgd}{I_O}}, \quad T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{I_O}{mgd}}
$$

**Equivalent simple pendulum length:**
$$
L_{eq} = \frac{I_O}{md} = \frac{I_G}{md} + d
$$

---

## Special Cases of Physical Pendulum

### 1. Uniform Rod (length $L$, mass $m$)
| Pivot Position | $d$ | $I_O$ | $T$ |
|----------------|-----|-------|-----|
| End | $L/2$ | $\frac{1}{3}mL^2$ | $2\pi\sqrt{\frac{2L}{3g}}$ |
| Centre | 0 | $\frac{1}{12}mL^2$ | — (no oscillation) |
| Distance $x$ from end | $x - L/2$ | $\frac{1}{12}mL^2 + m(x-L/2)^2$ | $2\pi\sqrt{\frac{L^2/12 + (x-L/2)^2}{g(x-L/2)}}$ |

**Minimum period:** Occurs when $d = \sqrt{I_G/m} = L/\sqrt{12} \approx 0.289L$ from centre.

---

### 2. Uniform Disc (radius $R$, mass $m$)
Pivot at distance $d$ from centre:
$$
I_O = \frac{1}{2}mR^2 + md^2, \quad T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{R^2/2 + d^2}{gd}}
$$
Minimum $T$ when $d = R/\sqrt{2}$.

---

### 3. Kater's Reversible Pendulum
Adjustable physical pendulum with two knife edges. By adjusting weights to make periods equal from both edges, $L_{eq}$ is found precisely $\to$ accurate $g$ measurement.

---

## Conical Pendulum (Related Topic)

**Not SHM** — uniform circular motion in horizontal plane.

**Setup:** String length $L$, angle $\theta$ with vertical, angular speed $\omega$.

**Force balance:**
$$
T\cos\theta = mg, \quad T\sin\theta = m\omega^2 L\sin\theta
$$

**Eliminate $T$:**
$$
\omega = \sqrt{\frac{g}{L\cos\theta}}, \quad T = \frac{mg}{\cos\theta}
$$

**Period:**
$$
T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{L\cos\theta}{g}} = T_0\sqrt{\cos\theta}
$$

**Note:** $T < T_0$ for conical pendulum; string traces a cone.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1: Simple Pendulum Period
Pendulum length 1 m. Find period on Earth ($g = 9.8$ m/s²) and Moon ($g = 1.6$ m/s²).

**Solution:**
$$
T_{Earth} = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{1}{9.8}} \approx 2.01 \text{ s}
$$
$$
T_{Moon} = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{1}{1.6}} \approx 4.97 \text{ s}
$$

---

### Example 2: Large Angle Correction
Pendulum length 1 m, amplitude $30^\circ$. Find period.

**Solution:**
$$
\theta_0 = 30^\circ = \pi/6 \approx 0.524 \text{ rad}
$$
$$
T \approx T_0\left(1 + \frac{\theta_0^2}{16}\right) = 2.01\left(1 + \frac{0.524^2}{16}\right) \approx 2.01(1.017) \approx 2.04 \text{ s}
$$
(0.03 s correction)

---

### Example 3: Physical Pendulum (Rod)
Uniform rod length 1 m, mass 0.5 kg, pivoted at one end. Find period.

**Solution:**
$$
d = 0.5 \text{ m}, \quad I_O = \frac{1}{3}(0.5)(1)^2 = \frac{1}{6} \text{ kg m}^2
$$
$$
T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{I_O}{mgd}} = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{1/6}{0.5 \times 9.8 \times 0.5}} = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{1/6}{2.45}} \approx 1.64 \text{ s}
$$

---

### Example 4: Find $g$ from Period
Pendulum $L = 0.994$ m, period $T = 2.000$ s. Find $g$.

**Solution:**
$$
g = \frac{4\pi^2 L}{T^2} = \frac{4\pi^2 \times 0.994}{4} = \pi^2 \times 0.994 \approx 9.81 \text{ m/s}^2
$$

---

### Example 5: Compound Pendulum Equivalent Length
Uniform disc radius $R$, pivot at edge. Find equivalent simple pendulum length.

**Solution:**
$$
d = R, \quad I_G = \frac{1}{2}mR^2, \quad I_O = \frac{1}{2}mR^2 + mR^2 = \frac{3}{2}mR^2
$$
$$
L_{eq} = \frac{I_O}{md} = \frac{3R^2/2}{R} = \frac{3}{2}R = 1.5R
$$

## Common GCE A/L Patterns

| Pattern | Method |
|---------|--------|
| "Show motion is SHM and find $T$" | $mgL\sin\theta \approx mgL\theta$, $\ddot{\theta} + (g/L)\theta = 0$ |
| "Period of simple pendulum" | $T = 2\pi\sqrt{L/g}$ |
| "Large amplitude correction" | Use $T \approx T_0(1 + \theta_0^2/16)$ or elliptic integral |
| "Physical pendulum period" | $T = 2\pi\sqrt{I_O/mgd}$ |
| "Equivalent simple pendulum length" | $L_{eq} = I_O/md$ |
| "Find $g$ from $T$ and $L$" | $g = 4\pi^2 L/T^2$ |
| "Conical pendulum period" | $T = 2\pi\sqrt{L\cos\theta/g}$ |
| "Energy method for $\omega$" | $U = mgL(1-\cos\theta) \approx \frac{1}{2}mgL\theta^2$, $\omega = \sqrt{g/L}$ |
