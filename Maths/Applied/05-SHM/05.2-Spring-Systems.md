# 5.2 Horizontal & Vertical Spring Systems

**Syllabus:** GCE A/L Combined Mathematics (021) — Paper II, Topic 5.2  
**Prerequisites:** Pure Maths Calculus (2nd order ODEs), Dynamics (Newton's 2nd Law, Hooke's Law)

---

## Hooke's Law

For a spring with **spring constant** $k$ (stiffness, N/m):

$$
F = -kx
$$

where $x$ = extension/compression from natural length. Force is restoring (opposite to displacement).

**Spring constant:** $k = \frac{\text{Force}}{\text{Extension}}$. Stiffer spring = larger $k$.

---

## Horizontal Spring-Mass System

**Setup:** Mass $m$ on frictionless horizontal surface, attached to spring fixed at one end. Equilibrium at natural length.

**Forces at displacement $x$:** Only spring force $F = -kx$.

**Equation of motion:**
$$
m\ddot{x} = -kx \quad \Rightarrow \quad \ddot{x} + \frac{k}{m}x = 0
$$

**Angular frequency:**
$$
\omega = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}
$$

**Period:**
$$
T = \frac{2\pi}{\omega} = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{m}{k}}
$$

**Frequency:**
$$
f = \frac{1}{T} = \frac{1}{2\pi}\sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}
$$

---

## Vertical Spring-Mass System

**Setup:** Spring suspended vertically, mass $m$ attached. Equilibrium extension $x_0$ where spring force balances weight.

**Equilibrium condition:**
$$
kx_0 = mg \quad \Rightarrow \quad x_0 = \frac{mg}{k}
$$

**Displacement from equilibrium:** Let $y$ be displacement downward from equilibrium ($y = x - x_0$).

**Forces at displacement $y$:**
- Weight: $mg$ (down)
- Spring force: $k(x_0 + y)$ (up)

**Net force (downward positive):**
$$
F_{net} = mg - k(x_0 + y) = mg - kx_0 - ky = -ky
$$

**Equation of motion:**
$$
m\ddot{y} = -ky \quad \Rightarrow \quad \ddot{y} + \frac{k}{m}y = 0
$$

**Result:** **Same $\omega$, $T$, $f$ as horizontal spring!**
$$
\omega = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}, \quad T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{m}{k}}
$$

**Key insight:** Gravity shifts equilibrium but does not affect oscillation frequency (for ideal spring).

## Springs in Combination

### Series Combination

Two springs $k_1$, $k_2$ end-to-end. Same force $F$ through both.

$$
x_1 = \frac{F}{k_1}, \quad x_2 = \frac{F}{k_2}, \quad x_{total} = x_1 + x_2 = F\left(\frac{1}{k_1} + \frac{1}{k_2}\right)
$$

**Effective spring constant:**
$$
\frac{1}{k_{eq}} = \frac{1}{k_1} + \frac{1}{k_2} \quad \Rightarrow \quad k_{eq} = \frac{k_1 k_2}{k_1 + k_2}
$$

**General (n springs in series):**
$$
\frac{1}{k_{eq}} = \sum_{i=1}^n \frac{1}{k_i}
$$

**Note:** $k_{eq} < \min(k_1, k_2)$ — series combination is **softer**.

---

### Parallel Combination

Two springs $k_1$, $k_2$ side-by-side, sharing same displacement $x$.

**Total force:**
$$
F = F_1 + F_2 = k_1 x + k_2 x = (k_1 + k_2)x
$$

**Effective spring constant:**
$$
k_{eq} = k_1 + k_2
$$

**General (n springs in parallel):**
$$
k_{eq} = \sum_{i=1}^n k_i
$$

**Note:** $k_{eq} > \max(k_1, k_2)$ — parallel combination is **stiffer**.

---

### Mixed Combinations

Reduce stepwise: combine series/parallel groups until single $k_{eq}$.

**Example:** $k_1$ and $k_2$ in parallel, then in series with $k_3$:
$$
k_{12} = k_1 + k_2, \quad \frac{1}{k_{eq}} = \frac{1}{k_{12}} + \frac{1}{k_3} = \frac{1}{k_1 + k_2} + \frac{1}{k_3}
$$

## Vertical Spring: Energy Perspective

**Potential energy at extension $x$ from natural length:**
$$
U_{spring} = \frac{1}{2}kx^2
$$

**Gravitational potential (reference at natural length, down positive):**
$$
U_{grav} = -mgx
$$

**Total potential energy:**
$$
U_{total} = \frac{1}{2}kx^2 - mgx
$$

Complete the square:
$$
U_{total} = \frac{1}{2}k\left(x^2 - 2\frac{mg}{k}x\right) = \frac{1}{2}k\left[\left(x - \frac{mg}{k}\right)^2 - \left(\frac{mg}{k}\right)^2\right]
$$

$$
U_{total} = \frac{1}{2}k(x - x_0)^2 - \frac{1}{2}kx_0^2
$$

**Equilibrium at $x_0 = mg/k$**, and near equilibrium $U \approx \frac{1}{2}ky^2 + \text{const}$.

## Massive Spring (Effective Mass)

If spring has mass $m_s$, not all parts move with same speed.

**Effective mass of spring:** $m_{eff} = \frac{1}{3}m_s$ (for uniform spring, one end fixed)

**Total oscillating mass:**
$$
m_{total} = m_{load} + \frac{1}{3}m_{spring}
$$

**Corrected period:**
$$
T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{m_{load} + \frac{1}{3}m_{spring}}{k}}
$$

**Derivation sketch:** Spring element at distance $x$ from fixed end moves at speed $v(x) = (x/L)v_{end}$. Integrate KE: $K_{spring} = \int_0^L \frac{1}{2}(\frac{m_s}{L}dx)(\frac{x}{L}v)^2 = \frac{1}{6}m_s v^2 = \frac{1}{2}(\frac{1}{3}m_s)v^2$.

## Two Masses on a Spring

### Both ends free (center of mass frame)

Masses $m_1$, $m_2$ connected by spring $k$. No external forces.

**Reduced mass:**
$$
\mu = \frac{m_1 m_2}{m_1 + m_2}
$$

**Relative coordinate:** $x = x_2 - x_1 - L_0$ (extension from natural length)

**Equation:** $\mu \ddot{x} + kx = 0$

**Angular frequency:**
$$
\omega = \sqrt{\frac{k}{\mu}} = \sqrt{\frac{k(m_1 + m_2)}{m_1 m_2}}
$$

**Period:** $T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{m_1 m_2}{k(m_1 + m_2)}}$

**Note:** COM remains stationary (or moves uniformly). Each mass oscillates about COM.

---

### One end fixed

Mass $m_1$ fixed to wall, mass $m_2$ attached to other end.

**Equation:** $m_2 \ddot{x} + kx = 0$ — standard single mass system.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1: Series/Parallel Springs
Three identical springs ($k = 100$ N/m). Two in parallel, then in series with third. Find $k_{eq}$ and $T$ for $m = 2$ kg.

**Solution:**
$$
k_{12} = k + k = 200 \text{ N/m}
$$
$$
\frac{1}{k_{eq}} = \frac{1}{200} + \frac{1}{100} = \frac{3}{200} \implies k_{eq} = \frac{200}{3} \approx 66.67 \text{ N/m}
$$
$$
T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{2}{66.67}} \approx 2\pi\sqrt{0.03} \approx 1.09 \text{ s}
$$

---

### Example 2: Vertical Spring
Spring ($k = 400$ N/m) hangs vertically. 2 kg mass attached. Find equilibrium extension and period.

**Solution:**
$$
x_0 = \frac{mg}{k} = \frac{2 \times 9.8}{400} = 0.049 \text{ m} = 4.9 \text{ cm}
$$
$$
T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{2}{400}} = 2\pi\sqrt{0.005} \approx 0.44 \text{ s}
$$

---

### Example 3: Massive Spring
Spring $k = 200$ N/m, mass 0.5 kg, load mass 1 kg. Find period.

**Solution:**
$$
m_{total} = 1 + \frac{1}{3}(0.5) = 1.167 \text{ kg}
$$
$$
T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{1.167}{200}} \approx 2\pi\sqrt{0.00583} \approx 0.48 \text{ s}
$$
(Without spring mass: $T = 2\pi\sqrt{1/200} \approx 0.44$ s — 9% difference)

---

### Example 4: Two Masses on Spring
Masses 2 kg and 3 kg connected by spring $k = 150$ N/m on smooth table. Find oscillation period.

**Solution:**
$$
\mu = \frac{2 \times 3}{2 + 3} = 1.2 \text{ kg}
$$
$$
T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{1.2}{150}} = 2\pi\sqrt{0.008} \approx 0.56 \text{ s}
$$

## Common GCE A/L Patterns

| Pattern | Formula / Method |
|---------|------------------|
| Horizontal spring, find $T$ | $T = 2\pi\sqrt{m/k}$ |
| Vertical spring, find equilibrium extension | $x_0 = mg/k$ |
| Vertical spring, show period same as horizontal | Use $y$ from equilibrium, $m\ddot{y} = -ky$ |
| Springs in series | $1/k_{eq} = 1/k_1 + 1/k_2$ |
| Springs in parallel | $k_{eq} = k_1 + k_2$ |
| Spring cut into $n$ equal parts | Each part has $k' = nk$ (stiffer) |
| Spring with mass | $T = 2\pi\sqrt{(m + m_s/3)/k}$ |
| Two masses on spring (free ends) | $T = 2\pi\sqrt{\mu/k}$, $\mu = m_1m_2/(m_1+m_2)$ |
| Find $k$ from period/mass | $k = 4\pi^2 m/T^2$ |
