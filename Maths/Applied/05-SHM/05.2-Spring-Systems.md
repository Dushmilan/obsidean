
## Definition

A mass $m$ on a spring of constant $k$ obeys Hooke's law $F = -kx$, undergoing SHM with

$$\omega = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}, \qquad T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{m}{k}}, \qquad f = \frac{1}{2\pi}\sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}$$

**Vertical springs:** gravity shifts the equilibrium by $x_0 = mg/k$ but **does not change the frequency** — displacement $y$ from the new equilibrium obeys $m\ddot{y} = -ky$, identical to horizontal.

## The Intuition

A trampoline: push down and it pushes back proportionally. For a vertical spring the mass hangs where $kx_0 = mg$; displacing it $y$ from there gives net force $-ky$ — gravity is "already accounted for" by the equilibrium extension. **In series**, the same force flows through both springs and the softer one dominates; **in parallel**, both share the displacement and the stiffer one dominates.

## The Toolkit

| System | Effective $k$ |
|--------|---------------|
| Single spring | $k$ |
| Series | $k_{\text{eff}} = \dfrac{k_1k_2}{k_1+k_2}$ (softer dominates) |
| Parallel | $k_{\text{eff}} = k_1 + k_2$ (stiffer dominates) |
| Vertical | same $\omega$; equilibrium shifted by $mg/k$ |

## Derivation

$m\ddot{x} = -kx \Rightarrow \omega = \sqrt{k/m}$. Series: equal force $F$, total extension $\frac{F}{k_1}+\frac{F}{k_2} = F/k_{\text{eff}}$. Parallel: equal extension, forces add: $F = (k_1+k_2)x$. Vertical: $m\ddot{y} = -k(x_0+y) + mg = -ky$ since $kx_0 = mg$. [Full derivations: 05.2-Proofs-Spring-Systems]

## Method

1. Find the effective spring constant (series/parallel rules).
2. $\omega = \sqrt{k_{\text{eff}}/m}$.
3. Vertical problems: work from the *equilibrium* position, not the natural length.

## Worked Examples

**Setup:** A 2 kg mass on a vertical spring with $k = 200$ N/m. Find the period.

**Solution:** $\omega = \sqrt{\frac{200}{2}} = 10$ rad/s, so $T = \frac{2\pi}{10} \approx 0.63$ s.

**Key insight:** The vertical equilibrium shift doesn't appear — same period as horizontal.

---

**Setup:** Two springs $k_1 = 100$, $k_2 = 400$ N/m in series carry mass 1 kg. Find $\omega$.

**Solution:** $k_{\text{eff}} = \frac{100\cdot400}{500} = 80$ N/m. $\omega = \sqrt{80} \approx 8.9$ rad/s.

**Key insight:** Series is *weaker* than either spring — the soft spring sets the pace.

## Common Traps

- Series/parallel swap — series is reciprocal, parallel is additive
- Measuring displacement from the natural length in vertical problems (use equilibrium)
- Forgetting gravity is balanced at the new equilibrium
- Using $k$ of one spring in a combined system
- Confusing $f$ with $\omega$
