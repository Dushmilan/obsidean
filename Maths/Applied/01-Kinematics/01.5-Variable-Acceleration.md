# 1.5 Variable Acceleration

**Syllabus:** GCE A/L Combined Mathematics (021) — Paper II, Topic 1.5
Not needed for AL.

---

## When SUVAT Fails

SUVAT equations assume **constant** acceleration. For **variable** acceleration, use **calculus**:

| Given $a$ as function of | Method | Key Equation |
|--------------------------|--------|--------------|
| Time: $a(t)$ | Direct integration | $v = \int a\,dt$, $s = \int v\,dt$ |
| Velocity: $a(v)$ | Separation of variables | $\frac{dv}{a(v)} = dt$, $\frac{v\,dv}{a(v)} = ds$ |
| Position: $a(s)$ | Chain rule $a = v\frac{dv}{ds}$ | $v\,dv = a(s)\,ds$ |

---

## Case 1: Acceleration as Function of Time, $a(t)$

### Velocity
$$
v(t) = u + \int_0^t a(\tau)\,d\tau
$$

### Displacement
$$
s(t) = \int_0^t v(\tau)\,d\tau = ut + \int_0^t \int_0^\tau a(\sigma)\,d\sigma\,d\tau
$$

### Examples

| $a(t)$ | $v(t)$ | $s(t)$ |
|--------|--------|--------|
| $kt$ (linear) | $u + \frac{1}{2}kt^2$ | $ut + \frac{1}{6}kt^3$ |
| $k\sin\omega t$ | $u + \frac{k}{\omega}(1-\cos\omega t)$ | $ut + \frac{k}{\omega}t - \frac{k}{\omega^2}\sin\omega t$ |
| $ke^{-\lambda t}$ | $u + \frac{k}{\lambda}(1-e^{-\lambda t})$ | $ut + \frac{k}{\lambda}t + \frac{k}{\lambda^2}(e^{-\lambda t}-1)$ |
| $\frac{k}{t}$ ($t>0$) | $u + k\ln t$ | $ut + k(t\ln t - t) + C$ |

---

## Case 2: Acceleration as Function of Velocity, $a(v)$

### Time as Function of Velocity
$$
t = \int_u^v \frac{dv'}{a(v')}
$$

### Displacement as Function of Velocity
$$
s = \int_u^v \frac{v'\,dv'}{a(v')}
$$

### Velocity as Function of Displacement
$$
v^2 = u^2 + 2\int_0^s a(s')\,ds'
$$

### Common Forms

| $a(v)$ | Time $t$ | Displacement $s$ | Notes |
|--------|----------|------------------|-------|
| $-kv$ (linear drag) | $\frac{1}{k}\ln\frac{u}{v}$ | $\frac{u-v}{k}$ | Exponential decay |
| $-kv^2$ (quadratic drag) | $\frac{1}{k}\left(\frac{1}{v} - \frac{1}{u}\right)$ | $\frac{1}{2k}\ln\frac{u}{v}$ | $v \to 0$ as $t \to \infty$ |
| $-g - kv$ (vertical, linear drag) | $\frac{1}{k}\ln\frac{g+ku}{g+kv}$ | $\frac{u-v}{k} - \frac{g}{k}t$ | Terminal $v = -g/k$ |
| $-g - kv^2$ (vertical, quadratic) | $\frac{1}{\sqrt{gk}}\tan^{-1}\left(v\sqrt{\frac{k}{g}}\right)$ | $\frac{1}{2k}\ln\frac{g+ku^2}{g+kv^2}$ | Terminal $v = -\sqrt{g/k}$ |
| $k(v_{\text{term}} - v)$ | $\frac{1}{k}\ln\frac{v_{\text{term}}-u}{v_{\text{term}}-v}$ | $\frac{1}{k}\left[(u-v) + v_{\text{term}}\ln\frac{v_{\text{term}}-u}{v_{\text{term}}-v}\right]$ | Approaches $v_{\text{term}}$ |

---

## Case 3: Acceleration as Function of Position, $a(s)$

### Velocity as Function of Position
$$
v^2 = u^2 + 2\int_0^s a(s')\,ds'
$$

### Time as Function of Position
$$
t = \int_0^s \frac{ds'}{\sqrt{u^2 + 2\int_0^{s'} a(s'')\,ds''}}
$$

### Common Forms

| $a(s)$ | $v^2$ | Motion Type |
|--------|-------|-------------|
| $-\omega^2 s$ | $u^2 - \omega^2 s^2$ | **SHM** (period $T = 2\pi/\omega$) |
| $-\frac{k}{s^2}$ (inverse square) | $u^2 + \frac{2k}{s} - \frac{2k}{s_0}$ | Gravitational/electrostatic |
| $k s$ (repulsive) | $u^2 + k s^2$ | Unbounded growth |
| $-g - \frac{k}{s^2}$ | $u^2 - 2gs + \frac{2k}{s} - \frac{2k}{s_0}$ | Rocket from planet |

---

## Special Case: Simple Harmonic Motion (SHM)

If $a = -\omega^2 s$:
$$
v^2 = u^2 - \omega^2 s^2
$$
$$
v = \frac{ds}{dt} = \pm\sqrt{u^2 - \omega^2 s^2}
$$
$$
t = \frac{1}{\omega}\sin^{-1}\left(\frac{\omega s}{u}\right) \quad \text{(from } s=0\text{)}
$$
$$
s = \frac{u}{\omega}\sin(\omega t) \quad \text{(if } s=0 \text{ at } t=0\text{)}
$$
**Period:** $T = \frac{2\pi}{\omega}$

---

## Motion with Resistance (Drag)

### Horizontal Motion with Linear Resistance
$$
m\frac{dv}{dt} = -kv \implies v = u e^{-kt/m}
$$
$$
x = \frac{mu}{k}(1 - e^{-kt/m}) \to \frac{mu}{k} \text{ as } t \to \infty
$$

### Horizontal Motion with Quadratic Resistance
$$
m\frac{dv}{dt} = -kv^2 \implies \frac{1}{v} = \frac{1}{u} + \frac{k}{m}t
$$
$$
x = \frac{m}{k}\ln\left(1 + \frac{ku}{m}t\right) \to \infty \text{ as } t \to \infty \text{ (logarithmic growth)}
$$

### Vertical Motion with Linear Resistance (Upward Projection)
$$
\frac{dv}{dt} = -g - \frac{k}{m}v
$$
- **Upward:** $v = \left(u + \frac{mg}{k}\right)e^{-kt/m} - \frac{mg}{k}$
- **Max height:** $t = \frac{m}{k}\ln\left(1 + \frac{ku}{mg}\right)$, $H = \frac{m}{k}\left[u - \frac{mg}{k}\ln\left(1 + \frac{ku}{mg}\right)\right]$
- **Terminal velocity (downward):** $v_{\text{term}} = -\frac{mg}{k}$

## Solving Strategy

| Step | Action |
|------|--------|
| 1 | Identify form of $a$: $a(t)$, $a(v)$, or $a(s)$ |
| 2 | Choose appropriate integration route |
| 3 | Set up definite integrals with correct limits |
| 4 | Integrate (use substitution, partial fractions, etc.) |
| 5 | Apply initial conditions to find constants |
| 6 | Check dimensions and limiting behaviour |

---

## Integration Techniques Needed

| Integral | Technique |
|----------|-----------|
| $\int \frac{dv}{a+bv}$ | Substitution $u = a+bv$ |
| $\int \frac{dv}{a+bv^2}$ | $\frac{1}{\sqrt{ab}}\tan^{-1}\left(v\sqrt{b/a}\right)$ |
| $\int \frac{v\,dv}{a+bv^2}$ | $\frac{1}{2b}\ln(a+bv^2)$ |
| $\int \frac{dv}{v(a+bv)}$ | Partial fractions: $\frac{1}{a}\left(\frac{1}{v} - \frac{b}{a+bv}\right)$ |
| $\int e^{-kt}\,dt$ | $-\frac{1}{k}e^{-kt}$ |
| $\int \sin\omega t\,dt$, $\int \cos\omega t\,dt$ | Standard trig integrals |

---

## Worked Example: Rocket with Variable Mass

Rocket of initial mass $M_0$, burning fuel at rate $\mu$, exhaust speed $u$ relative to rocket. No external forces.

**Momentum conservation:**
$$
(M_0 - \mu t)\frac{dv}{dt} = \mu u
$$
$$
v = u\ln\left(\frac{M_0}{M_0 - \mu t}\right) \quad \text{(Tsiolkovsky rocket equation)}
$$
