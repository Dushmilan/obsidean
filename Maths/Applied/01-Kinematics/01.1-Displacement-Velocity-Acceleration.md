
## Definition

Rectilinear motion quantities form a chain — differentiate down, integrate up:

$$s = r(t) - r(0), \qquad v = \frac{ds}{dt}, \qquad a = \frac{dv}{dt} = \frac{d^2s}{dt^2}$$

- **Displacement** $s$ — signed change in position (can be negative; $|s| \le$ distance).
- **Velocity** $v$ — rate of change of displacement (signed); **speed** $= |v|$.
- **Acceleration** $a$ — rate of change of velocity.
- **Chain-rule form:** $a = v\dfrac{dv}{ds}$ — connects $a$ to $v$ and $s$ without time.

Valid for all motion. The SUVAT equations are the **constant-acceleration special case**.

## The Intuition

A car journey: the odometer (distance) only goes up; displacement from home can go up or down — drive away and back, displacement is zero while the odometer shows miles. The speedometer shows speed; velocity carries the sign of direction. A number line with a sliding dot captures 1D motion exactly.

## The Toolkit

| Quantity | Definition | Notes |
|----------|-----------|-------|
| Displacement | $s = r(t) - r(0)$ | signed vector (1D: signed scalar) |
| Velocity | $v = \dfrac{ds}{dt}$ | speed $= |v|$ |
| Acceleration | $a = \dfrac{dv}{dt}$ | "deceleration" = $a$ opposite to $v$ |
| Chain-rule | $a = v\dfrac{dv}{ds}$ | use when time isn't given |
| SUVAT | $v = u + at$, $s = ut + \tfrac12 at^2$, $v^2 = u^2 + 2as$ | **only** if $a$ constant |

## Derivation

"Instantaneous" means the limit of a small change over a small time — $v = \lim_{\Delta t\to 0}\frac{\Delta s}{\Delta t}$. The chain-rule form follows from $a = \frac{dv}{dt} = \frac{dv}{ds}\cdot\frac{ds}{dt} = v\frac{dv}{ds}$. SUVAT come from integrating constant $a$ (see 01.2-Equations-of-Motion). [Full derivations: 01.1-Proofs-Displacement-Velocity]

## Method

1. **Pick a positive direction** and stick to it — every signed quantity follows.
2. Ask: *is acceleration constant?* → SUVAT; otherwise → calculus.
3. **No time in the problem?** Use $v^2 = u^2 + 2as$ or the chain-rule form.
4. Check signs: same sign for $a, v$ → speeding up; opposite → slowing down.

## Worked Examples

**Setup:** A particle moves from $x = 0$ to $x = 5$ and back to $x = 2$. Find displacement and distance.

**Solution:** Displacement $= 2 - 0 = +2$; distance $= 5 + 3 = 8$.

**Key insight:** Displacement is the signed endpoint change; distance adds up every move.

---

**Setup:** Given $s = t^3 - 6t^2 + 9t$, find when the particle is at rest.

**Solution:** $v = 3t^2 - 12t + 9 = 3(t-1)(t-3) = 0 \Rightarrow t = 1,\ 3$.

**Key insight:** "At rest" means $v = 0$ — differentiate $s$ and solve.

## Common Traps

- Distance ≠ displacement — one is always non-negative
- "Deceleration" isn't negative $a$ — it's $a$ opposite to $v$
- Applying SUVAT when $a$ varies (wrong answers)
- Forgetting the chain-rule form when time is absent
- Mixing up average velocity (displacement/time) and average speed (distance/time)
