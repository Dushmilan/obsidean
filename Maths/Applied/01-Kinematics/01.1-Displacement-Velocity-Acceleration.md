# 1.1 Displacement, Velocity, Acceleration (Rectilinear Motion)

**Syllabus:** GCE A/L Combined Mathematics (021) — Paper II, Topic 1.1

---

## Definitions

### Position and Displacement
- **Position vector:** $\vec{r}(t)$ — position relative to origin O
- **Displacement:** $\vec{s}(t) = \vec{r}(t) - \vec{r}(0)$ — change in position
- **Distance:** scalar, total path length travelled ($s \geq |\vec{s}|$)

### Velocity
- **Average velocity:** $\vec{v}_{avg} = \frac{\Delta \vec{s}}{\Delta t}$
- **Instantaneous velocity:** $\vec{v}(t) = \lim_{\Delta t \to 0} \frac{\Delta \vec{s}}{\Delta t} = \frac{d\vec{s}}{dt} = \frac{d\vec{r}}{dt}$
- **Speed:** $v = |\vec{v}|$ (scalar)

### Acceleration
- **Average acceleration:** $\vec{a}_{avg} = \frac{\Delta \vec{v}}{\Delta t}$
- **Instantaneous acceleration:** $\vec{a}(t) = \lim_{\Delta t \to 0} \frac{\Delta \vec{v}}{\Delta t} = \frac{d\vec{v}}{dt} = \frac{d^2\vec{s}}{dt^2}$

---

## Rectilinear Motion (1D)

Motion along a straight line. Choose axis direction as positive.

| Quantity | Symbol | Units | Sign Convention |
|----------|--------|-------|-----------------|
| Displacement | $s$ | m | +ve in chosen direction |
| Velocity | $v$ | m/s | +ve = moving in +ve direction |
| Acceleration | $a$ | m/s² | +ve = acceleration in +ve direction |

### Key Relationships
$$
v = \frac{ds}{dt}, \quad a = \frac{dv}{dt} = \frac{d^2s}{dt^2}
$$

Alternative form (eliminating $dt$):
$$
a = \frac{dv}{dt} = \frac{dv}{ds} \cdot \frac{ds}{dt} = v \frac{dv}{ds}
$$

---

## Constant Acceleration (Uniform Acceleration)

If $a = \text{constant}$:

### Velocity-Time
$$
v = u + at \quad \text{where } u = v(0)
$$

### Displacement-Time
$$
s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2
$$

### Velocity-Displacement
$$
v^2 = u^2 + 2as
$$

### Average Velocity
$$
v_{avg} = \frac{s}{t} = \frac{u+v}{2} \quad (\text{only for constant } a)
$$

---

## Variable Acceleration

When $a = a(t)$, $a = a(v)$, or $a = a(s)$:

### Given $a(t)$
$$
v = u + \int_0^t a(t')\,dt', \quad s = \int_0^t v(t')\,dt'
$$

### Given $a(v)$
$$
t = \int_u^v \frac{dv'}{a(v')}, \quad s = \int_u^v \frac{v'\,dv'}{a(v')}
$$

### Given $a(s)$
$$
v^2 = u^2 + 2\int_0^s a(s')\,ds', \quad t = \int_0^s \frac{ds'}{v(s')}
$$

## Kinematic Graphs

| Graph | Gradient | Area Under Curve |
|-------|----------|------------------|
| $s$-$t$ | velocity $v$ | — |
| $v$-$t$ | acceleration $a$ | displacement $s$ |
| $a$-$t$ | jerk | change in velocity $\Delta v$ |

### Graphical Interpretation
- **$s$-$t$ straight line** → constant velocity
- **$s$-$t$ parabola** → constant acceleration
- **$v$-$t$ straight line** → constant acceleration
- **Area of $v$-$t$ graph** = displacement (signed)

---

## Sign Conventions & Common Pitfalls

1. **Distance vs Displacement:** Distance $\geq$ |Displacement|
2. **Speed vs Velocity:** Speed = |Velocity|
3. **Deceleration:** $a$ opposite to $v$ (not necessarily negative)
4. **Constant speed ≠ constant velocity** (direction can change)
5. **$a = 0$** means constant velocity (not necessarily at rest)

## Worked Example

> A particle moves along x-axis with $a = 6t - 12$ m/s². At $t=0$, $x=0$, $v=9$ m/s. Find when it comes to rest and its position then.

**Solution:**
$$
v = 9 + \int_0^t (6t' - 12)\,dt' = 9 + 3t^2 - 12t = 3(t-1)(t-3)
$$
At rest when $v=0$: $t = 1$ s or $t = 3$ s.

$$
x = \int_0^t v(t')\,dt' = \int_0^t (3t'^2 - 12t' + 9)\,dt' = t^3 - 6t^2 + 9t
$$
At $t=1$: $x = 4$ m. At $t=3$: $x = 0$ m.
