# 1.2 Equations of Motion (Constant Acceleration — SUVAT)

**Syllabus:** GCE A/L Combined Mathematics (021) — Paper II, Topic 1.2

---

## The Five SUVAT Equations

For **constant acceleration** $a$, initial velocity $u$ at $t=0$, displacement $s$ measured from start:

| Eq | Formula | Variables Involved | Missing |
|----|---------|-------------------|---------|
| 1 | $v = u + at$ | $u, v, a, t$ | $s$ |
| 2 | $s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2$ | $u, s, a, t$ | $v$ |
| 3 | $v^2 = u^2 + 2as$ | $u, v, a, s$ | $t$ |
| 4 | $s = \frac{1}{2}(u+v)t$ | $u, v, s, t$ | $a$ |
| 5 | $s = vt - \frac{1}{2}at^2$ | $v, s, a, t$ | $u$ |

> **Notation:** $u$ = initial velocity, $v$ = final velocity, $a$ = constant acceleration, $s$ = displacement, $t$ = time elapsed

---

## Derivations (Calculus)

### Eq 1: $v = u + at$
$$
a = \frac{dv}{dt} \implies dv = a\,dt \implies \int_u^v dv = \int_0^t a\,dt \implies v - u = at
$$

### Eq 2: $s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2$
$$
v = \frac{ds}{dt} = u + at \implies ds = (u+at)\,dt \implies \int_0^s ds = \int_0^t (u+at)\,dt \implies s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2
$$

### Eq 3: $v^2 = u^2 + 2as$
$$
a = \frac{dv}{dt} = \frac{dv}{ds}\frac{ds}{dt} = v\frac{dv}{ds} \implies v\,dv = a\,ds \implies \int_u^v v'\,dv' = \int_0^s a\,ds' \implies \frac{1}{2}(v^2 - u^2) = as
$$

### Eq 4: $s = \frac{1}{2}(u+v)t$
Average velocity = $\frac{\text{initial} + \text{final}}{2} = \frac{u+v}{2}$. Displacement = average velocity × time.

### Eq 5: $s = vt - \frac{1}{2}at^2$
Substitute $u = v - at$ (from Eq 1) into Eq 2:
$$
s = (v-at)t + \frac{1}{2}at^2 = vt - \frac{1}{2}at^2
$$

## Choosing the Right Equation

| Known | Unknown | Use |
|-------|---------|-----|
| $u, a, t$ | $v$ | Eq 1 |
| $u, a, t$ | $s$ | Eq 2 |
| $u, a, s$ | $v$ | Eq 3 |
| $u, v, t$ | $s$ | Eq 4 |
| $v, a, t$ | $s$ | Eq 5 |
| $u, v, s$ | $a$ | Eq 3 |
| $u, v, s$ | $t$ | Eq 4 then Eq 1 |

## Sign Convention

- Choose **positive direction** (e.g., upward = +, rightward = +)
- $u, v, s, a$ are **signed scalars** (components along chosen axis)
- **Consistency is essential** — stick to one convention per problem

### Free Fall Examples
| Scenario | $a$ | Notes |
|----------|-----|-------|
| Upward projection (up = +) | $-g$ | At max height: $v=0$ |
| Downward projection (down = +) | $+g$ | $u > 0$, $v > 0$ |
| Upward projection (down = +) | $+g$ | $u < 0$, find $v$ when $s = +H$ |

## Free Fall Under Gravity ($a = \pm g$)

### Vertical Projection Upwards (up = +)
- $a = -g$
- Time to max height: $t = \frac{u}{g}$
- Max height: $H = \frac{u^2}{2g}$
- Time of flight (return to start): $T = \frac{2u}{g}$
- Velocity on return: $v = -u$

### Projection from Height $h$ Above Ground
Set $s = -h$ (if up = +) and solve for $t$:
$$
-h = ut - \frac{1}{2}gt^2 \implies \frac{1}{2}gt^2 - ut - h = 0
$$
Take positive root for $t$.

## Distance Travelled in $n$th Second

$$
s_n = s(n) - s(n-1) = u + a\left(n - \frac{1}{2}\right)
$$

**Derivation:**
$$
s_n = \left[un + \frac{1}{2}an^2\right] - \left[u(n-1) + \frac{1}{2}a(n-1)^2\right] = u + a\left(n - \frac{1}{2}\right)
$$

## Multi-Stage Motion

### Example: Particle projected up, rebounds with coefficient of restitution $e$
1. **Ascent:** $v^2 = u^2 - 2gh$ at height $h$
2. **Impact:** $v_{\text{after}} = e \cdot v_{\text{before}}$ (magnitudes)
3. **Descent:** New $u' = e v$, solve for ground impact

### Example: Two Particles
- Particle A: projected up from ground with $u_A$
- Particle B: dropped from height $H$ after delay $t_0$
- Equate positions: $y_A(t) = y_B(t)$

## Common Exam Patterns

| Pattern | Approach |
|---------|----------|
| **Meeting of two particles** | Write $s_1(t)$, $s_2(t)$; equate; solve for $t$ |
| **Overtaking** | Relative motion: $u_{rel} = u_1 - u_2$, $a_{rel} = a_1 - a_2$ |
| **Passing a window/pole** | Known $s$, $t$ for segment → find $v$ at start/end of segment |
| **Successive equal distances** | Use $s_n$ formula or $s_{2n} - s_n$ relationships |
| **Rebounds** | Apply $e$ at each impact; treat each flight as separate SUVAT |

---

## Pitfalls

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| Using SUVAT for non-constant $a$ | Only valid when $a$ is constant |
| Mixing sign conventions | Pick one direction as +ve and use throughout |
| Confusing $s$ (displacement) with distance | $s$ can be negative; distance is $\int |v|\,dt$ |
| Using $v = s/t$ for non-constant $v$ | Only valid for constant $v$ or average over interval |
| Forgetting $v=0$ at max height (vertical) | Critical for time-to-peak calculations |
