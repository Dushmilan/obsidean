
## Definition

For **constant acceleration**, five equations relate $s$ (displacement), $u$ (initial velocity), $v$ (final velocity), $a$ (acceleration), $t$ (time):

| Equation | Missing |
|----------|---------|
| $v = u + at$ | $s$ |
| $s = ut + \tfrac12 at^2$ | $v$ |
| $v^2 = u^2 + 2as$ | $t$ |
| $s = \frac{1}{2}(u+v)t$ | $a$ |
| $s = vt - \tfrac12 at^2$ | $u$ |

## The Intuition

A ball rolling down a ramp: start from rest, speed up by 2 m/s each second → after 1 s it's 2 m/s, after 2 s it's 4 m/s ($v = u + at$). Distance isn't $u \times t$ because it's speeding up — the average speed is $\frac{u+v}{2}$, giving $s = \frac{u+v}{2}\cdot t$. On a $v$-$t$ graph the motion is a straight line (trapezium); the area is displacement, the slope is acceleration.

## The Toolkit

| Equation | Use when |
|----------|----------|
| $v = u + at$ | you don't need $s$ |
| $s = ut + \tfrac12 at^2$ | you don't need $v$ |
| $v^2 = u^2 + 2as$ | you don't need $t$ |
| $s = \frac12(u+v)t$ | you don't need $a$ |
| $s = vt - \tfrac12 at^2$ | you don't need $u$ |

**Sign convention:** choose upward positive → vertical motion has $a = -g$.

## Derivation

Integrate constant $a$: $v = \int a\,dt = u + at$. Then $s = \int v\,dt = ut + \frac12 at^2$. Eliminate $t$ via $t = \frac{v-u}{a}$ to get $v^2 = u^2 + 2as$. [Full derivations: 01.2-Proofs-SUVAT]

## Method

**Equation selection:** list the five quantities $s, u, v, a, t$; mark the three you know and the one you want; pick the equation that avoids the unused variable. No algebra guessing needed.

## Worked Examples

**Setup:** A stone is thrown upward at 20 m/s from the ground ($g = 10$). Find (a) max height, (b) time of flight, (c) speed on landing.

**Solution:** Upward positive, $a = -10$.
(a) At peak $v = 0$: $0 = 400 - 2(10)s \Rightarrow s = 20$ m.
(b) Return: $0 = 20t - 5t^2 \Rightarrow t = 4$ s.
(c) $v^2 = 400 - 2(10)(0) \Rightarrow v = -20$ m/s (downward).

**Key insight:** At the peak $v = 0$; time of flight is symmetric about the peak.

---

**Setup:** A car brakes from 30 m/s to rest in 75 m. Find the deceleration.

**Solution:** $v^2 = u^2 + 2as$: $0 = 900 + 2a(75) \Rightarrow a = -6$ m/s².

**Key insight:** No time given → use the $t$-free equation.

## Common Traps

- Wrong sign of $a$ for vertical motion (upward positive → $a = -g$)
- Using $s = ut + \frac12 at^2$ when $a$ isn't constant
- Confusing displacement (signed) with distance in "how far" questions
- Reading $v = 0$ at the top of a throw and forgetting it's instantaneous
- Picking an equation before listing known/unknown quantities
