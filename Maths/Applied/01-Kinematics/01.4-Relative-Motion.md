
## Definition

The velocity/acceleration of one object **as observed from another moving object**:

$$\vec{v}_{A/B} = \vec{v}_A - \vec{v}_B, \qquad \vec{a}_{A/B} = \vec{a}_A - \vec{a}_B$$

1D: signed scalar subtraction. 2D: vector subtraction, magnitude via the cosine rule:
$$|\vec{v}_{A/B}| = \sqrt{v_A^2 + v_B^2 - 2v_Av_B\cos\theta}$$

## The Intuition

Two cars on a motorway: A at 70 mph, B at 60 mph. From B's perspective, A moves forward at 10 mph; from A's perspective, B moves backward at 10 mph. Stand on one moving object and *your* velocity subtracts from everything you observe. Subtracting a constant velocity from all objects doesn't change accelerations — **Galilean relativity** (Newton's laws hold in all inertial frames).

## The Toolkit

| Result | Formula |
|--------|---------|
| Relative velocity | $\vec{v}_{A/B} = \vec{v}_A - \vec{v}_B$ |
| Relative acceleration | $\vec{a}_{A/B} = \vec{a}_A - \vec{a}_B$ |
| Relative SUVAT | $s_{A/B} = u_{A/B}t + \tfrac12 a_{A/B}t^2$ (constant $a$) |
| Closest approach | $t_{\min} = -\dfrac{\vec{r}_{A/B}(0)\cdot\vec{v}_{A/B}}{\|\vec{v}_{A/B}\|^2}$ |
| Collision condition | $\vec{r}_{A/B}(t) = \vec{0}$ for some $t > 0$ |
| String constraint | constant length → differentiate for velocity/acceleration constraints |

## Derivation

Standing on object B means every position is measured from B: $\vec{r}_{A/B} = \vec{r}_A - \vec{r}_B$; differentiating gives the velocity, twice the acceleration. Closest approach minimises $d^2(t) = |\vec{r}_{A/B}(0) + \vec{v}_{A/B}t|^2$ — differentiate, set to zero. Collision requires the relative *position* to vanish (stronger than closest approach). [Full derivations: 01.4-Proofs-Relative-Motion]

## Method

1. **Pick a frame** — ground or moving; a moving frame often simplifies overtaking/collision problems.
2. Compute relative velocity $\vec{v}_{A/B} = \vec{v}_A - \vec{v}_B$.
3. For **closest approach**, apply the $t_{\min}$ formula directly.
4. For **collision**, solve $\vec{r}_{A/B}(t) = \vec{0}$; check $t > 0$.
5. For **pulley/string systems**, differentiate the constant-length constraint.

## Worked Examples

**Setup:** Car A starts from rest, accelerates at 2 m/s². Car B passes at 30 m/s constant. When does A catch B?

**Solution:** In the ground frame: $s_A = \frac12(2)t^2 = t^2$, $s_B = 30t$. Catch: $t^2 = 30t \Rightarrow t = 30$ s.

**Key insight:** Equating ground-frame positions is simplest when both are measured from the same origin.

---

**Setup:** A and B are at $\vec{r}_{A/B}(0) = (100, 0)$ m with $\vec{v}_{A/B} = (-10, 5)$ m/s. Find the time of closest approach.

**Solution:** $t_{\min} = -\frac{(100,0)\cdot(-10,5)}{|-10,5|^2} = \frac{1000}{125} = 8$ s.

**Key insight:** The formula is a projection — closest approach happens when the relative position is perpendicular to the relative velocity.

## Common Traps

- Subtracting in the wrong order — $\vec{v}_{A/B}$ vs $\vec{v}_{B/A}$ are opposites
- Using relative SUVAT when $a_{A/B}$ isn't constant
- Confusing "collision" (position zero) with "closest approach" (distance minimised, possibly non-zero)
- Forgetting to check $t > 0$ for collision times
- String constraints: differentiating the length once gives velocity; twice gives acceleration
