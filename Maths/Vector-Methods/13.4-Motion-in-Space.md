# Motion in Space: Velocity and Acceleration

This section applies everything from 13.1-Vector-Functions-and-Space-Curves to physics. Position $\mathbf{r}(t)$ describes where an object is. Velocity is the derivative of position, acceleration is the derivative of velocity, and acceleration splits into tangential and normal components that reveal what the motion is actually doing.

**The Intuition:** Driving a car. The gas pedal controls $a_T$ (speeding up or slowing down). The steering wheel controls $a_N$ (turning). You can brake while turning ($a_T < 0$, $a_N > 0$), or accelerate on a straight road ($a_T > 0$, $a_N = 0$). At any point on the curve, the acceleration vector splits into two arrows: one along the curve ($a_T\mathbf{T}$), one toward the inside of the bend ($a_N\mathbf{N}$).

**The Math:** For position $\mathbf{r}(t)$:

- **Velocity:** $\mathbf{v}(t) = \mathbf{r}'(t)$
- **Acceleration:** $\mathbf{a}(t) = \mathbf{v}'(t) = \mathbf{r}''(t)$
- **Speed:** $v(t) = \|\mathbf{v}(t)\| = \dfrac{ds}{dt}$
- **Tangential component:** $a_T = \dfrac{dv}{dt} = \dfrac{\mathbf{v} \cdot \mathbf{a}}{\|\mathbf{v}\|}$
- **Normal component:** $a_N = \kappa v^2 = \dfrac{\|\mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{a}\|}{\|\mathbf{v}\|}$
- **Decomposition:** $\mathbf{a} = a_T\mathbf{T} + a_N\mathbf{N}$
- **Pythagorean check:** $\|\mathbf{a}\|^2 = a_T^2 + a_N^2$

Newton's second law in vector form is $\mathbf{F} = m\mathbf{a}$.

**Setup:** $\mathbf{r}(t) = \langle t^2, t^3, t \rangle$. Find $\mathbf{v}$, $\mathbf{a}$, and speed.

**Solution:** $\mathbf{v} = \langle 2t, 3t^2, 1 \rangle$. $\mathbf{a} = \langle 2, 6t, 0 \rangle$. $v = \sqrt{4t^2 + 9t^4 + 1}$.

**Key insight:** Velocity and acceleration are found by successive differentiation. Speed is the magnitude of velocity.

**Setup:** At $t = 1$ for the same curve, find $a_T$ and $a_N$.

**Solution:** $\mathbf{v}(1) = \langle 2, 3, 1 \rangle$, $\mathbf{a}(1) = \langle 2, 6, 0 \rangle$. $v = \sqrt{14}$. $a_T = \dfrac{4 + 18}{\sqrt{14}} = \dfrac{22}{\sqrt{14}}$. $\mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{a} = \langle -6, 2, 8 \rangle$, so $a_N = \dfrac{\sqrt{104}}{\sqrt{14}}$. Verify: $a_T^2 + a_N^2 = 42 = \|\mathbf{a}\|^2$.

**Key insight:** The dot product formula for $a_T$ and cross product formula for $a_N$ are the most direct methods. Always verify with $\|\mathbf{a}\|^2 = a_T^2 + a_N^2$.

**Setup:** A ball is thrown with initial velocity $\mathbf{v}_0 = \langle v_0 \cos\theta, 0, v_0 \sin\theta \rangle$. Find $\mathbf{r}(t)$.

**Solution:** $\mathbf{a} = \langle 0, 0, -g \rangle$ (constant). Integrate twice: $\mathbf{r}(t) = \langle v_0 t \cos\theta,\; 0,\; v_0 t \sin\theta - \frac{1}{2}gt^2 \rangle$.

**Key insight:** Projectile motion traces a parabola. The path has $a_N > 0$ except at the vertex, where the curvature is smallest.


### Geometry

**Triangle centroid:** $\mathbf{g} = \dfrac{\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b} + \mathbf{c}}{3}$. The centroid is the average of the three vertex position vectors — it's the balance point of the triangle.

**Tetrahedron volume:** $V = \dfrac{1}{6}\left|(\mathbf{b} - \mathbf{a}) \cdot \left((\mathbf{c} - \mathbf{a}) \times (\mathbf{d} - \mathbf{a})\right)\right|$. The scalar triple product gives the volume of the parallelepiped; dividing by 6 gives the tetrahedron.

**Reciprocal vectors:** $\mathbf{a}' = \dfrac{\mathbf{b} \times \mathbf{c}}{[\mathbf{a}\;\mathbf{b}\;\mathbf{c}]}$, with cyclic permutations for $\mathbf{b}'$ and $\mathbf{c}'$. These satisfy $\mathbf{a}' \cdot \mathbf{a} = 1$ and $\mathbf{a}' \cdot \mathbf{b} = 0$, useful in crystallography and solving vector equations.

### Electromagnetism

**Lorentz force:** $\mathbf{F} = q(\mathbf{E} + \mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{B})$. The electric force is along $\mathbf{E}$; the magnetic force is perpendicular to both $\mathbf{v}$ and $\mathbf{B}$. Since $\mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{B} \perp \mathbf{v}$, the magnetic component does no work.

**Current force:** $d\mathbf{F} = I\,d\mathbf{l} \times \mathbf{B}$. A current-carrying wire in a magnetic field experiences a force perpendicular to both the current direction and the field.

**Poynting vector:** $\mathbf{S} = \dfrac{1}{\mu_0}\mathbf{E} \times \mathbf{B}$. Points in the direction of electromagnetic energy flow; its magnitude is the power per unit area.

### Mechanics

**Relative velocity:** $\mathbf{v}_{A/B} = \mathbf{v}_A - \mathbf{v}_B$. The velocity of A as observed from B. This is just vector subtraction — the same operation regardless of context.

**Closest approach:** $t = -\dfrac{\mathbf{r} \cdot \mathbf{v}}{\|\mathbf{v}\|^2}$. Closest approach occurs when the relative position is perpendicular to the relative velocity. The formula gives the time at which this happens.

### A/L Additional Notes

**What does this mean for Pure Mathematics?** Seeing how the cross product naturally describes torque or magnetic force gives geometric intuition for why the cross product has the properties it does. These applications deepen your grasp of the vector operations themselves.
