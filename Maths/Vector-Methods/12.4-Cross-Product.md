# The Cross Product

The dot product measures alignment. The cross product measures the opposite — how much one vector goes perpendicular to the other. It produces a new vector orthogonal to both inputs, with magnitude equal to the area of the parallelogram they span.

**The Intuition:** Hold your right hand flat with fingers pointing along $\mathbf{u}$. Curl them toward $\mathbf{v}$. Your thumb points along $\mathbf{u} \times \mathbf{v}$. The area your fingers sweep is $\|\mathbf{u} \times \mathbf{v}\|$. Physics needs this construction for rotation and torque — quantities that require a direction perpendicular to both the lever arm and the force.

**The Math:** For $\mathbf{u} = \langle u_1, u_2, u_3 \rangle$ and $\mathbf{v} = \langle v_1, v_2, v_3 \rangle$:

$$\mathbf{u} \times \mathbf{v} = \begin{vmatrix} \mathbf{i} & \mathbf{j} & \mathbf{k} \\ u_1 & u_2 & u_3 \\ v_1 & v_2 & v_3 \end{vmatrix} = \langle u_2 v_3 - u_3 v_2,\; u_3 v_1 - u_1 v_3,\; u_1 v_2 - u_2 v_1 \rangle$$

The magnitude is $\|\mathbf{u} \times \mathbf{v}\| = \|\mathbf{u}\|\;\|\mathbf{v}\|\sin\theta$, and the direction follows the right-hand rule. The cross product is anti-commutative: $\mathbf{u} \times \mathbf{v} = -(\mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{u})$. If $\mathbf{u}$ and $\mathbf{v}$ are parallel, the cross product is $\mathbf{0}$.

The area of a parallelogram spanned by $\mathbf{u}$ and $\mathbf{v}$ is $\|\mathbf{u} \times \mathbf{v}\|$; the triangle area is half that. The scalar triple product $\mathbf{u} \cdot (\mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{w})$ gives the volume of the parallelepiped, and equals zero iff the three vectors are coplanar. Torque is $\boldsymbol{\tau} = \mathbf{r} \times \mathbf{F}$.

**Setup:** Let $\mathbf{u} = \langle 1, 2, 3 \rangle$ and $\mathbf{v} = \langle 4, 5, 6 \rangle$. Find $\mathbf{u} \times \mathbf{v}$.

**Solution:** Expanding the determinant: $\mathbf{u} \times \mathbf{v} = \langle 2(6)-3(5),\; 3(4)-1(6),\; 1(5)-2(4) \rangle = \langle -3, 6, -3 \rangle$.

**Key insight:** Each component uses the 2×2 minor from the remaining rows and columns, with alternating signs.

**Setup:** Find the area of the triangle with vertices $A(1,0,0)$, $B(0,1,0)$, $C(0,0,1)$.

**Solution:** $\overrightarrow{AB} = \langle -1,1,0 \rangle$, $\overrightarrow{AC} = \langle -1,0,1 \rangle$. $\overrightarrow{AB} \times \overrightarrow{AC} = \langle 1, 1, 1 \rangle$. Area $= \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{3}$.

**Key insight:** The cross product of two edge vectors gives a vector whose magnitude is the parallelogram area. Halve it for the triangle.

**Setup:** Find the volume of the parallelepiped spanned by $\mathbf{u} = \langle 1,0,0 \rangle$, $\mathbf{v} = \langle 0,2,0 \rangle$, $\mathbf{w} = \langle 0,0,3 \rangle$.

**Solution:** $\mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{w} = \langle 6, 0, 0 \rangle$. $\mathbf{u} \cdot (\mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{w}) = 6$. Volume $= 6$.

**Key insight:** When vectors align with the axes, the scalar triple product is just the product of the component magnitudes — the 3D analogue of base times height.

### A/L Additional Notes: Cross Product

**What does this mean for Pure Mathematics?** The cross product is only defined in 3D. The determinant computation encodes a deep geometric fact: the cross product constructs a vector whose length is the area of the parallelogram formed by the two input vectors. The right-hand rule determines the sign (direction) of the result.

**Setup:** $\mathbf{a} = 2\mathbf{i} - \mathbf{j} + 3\mathbf{k}$, $\mathbf{b} = \mathbf{i} + 2\mathbf{j} - \mathbf{k}$. Find $\mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{b}$. **Solution:** $\mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{b} = \mathbf{i}(1 - 6) - \mathbf{j}(-2 - 3) + \mathbf{k}(4 + 1) = -5\mathbf{i} + 5\mathbf{j} + 5\mathbf{k}$. $\|\mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{b}\| = 5\sqrt{3}$. **Key insight:** Check the $\mathbf{j}$-component carefully — the minus sign in the cofactor expansion gives $-(-2-3) = +5$.

## Triple Products

Triple products combine three vectors using dot and cross products. The scalar triple gives volume; the vector triple expands via the BAC-CAB identity. They compute volumes of parallelepipeds and tetrahedra, test coplanarity, and simplify complicated vector expressions.

**The Intuition:** The scalar triple product is a volume calculator. The cross product $\mathbf{b} \times \mathbf{c}$ gives the area of the base parallelogram and a normal direction. Dotting with $\mathbf{a}$ multiplies by the height, giving volume. A skewed box (parallelepiped) with edges $\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b}, \mathbf{c}$ — the scalar triple product measures its signed volume. If $\mathbf{a}$ lies in the base plane, the volume is zero (coplanar).

**The Math:** Scalar triple product: $[\mathbf{a}\;\mathbf{b}\;\mathbf{c}] = \mathbf{a} \cdot (\mathbf{b} \times \mathbf{c}) = \begin{vmatrix} a_1 & a_2 & a_3 \\ b_1 & b_2 & b_3 \\ c_1 & c_2 & c_3 \end{vmatrix}$. Volume of parallelepiped: $|[\mathbf{a}\;\mathbf{b}\;\mathbf{c}]|$. Volume of tetrahedron: $\frac{1}{6}|[\mathbf{a}\;\mathbf{b}\;\mathbf{c}]|$. Coplanarity test: $[\mathbf{a}\;\mathbf{b}\;\mathbf{c}] = 0$.

Vector triple product: $\mathbf{a} \times (\mathbf{b} \times \mathbf{c}) = (\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{c})\mathbf{b} - (\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b})\mathbf{c}$ (BAC-CAB). The result lies in the plane of $\mathbf{b}$ and $\mathbf{c}$.

Cyclic permutation preserves the scalar triple: $\mathbf{a} \cdot (\mathbf{b} \times \mathbf{c}) = \mathbf{b} \cdot (\mathbf{c} \times \mathbf{a}) = \mathbf{c} \cdot (\mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{b})$. Dot and cross can be interchanged: $\mathbf{a} \cdot (\mathbf{b} \times \mathbf{c}) = (\mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{b}) \cdot \mathbf{c}$. The vector triple product is NOT associative.

**What does this mean for Pure Mathematics?** Triple products are where the dot and cross products combine to solve 3D geometry problems. The scalar triple product turns three vectors into a single number — the volume. The BAC-CAB identity converts a complicated nested cross product into simple dot products and scalar multiplications.

### A/L Worked Example: Triple Products

**Setup:** $\mathbf{a} = 2\mathbf{i} - \mathbf{j} + \mathbf{k}$, $\mathbf{b} = \mathbf{i} + 2\mathbf{j} - \mathbf{k}$, $\mathbf{c} = 3\mathbf{i} - \mathbf{j} + 2\mathbf{k}$. Find $[\mathbf{a}\;\mathbf{b}\;\mathbf{c}]$. **Solution:** $\begin{vmatrix} 2 & -1 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 & -1 \\ 3 & -1 & 2 \end{vmatrix} = 2(4-1) + 1(2+3) + 1(-1-6) = 6 + 5 - 7 = 4$. Volume $= 4$. **Key insight:** The determinant gives the signed volume directly. Take absolute value for actual volume.

**Setup:** Are $\mathbf{a} = \mathbf{i} + \mathbf{j} + \mathbf{k}$, $\mathbf{b} = 2\mathbf{i} + 3\mathbf{j} + 4\mathbf{k}$, $\mathbf{c} = 3\mathbf{i} + 4\mathbf{j} + 5\mathbf{k}$ coplanar? **Solution:** $\begin{vmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 2 & 3 & 4 \\ 3 & 4 & 5 \end{vmatrix} = -1 + 2 - 1 = 0$. Coplanar. **Key insight:** A zero scalar triple product means zero volume, which means the vectors lie in a plane.
