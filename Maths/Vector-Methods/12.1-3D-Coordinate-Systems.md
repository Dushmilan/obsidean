# Three-Dimensional Coordinate Systems

We already know that two perpendicular axes pin down a point on a flat surface. Three-dimensional coordinate geometry adds a third axis — the z-axis — which points out of the plane. Every point in space is now described by an ordered triple $(x, y, z)$ in $\mathbb{R}^3$.

**The Intuition:** Think of a point in space as GPS coordinates: latitude, longitude, and altitude. Each coordinate tells you how far you are in one orthogonal direction. To locate $(3, 2, 1)$, walk 3 units along $x$, 2 units along $y$, then rise 1 unit along $z$. Three mutually perpendicular number lines meeting at the origin — that is the entire setup.

**The Math:** A point in $\mathbb{R}^3$ is an ordered triple $(x, y, z)$ where $x$, $y$, $z$ are real numbers measuring signed distances from the $yz$-plane, $xz$-plane, and $xy$-plane respectively. The distance formula extends Pythagoras twice:

$$d(P_1, P_2) = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2 + (z_2 - z_1)^2}$$

A sphere centred at $(h, k, l)$ with radius $r$ satisfies:

$$(x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 + (z - l)^2 = r^2$$

The axes are assumed mutually perpendicular (orthonormal Cartesian system). This breaks down in non-Cartesian coordinates (spherical, cylindrical) without conversion.

**Setup:** Find the distance between $P(1, -2, 3)$ and $Q(4, 0, -1)$.

**Solution:** $d = \sqrt{(4-1)^2 + (0+2)^2 + (-1-3)^2} = \sqrt{9 + 4 + 16} = \sqrt{29} \approx 5.39$ units.

**Key insight:** Treat each coordinate difference independently, square it, sum, and take the square root — Pythagoras in three dimensions.

**Setup:** A sphere has centre $C(2, -1, 3)$ and passes through $P(5, 3, 3)$. Find its equation.

**Solution:** $r^2 = (5-2)^2 + (3+1)^2 + (3-3)^2 = 9 + 16 + 0 = 25$. The equation is $(x-2)^2 + (y+1)^2 + (z-3)^2 = 25$.

**Key insight:** Find the radius by computing the distance from centre to the given point, then substitute into standard form.

**Setup:** Describe the surface $x^2 + y^2 = 9$ in $\mathbb{R}^3$.

**Solution:** The variable $z$ is missing. In 2D this is a circle of radius 3. In 3D, since $z$ is free, the circle extends along the entire $z$-axis, forming a right circular cylinder.

**Key insight:** A missing variable in 3D always means the surface extends infinitely along that variable's axis.
