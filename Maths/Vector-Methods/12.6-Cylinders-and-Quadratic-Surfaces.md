# Cylinders and Quadratic Surfaces

In 2D, second-degree equations produce conic sections. In 3D, second-degree equations in $x$, $y$, $z$ produce quadric surfaces — the 3D versions of those same shapes.

**The Intuition:** An ellipsoid is a stretched sphere. A hyperboloid of one sheet looks like a cooling tower (narrowest in the middle). A hyperboloid of two sheets looks like two separate bowls facing away. A cone is the boundary case between them. A paraboloid is a 3D parabola — a satellite dish. These surfaces arise naturally when you rotate 2D conic sections around an axis.

**The Math:** The classification depends on the signs of the squared terms. Assume the coordinate system is aligned with the principal axes (no cross-terms):

- **Ellipsoid:** $\dfrac{x^2}{a^2} + \dfrac{y^2}{b^2} + \dfrac{z^2}{c^2} = 1$ — all positive, traces are ellipses
- **Hyperboloid of one sheet:** one minus sign, e.g. $\dfrac{x^2}{a^2} + \dfrac{y^2}{b^2} - \dfrac{z^2}{c^2} = 1$ — the axis of the hyperboloid is the variable whose term is negative
- **Hyperboloid of two sheets:** two minus signs, surface has two separate parts
- **Elliptic cone:** one minus sign equals zero, e.g. $\dfrac{x^2}{a^2} + \dfrac{y^2}{b^2} - \dfrac{z^2}{c^2} = 0$
- **Elliptic paraboloid:** $z = \dfrac{x^2}{a^2} + \dfrac{y^2}{b^2}$ — one linear variable, two squared with same sign
- **Hyperbolic paraboloid:** $z = \dfrac{x^2}{a^2} - \dfrac{y^2}{b^2}$ — saddle-shaped
- **Cylinder:** $f(x,y) = 0$ with $z$ free extends along the $z$-axis

**Setup:** Identify $4x^2 + 9y^2 + z^2 = 36$.

**Solution:** Divide by 36: $\dfrac{x^2}{9} + \dfrac{y^2}{4} + \dfrac{z^2}{36} = 1$. All terms positive, equals 1 — this is an ellipsoid with semi-axes 3, 2, 6.

**Key insight:** Divide through to get 1 on the right. Count the signs: all positive means ellipsoid.

**Setup:** Identify $x^2 + \dfrac{y^2}{4} - \dfrac{z^2}{9} = 1$.

**Solution:** One negative term, right side 1 — hyperboloid of one sheet, opening along the $z$-axis.

**Key insight:** One minus sign with right side = 1 gives one sheet. The axis is the variable whose term is negative.

**Setup:** Describe $z = x^2 + y^2$ using traces.

**Solution:** Horizontal traces ($z = k > 0$): circles of radius $\sqrt{k}$. Vertical traces: parabolas opening upward. This is a circular elliptic paraboloid, vertex at the origin.

**Key insight:** Horizontal traces tell you the cross-sectional shape; vertical traces tell you the profile. Parabolic profile + circular cross-section = paraboloid.
