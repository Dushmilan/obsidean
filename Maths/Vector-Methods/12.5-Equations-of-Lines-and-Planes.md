# Equations of Lines and Planes

In 2D, a line is described by a single linear equation. In 3D, a line requires two equations (the intersection of two planes) or, more elegantly, a vector equation. A plane needs one constraint — reducing 3 dimensions to 2 — and the normal vector provides exactly that.

**The Intuition:** A line in 3D is like a flight path: start at an airport ($\mathbf{r}_0$), fly in a fixed direction ($\mathbf{v}$) at constant speed. Different values of $t$ give different points along the path. A plane is like a wall: it has a fixed orientation (the normal vector $\mathbf{n}$) and passes through a specific point.

**The Math:** A line through point $P_0(x_0, y_0, z_0)$ with direction vector $\mathbf{v} = \langle a, b, c \rangle$ has three equivalent descriptions:

- **Vector equation:** $\mathbf{r} = \mathbf{r}_0 + t\mathbf{v}$
- **Parametric equations:** $x = x_0 + at$, $y = y_0 + bt$, $z = z_0 + ct$
- **Symmetric equations:** $\dfrac{x - x_0}{a} = \dfrac{y - y_0}{b} = \dfrac{z - z_0}{c}$ (when no component of $\mathbf{v}$ is zero)

A plane through $P_0$ with normal vector $\mathbf{n} = \langle a, b, c \rangle$ has scalar equation $a(x - x_0) + b(y - y_0) + c(z - z_0) = 0$, or equivalently $ax + by + cz = d$. The distance from a point $Q$ to the plane is $D = \dfrac{|a x_Q + b y_Q + c z_Q - d|}{\|\mathbf{n}\|}$.

**Setup:** Find the vector and parametric equations of the line through $P(1, -2, 3)$ with direction $\mathbf{v} = \langle 2, 4, -1 \rangle$.

**Solution:** Vector: $\mathbf{r} = \langle 1, -2, 3 \rangle + t\langle 2, 4, -1 \rangle$. Parametric: $x = 1 + 2t$, $y = -2 + 4t$, $z = 3 - t$.

**Key insight:** Choose any point on the line as $\mathbf{r}_0$ and any non-zero vector parallel to the line as $\mathbf{v}$. The parameter $t$ is free.

**Setup:** Find the equation of the plane through $P(1, 2, -1)$ with normal $\mathbf{n} = \langle 3, -2, 4 \rangle$.

**Solution:** $3(x-1) - 2(y-2) + 4(z+1) = 0$ simplifies to $3x - 2y + 4z = -5$.

**Key insight:** The normal vector's components become the coefficients of $x$, $y$, $z$ in the scalar equation.

**Setup:** Find the distance from $Q(3, 1, -2)$ to the plane $2x - y + 2z = 4$.

**Solution:** $D = \dfrac{|2(3) - 1(1) + 2(-2) - 4|}{\sqrt{4+1+4}} = \dfrac{|-3|}{3} = 1$.

**Key insight:** Substitute the point's coordinates into the plane equation, take the absolute value, and divide by the magnitude of the normal vector.

### A/L Additional Notes

**What does this mean for Pure Mathematics?** The key insight is that intersections, distances, and angles all reduce to the same underlying operations: substitution, dot products, and cross products. Cartesian and vector forms are interchangeable.

**Distance formulas (extended):**

- Point-to-line: $\dfrac{\|(\mathbf{p} - \mathbf{a}) \times \mathbf{d}\|}{\|\mathbf{d}\|}$
- Skew lines distance: $\dfrac{|(\mathbf{b} - \mathbf{a}) \cdot (\mathbf{d} \times \mathbf{e})|}{\|\mathbf{d} \times \mathbf{e}\|}$
- Line-plane angle: $\sin\theta = \dfrac{|\mathbf{d} \cdot \mathbf{n}|}{\|\mathbf{d}\|\|\mathbf{n}\|}$ (note: sine, not cosine)
- Two planes angle: $\cos\theta = \dfrac{|\mathbf{n}_1 \cdot \mathbf{n}_2|}{\|\mathbf{n}_1\|\|\mathbf{n}_2\|}$

### A/L Worked Examples

**Setup:** Line through $A(1,2,3)$ parallel to $2\mathbf{i} - \mathbf{j} + \mathbf{k}$. Write vector and cartesian equations.

**Solution:** Vector: $\mathbf{r} = \mathbf{i} + 2\mathbf{j} + 3\mathbf{k} + \lambda(2\mathbf{i} - \mathbf{j} + \mathbf{k})$. Cartesian: $\frac{x-1}{2} = \frac{y-2}{-1} = \frac{z-3}{1}$.

**Key insight:** The direction vector components become the denominators in cartesian form.
