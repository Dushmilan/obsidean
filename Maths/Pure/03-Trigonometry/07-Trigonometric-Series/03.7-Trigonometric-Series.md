
## Definition

Trigonometric series are finite sums of sines/cosines whose angles form an **arithmetic progression**:

$$\sin\theta + \sin(\theta+\alpha) + \cdots + \sin(\theta+(n-1)\alpha)$$

The **C + iS method** converts these into geometric series in $e^{i\alpha}$, giving closed forms.

## The Intuition

Adding equally spaced rotations on the complex plane: each term is a unit vector at a specific angle — a "walk" with equally spaced steps. Multiplying by $e^{i\alpha}$ rotates each step by $\alpha$, making the whole sum a geometric series, which collapses into a single closed-form expression.

## The Toolkit

| Sum | Closed form | Valid when |
|-----|-------------|-----------|
| $\sum_{r=0}^{n-1}\sin(\theta+r\alpha)$ | $\dfrac{\sin\frac{n\alpha}{2}}{\sin\frac{\alpha}{2}}\sin\left(\theta+\frac{(n-1)\alpha}{2}\right)$ | $\sin\frac{\alpha}{2} \neq 0$ |
| $\sum_{r=0}^{n-1}\cos(\theta+r\alpha)$ | $\dfrac{\sin\frac{n\alpha}{2}}{\sin\frac{\alpha}{2}}\cos\left(\theta+\frac{(n-1)\alpha}{2}\right)$ | $\sin\frac{\alpha}{2} \neq 0$ |
| Fourier sum | $\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\sin k\theta}{k} = \frac{\pi-\theta}{2}$ | $0 < \theta < 2\pi$ |
| Product identity | $\cos\frac{\pi}{7}\cos\frac{2\pi}{7}\cos\frac{3\pi}{7} = \frac18$ | telescope with $2\sin$ |

## Derivation

Write $S = \sum e^{i(\theta+r\alpha)} = e^{i\theta}\sum (e^{i\alpha})^r$ — a geometric series with ratio $e^{i\alpha}$. Sum it: $S = e^{i\theta}\frac{1-e^{in\alpha}}{1-e^{i\alpha}}$. Take the imaginary part for sines, real part for cosines; the half-angle factor appears from $\frac{1-e^{i\phi}}{1-e^{i\phi/2}}$. Product identities telescope via repeated double-angle. [Full derivations: 03.7-Trigonometric-Series-Proof]

## Method

1. **Identify $n, \alpha, \theta$** from the series.
2. **Check the condition** $\sin(\alpha/2) \neq 0$ (i.e. $\alpha \neq 2k\pi$).
3. Apply the closed form directly.
4. For product identities, multiply by $2\sin(\text{first angle})$ and telescope.

## Worked Examples

**Setup:** Find $\sin 10^\circ + \sin 20^\circ + \cdots + \sin 170^\circ$.

**Solution:** $n = 17$, $\alpha = 10^\circ$, $\theta = 10^\circ$:
$$S = \frac{\sin 85^\circ \cdot \sin 90^\circ}{\sin 5^\circ} = \frac{\cos 5^\circ}{\sin 5^\circ} = \cot 5^\circ$$

**Key insight:** The formula collapses 17 terms into a single expression.

---

**Setup:** Prove $\cos\frac{\pi}{7}\cos\frac{2\pi}{7}\cos\frac{3\pi}{7} = \frac18$.

**Solution:** Let $P$ = product. Multiply by $8\sin\frac{\pi}{7}$:
$$8\sin\frac{\pi}{7}P = 4\sin\frac{2\pi}{7}\cos\frac{2\pi}{7}\cos\frac{3\pi}{7} = 2\sin\frac{4\pi}{7}\cos\frac{3\pi}{7} = \sin\pi + \sin\frac{\pi}{7} = \sin\frac{\pi}{7}$$
So $8P = 1$, i.e. $P = \frac18$.

**Key insight:** Multiplying by $2\sin$ of the first angle creates a telescoping product via double-angle formulas.

## Common Traps

- $\sin(\alpha/2) = 0$ (i.e. $\alpha = 2k\pi$) — the formula blows up; handle separately
- Confusing the C and S formulae — the $\sin\frac{n\alpha}{2}$ factor is shared, only the final trig function differs
- Sign errors in the average angle $\theta + \frac{(n-1)\alpha}{2}$
- Forgetting that the formula sums $n$ terms starting at $r = 0$
- Telescoping products: missing the $\frac12$ factors from repeated double angles
