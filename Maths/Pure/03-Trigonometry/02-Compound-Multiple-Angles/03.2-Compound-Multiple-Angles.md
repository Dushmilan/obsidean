
## Definition

Compound angle formulas express trig functions of $A \pm B$ in terms of functions of $A$ and $B$ alone:

$$\sin(A\pm B) = \sin A\cos B \pm \cos A\sin B$$
$$\cos(A\pm B) = \cos A\cos B \mp \sin A\sin B$$
$$\tan(A\pm B) = \frac{\tan A \pm \tan B}{1 \mp \tan A\tan B}$$

Setting $A = B$ gives double-angle; $B = A/2$ gives half-angle; adding/subtracting gives sum-to-product. The tangent half-angle substitution $t = \tan(\theta/2)$ turns any trig expression into rational algebra.

## The Intuition

Rotate a point on the unit circle by angle $A$, then by angle $B$ — the total rotation is $A + B$. The compound formula gives the coordinates after the combined rotation in terms of the individual rotations. Rotation is a linear transformation, and composing two rotations multiplies their rotation matrices — the formulas are just matrix multiplication written out.

## The Toolkit

| Result | Formula |
|--------|---------|
| Sine sum/diff | $\sin(A\pm B) = \sin A\cos B \pm \cos A\sin B$ |
| Cosine sum/diff | $\cos(A\pm B) = \cos A\cos B \mp \sin A\sin B$ |
| Tangent sum/diff | $\tan(A\pm B) = \dfrac{\tan A \pm \tan B}{1 \mp \tan A\tan B}$ |
| Double angle | $\sin 2A = 2\sin A\cos A$ |
| Double angle (cos) | $\cos 2A = \cos^2 A - \sin^2 A = 2\cos^2 A - 1 = 1 - 2\sin^2 A$ |
| Triple angle | $\sin 3A = 3\sin A - 4\sin^3 A$; $\cos 3A = 4\cos^3 A - 3\cos A$ |
| Half-angle tangent | $\tan\frac{A}{2} = \dfrac{1-\cos A}{\sin A} = \dfrac{\sin A}{1+\cos A}$ |
| $t$-substitution | $\sin\theta = \dfrac{2t}{1+t^2}$, $\cos\theta = \dfrac{1-t^2}{1+t^2}$, $t = \tan\frac{\theta}{2}$ |
| Sum-to-product | $\sin A + \sin B = 2\sin\frac{A+B}{2}\cos\frac{A-B}{2}$ |
| Amplitude-phase | $a\sin\theta + b\cos\theta = R\sin(\theta+\alpha)$ |

## Derivation

The cosine difference formula is the one to memorize first — all others derive from it (substitute $B \to -B$, set $A = B$, etc.). It follows from the dot product of two unit vectors at angles $A$ and $B$: $\cos(A-B) = \cos A\cos B + \sin A\sin B$. Double angles: set $A = B$. Sum-to-product: let $A = \frac{P+Q}{2}$, $B = \frac{P-Q}{2}$ in the product formulas. [Full derivations: 03.2-Compound-Multiple-Angles-Proof]

## Method

1. **Exact values:** express the angle as a sum/difference of standard angles ($45^\circ, 30^\circ, 60^\circ$) and apply the compound formula.
2. **Solving equations:** expand compound terms first, or convert to single angles with double-angle formulas; **factorise — never divide by a trig function**.
3. **Choosing the cosine double form:** pick $\cos 2A = 2\cos^2 A - 1$ or $1 - 2\sin^2 A$ depending on what you know.
4. **$t$-substitution:** use when the equation mixes $\sin\theta, \cos\theta$ and constants — it becomes rational algebra in $t$.

## Worked Examples

**Setup:** Find the exact value of $\sin 15^\circ$.

**Solution:** $15^\circ = 45^\circ - 30^\circ$:
$$\sin(45-30) = \sin45\cos30 - \cos45\sin30 = \frac{1}{\sqrt2}\cdot\frac{\sqrt3}{2} - \frac{1}{\sqrt2}\cdot\frac12 = \frac{\sqrt6 - \sqrt2}{4}$$

**Key insight:** Any angle that is a sum/difference of standard angles can be found exactly.

---

**Setup:** Solve $\sin 2\theta = \cos\theta$ for $\theta \in [0, 2\pi]$.

**Solution:** $2\sin\theta\cos\theta = \cos\theta \Rightarrow \cos\theta(2\sin\theta - 1) = 0$. So $\theta = \frac{\pi}{2}, \frac{3\pi}{2}$ from $\cos\theta = 0$, and $\theta = \frac{\pi}{6}, \frac{5\pi}{6}$ from $\sin\theta = \frac12$.

**Key insight:** Factorise first. Never divide by a trig function — you might lose solutions.

## Common Traps

- Wrong sign in $\cos(A+B) = \cos A\cos B - \sin A\sin B$ (the "mp" pattern)
- Dividing by $\sin\theta$ or $\cos\theta$ — drops solutions; factor instead
- Forgetting both signs/quadrants when solving
- Triple-angle forms get confused with double-angle — derive, don't guess
- $t$-substitution domain: $\theta = \pi$ (where $\tan(\theta/2)$ is undefined) needs separate checking
