
## Definition

**De Moivre's Theorem** — raising a complex number to a power is easy in polar form:

$$(\cos\theta + i\sin\theta)^n = \cos n\theta + i\sin n\theta$$

Linked to **Euler's formula**: $e^{i\theta} = \cos\theta + i\sin\theta$, so $z = re^{i\theta} = r(\cos\theta + i\sin\theta)$ and $z^n = r^n e^{in\theta}$.

## The Intuition

Complex multiplication = **scaling and rotating**. Raising to a power multiplies $n$ times: scale by $r$ each time ($r^n$ total) and rotate by $\theta$ each time ($n\theta$ total). Multiplication in polar form multiplies moduli and adds arguments — that one rule powers De Moivre, roots, and Euler.

## The Toolkit

| Result | Formula |
|--------|---------|
| De Moivre | $(\cos\theta + i\sin\theta)^n = \cos n\theta + i\sin n\theta$ |
| Euler | $e^{i\theta} = \cos\theta + i\sin\theta$ |
| Euler forms | $\cos\theta = \dfrac{e^{i\theta}+e^{-i\theta}}{2}$, $\sin\theta = \dfrac{e^{i\theta}-e^{-i\theta}}{2i}$ |
| Roots of unity | $\omega_k = e^{2\pi i k/n}$, $k = 0,\dots,n-1$ |
| Sum of unity roots | $1 + \omega + \cdots + \omega^{n-1} = 0$ |
| $n$-th roots of $z$ | $z_k = r^{1/n}\left[\cos\frac{\theta+2k\pi}{n} + i\sin\frac{\theta+2k\pi}{n}\right]$ |
| $\cos^n\theta$ expansion | $\dfrac{1}{2^n}\sum_{k=0}^n \binom{n}{k}\cos((n-2k)\theta)$ |
| Hyperbolic link | $\cos(i\theta) = \cosh\theta$, $\sin(i\theta) = i\sinh\theta$ |

## Derivation

Euler's formula comes from the Taylor series of $e^{i\theta}$, $\cos\theta$, $\sin\theta$. De Moivre follows: $(e^{i\theta})^n = e^{in\theta}$. The $n$-th root formula: $z = re^{i(\theta + 2k\pi)}$ (the same angle plus full rotations), then raise to $1/n$ for each $k$. Sum of unity roots: a geometric series summing to $\frac{1-\omega^n}{1-\omega} = 0$. [Full derivations: 03.8-Complex-Numbers-De-Moivre-Proof]

## Method

1. **Power a complex number:** convert to polar form $re^{i\theta}$, raise to $r^n e^{in\theta}$, convert back.
2. **Multiple angles:** expand $(\cos\theta + i\sin\theta)^n$ by the binomial theorem; take the real part for $\cos n\theta$, imaginary for $\sin n\theta$.
3. **Find $n$-th roots:** write the target in polar form (add $2k\pi$), apply the root formula for $k = 0,\dots,n-1$.
4. **Roots of unity:** $\omega_k = e^{2\pi i k/n}$ — they form a regular $n$-gon on the unit circle, summing to zero.

## Worked Examples

**Setup:** Express $\cos 5\theta$ in powers of $\cos\theta$.

**Solution:** $(\cos\theta + i\sin\theta)^5 = \cos 5\theta + i\sin 5\theta$. Binomial expansion, take the real part:
$$\cos 5\theta = 16\cos^5\theta - 20\cos^3\theta + 5\cos\theta$$

**Key insight:** Binomial expansion on the polar form directly gives the multiple-angle polynomial.

---

**Setup:** Evaluate $(1+i)^{20}$.

**Solution:** $1+i = \sqrt2\, e^{i\pi/4}$, so $(1+i)^{20} = 2^{10} e^{i\cdot5\pi} = 1024(-1) = -1024$.

**Key insight:** Convert to polar form first, then apply De Moivre.

---

**Setup:** Find all roots of $z^6 + 1 = 0$.

**Solution:** $z^6 = -1 = e^{i\pi}$, so $z = e^{i(\pi+2k\pi)/6}$ for $k = 0,\dots,5$ — six roots forming a regular hexagon on the unit circle.

**Key insight:** Write in polar form, then apply the $n$-th root formula with all values of $k$.

## Common Traps

- Forgetting to add $2k\pi$ before taking roots — you'd only get one root
- Confusing $e^{i\theta}$ with $-e^{i\theta}$; check the sign of the imaginary part
- $\cos n\theta$ vs $\sin n\theta$ from the binomial — real part is cosine
- Roots of unity sum to zero only for the **complete** set
- $\cos(i\theta) = \cosh\theta$ — trigonometric functions of imaginary arguments are hyperbolic
