Trigonometric series are sums of the form $\sin\theta + \sin(\theta+\alpha) + \cdots + \sin(\theta+(n-1)\alpha)$ -- arithmetic progressions in the angle. The C+iS method converts these to geometric series in $e^{i\alpha}$, giving elegant closed forms that simplify sums arising in signal processing and Fourier analysis.

**The Intuition:** Imagine adding equally spaced rotations on the complex plane. Each term is a unit vector at a specific angle. The sum is like a "walk" with equally spaced steps. Multiplying by $e^{i\alpha}$ rotates each term by $\alpha$, making the sum a geometric series. The geometric series formula collapses the spiral into a single point.

**The Math:** Sum of sines (AP): $S_{\sin} = \frac{\sin(n\alpha/2)}{\sin(\alpha/2)} \cdot \sin\!\left(\theta + \frac{(n-1)\alpha}{2}\right)$

Sum of cosines (AP): $S_{\cos} = \frac{\sin(n\alpha/2)}{\sin(\alpha/2)} \cdot \cos\!\left(\theta + \frac{(n-1)\alpha}{2}\right)$

Condition: $\sin(\alpha/2) \neq 0$, i.e. $\alpha \neq 2k\pi$

Fourier sums: $\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \frac{\sin k\theta}{k} = \frac{\pi-\theta}{2}$ for $0 < \theta < 2\pi$

Product identity: $\cos\frac{\pi}{7}\cos\frac{2\pi}{7}\cos\frac{3\pi}{7} = \frac{1}{8}$ (multiply by $8\sin\frac{\pi}{7}$ and telescope)

**What does this mean for Pure Mathematics?** The C+iS method reveals the deep connection between trigonometry and complex exponentials. Every trig identity can be derived this way. The convergence properties of Fourier series are the foundation of harmonic analysis.

### Worked Examples

**Setup:** Find $\sin 10^\circ + \sin 20^\circ + \cdots + \sin 170^\circ$.

**Solution:**
1. $n = 17$, $\alpha = 10^\circ$, $\theta = 10^\circ$
2. $S = \frac{\sin 85^\circ \cdot \sin 90^\circ}{\sin 5^\circ} = \frac{\cos 5^\circ}{\sin 5^\circ} = \cot 5^\circ$

**Key insight:** The formula collapses 17 terms into a single expression.

**Setup:** Prove $\cos\frac{\pi}{7}\cos\frac{2\pi}{7}\cos\frac{3\pi}{7} = \frac{1}{8}$.

**Solution:**
1. Let $P$ = product. Multiply by $8\sin\frac{\pi}{7}$.
2. $8\sin\frac{\pi}{7} \cdot P = 4\sin\frac{2\pi}{7}\cos\frac{2\pi}{7}\cos\frac{3\pi}{7} = 2\sin\frac{4\pi}{7}\cos\frac{3\pi}{7}$
3. $= \sin\frac{7\pi}{7} + \sin\frac{\pi}{7} = \sin\frac{\pi}{7}$
4. So $8P = 1$, $P = 1/8$

**Key insight:** Multiplying by $8\sin(\text{first angle})$ creates a telescoping product using double angle formulas.
