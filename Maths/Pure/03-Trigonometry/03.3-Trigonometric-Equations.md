Trigonometric equations have infinitely many solutions because sin, cos, and tan are periodic. The first task is finding principal values -- the smallest angles that work. The second is expressing all solutions using an integer parameter $n$. Every trig equation ultimately reduces to one of three master equations.

**The Intuition:** Imagine a sine wave repeating forever. If $\sin\theta = 1/2$, you're asking: where does the horizontal line $y = 1/2$ cross the sine wave? It crosses infinitely many times, but the crossings come in a predictable pattern. The general solution formula encodes those intervals.

**The Math:** General solutions:

$\sin\theta = \sin\alpha \Rightarrow \theta = n\pi + (-1)^n\alpha$

$\cos\theta = \cos\alpha \Rightarrow \theta = 2n\pi \pm \alpha$

$\tan\theta = \tan\alpha \Rightarrow \theta = n\pi + \alpha$

Amplitude-phase: $a\sin\theta + b\cos\theta = c$ becomes $R\sin(\theta+\alpha) = c$ where $R = \sqrt{a^2+b^2}$. Solutions exist iff $|c| \le R$.

Principal ranges: $\sin^{-1}$ in $[-\pi/2, \pi/2]$, $\cos^{-1}$ in $[0, \pi]$, $\tan^{-1}$ in $(-\pi/2, \pi/2)$

**What does this mean for Pure Mathematics?** The key solving strategies are factorisation, identity substitution, the amplitude-phase form, and the tangent half-angle substitution. Always verify solutions -- squaring and substitution can introduce extraneous roots. Never divide by a trig function; always factor instead.

### Worked Examples

**Setup:** Solve $2\cos^2\theta - 3\cos\theta + 1 = 0$.

**Solution:**
1. Let $u = \cos\theta$: $(2u-1)(u-1) = 0$
2. $\cos\theta = 1/2 \Rightarrow \theta = 2n\pi \pm \pi/3$; $\cos\theta = 1 \Rightarrow \theta = 2n\pi$

**Key insight:** Substitution $u = \cos\theta$ turns it into a standard quadratic.

---

**Setup:** Solve $3\sin\theta + 4\cos\theta = 5$.

**Solution:**
1. $R = 5$, $\tan\alpha = 4/3$. So $5\sin(\theta+\alpha) = 5$, $\sin(\theta+\alpha) = 1$
2. $\theta = \pi/2 - \arctan(4/3) + 2n\pi$

**Key insight:** Since $|c| = R$ exactly, there is exactly one family of solutions.
