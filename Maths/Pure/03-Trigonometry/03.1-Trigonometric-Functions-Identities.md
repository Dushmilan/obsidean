The six trigonometric functions (sin, cos, tan, csc, sec, cot) are defined via the unit circle. Any point on that circle gives you an x and y coordinate, and those two numbers define all six functions. From these, a web of identities emerges, with the Pythagorean identity $\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta = 1$ as the deepest one.

**The Intuition:** Imagine walking around a clock face. At each position, your distance from the centre is 1 (the unit circle), but your horizontal and vertical distances change. Cosine tracks your left-right shadow on the x-axis. Sine tracks your up-down shadow on the y-axis. As the point moves, these shadows stretch and shrink between $-1$ and $1$.

**The Math:** Pythagorean identities: $\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta = 1$, $1 + \tan^2\theta = \sec^2\theta$, $1 + \cot^2\theta = \csc^2\theta$

Reciprocal: $\csc\theta = 1/\sin\theta$, $\sec\theta = 1/\cos\theta$, $\cot\theta = 1/\tan\theta$

Quotient: $\tan\theta = \sin\theta/\cos\theta$, $\cot\theta = \cos\theta/\sin\theta$

Double angle: $\sin 2\theta = 2\sin\theta\cos\theta$, $\cos 2\theta = \cos^2\theta - \sin^2\theta = 2\cos^2\theta - 1 = 1 - 2\sin^2\theta$

Half angle: $\sin(\theta/2) = \pm\sqrt{(1-\cos\theta)/2}$, $\cos(\theta/2) = \pm\sqrt{(1+\cos\theta)/2}$

Amplitude-phase: $a\sin\theta + b\cos\theta = R\sin(\theta+\alpha)$ where $R = \sqrt{a^2+b^2}$, $\tan\alpha = b/a$

**What does this mean for Pure Mathematics?** The circle equation $x^2+y^2=1$ directly gives $\sin^2+\cos^2=1$. The other identities follow by algebraic manipulation. Any periodic phenomenon -- sound waves, alternating current, tides -- is fundamentally trigonometric.

### Worked Examples

**Setup:** Prove $\sin\theta/(1-\cos\theta) = \csc\theta + \cot\theta$.

**Solution:**
1. Multiply by $(1+\cos\theta)$: $\frac{\sin\theta(1+\cos\theta)}{1-\cos^2\theta} = \frac{\sin\theta(1+\cos\theta)}{\sin^2\theta}$
2. $= \frac{1+\cos\theta}{\sin\theta} = \csc\theta + \cot\theta$

**Key insight:** Multiplying by the conjugate creates a Pythagorean identity in the denominator.

---

**Setup:** Find the maximum of $3\sin\theta + 4\cos\theta$.

**Solution:**
1. $R = \sqrt{9+16} = 5$, so the expression equals $5\sin(\theta+\alpha)$
2. Maximum value = 5

**Key insight:** Any $a\sin\theta + b\cos\theta$ has maximum $\sqrt{a^2+b^2}$.
