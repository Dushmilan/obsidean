Right-angled trigonometry only works when you have a right angle. For general triangles, you need the sine rule and cosine rule. The sine rule connects sides to opposite angles through the circumdiameter. The cosine rule generalises Pythagoras to any angle. Beyond solving triangles, Heron's formula gives the area from just the three sides.

**The Intuition:** The sine rule says that in any triangle, the ratio of a side to the sine of its opposite angle is constant. Think of it as: "longer sides face bigger angles, and the ratio is exactly $2R$." Every triangle can be inscribed in a circumcircle, and the sine rule connects each side-angle pair to the diameter.

**The Math:** Sine rule: $\frac{a}{\sin A} = \frac{b}{\sin B} = \frac{c}{\sin C} = 2R$

Cosine rule: $a^2 = b^2 + c^2 - 2bc\cos A$; rearranged: $\cos A = \frac{b^2+c^2-a^2}{2bc}$

Area: $\Delta = \frac{1}{2}bc\sin A = \sqrt{s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)} = \frac{abc}{4R} = rs$

Circumradius: $R = \frac{a}{2\sin A} = \frac{abc}{4\Delta}$

Inradius: $r = \frac{\Delta}{s} = (s-a)\tan\frac{A}{2}$

Half-angle: $\sin\frac{A}{2} = \sqrt{\frac{(s-b)(s-c)}{bc}}$, $\tan\frac{A}{2} = \frac{r}{s-a}$

Euler's: $OI^2 = R^2 - 2Rr$; Exradii: $r_1 + r_2 + r_3 = 4R + r$

**What does this mean for Pure Mathematics?** The ambiguous case (SSA) is the trickiest part: given two sides and a non-included angle, you might get zero, one, or two triangles. When solving SSS, compute the largest angle first with the cosine rule (it's the only one uniquely determined by cosine).

### Worked Examples

**Setup:** $a=7, b=8, c=9$. Find all angles and area.

**Solution:**
1. $\cos A = \frac{64+81-49}{144} = \frac{2}{3}$, $A \approx 48.19^\circ$
2. $\cos B = \frac{81+49-64}{126} = \frac{11}{21}$, $B \approx 58.41^\circ$
3. $C \approx 73.4^\circ$; $s = 12$; $\Delta = \sqrt{12 \cdot 5 \cdot 4 \cdot 3} = 12\sqrt{5}$

**Key insight:** Start with the cosine rule for the largest angle (opposite the longest side).

**Setup:** $a=5, b=7, A=30^\circ$. Find all possible triangles.

**Solution:**
1. $b\sin A = 3.5$. Since $3.5 < 5 < 7$, TWO triangles exist.
2. Triangle 1: $B \approx 44.43^\circ$, $C \approx 105.57^\circ$
3. Triangle 2: $B \approx 135.57^\circ$, $C \approx 14.43^\circ$

**Key insight:** Always check $b\sin A$ first to determine how many triangles exist.
