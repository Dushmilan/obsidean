Heights and distances problems use right-triangle trigonometry to find unknown lengths from observations of elevation, depression, and bearings. You observe something from a known position, measure an angle, and use $\tan$ to find the height or distance. The two key concepts are angle of elevation (looking up) and angle of depression (looking down), which are always equal for parallel horizontals.

**The Intuition:** Imagine standing at a known distance from a building and looking up at the top. Your line of sight, the horizontal ground, and the vertical building form a right triangle. The angle you look up is $\theta$, the ground distance is $d$, and the building height is $h$. So $h = d\tan\theta$. The geometry is always a right triangle.

**The Math:** Single observation: $h = d\tan\theta$

Two points, opposite sides: $h = \frac{D\tan\alpha\tan\beta}{\tan\alpha + \tan\beta}$

Two points, same side: $h = \frac{D\tan\alpha\tan\beta}{\tan\alpha - \tan\beta}$

Bearing to standard angle: $\theta_{\text{std}} = 90^\circ - \text{bearing}$

Elevation equals depression (alternate interior angles between parallel horizontals)

**What does this mean for Pure Mathematics?** These are the most direct applications of trigonometry -- measuring things you cannot reach directly. The strategy is always: draw a diagram, identify right triangles, label everything, and solve. Bearings measure angles clockwise from North, different from the standard mathematical convention.

### Worked Examples

**Setup:** From 50 m away, angle of elevation to a tower is $30^\circ$. Find height.

**Solution:**
1. $h = 50\tan 30^\circ = 50/\sqrt{3} = 50\sqrt{3}/3 \approx 28.87$ m

**Key insight:** Direct application of $h = d\tan\theta$.

---

**Setup:** From point A, elevation to cliff top is $30^\circ$. Moving 100 m closer to B, elevation is $45^\circ$. Find height.

**Solution:**
1. Let $h$ = height, $x$ = distance from B to cliff
2. From B: $h = x\tan 45 = x$; From A: $h = (x+100)\tan 30 = (x+100)/\sqrt{3}$
3. $x = (x+100)/\sqrt{3}$, solve: $x = 50(\sqrt{3}+1)$
4. $h = 50(\sqrt{3}+1) \approx 136.6$ m

**Key insight:** Set up two equations and solve simultaneously.
