# 3.6 Heights & Distances

## Definition

Heights and distances problems use right-triangle trigonometry to find unknown lengths and angles from observations. The **angle of elevation** is measured upward from horizontal; the **angle of depression** is measured downward from horizontal. These two angles are equal (alternate interior angles between parallel horizontals).

## Formulae

| Formula | Expression |
|---------|------------|
| Single observation | $h = d\tan\theta$ |
| Two points (opposite sides) | $h = \frac{D\tan\alpha\tan\beta}{\tan\alpha + \tan\beta}$ |
| Two points (same side) | $h = \frac{D\tan\alpha\tan\beta}{\tan\alpha - \tan\beta}$ |
| Different heights | $H - h_1 = d_1\tan\alpha,\; H - h_2 = d_2\tan\beta,\; d_1 + d_2 = D$ |

### Single Right Triangle

| Given | Find | Formula |
|-------|------|---------|
| Distance $d$, elevation $\theta$ | Height $h$ | $h = d\tan\theta$ |
| Height $h$, elevation $\theta$ | Distance $d$ | $d = h\cot\theta$ |
| Height $h$, distance $d$ | Elevation $\theta$ | $\theta = \tan^{-1}(h/d)$ |

## Properties / Key Concepts

### Standard Problem Types
1. **Height from single observation:** $h = d\tan\theta$
2. **Distance between objects:** Two right triangles sharing a vertical side
3. **Height from two observations:** Two equations, solve simultaneously
4. **Angles from different heights:** Adjust for observer height
5. **Bearings:** Angle measured clockwise from North ($0^\circ$ to $360^\circ$); convert to standard position: $\theta_{\text{std}} = 90^\circ - \text{bearing}$

### General Strategy
1. Draw a clear diagram with all given information
2. Identify right triangles (look for horizontal/vertical lines)
3. Label known sides and angles
4. Choose appropriate trig ratio: $\tan$ for height/distance, $\sin$ for hypotenuse/opposite
5. Set up equations and solve
6. Check units and reasonableness of answer

## Worked Examples

### Example 1: Tower Height
From a point 50 m from the foot of a tower, the angle of elevation of the top is $30^\circ$. Find the height.
$$h = 50\tan 30^\circ = 50 \times \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} = \frac{50\sqrt{3}}{3} \approx 28.87 \text{ m}$$

### Example 2: Cliff Height from Two Points
From a point $A$, angle of elevation to top of cliff is $30^\circ$. Moving 100 m closer to $B$, angle is $45^\circ$. Find height.
Let $h$ = height, $x$ = distance from $B$ to cliff.
From $B$: $h = x\tan 45^\circ = x$
From $A$: $h = (x+100)\tan 30^\circ = \frac{x+100}{\sqrt{3}}$
Equate: $x = \frac{x+100}{\sqrt{3}} \Rightarrow x(\sqrt{3}-1) = 100 \Rightarrow x = \frac{100}{\sqrt{3}-1} = 50(\sqrt{3}+1) \approx 136.6 \text{ m}$

### Example 3: Building and Tower
From top of building 20 m high, angles of elevation to top and bottom of tower are $45^\circ$ and $60^\circ$. Find tower height.
Let $H$ = tower height, $d$ = horizontal distance.
Top: $H - 20 = d\tan 45^\circ = d$
Bottom: $20 = d\tan 60^\circ = d\sqrt{3} \Rightarrow d = \frac{20}{\sqrt{3}}$
$H = 20 + d = 20 + \frac{20}{\sqrt{3}} = 20\left(1+\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\right) \approx 31.55 \text{ m}$

### Example 4: Shadow Problem
Shadow of a tower increases by 10 m when sun's altitude changes from $60^\circ$ to $45^\circ$. Find tower height.
Let $h$ = height, $x$ = initial shadow length.
$h = x\tan 60^\circ = x\sqrt{3}$
$h = (x+10)\tan 45^\circ = x+10$
$x\sqrt{3} = x+10 \Rightarrow x(\sqrt{3}-1) = 10 \Rightarrow x = \frac{10}{\sqrt{3}-1} = 5(\sqrt{3}+1)$
$h = 5(\sqrt{3}+1)\sqrt{3} = 15 + 5\sqrt{3} \approx 23.66 \text{ m}$

### Example 5: Bearing Problem
Ship $A$ is 10 km due West of ship $B$. Ship $A$ observes a lighthouse at bearing $030^\circ$. Ship $B$ observes it at bearing $045^\circ$. Find distance of lighthouse from $A$.
Place $A$ at origin, $B$ at $(10, 0)$. Lighthouse $L$ has bearing $30^\circ$ from $A$ (standard angle $60^\circ$) and bearing $45^\circ$ from $B$ (standard angle $45^\circ$).
From $A$: Line at $60^\circ$. From $B$: Line at $45^\circ$.
Solve intersection: $y = \sqrt{3}x$ and $y = x - 10$
$\sqrt{3}x = x - 10 \Rightarrow x(\sqrt{3}-1) = -10 \Rightarrow x = -5(\sqrt{3}+1)$
Distance $AL = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2} = \sqrt{x^2 + 3x^2} = 2|x| = 10(\sqrt{3}+1) \approx 27.32 \text{ km}$

## Common Traps

| ❌ Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| Using $\sin$ instead of $\tan$ for height/distance | Height/distance ratio uses $\tan$ |
| Confusing elevation with depression | Draw diagram; they're equal for parallel horizontals |
| Forgetting to add observer's eye height | Total height = eye height + calculated height |
| Mixing degrees and radians | Stick to one (usually degrees for these problems) |
| Not checking if object is accessible | In depression problems, object must be below observer |
| Incorrect bearing conversion | Bearing $030^\circ \to$ standard angle $60^\circ$ |
