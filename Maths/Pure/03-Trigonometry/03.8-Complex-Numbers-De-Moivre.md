De Moivre's Theorem says that raising a complex number to a power is easy in polar form: just multiply the angle by $n$. This gives $\cos(n\theta)$ and $\sin(n\theta)$ as polynomials in $\cos\theta$ and $\sin\theta$, computes roots of unity (solutions of $z^n=1$), and connects to Euler's formula $e^{i\theta} = \cos\theta + i\sin\theta$.

**The Intuition:** Think of complex multiplication as scaling and rotating. When you raise a complex number to a power, you're multiplying it by itself $n$ times. Each multiplication scales by $r$ and rotates by $\theta$. So $r^n$ scales and $n\theta$ rotates. Multiplication of complex numbers in polar form multiplies moduli and adds arguments.

**The Math:** De Moivre: $(\cos\theta + i\sin\theta)^n = \cos n\theta + i\sin n\theta$

Euler: $e^{i\theta} = \cos\theta + i\sin\theta$

Exponential forms: $\cos\theta = \frac{e^{i\theta}+e^{-i\theta}}{2}$, $\sin\theta = \frac{e^{i\theta}-e^{-i\theta}}{2i}$

$n$-th roots of unity: $\omega_k = e^{2\pi ik/n}$, $k = 0, \ldots, n-1$

Sum of roots of unity: $1 + \omega + \omega^2 + \cdots + \omega^{n-1} = 0$

General $n$-th root: $z_k = r^{1/n}\left[\cos\frac{\theta+2k\pi}{n} + i\sin\frac{\theta+2k\pi}{n}\right]$

$\cos^n\theta$ expansion: $\frac{1}{2^n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\cos((n-2k)\theta)$

**What does this mean for Pure Mathematics?** The roots of unity form a regular $n$-gon on the unit circle and are the foundation of Fourier analysis, number theory, and algebra. The hyperbolic connection: $\cos(i\theta) = \cosh\theta$, $\sin(i\theta) = i\sinh\theta$.

### Worked Examples

**Setup:** Express $\cos 5\theta$ in powers of $\cos\theta$.

**Solution:**
1. $(\cos\theta + i\sin\theta)^5 = \cos 5\theta + i\sin 5\theta$
2. Binomial expansion, real part: $\cos 5\theta = 16\cos^5\theta - 20\cos^3\theta + 5\cos\theta$

**Key insight:** Binomial expansion on the polar form directly gives the multiple-angle polynomial.

---

**Setup:** Evaluate $(1+i)^{20}$.

**Solution:**
1. $1+i = \sqrt{2}\,e^{i\pi/4}$
2. $(1+i)^{20} = 2^{10} \cdot e^{i \cdot 5\pi} = 1024(-1) = -1024$

**Key insight:** Convert to polar form first, then apply De Moivre.

---

**Setup:** Find all roots of $z^6 + 1 = 0$.

**Solution:**
1. $z^6 = -1 = e^{i\pi}$, so $z = e^{i(\pi+2k\pi)/6}$ for $k = 0, \ldots, 5$
2. Six roots forming a regular hexagon on the unit circle

**Key insight:** Write in polar form, then apply the $n$-th root formula with all values of $k$.
