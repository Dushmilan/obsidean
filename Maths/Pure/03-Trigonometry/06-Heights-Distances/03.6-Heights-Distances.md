
## Definition

Heights-and-distances problems use right-triangle trigonometry to find unknown lengths from observations of **elevation**, **depression**, and **bearings**:

- **Angle of elevation** — the angle looking *up* from the horizontal to a point.
- **Angle of depression** — the angle looking *down* from the horizontal.
- **Bearing** — angle measured **clockwise from North**.

Elevation equals the corresponding depression angle (alternate interior angles between parallel horizontals).

## The Intuition

Standing a known distance $d$ from a building and looking up at the top: your line of sight, the horizontal ground, and the vertical building form a **right triangle**. Looking up at angle $\theta$: $h = d\tan\theta$. Every problem is this same right triangle, possibly repeated from two viewpoints.

## The Toolkit

| Situation | Formula |
|-----------|---------|
| Single observation | $h = d\tan\theta$ |
| Two points, opposite sides | $h = \dfrac{D\tan\alpha\tan\beta}{\tan\alpha + \tan\beta}$ |
| Two points, same side | $h = \dfrac{D\tan\alpha\tan\beta}{\tan\alpha - \tan\beta}$ |
| Bearing → standard angle | $\theta_{\text{std}} = 90^\circ - \text{bearing}$ |
| Elevation = depression | equal (parallel horizontals) |

## Derivation

Single observation: $\tan\theta = \frac{h}{d}$ by definition of tangent in the right triangle. Two-point formulas: set up $h = x\tan\beta$ and $h = (x \pm D)\tan\alpha$ from each viewpoint, eliminate $x$ ($-$ for opposite sides, $+$ for same side), and solve for $h$. [Full derivations: 03.6-Heights-Distances-Proof]

## Method

1. **Draw the diagram** — identify every right triangle.
2. **Label** all known distances and angles; introduce $x$ for unknowns on shared horizontal baselines.
3. **Write one $\tan$ equation per triangle**, in terms of the same height $h$.
4. **Solve simultaneously** (eliminate $x$).
5. Convert bearings to standard angles if needed.

## Worked Examples

**Setup:** From 50 m away, the angle of elevation to a tower is $30^\circ$. Find the height.

**Solution:** $h = 50\tan 30^\circ = \frac{50}{\sqrt3} = \frac{50\sqrt3}{3} \approx 28.87$ m.

**Key insight:** Direct application of $h = d\tan\theta$.

---

**Setup:** From A, elevation to a cliff top is $30^\circ$. Moving 100 m closer to B, elevation is $45^\circ$. Find the height.

**Solution:** Let $h$ = height, $x$ = distance from B. From B: $h = x\tan45 = x$. From A: $h = (x+100)\tan30 = \frac{x+100}{\sqrt3}$. So $x = \frac{x+100}{\sqrt3} \Rightarrow x = 50(\sqrt3+1)$, and $h = 50(\sqrt3+1) \approx 136.6$ m.

**Key insight:** Set up two equations in $h$ and $x$, then solve simultaneously.

## Common Traps

- Mixing elevation and depression — they're equal only across *parallel* horizontals
- Bearings are clockwise from North; standard math angles are counterclockwise from the $x$-axis
- Forgetting which formula is for same-side vs opposite-side observations
- Not drawing the diagram first — guessing the right triangle wrong
- Using degrees in one equation and radians in another
