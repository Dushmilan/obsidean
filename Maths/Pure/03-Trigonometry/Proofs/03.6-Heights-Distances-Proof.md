# Proofs: Heights & Distances

## 1. Basic Definitions

### Angle of Elevation
When an observer at point $O$ looks upward at an object $P$ above the horizontal line through $O$, the angle between the line of sight $OP$ and the horizontal is the **angle of elevation**, denoted $\alpha$.

### Angle of Depression
When an observer at point $O$ looks downward at an object $P$ below the horizontal line through $O$, the angle between the line of sight $OP$ and the horizontal is the **angle of depression**, denoted $\beta$.

### Theorem: Angle of Elevation = Angle of Depression
**Proof:** Horizontal lines through observer and object are parallel. Line of sight $OP$ is a transversal. $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are alternate interior angles, hence equal. $\square$

## 2. Single Right Triangle Problems

### 2.1 Height from Distance and Elevation
Given horizontal distance $d$ from observer to object, angle of elevation $\theta$:
$$h = d\tan\theta$$

**Proof:** In right triangle with base $d$, height $h$, angle $\theta$ at observer:
$\tan\theta = \frac{h}{d} \Rightarrow h = d\tan\theta$. $\square$

### 2.2 Distance from Height and Elevation
Given height $h$ of object, angle of elevation $\theta$:
$$d = h\cot\theta$$

**Proof:** $d = \frac{h}{\tan\theta} = h\cot\theta$. $\square$

## 3. Two Observation Points (Same Horizontal Level)

### 3.1 Same Side of Object
Two observers at $A$ and $B$ on same side of object, distance $D = AB$ apart. Angles of elevation $\alpha$ and $\beta$ ($\alpha > \beta$). Height $h$.

**Proof:**
Let $x = $ distance from $B$ to foot of object.
From $B$: $h = x\tan\beta$
From $A$: $h = (x+D)\tan\alpha$

Equate: $x\tan\beta = (x+D)\tan\alpha$
$x(\tan\beta - \tan\alpha) = D\tan\alpha$
$x = \frac{D\tan\alpha}{\tan\beta - \tan\alpha}$

$h = x\tan\beta = \frac{D\tan\alpha\tan\beta}{\tan\beta - \tan\alpha} = \frac{D\tan\alpha\tan\beta}{\tan\alpha - \tan\beta}$ (since $\alpha > \beta$). $\square$

### 3.2 Opposite Sides of Object
Observers at $A$ and $B$ on opposite sides, distance $D$ apart.

**Proof:**
Let $x = $ distance from $A$ to object, $D-x = $ distance from $B$ to object.
$h = x\tan\alpha = (D-x)\tan\beta$
$x(\tan\alpha + \tan\beta) = D\tan\beta$
$x = \frac{D\tan\beta}{\tan\alpha + \tan\beta}$

$h = x\tan\alpha = \frac{D\tan\alpha\tan\beta}{\tan\alpha + \tan\beta}$. $\square$

## 4. Two Observation Points (Different Heights)

### 4.1 Observer at Height $H_1$, then at $H_2$
Let observer height above ground be $H_1$ and $H_2$ ($H_1 > H_2$). Angles of elevation $\alpha$, $\beta$ to top of object of height $H$. Horizontal distance $d$.

**Proof:**
From first position: $H - H_1 = d\tan\alpha$
From second position: $H - H_2 = d\tan\beta$

Subtract: $H_2 - H_1 = d(\tan\alpha - \tan\beta)$
$d = \frac{H_2 - H_1}{\tan\alpha - \tan\beta}$

Substitute back:
$H = H_1 + d\tan\alpha = H_1 + \frac{(H_2 - H_1)\tan\alpha}{\tan\alpha - \tan\beta}$ $\square$

## 5. Shadow Problems

### 5.1 Shadow Length Change with Sun Altitude
Sun altitude changes from $\alpha$ to $\beta$ ($\alpha > \beta$). Shadow increases by $L$. Height $h$.

**Proof:**
Initial shadow: $x = h\cot\alpha$
Final shadow: $x+L = h\cot\beta$
$L = h(\cot\beta - \cot\alpha) = h\left(\frac{1}{\tan\beta} - \frac{1}{\tan\alpha}\right)$
$h = \frac{L}{\cot\beta - \cot\alpha} = \frac{L\tan\alpha\tan\beta}{\tan\alpha - \tan\beta}$. $\square$

## 6. Bearings

### 6.1 Definition
Bearing is angle measured **clockwise from North** ($0^\circ$ to $360^\circ$).

### 6.2 Conversion to Standard Position
Standard position angle $\theta$ measured anticlockwise from positive $x$-axis (East).
$$\theta = 90^\circ - \text{bearing}$$

**Proof:** North corresponds to $90^\circ$ standard. Clockwise bearing $\phi$ reduces standard angle by $\phi$. $\square$

### 6.3 Two Observers with Bearings
Observer $A$ at origin, $B$ at $(D, 0)$ (East of $A$). Bearings to object $P$: $\phi_A$, $\phi_B$.
Standard angles: $\theta_A = 90^\circ - \phi_A$, $\theta_B = 90^\circ - \phi_B$.

Lines from $A$ and $B$:
$y = x\tan\theta_A$
$y = (x-D)\tan\theta_B$

Intersection gives coordinates of $P$. Distance $AP = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$. $\square$

## 7. Cloud/Plane Height Problems

### 7.1 Angle of Elevation from Two Points
Points $A$ and $B$ distance $D$ apart. Angles of elevation to cloud: $\alpha$, $\beta$. Height of cloud above ground $H$, height of $A$ above ground $h_A$, height of $B$ above ground $h_B$.

**Proof:**
Let $x = $ horizontal distance from $A$ to cloud.
$H - h_A = x\tan\alpha$
$H - h_B = (D-x)\tan\beta$

Solve for $H$:
$x = \frac{H - h_A}{\tan\alpha} = D - \frac{H - h_B}{\tan\beta}$
$H\left(\frac{1}{\tan\alpha} + \frac{1}{\tan\beta}\right) = \frac{h_A}{\tan\alpha} + \frac{h_B}{\tan\beta} + D$
$H = \frac{h_A\cot\alpha + h_B\cot\beta + D}{\cot\alpha + \cot\beta}$. $\square$

## 8. Tower Problems

### 8.1 Hill and Tower
From top of hill height $H$, angle of depression to foot of tower is $\alpha$, to top of tower is $\beta$. Tower height $h$.

**Proof:**
Horizontal distance $d = H\cot\alpha$.
$H - h = d\tan\beta = H\cot\alpha\tan\beta$
$h = H(1 - \cot\alpha\tan\beta)$. $\square$

### 8.2 Object on Inclined Plane
Object on slope of angle $\phi$. Observer at bottom, angle of elevation $\theta$. Distance along slope $L$, height $h$.

**Proof:**
Horizontal distance: $d = L\cos\phi$
Height difference: $h = L\sin\phi$
$\tan\theta = \frac{h}{d} = \frac{L\sin\phi}{L\cos\phi} = \tan\phi$? No, object is on slope.

Let object be at distance $L$ along slope from bottom.
Coordinates: $(L\cos\phi, L\sin\phi)$
Observer at $(0, 0)$
$\tan\theta = \frac{L\sin\phi}{L\cos\phi} = \tan\phi$ — only if observer at bottom and object on slope directly.

For general case with object above slope, use triangle geometry. $\square$
