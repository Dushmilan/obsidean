Compound angle formulas express trigonometric functions of $A \pm B$ in terms of functions of $A$ and $B$ individually. From these base formulas, everything cascades: set $A = B$ for double-angle, set $B = A/2$ for half-angle, add or subtract for sum-to-product and product-to-sum. The tangent half-angle substitution $t = \tan(\theta/2)$ converts any trig expression into rational algebra.

**The Intuition:** Think of rotating a point on the unit circle by angle $A$, then by angle $B$. The total rotation is $A+B$. The compound angle formula tells you the coordinates after the combined rotation in terms of the coordinates after each individual rotation. Rotation is a linear transformation, and composing two rotations multiplies their rotation matrices.

**The Math:** $\sin(A \pm B) = \sin A\cos B \pm \cos A\sin B$

$\cos(A \pm B) = \cos A\cos B \mp \sin A\sin B$

$\tan(A \pm B) = \frac{\tan A \pm \tan B}{1 \mp \tan A\tan B}$

Double angle: $\sin 2A = 2\sin A\cos A$; $\cos 2A = \cos^2 A - \sin^2 A = 2\cos^2 A - 1 = 1 - 2\sin^2 A$

Triple angle: $\sin 3A = 3\sin A - 4\sin^3 A$; $\cos 3A = 4\cos^3 A - 3\cos A$

Half angle: $\tan(A/2) = \frac{1-\cos A}{\sin A} = \frac{\sin A}{1+\cos A}$

Tangent substitution: $t = \tan(\theta/2)$ gives $\sin\theta = \frac{2t}{1+t^2}$, $\cos\theta = \frac{1-t^2}{1+t^2}$

Sum-to-product: $\sin A + \sin B = 2\sin\frac{A+B}{2}\cos\frac{A-B}{2}$

Amplitude-phase: $a\sin\theta + b\cos\theta = R\sin(\theta+\alpha)$

**What does this mean for Pure Mathematics?** The cosine difference formula is the one to memorize first -- from it you can derive all other compound angle formulas. The double angle for cosine has three forms; choose based on what you know or want to find.

### Worked Examples

**Setup:** Find the exact value of $\sin 15^\circ$.

**Solution:**
1. $15^\circ = 45^\circ - 30^\circ$
2. $\sin(45-30) = \sin 45\cos 30 - \cos 45\sin 30$
3. $= \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} - \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\cdot\frac{1}{2} = \frac{\sqrt{6}-\sqrt{2}}{4}$

**Key insight:** Any angle that is a difference of two standard angles can be found exactly.

---

**Setup:** Solve $\sin 2\theta = \cos\theta$ for $\theta \in [0, 2\pi]$.

**Solution:**
1. $2\sin\theta\cos\theta = \cos\theta$
2. $\cos\theta(2\sin\theta - 1) = 0$
3. $\theta = \pi/6, \pi/2, 5\pi/6, 3\pi/2$

**Key insight:** Factorise first. Never divide by a trig function -- you might lose solutions.
