# Proofs: Parabola

## 1. Standard Equation from Focus-Directrix Definition

**Theorem:** Locus of point $P(x,y)$ equidistant from focus $F(a,0)$ and directrix $x = -a$ is $y^2 = 4ax$.

**Proof:**
By definition: $PF = \text{distance to directrix}$
$$PF = \sqrt{(x-a)^2 + y^2}$$
Distance to directrix $x = -a$: $|x + a|$

Square both sides:
$$(x-a)^2 + y^2 = (x+a)^2$$
$$x^2 - 2ax + a^2 + y^2 = x^2 + 2ax + a^2$$
$$y^2 = 4ax \quad \square$$

**Other orientations:** Similar derivation with appropriate focus/directrix.

---

## 2. Parametric Equations

**Theorem:** For $y^2 = 4ax$, parametric form is $x = at^2, y = 2at$.

**Proof:**
Let $t = \frac{y}{2a}$ (gradient of tangent at vertex).
Then $y = 2at$.
Substitute in $y^2 = 4ax$:
$$(2at)^2 = 4ax \Rightarrow 4a^2t^2 = 4ax \Rightarrow x = at^2 \quad \square$$

---

## 3. Tangent at Point $(x_1, y_1)$

**Theorem:** Tangent to $y^2 = 4ax$ at $(x_1, y_1)$ is $yy_1 = 2a(x + x_1)$.

**Proof 1 (Calculus):**
Implicit differentiation: $2y\frac{dy}{dx} = 4a \Rightarrow \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{2a}{y}$.
At $(x_1, y_1)$: $m = \frac{2a}{y_1}$.
Equation: $y - y_1 = \frac{2a}{y_1}(x - x_1)$.
Multiply by $y_1$: $yy_1 - y_1^2 = 2a(x - x_1)$.
Since $y_1^2 = 4ax_1$: $yy_1 = 2ax + 2ax_1 = 2a(x + x_1) \quad \square$

**Proof 2 (Algebraic - Limit of Chord):**
Chord joining $(x_1, y_1)$ and $(x_2, y_2)$:
$$\frac{y - y_1}{y_2 - y_1} = \frac{x - x_1}{x_2 - x_1}$$
Since $y_2^2 - y_1^2 = 4a(x_2 - x_1)$:
$$\frac{x_2 - x_1}{y_2 - y_1} = \frac{y_2 + y_1}{4a}$$
As $(x_2, y_2) \to (x_1, y_1)$:
$$\frac{y - y_1}{1} = \frac{2y_1}{4a}(x - x_1) \Rightarrow y - y_1 = \frac{y_1}{2a}(x - x_1)$$
Wait — this gives wrong sign. Let's redo:

$$\frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1} = \frac{4a}{y_2 + y_1} \to \frac{4a}{2y_1} = \frac{2a}{y_1}$$
So tangent slope $= \frac{2a}{y_1}$. Equation:
$$y - y_1 = \frac{2a}{y_1}(x - x_1) \Rightarrow yy_1 = 2a(x + x_1) \quad \square$$

---

## 4. Tangent at Parameter $t$

**Theorem:** Tangent at $P(at^2, 2at)$ is $ty = x + at^2$.

**Proof:**
Substitute $(x_1, y_1) = (at^2, 2at)$ in $yy_1 = 2a(x + x_1)$:
$$y(2at) = 2a(x + at^2)$$
$$ty = x + at^2 \quad \square$$

---

## 5. Tangent with Given Slope $m$

**Theorem:** Line $y = mx + c$ is tangent to $y^2 = 4ax$ iff $c = \frac{a}{m}$ ($m \neq 0$).
Tangent: $y = mx + \frac{a}{m}$.

**Proof:**
Substitute $y = mx + c$ in $y^2 = 4ax$:
$$(mx + c)^2 = 4ax$$
$$m^2x^2 + 2(mc - 2a)x + c^2 = 0$$
For tangency, discriminant $= 0$:
$$(mc - 2a)^2 - m^2c^2 = 0$$
$$m^2c^2 - 4amc + 4a^2 - m^2c^2 = 0$$
$$4a^2 = 4amc \Rightarrow c = \frac{a}{m} \quad \square$$

---

## 6. Normal at Point $(x_1, y_1)$

**Theorem:** Normal to $y^2 = 4ax$ at $(x_1, y_1)$ is $y - y_1 = -\frac{y_1}{2a}(x - x_1)$.

**Proof:**
Tangent gradient $= \frac{2a}{y_1}$.
Normal gradient $= -\frac{y_1}{2a}$.
Equation: $y - y_1 = -\frac{y_1}{2a}(x - x_1) \quad \square$

---

## 7. Normal at Parameter $t$

**Theorem:** Normal at $P(at^2, 2at)$ is $y = -tx + 2at + at^3$.

**Proof:**
Substitute $(x_1, y_1) = (at^2, 2at)$ in normal equation:
$$y - 2at = -\frac{2at}{2a}(x - at^2)$$
$$y - 2at = -t(x - at^2)$$
$$y = -tx + at^3 + 2at \quad \square$$

---

## 8. Normal with Given Slope $m$

**Theorem:** Normal to $y^2 = 4ax$ with slope $m$ is $y = mx - 2am - am^3$.

**Proof:**
Normal at $t$ has gradient $-t$. Set $-t = m \Rightarrow t = -m$.
Substitute in $y = -tx + 2at + at^3$:
$$y = mx + 2a(-m) + a(-m)^3$$
$$y = mx - 2am - am^3 \quad \square$$

---

## 9. Chord Joining $t_1$ and $t_2$

**Theorem:** Chord joining $P_1(at_1^2, 2at_1)$ and $P_2(at_2^2, 2at_2)$ is $(t_1 + t_2)y = 2x + 2at_1t_2$.

**Proof:**
Gradient $m = \frac{2at_2 - 2at_1}{at_2^2 - at_1^2} = \frac{2a(t_2 - t_1)}{a(t_2 - t_1)(t_2 + t_1)} = \frac{2}{t_1 + t_2}$.
Using point $P_1$:
$$y - 2at_1 = \frac{2}{t_1 + t_2}(x - at_1^2)$$
Multiply by $(t_1 + t_2)$:
$$(t_1 + t_2)(y - 2at_1) = 2x - 2at_1^2$$
$$(t_1 + t_2)y - 2at_1(t_1 + t_2) = 2x - 2at_1^2$$
$$(t_1 + t_2)y = 2x + 2at_1^2 + 2at_1t_2 - 2at_1^2$$
$$(t_1 + t_2)y = 2x + 2at_1t_2 \quad \square$$

---

## 10. Focal Chord Property

**Theorem:** If chord through focus $(a, 0)$ joins $t_1$ and $t_2$, then $t_1t_2 = -1$.

**Proof:**
Focus $(a, 0)$ lies on chord: $(t_1 + t_2)(0) = 2(a) + 2at_1t_2$
$0 = 2a(1 + t_1t_2) \Rightarrow t_1t_2 = -1 \quad \square$

---

## 11. Tangents at Ends of Focal Chord

**Theorem:** Tangents at ends of focal chord intersect at right angles on the directrix.

**Proof:**
Ends: $t_1, t_2$ with $t_1t_2 = -1$.
Tangents: $t_1y = x + at_1^2$ and $t_2y = x + at_2^2$.
Intersection: subtract equations:
$$y(t_1 - t_2) = a(t_1^2 - t_2^2) = a(t_1 - t_2)(t_1 + t_2)$$
$$y = a(t_1 + t_2)$$
Substitute in first: $t_1a(t_1 + t_2) = x + at_1^2$
$$x = at_1t_2 = a(-1) = -a$$
Intersection point: $(-a, a(t_1 + t_2))$.
$x = -a$ is the directrix.

Angle between tangents: slopes $m_1 = 1/t_1$, $m_2 = 1/t_2$.
$m_1m_2 = \frac{1}{t_1t_2} = -1 \Rightarrow$ perpendicular. $\quad \square$

---

## 12. Subtangent and Subnormal

**Theorem:** At $P(at^2, 2at)$ on $y^2 = 4ax$:
- Subtangent $= 2at^2 = 2x_1$ (projection of tangent on x-axis)
- Subnormal $= 2a$ (constant!)

**Proof:**
Tangent: $ty = x + at^2$. Meets x-axis at $y=0$: $x = -at^2$.
Distance from $P$ to x-intercept: $|at^2 - (-at^2)| = 2at^2 = 2x_1$.

Normal: $y = -tx + 2at + at^3$. Meets x-axis at $y=0$:
$0 = -tx + 2at + at^3 \Rightarrow x = 2a + at^2$.
Distance from $P$ to x-intercept: $|at^2 - (2a + at^2)| = 2a$. $\quad \square$

---

## 13. Polar of Point $(x_1, y_1)$

**Theorem:** Polar of $P(x_1, y_1)$ w.r.t $y^2 = 4ax$ is $yy_1 = 2a(x + x_1)$.

**Proof:**
Let line through $P$ with slope $m$ cut parabola at $Q, R$.
Tangents at $Q, R$ intersect at $M$. Locus of $M$ is polar.

Line: $y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$.
Intersect with parabola: $(m(x-x_1) + y_1)^2 = 4ax$.
Quadratic in $x$: roots $x_2, x_3$ for $Q, R$.

Tangents at $Q, R$:
$$yy_2 = 2a(x + x_2), \quad yy_3 = 2a(x + x_3)$$
Intersect $M$: subtract $\Rightarrow y(y_2 - y_3) = 2a(x_2 - x_3) \Rightarrow \frac{y}{2a} = \frac{x_2 - x_3}{y_2 - y_3} = \frac{1}{m}$ (since slope $m = \frac{y_2 - y_3}{x_2 - x_3}$).
So $y = \frac{2a}{m}$.

Substitute back: $\frac{2a}{m}y_2 = 2a(x + x_2) \Rightarrow \frac{y_2}{m} = x + x_2 \Rightarrow x = \frac{y_2}{m} - x_2$.
But $y_2 = m(x_2 - x_1) + y_1$, so $\frac{y_2}{m} = x_2 - x_1 + \frac{y_1}{m}$.
Thus $x = -x_1 + \frac{y_1}{m}$.

Eliminate $m$: from $y = \frac{2a}{m}$, $m = \frac{2a}{y}$.
$x = -x_1 + \frac{y_1 y}{2a} \Rightarrow yy_1 = 2a(x + x_1) \quad \square$

---

## 14. Normals from Point $(x_1, y_1)$

**Theorem:** Normals from $P(x_1, y_1)$ to $y^2 = 4ax$ correspond to roots of cubic:
$$at^3 + (2a - x_1)t - y_1 = 0$$

**Proof:**
Normal at $t$: $y = -tx + 2at + at^3$.
Passes through $(x_1, y_1)$:
$$y_1 = -tx_1 + 2at + at^3$$
$$at^3 + (2a - x_1)t - y_1 = 0 \quad \square$$

**Roots $t_1, t_2, t_3$:**
- Sum: $t_1 + t_2 + t_3 = 0$ (no $t^2$ term)
- Sum of products: $t_1t_2 + t_2t_3 + t_3t_1 = \frac{2a - x_1}{a}$
- Product: $t_1t_2t_3 = \frac{y_1}{a}$

---

## 15. Properties of Conormal Points

If $t_1, t_2, t_3$ are roots (feet of normals from $P$):

1. **Sum of parameters:** $t_1 + t_2 + t_3 = 0$
2. **Centroid of feet:** $\left(\frac{a}{3}(t_1^2+t_2^2+t_3^2), 0\right)$ — lies on x-axis.
3. **Circumcircle** of feet passes through vertex $(0,0)$.
4. **If two normals coincide** (repeated root), the third is the axis ($t=0$).

**Proof of (2):** Feet: $(at_i^2, 2at_i)$.
Centroid x: $\frac{a}{3}\sum t_i^2 = \frac{a}{3}((\sum t_i)^2 - 2\sum t_it_j) = -\frac{2a}{3}\cdot\frac{2a-x_1}{a} = \frac{2(x_1-2a)}{3}$.
Centroid y: $\frac{2a}{3}\sum t_i = 0$. $\square$

---

## 16. Intersection of Line and Parabola

Line $y = mx + c$ with $y^2 = 4ax$:
$$(mx+c)^2 = 4ax \Rightarrow m^2x^2 + 2(mc-2a)x + c^2 = 0$$

Discriminant $\Delta = 4[(mc-2a)^2 - m^2c^2] = 4[4a^2 - 4amc] = 16a(a - mc)$.

- $\Delta > 0 \Leftrightarrow mc < a$: two intersections
- $\Delta = 0 \Leftrightarrow mc = a \Leftrightarrow c = a/m$: tangent
- $\Delta < 0 \Leftrightarrow mc > a$: no intersection

---

## 17. Diameter

**Theorem:** Diameter of $y^2 = 4ax$ for chords of slope $m$ is $y = \frac{2a}{m}$ (line parallel to axis).

**Proof:**
Chord of slope $m$: $y = mx + c$. Midpoint of intersection:
Quadratic: $m^2x^2 + 2(mc-2a)x + c^2 = 0$.
Sum of roots $= x_1 + x_2 = \frac{2(2a-mc)}{m^2}$.
Midpoint x: $\frac{x_1+x_2}{2} = \frac{2a-mc}{m^2}$.
Midpoint y: $y_m = m\left(\frac{2a-mc}{m^2}\right) + c = \frac{2a}{m}$.
Locus: $y = \frac{2a}{m}$ (independent of $c$). $\square$
