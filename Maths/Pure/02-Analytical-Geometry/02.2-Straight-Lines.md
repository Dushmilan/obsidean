# 2.2 Straight Lines

## Definition

A **straight line** is the set of all points $(x, y)$ satisfying a linear equation $Ax + By + C = 0$.

### Standard Forms

| Form | Equation | Parameters |
|------|----------|------------|
| **Slope-intercept** | $y = mx + c$ | $m$ = gradient, $c$ = y-intercept |
| **Point-slope** | $y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$ | Through $(x_1,y_1)$, gradient $m$ |
| **Two-point** | $\frac{y-y_1}{y_2-y_1} = \frac{x-x_1}{x_2-x_1}$ | Through $(x_1,y_1), (x_2,y_2)$ |
| **Intercept** | $\frac{x}{a} + \frac{y}{b} = 1$ | x-intercept $a$, y-intercept $b$ |
| **Normal (perpendicular)** | $x\cos\alpha + y\sin\alpha = p$ | $p$ = perpendicular distance from origin, $\alpha$ = angle of perpendicular |

### Parametric Form
Line through $(x_1, y_1)$ with direction ratios $(l, m)$:
$$\frac{x-x_1}{l} = \frac{y-y_1}{m} = t \quad \Rightarrow \quad x = x_1 + lt,\; y = y_1 + mt$$

### Vector Form
$$\vec{r} = \vec{a} + \lambda \vec{b}$$
where $\vec{a}$ = position vector of known point, $\vec{b}$ = direction vector.

## Formulae

| Formula | Expression |
|---------|------------|
| **Gradient** | $m = \frac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1} = \tan\theta$ |
| **From general form** | $m = -\frac{A}{B}$ (if $B \neq 0$) |
| **Angle between lines** | $\tan\theta = \left|\frac{m_1-m_2}{1+m_1m_2}\right|$ |
| **Point-to-line distance** | $d = \frac{|Ax_1+By_1+C|}{\sqrt{A^2+B^2}}$ |
| **Parallel lines distance** | $d = \frac{|C_1-C_2|}{\sqrt{A^2+B^2}}$ |
| **Parallel condition** | $m_1 = m_2$ or $\frac{A_1}{A_2}=\frac{B_1}{B_2}$ |
| **Perpendicular condition** | $m_1m_2 = -1$ or $A_1A_2+B_1B_2=0$ |
| **Angle bisectors** | $\frac{A_1x+B_1y+C_1}{\sqrt{A_1^2+B_1^2}} = \pm \frac{A_2x+B_2y+C_2}{\sqrt{A_2^2+B_2^2}}$ |
| **Family through intersection** | $L_1 + \lambda L_2 = 0$ |
| **Concurrency condition** | $\det\begin{pmatrix}A_1&B_1&C_1\\A_2&B_2&C_2\\A_3&B_3&C_3\end{pmatrix}=0$ |

### Gradient (Slope)

For line through $(x_1,y_1)$ and $(x_2,y_2)$:
$$m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1} = \tan\theta$$
where $\theta$ = angle with positive x-axis ($0 \le \theta < \pi$).

**Special cases:**
- Horizontal line: $m = 0$ ($\theta = 0$)
- Vertical line: $m$ undefined ($\theta = \frac{\pi}{2}$)

### Angle Between Two Lines

Lines with gradients $m_1, m_2$:
$$\tan\theta = \left|\frac{m_1 - m_2}{1 + m_1m_2}\right|$$

**From general form $A_1x+B_1y+C_1=0$ and $A_2x+B_2y+C_2=0$:**
- Parallel: $\frac{A_1}{A_2} = \frac{B_1}{B_2} \neq \frac{C_1}{C_2}$
- Perpendicular: $A_1A_2 + B_1B_2 = 0$

### Distance from Point to Line

**Perpendicular distance from $P(x_1, y_1)$ to line $Ax+By+C=0$:**
$$d = \frac{|Ax_1 + By_1 + C|}{\sqrt{A^2 + B^2}}$$

**Distance between parallel lines $Ax+By+C_1=0$ and $Ax+By+C_2=0$:**
$$d = \frac{|C_1 - C_2|}{\sqrt{A^2 + B^2}}$$

## Properties / Key Concepts

### Position of Point Relative to Line

For point $P(x_1,y_1)$ and line $L: Ax+By+C=0$:
- $L(x_1,y_1) > 0$ → $P$ on one side
- $L(x_1,y_1) < 0$ → $P$ on opposite side
- $L(x_1,y_1) = 0$ → $P$ lies on line

**Same side condition:** $L(x_1,y_1) \cdot L(x_2,y_2) > 0$

### Angle Bisectors of Two Lines

Lines: $L_1: A_1x+B_1y+C_1=0$, $L_2: A_2x+B_2y+C_2=0$

**Equations of angle bisectors:**
$$\frac{A_1x+B_1y+C_1}{\sqrt{A_1^2+B_1^2}} = \pm \frac{A_2x+B_2y+C_2}{\sqrt{A_2^2+B_2^2}}$$

- **$+$ sign:** Bisector of angle containing origin (if $C_1, C_2$ same sign)
- **$-$ sign:** Bisector of other angle

**Acute/Obtuse bisector:**
- If $A_1A_2 + B_1B_2 > 0$, $+$ gives obtuse angle bisector
- If $A_1A_2 + B_1B_2 < 0$, $+$ gives acute angle bisector

### Family of Lines

**Through Intersection of Two Lines:**
Line through intersection of $L_1=0$ and $L_2=0$:
$$L_1 + \lambda L_2 = 0 \quad (\lambda \in \mathbb{R})$$

**Parallel to $Ax+By+C=0$:**
$$Ax+By+k=0 \quad (k \in \mathbb{R})$$

**Perpendicular to $Ax+By+C=0$:**
$$Bx-Ay+k=0 \quad (k \in \mathbb{R})$$

### Concurrency of Three Lines

Lines $L_1=0, L_2=0, L_3=0$ are **concurrent** iff:
$$\begin{vmatrix}
A_1 & B_1 & C_1 \\
A_2 & B_2 & C_2 \\
A_3 & B_3 & C_3
\end{vmatrix} = 0$$

**Point of concurrency:** Solve any two equations.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1: Line Through Point, Given Gradient
Through $(2, -3)$, gradient $4$:
$$y + 3 = 4(x - 2) \Rightarrow 4x - y - 11 = 0$$

### Example 2: Angle Between Lines
$3x - 4y + 5 = 0$ and $4x + 3y - 2 = 0$
$m_1 = \frac{3}{4}, m_2 = -\frac{4}{3} \Rightarrow m_1m_2 = -1 \Rightarrow$ **Perpendicular**

### Example 3: Distance from Point to Line
$P(1, -2)$ to $3x - 4y + 12 = 0$:
$$d = \frac{|3(1) - 4(-2) + 12|}{\sqrt{9+16}} = \frac{|3+8+12|}{5} = \frac{23}{5} = 4.6$$

## Common Traps

| Pitfall | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| ❌ Forgetting absolute value in distance | Distance $\ge 0$ always |
| ❌ Confusing acute/obtuse bisector | Check sign of $A_1A_2+B_1B_2$ |
| ❌ Using wrong sign in family of lines | $L_1 + \lambda L_2 = 0$ (not $L_1 - \lambda L_2$) |
| ❌ Vertical line gradient | Gradient undefined; use $x = \text{constant}$ |
