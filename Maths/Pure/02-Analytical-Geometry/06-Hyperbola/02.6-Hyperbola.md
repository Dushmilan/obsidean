
## Definition

A hyperbola is the locus of points where the **difference of distances from two foci is constant**, or equivalently the focus-directrix ratio satisfies $e > 1$. Standard form:

$$\frac{x^2}{a^2} - \frac{y^2}{b^2} = 1$$

Two separate branches approach (but never reach) two straight lines — the **asymptotes** $y = \pm\frac{b}{a}x$. Special case: the **rectangular hyperbola** $xy = c^2$, with $e = \sqrt{2}$ and especially clean formulae.

## The Intuition

Two magnets pulling in opposite directions — the hyperbola is the boundary between which magnet "wins." The asymptotes are the directions where the pulls balance. Two mirror-image curves hugging the asymptotes: curved near the centre, almost straight far away.

## The Toolkit

For $\dfrac{x^2}{a^2} - \dfrac{y^2}{b^2} = 1$:

| Result | Formula |
|--------|---------|
| Eccentricity | $e = \sqrt{1 + b^2/a^2} > 1$ |
| Asymptotes | $y = \pm\dfrac{b}{a}x$ |
| Foci | $(\pm ae, 0)$ |
| Directrices | $x = \pm \dfrac{a}{e}$ |
| Latus rectum | $\dfrac{2b^2}{a}$ |
| Parametric | $(a\sec\theta,\ b\tan\theta)$ |
| Tangent at $(x_1,y_1)$ | $\dfrac{xx_1}{a^2} - \dfrac{yy_1}{b^2} = 1$ |
| Tangent, slope $m$ | $y = mx \pm \sqrt{a^2m^2 - b^2}$ |
| Condition of tangency | $c^2 = a^2m^2 - b^2$ **(minus!)** |
| Director circle | $x^2 + y^2 = a^2 - b^2$ (only if $a > b$) |
| Rectangular hyperbola $xy=c^2$ | parametric $(ct, c/t)$, tangent $x + t^2y = 2ct$ |

## Derivation

Difference-of-distances definition: $\sqrt{(x-c)^2+y^2} - \sqrt{(x+c)^2+y^2} = \pm 2a$ — squaring twice with $c^2 = a^2 + b^2$ gives $\frac{x^2}{a^2} - \frac{y^2}{b^2} = 1$. The asymptotes come from setting the equation to zero: $\frac{x^2}{a^2} = \frac{y^2}{b^2}$. Tangent formula mirrors the ellipse but with the minus sign. [Full derivations: 02.6-Hyperbola-Proof]

## Method

1. **Identify $a, b$** from $\frac{x^2}{a^2} - \frac{y^2}{b^2} = 1$ — note the **minus** distinguishes it from the ellipse.
2. **Elements:** $e = \sqrt{1+b^2/a^2}$, foci $(\pm ae, 0)$, asymptotes $y = \pm\frac{b}{a}x$.
3. **Tangents of slope $m$:** $c^2 = a^2m^2 - b^2$ — real tangents only when $a^2m^2 > b^2$.
4. **Rectangular hyperbola:** use $xy = c^2$ parametric $(ct, c/t)$ for cleaner algebra.

## Worked Examples

**Setup:** $\frac{x^2}{9} - \frac{y^2}{16} = 1$. Find all elements.

**Solution:** $a = 3$, $b = 4$. $e = \sqrt{1 + 16/9} = 5/3$. Foci $(\pm 5, 0)$; directrices $x = \pm 1.8$; asymptotes $y = \pm\frac43 x$.

**Key insight:** Asymptote slopes are $b/a$, not $a/b$.

---

**Setup:** Tangent to $\frac{x^2}{25} - \frac{y^2}{9} = 1$ with $m = 2$.

**Solution:** $c^2 = 25(4) - 9 = 91$, so $c = \pm\sqrt{91}$. Tangents: $y = 2x \pm \sqrt{91}$.

**Key insight:** The minus in the condition means we need $a^2m^2 > b^2$ for real tangents.

## Common Traps

- The minus sign: hyperbola tangent condition is $c^2 = a^2m^2 - b^2$ (ellipse uses $+$)
- Asymptote slope $b/a$ — easy to invert
- $e > 1$ for hyperbolas (unlike ellipse $e < 1$)
- $a$ may be smaller than $b$ here — no "major/minor axis" rule like the ellipse
- Director circle exists only if $a > b$
