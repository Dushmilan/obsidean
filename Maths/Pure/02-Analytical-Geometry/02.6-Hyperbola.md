A hyperbola is the set of all points where the difference of distances from two fixed foci is constant, or where the focus-directrix ratio is $e > 1$. It has two separate branches that approach (but never reach) two straight lines called asymptotes. The rectangular hyperbola ($xy=c^2$) is a special case with eccentricity $\sqrt{2}$ and particularly clean formulae.

**The Intuition:** Imagine two magnets pulling in opposite directions. The hyperbola is the boundary between which magnet "wins." The asymptotes are the directions where the two pulls exactly balance. Two mirror-image curves opening away from each other, hugging the asymptotes. Near the centre they are curved; far away they are almost straight.

**The Math:** Standard form: $\frac{x^2}{a^2} - \frac{y^2}{b^2} = 1$

Eccentricity: $e = \sqrt{1 + b^2/a^2} > 1$

Asymptotes: $y = \pm \frac{b}{a}x$ (from setting equation to zero)

Foci: $(\pm ae, 0)$; Directrices: $x = \pm a/e$; Latus rectum: $2b^2/a$

Parametric: $(a\sec\theta, b\tan\theta)$

Tangent at $(x_1,y_1)$: $\frac{xx_1}{a^2} - \frac{yy_1}{b^2} = 1$; with slope $m$: $y = mx \pm \sqrt{a^2m^2 - b^2}$

Condition of tangency: $c^2 = a^2m^2 - b^2$ (note the MINUS)

Director circle: $x^2 + y^2 = a^2 - b^2$ (exists only if $a > b$)

Rectangular hyperbola $xy=c^2$: parametric $(ct, c/t)$, tangent $x + t^2y = 2ct$

**What does this mean for Pure Mathematics?** The same structural pattern (tangent, normal, chord, focal chord) as the ellipse applies, with the key difference being the minus sign and the presence of asymptotes. The conjugate hyperbola shares the same asymptotes.

### Worked Examples

**Setup:** $\frac{x^2}{9}-\frac{y^2}{16}=1$. Find all elements.

**Solution:**
1. $a=3, b=4$. $e=\sqrt{1+16/9}=5/3$
2. Foci: $(\pm 5,0)$; Directrices: $x=\pm 1.8$; Asymptotes: $y=\pm\frac{4}{3}x$

**Key insight:** The asymptote slopes are $b/a$, not $a/b$.

---

**Setup:** Tangent to $\frac{x^2}{25}-\frac{y^2}{9}=1$ with $m=2$.

**Solution:**
1. $c^2=25(4)-9=91$, $c=\pm\sqrt{91}$
2. Tangents: $y=2x\pm\sqrt{91}$

**Key insight:** The minus in the condition means we need $a^2m^2>b^2$ for real tangents.
