
## Definition

A circle is the locus of points at a **fixed distance** (radius $r$) from a **fixed point** (centre $(a,b)$):

$$(x-a)^2 + (y-b)^2 = r^2$$

General form (expanded):
$$x^2 + y^2 + 2gx + 2fy + c = 0, \qquad \text{centre } (-g,-f),\ \text{radius } r = \sqrt{g^2+f^2-c}$$

The circle equation is the distance formula squared: "the distance from $(x,y)$ to $(a,b)$ is always $r$."

## The Intuition

A dog on a leash. The leash length is $r$; the point where you hold the leash is the centre. The dog can reach every point exactly $r$ away, tracing the circle. No new geometry — Pythagoras in disguise.

## The Toolkit

| Result | Formula | Notes |
|--------|---------|-------|
| Standard form | $(x-a)^2 + (y-b)^2 = r^2$ | centre $(a,b)$ |
| General form | $x^2+y^2+2gx+2fy+c=0$ | centre $(-g,-f)$, $r=\sqrt{g^2+f^2-c}$ |
| Parametric | $x = a + r\cos\theta,\ y = b + r\sin\theta$ | $\theta$ parameter |
| Tangent at $(x_1,y_1)$ | $xx_1 + yy_1 = r^2$ | on $x^2+y^2=r^2$ |
| Tangent, slope $m$ | $y = mx \pm r\sqrt{1+m^2}$ | two tangents |
| Normal at $(x_1,y_1)$ | through centre, ⟂ tangent | — |
| Orthogonal circles | $2g_1g_2 + 2f_1f_2 = c_1 + c_2$ | circles cut at $90^\circ$ |
| Radical axis | $S_1 - S_2 = 0$ | common chord line |

## Derivation

Standard form is the distance formula squared. General form comes from expanding and renaming $2g = -2a$, $2f = -2b$, $c = a^2+b^2-r^2$. The tangent at $(x_1,y_1)$: the radius to the point has gradient $y_1/x_1$, the tangent is perpendicular, giving $xx_1 + yy_1 = r^2$ after manipulation. [Full derivations: 02.3-Circles-Proof]

## Method

1. **Given general form:** complete the square separately on $x$ and $y$ terms to reach standard form.
2. **Radius from general form:** $r = \sqrt{g^2+f^2-c}$ — must be real (else no real circle).
3. **Tangent at a point on the circle:** replace $x^2 \to xx_1$, $y^2 \to yy_1$, $x \to \frac{x+x_1}{2}$, $y \to \frac{y+y_1}{2}$, constant stays.
4. **Tangents of given slope:** $y = mx \pm r\sqrt{1+m^2}$ — the $\pm$ gives the two parallel tangents.
5. **Intersection/radical axis:** subtract the two circle equations — the $x^2,y^2$ terms cancel, leaving the line $S_1 - S_2 = 0$.

## Worked Examples

**Setup:** $x^2 + y^2 - 6x + 4y - 12 = 0$. Find centre and radius.

**Solution:** Complete the square: $(x^2-6x+9) + (y^2+4y+4) = 12+9+4 \Rightarrow (x-3)^2 + (y+2)^2 = 25$.

**Key insight:** Centre $(3,-2)$, radius $5$. Group $x$ and $y$ separately, then complete the square.

---

**Setup:** Circle $x^2+y^2=16$, slope $m=\frac34$. Find tangent equations.

**Solution:** $y = \frac34 x \pm 4\sqrt{1+\frac{9}{16}} = \frac34 x \pm 5 \Rightarrow 3x - 4y \pm 20 = 0$.

**Key insight:** The $\pm$ gives two tangents — same slope, touching opposite sides.

## Common Traps

- Forgetting to halve $g, f$ when reading the centre from general form
- Radius $\sqrt{g^2+f^2-c}$ imaginary → no real circle (check first)
- Tangent formula only valid for points **on** the circle
- Completing the square: adding constants to both sides correctly
- Confusing the radical axis (a line) with the circle itself
