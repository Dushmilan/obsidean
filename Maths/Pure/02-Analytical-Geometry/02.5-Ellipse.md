An ellipse is a "stretched circle" with two foci, where the sum of distances from any point to the foci is constant ($=2a$), or equivalently the focus-directrix ratio is $e$ with $0 < e < 1$. If $a=b$, you get a circle. The eccentricity measures how "squashed" the ellipse is: $e=0$ is a circle, $e$ close to $1$ is very elongated.

**The Intuition:** Take a circle and stretch it horizontally. Points that were at distance $r$ from the centre are now at different distances depending on direction. The foci move apart from the centre. A physical construction: two pins stuck in a board, a loop of string around both pins, and a pencil pulling the string taut traces an ellipse. The total string length is constant ($=2a$).

**The Math:** Standard form: $\frac{x^2}{a^2} + \frac{y^2}{b^2} = 1$ where $a > b > 0$

Eccentricity: $e = \sqrt{1 - b^2/a^2}$, so $b^2 = a^2(1-e^2)$

Foci: $(\pm ae, 0)$; Directrices: $x = \pm a/e$; Latus rectum: $2b^2/a$

Parametric: $(a\cos\theta, b\sin\theta)$

Tangent at $(x_1,y_1)$: $\frac{xx_1}{a^2} + \frac{yy_1}{b^2} = 1$; with slope $m$: $y = mx \pm \sqrt{a^2m^2 + b^2}$

Condition of tangency: $c^2 = a^2m^2 + b^2$

Normal at $(x_1,y_1)$: $\frac{a^2x}{x_1} - \frac{b^2y}{y_1} = a^2 - b^2$

Director circle: $x^2 + y^2 = a^2 + b^2$ (perpendicular tangents)

**What does this mean for Pure Mathematics?** The tangent and normal formulae follow the same pattern as circles but with $a^2$ and $b^2$ denominators. The auxiliary circle connects the eccentric angle parameter to the actual position on the ellipse, useful for construction and parametric understanding.

### Worked Examples

**Setup:** Ellipse $\frac{x^2}{25} + \frac{y^2}{9} = 1$. Find all elements.

**Solution:**
1. $a=5, b=3$. $e = \sqrt{1-9/25} = 4/5 = 0.8$
2. Foci: $(\pm 4, 0)$; Directrices: $x = \pm 6.25$; Latus rectum: $18/5 = 3.6$

**Key insight:** Identify $a$ (larger) and $b$ (smaller) first, then everything else follows.

**Setup:** Tangent to $\frac{x^2}{16}+\frac{y^2}{4}=1$ with $m=\frac{1}{2}$.

**Solution:**
1. $c^2 = 16(\frac{1}{4})+4 = 8$, so $c = \pm 2\sqrt{2}$
2. Tangents: $y = \frac{1}{2}x \pm 2\sqrt{2}$

**Key insight:** The $\pm$ gives two tangents with the same slope.
