A Cartesian coordinate system assigns each point in a plane an ordered pair $(x, y)$ relative to two perpendicular axes meeting at the origin. The distance formula, midpoint, section formulae, and triangle area are all built from this single idea: every location is two numbers, and every measurement follows from Pythagoras.

**The Intuition:** Imagine walking in a city grid. To get from one corner to another, you walk some blocks east and some blocks north. The straight-line shortcut is the hypotenuse of the right triangle formed by those two walks. If you move $a$ units horizontally and $b$ units vertically, the direct path is $\sqrt{a^2 + b^2}$. This is not a new theorem -- it is Pythagoras applied to coordinate differences.

**The Math:** For points $A(x_1, y_1)$ and $B(x_2, y_2)$:

Distance: $AB = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}$

Midpoint: $\left(\frac{x_1+x_2}{2}, \frac{y_1+y_2}{2}\right)$

Internal division $m:n$: $\left(\frac{mx_2+nx_1}{m+n}, \frac{my_2+ny_1}{m+n}\right)$

External division $m:n$: $\left(\frac{mx_2-nx_1}{m-n}, \frac{my_2-ny_1}{m-n}\right)$

Triangle area: $\frac{1}{2}|x_1(y_2-y_3)+x_2(y_3-y_1)+x_3(y_1-y_2)|$

Centroid: $\left(\frac{x_1+x_2+x_3}{3}, \frac{y_1+y_2+y_3}{3}\right)$

Polar conversions: $x=r\cos\theta,\; y=r\sin\theta$ and $r=\sqrt{x^2+y^2},\; \theta=\tan^{-1}(y/x)$

**What does this mean for Pure Mathematics?** These tools are the raw ingredients for proving collinearity, finding centres of triangles, converting between coordinate systems, and describing every conic section. Without coordinates, there are no equations for curves.

### Worked Examples

**Setup:** Points $A(3, -2)$ and $B(-1, 4)$. Find the distance and midpoint.

**Solution:**
1. $AB = \sqrt{(-1-3)^2 + (4-(-2))^2} = \sqrt{16+36} = \sqrt{52} = 2\sqrt{13}$
2. Midpoint $= \left(\frac{3+(-1)}{2}, \frac{-2+4}{2}\right) = (1, 1)$

**Key insight:** Always subtract in the same order for both coordinates: $(x_2-x_1, y_2-y_1)$.

---

**Setup:** $A(1,2), B(4,6), C(5,2)$. Find the area.

**Solution:**
1. $\frac{1}{2}|1(6-2) + 4(2-2) + 5(2-6)|$
2. $= \frac{1}{2}|4 + 0 - 20| = \frac{1}{2}|-16| = 8$

**Key insight:** The absolute value turns $-16$ into $+16$. Vertex ordering changes the sign but not the area.

---

**Setup:** Convert $(3, 4)$ to polar.

**Solution:**
1. $r = \sqrt{9+16} = 5$
2. $\theta = \tan^{-1}(4/3) \approx 53.13^\circ$ (Quadrant I, no adjustment)

**Key insight:** When both coordinates are positive, the point is in Quadrant I and no angle adjustment is needed.
