## 1. Polar-Cartesian Conversion

**Theorem:** Point $P$ with polar coordinates $(r, \theta)$ has Cartesian coordinates:
$$x = r\cos\theta,\quad y = r\sin\theta$$

**Proof:**
By definition, $r = OP$, $\theta = \angle XOP$.
In right $\triangle OPM$ (where $M$ is foot of perpendicular from $P$ to x-axis):
$$\cos\theta = \frac{OM}{OP} = \frac{x}{r} \Rightarrow x = r\cos\theta$$
$$\sin\theta = \frac{PM}{OP} = \frac{y}{r} \Rightarrow y = r\sin\theta \quad \square$$

**Inverse:** $r = \sqrt{x^2+y^2}$, $\theta = \tan^{-1}(y/x)$ (adjust quadrant).

## 2. Distance Formula in Polar Coordinates

**Theorem:** Distance between $P_1(r_1, \theta_1)$ and $P_2(r_2, \theta_2)$:
$$P_1P_2 = \sqrt{r_1^2 + r_2^2 - 2r_1r_2\cos(\theta_1 - \theta_2)}$$

**Proof:**
Cartesian coordinates:
$P_1(r_1\cos\theta_1, r_1\sin\theta_1)$, $P_2(r_2\cos\theta_2, r_2\sin\theta_2)$.

Using distance formula:
$$P_1P_2^2 = (r_1\cos\theta_1 - r_2\cos\theta_2)^2 + (r_1\sin\theta_1 - r_2\sin\theta_2)^2$$
$$= r_1^2(\cos^2\theta_1+\sin^2\theta_1) + r_2^2(\cos^2\theta_2+\sin^2\theta_2) - 2r_1r_2(\cos\theta_1\cos\theta_2 + \sin\theta_1\sin\theta_2)$$
$$= r_1^2 + r_2^2 - 2r_1r_2\cos(\theta_1 - \theta_2) \quad \square$$

### Circle with Centre at Pole
**Theorem:** Circle radius $a$, centre at pole: $r = a$.

**Proof:** All points satisfy $OP = a \Rightarrow r = a$. $\square$

### Circle with Diameter on Initial Line
**Theorem:** Circle radius $a$, diameter on initial line through pole: $r = 2a\cos\theta$.

**Proof:**
Circle passes through pole $O$, centre $C(a, 0)$, radius $a$.
For point $P(r, \theta)$ on circle: $\angle OPC = 90^\circ$ (angle in semicircle).
In $\triangle OPC$: $OC = a$, $OP = r$, $\angle POC = \theta$.
$r = OC\cos\theta = a\cos\theta$? Wait - the diameter is $2a$.

Correct: Centre $(a, 0)$, radius $a$. Equation: $(x-a)^2 + y^2 = a^2$.
$x^2 - 2ax + a^2 + y^2 = a^2 \Rightarrow x^2 + y^2 = 2ax$.
$r^2 = 2ar\cos\theta \Rightarrow r = 2a\cos\theta \quad \square$

---


### Line Through Pole
**Theorem:** Line through pole making angle $\alpha$ with initial line: $\theta = \alpha$.

**Proof:** All points on line have same angle $\alpha$ from initial line. $\square$

### Line Not Through Pole
**Theorem:** Line whose perpendicular from pole has length $p$ and makes angle $\alpha$ with initial line: $r = \frac{p}{\cos(\theta - \alpha)}$.

**Proof:**
Let $N$ be foot of perpendicular from pole to line. $ON = p$, $\angle XON = \alpha$.
For any point $P(r, \theta)$ on line, in $\triangle ONP$:
$\cos(\theta - \alpha) = \frac{ON}{OP} = \frac{p}{r} \Rightarrow r = \frac{p}{\cos(\theta - \alpha)} \quad \square$

## 5. Conic Sections with Focus at Pole

**Theorem:** Conic with focus at pole, directrix $x = \frac{l}{e}$, eccentricity $e$:
$$r = \frac{l}{1 + e\cos\theta}$$
where $l$ = semi-latus rectum.

**Proof:**
By definition: $PF = e \cdot PM$ where $PM$ is perpendicular distance to directrix.
$PF = r$, $PM = \frac{l}{e} - r\cos\theta$ (distance to line $x = \frac{l}{e}$).
$$r = e\left(\frac{l}{e} - r\cos\theta\right) = l - er\cos\theta$$
$$r + er\cos\theta = l \Rightarrow r(1 + e\cos\theta) = l \Rightarrow r = \frac{l}{1 + e\cos\theta} \quad \square$$

**Special Cases:**
- Parabola: $e = 1$, $r = \frac{l}{1 + \cos\theta}$
- Ellipse: $0 < e < 1$, $l = a(1-e^2)$
- Hyperbola: $e > 1$, $l = a(e^2-1)$

## 6. Tangent to Polar Curve

**Theorem:** For curve $r = f(\theta)$, gradient of tangent in Cartesian coordinates:
$$\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{r\cos\theta + r'\sin\theta}{-r\sin\theta + r'\cos\theta}$$
where $r = f(\theta)$, $r' = f'(\theta) = \frac{dr}{d\theta}$.

**Proof:**
$x = r\cos\theta = f(\theta)\cos\theta$
$y = r\sin\theta = f(\theta)\sin\theta$

$$\frac{dx}{d\theta} = f'(\theta)\cos\theta - f(\theta)\sin\theta = r'\cos\theta - r\sin\theta$$
$$\frac{dy}{d\theta} = f'(\theta)\sin\theta + f(\theta)\cos\theta = r'\sin\theta + r\cos\theta$$

$$\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{dy/d\theta}{dx/d\theta} = \frac{r'\sin\theta + r\cos\theta}{r'\cos\theta - r\sin\theta} = \frac{r\cos\theta + r'\sin\theta}{-r\sin\theta + r'\cos\theta} \quad \square$$

**Equation of tangent (polar form):**
$$\frac{1}{r} = \frac{1}{r_0}\cos(\theta - \theta_0) + \frac{1}{r_0'}\sin(\theta - \theta_0)$$

## 7. Area in Polar Coordinates

**Theorem:** Area bounded by curve $r = f(\theta)$, rays $\theta = \alpha$, $\theta = \beta$:
$$\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2}\int_{\alpha}^{\beta} r^2 d\theta = \frac{1}{2}\int_{\alpha}^{\beta} [f(\theta)]^2 d\theta$$

**Proof:**
Area of sector between $\theta$ and $\theta + d\theta$:
$\approx \frac{1}{2}r^2 d\theta$ (circular sector area).

Summing (integrating) from $\alpha$ to $\beta$:
$$\text{Area} = \int_{\alpha}^{\beta} \frac{1}{2}r^2 d\theta \quad \square$$

---


### Area of Circle $r = a$
$$\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2}\int_0^{2\pi} a^2 d\theta = \frac{a^2}{2}(2\pi) = \pi a^2 \quad \checkmark$$

### Area of Circle $r = 2a\cos\theta$ (from $-\pi/2$ to $\pi/2$)
$$\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2}\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2} 4a^2\cos^2\theta d\theta = 2a^2\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2} \frac{1+\cos2\theta}{2} d\theta = a^2[\theta + \frac{1}{2}\sin2\theta]_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2} = a^2\pi \quad \checkmark$$

### Area of Ellipse $\frac{x^2}{a^2} + \frac{y^2}{b^2} = 1$ in Polar (focus at pole)
$r = \frac{l}{1+e\cos\theta}$, $l = a(1-e^2)$.
$$\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2}\int_0^{2\pi} \frac{l^2}{(1+e\cos\theta)^2} d\theta = \pi ab$$
(Standard integral result)

## 9. Arc Length in Polar Coordinates

**Theorem:** Arc length of $r = f(\theta)$ from $\theta = \alpha$ to $\beta$:
$$L = \int_{\alpha}^{\beta} \sqrt{r^2 + \left(\frac{dr}{d\theta}\right)^2} d\theta$$

**Proof:**
$ds^2 = dx^2 + dy^2 = (r'\cos\theta - r\sin\theta)^2 + (r'\sin\theta + r\cos\theta)^2 d\theta^2$
$= (r'^2 + r^2)d\theta^2$
$ds = \sqrt{r^2 + r'^2} d\theta \quad \square$
