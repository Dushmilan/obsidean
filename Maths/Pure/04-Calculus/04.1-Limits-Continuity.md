# 4.1 Limits & Continuity

## Definition

$\lim_{x \to a} f(x) = L$ means $f(x)$ can be made arbitrarily close to $L$ by taking $x$ sufficiently close to $a$ (but $x \neq a$).

### Formal ($\epsilon$-$\delta$) Definition
$$\lim_{x \to a} f(x) = L \iff \forall \epsilon > 0,\; \exists \delta > 0 \text{ such that } 0 < |x - a| < \delta \implies |f(x) - L| < \epsilon$$

### One-Sided Limits
- **Right-hand limit:** $\lim_{x \to a^+} f(x) = L$
- **Left-hand limit:** $\lim_{x \to a^-} f(x) = L$
- **Two-sided limit exists** $\iff$ both one-sided limits exist and are equal.

### Infinite Limits
- $\lim_{x \to a} f(x) = \infty$ means $f(x)$ grows without bound as $x \to a$
- $\lim_{x \to a} f(x) = -\infty$ means $f(x)$ decreases without bound

### Limits at Infinity
- $\lim_{x \to \infty} f(x) = L$ means $f(x) \to L$ as $x$ grows arbitrarily large
- $\lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{1}{x^n} = 0$ for $n > 0$

### Continuity at a Point
$f$ is **continuous at $x = a$** iff all three hold:
1. $f(a)$ is defined
2. $\lim_{x \to a} f(x)$ exists
3. $\lim_{x \to a} f(x) = f(a)$

### Types of Discontinuity

| Type | Condition | Example |
|------|-----------|---------|
| **Removable** | Limit exists $\neq f(a)$ (or $f(a)$ undefined) | $\frac{\sin x}{x}$ at $x=0$ |
| **Jump** | Left & right limits exist but unequal | $f(x) = \lfloor x \rfloor$ at integers |
| **Infinite** | Limit is $\pm\infty$ | $\frac{1}{x}$ at $x=0$ |
| **Oscillatory** | Limit does not exist (oscillates) | $\sin\frac{1}{x}$ at $x=0$ |

## Formulae

### Limit Laws

If $\lim_{x \to a} f(x) = L$ and $\lim_{x \to a} g(x) = M$ exist:

| Formula | Expression |
|---------|------------|
| **Sum** | $\lim_{x \to a} [f(x) + g(x)] = L + M$ |
| **Difference** | $\lim_{x \to a} [f(x) - g(x)] = L - M$ |
| **Product** | $\lim_{x \to a} [f(x) \cdot g(x)] = L \cdot M$ |
| **Quotient** | $\lim_{x \to a} \frac{f(x)}{g(x)} = \frac{L}{M}$ (if $M \neq 0$) |
| **Constant Multiple** | $\lim_{x \to a} [c \cdot f(x)] = cL$ |
| **Power** | $\lim_{x \to a} [f(x)]^n = L^n$ ($n \in \mathbb{N}$) |
| **Root** | $\lim_{x \to a} \sqrt[n]{f(x)} = \sqrt[n]{L}$ (if $L \ge 0$ for even $n$) |
| **Composition** | $\lim_{x \to a} g(f(x)) = g(L)$ if $g$ continuous at $L$ |

### Standard Limits (Memorise)

| Formula | Expression |
|---------|------------|
| $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin x}{x}$ | $1$ |
| $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\tan x}{x}$ | $1$ |
| $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{1 - \cos x}{x}$ | $0$ |
| $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{1 - \cos x}{x^2}$ | $\frac{1}{2}$ |
| $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{e^x - 1}{x}$ | $1$ |
| $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\ln(1+x)}{x}$ | $1$ |
| $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{a^x - 1}{x}$ | $\ln a$ ($a > 0$) |
| $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\log_a(1+x)}{x}$ | $\frac{1}{\ln a}$ |
| $\lim_{x \to \infty} \left(1 + \frac{1}{x}\right)^x$ | $e$ |
| $\lim_{x \to 0} (1 + x)^{1/x}$ | $e$ |

### L'Hôpital's Rule

$$\lim_{x \to a} \frac{f(x)}{g(x)} = \lim_{x \to a} \frac{f'(x)}{g'(x)}$$

### Indeterminate Forms

| Form | Resolution Strategy |
|------|---------------------|
| $\frac{0}{0}$ | Factorise, rationalise, L'Hôpital |
| $\frac{\infty}{\infty}$ | Divide by highest power, L'Hôpital |
| $0 \cdot \infty$ | Rewrite as $\frac{0}{0}$ or $\frac{\infty}{\infty}$ |
| $\infty - \infty$ | Combine into single fraction |
| $1^\infty$, $0^0$, $\infty^0$ | Take $\ln$, evaluate limit, then exponentiate |

### Continuity on an Interval
- **Continuous on $(a,b)$:** Continuous at every point in $(a,b)$
- **Continuous on $[a,b]$:** Continuous on $(a,b)$, right-continuous at $a$, left-continuous at $b$

### Continuity of Standard Functions

| Function | Domain | Continuity |
|----------|--------|------------|
| Polynomials | $\mathbb{R}$ | Everywhere |
| Rational $\frac{P(x)}{Q(x)}$ | $\mathbb{R} \setminus \{Q(x)=0\}$ | Everywhere in domain |
| $\sin x, \cos x$ | $\mathbb{R}$ | Everywhere |
| $\tan x$ | $x \neq \frac{\pi}{2} + k\pi$ | Everywhere in domain |
| $e^x$ | $\mathbb{R}$ | Everywhere |
| $\ln x$ | $(0,\infty)$ | Everywhere in domain |
| $a^x$ ($a>0$) | $\mathbb{R}$ | Everywhere |
| $|x|$ | $\mathbb{R}$ | Everywhere (not differentiable at 0) |

## Properties / Key Concepts

### Important Theorems

**Intermediate Value Theorem (IVT):** If $f$ continuous on $[a,b]$ and $f(a) \neq f(b)$, then for any $k$ between $f(a)$ and $f(b)$, $\exists c \in (a,b)$ such that $f(c) = k$.
- **Application:** Root finding — if $f(a)f(b) < 0$, there's a root in $(a,b)$.

**Extreme Value Theorem:** If $f$ continuous on closed interval $[a,b]$, then $f$ attains absolute maximum and minimum on $[a,b]$.

**Sandwich (Squeeze) Theorem:** If $g(x) \le f(x) \le h(x)$ near $a$, and $\lim_{x \to a} g(x) = \lim_{x \to a} h(x) = L$, then $\lim_{x \to a} f(x) = L$.

### L'Hôpital's Rule Conditions
1. Indeterminate form $\frac{0}{0}$ or $\frac{\infty}{\infty}$
2. $f,g$ differentiable on $(a-\delta, a+\delta)\setminus\{a\}$
3. $g'(x) \neq 0$ near $a$
4. Limit on RHS exists (or is $\pm\infty$)
- **Can apply repeatedly** if indeterminate form persists.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1: $\lim_{x \to 2} \frac{x^3 - 8}{x^2 - 4}$
**$0/0$ form.** Factorise:
$$\frac{(x-2)(x^2+2x+4)}{(x-2)(x+2)} = \frac{x^2+2x+4}{x+2} \xrightarrow{x \to 2} \frac{4+4+4}{4} = 3$$

### Example 2: $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin 5x}{\sin 2x}$
**Standard limits:** $\frac{\sin 5x}{\sin 2x} = \frac{5x}{2x} \cdot \frac{\sin 5x}{5x} \cdot \frac{2x}{\sin 2x} \to \frac{5}{2}$

### Example 3: $\lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{3x^2 + 2x + 1}{5x^2 - 4x + 7}$
Divide by $x^2$: $\lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{3 + 2/x + 1/x^2}{5 - 4/x + 7/x^2} = \frac{3}{5}$

### Example 4: $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{e^{2x} - 1 - 2x}{x^2}$
**L'Hôpital (twice):**
$$\lim \frac{2e^{2x} - 2}{2x} \xrightarrow{0/0} \lim \frac{4e^{2x}}{2} = 2$$

### Example 5: Continuity at $x=1$
$$f(x) = \begin{cases} x^2 + k & x \le 1 \\ 3x - 1 & x > 1 \end{cases}$$
Need $\lim_{x \to 1^-} f(x) = \lim_{x \to 1^+} f(x) = f(1)$
$1 + k = 3 - 1 = 2 \Rightarrow k = 1$

### Example 6: $\lim_{x \to 0} x \ln x$ ($0 \cdot (-\infty)$ form)
Rewrite: $\lim_{x \to 0^+} \frac{\ln x}{1/x}$ ($\infty/\infty$)
L'Hôpital: $\lim \frac{1/x}{-1/x^2} = \lim (-x) = 0$

### Example 7: $\lim_{x \to \infty} \left(1 + \frac{3}{x}\right)^{2x}$
Let $y = \left(1 + \frac{3}{x}\right)^{2x}$. $\ln y = 2x \ln(1 + 3/x) = 2 \cdot \frac{\ln(1+3/x)}{1/x}$
$\ln y \xrightarrow{x \to \infty} 2 \cdot 3 = 6$ (standard limit or L'Hôpital)
$y \to e^6$

### Pattern 1: Evaluate $\lim_{x \to a} \frac{f(x)}{g(x)}$ ($0/0$ form)
**Strategy:**
1. Try factorisation: $\frac{x^2-4}{x-2} = \frac{(x-2)(x+2)}{x-2} = x+2 \to 4$
2. Rationalise numerator/denominator: $\frac{\sqrt{x+2}-2}{x-2} \cdot \frac{\sqrt{x+2}+2}{\sqrt{x+2}+2} = \frac{1}{4}$
3. Use standard limits: $\frac{\sin 3x}{x} = 3 \cdot \frac{\sin 3x}{3x} \to 3$
4. L'Hôpital's Rule: $\lim \frac{f}{g} = \lim \frac{f'}{g'}$

### Pattern 2: Limits at Infinity ($\infty/\infty$)
**Strategy:**
- Divide numerator & denominator by highest power of $x$
- $\lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{3x^2+2x}{5x^2-x} = \frac{3}{5}$
- For $\lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{e^x}{x^n} = \infty$ (exponential beats polynomial)

### Pattern 3: Continuity & Parameters
"Find $k$ such that $f$ is continuous at $x=a$"
**Method:**
1. Compute $\lim_{x \to a^-} f(x)$ and $\lim_{x \to a^+} f(x)$
2. Set equal to $f(a)$ (or each other if $f(a)$ undefined)
3. Solve for parameter

### Pattern 4: Piecewise Functions
Check continuity at boundary points:
- $\lim_{x \to c^-} f(x) = \lim_{x \to c^+} f(x) = f(c)$

## Common Traps

| Trap | ❌ Wrong | Correct |
|------|-------|---------|
| $\lim \frac{f}{g}$ | Split as $\frac{\lim f}{\lim g}$ without checking | Check $g \neq 0$ |
| L'Hôpital | Apply when not $0/0$ or $\infty/\infty$ | Verify form first |
| $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin x}{x}$ | Use degrees | Must use **radians** |
| One-sided limits | Ignore for piecewise/abs value | Always check both sides |
| $f(a)$ undefined | "Limit doesn't exist" | Limit can exist even if $f(a)$ undefined |
| $|x|$ at 0 | Differentiable | Continuous but **not differentiable** |
