### 1.1 Definition via Riemann Sums
Partition rectangle $R = [a,b] \times [c,d]$ into $mn$ subrectangles of area $\Delta A = \Delta x \Delta y$.
Choose sample point $(x_{ij}^*, y_{ij}^*)$ in each subrectangle.
Riemann sum: $\sum_{i=1}^m \sum_{j=1}^n f(x_{ij}^*, y_{ij}^*) \Delta A$.
Double integral: $\iint_R f(x,y) dA = \lim_{m,n \to \infty} \sum_{i=1}^m \sum_{j=1}^n f(x_{ij}^*, y_{ij}^*) \Delta A$.

**Existence:** If $f$ continuous on closed bounded $R$, integral exists.

---

### 1.2 Fubini's Theorem

**Theorem:** If $f$ continuous on rectangle $R = [a,b] \times [c,d]$, then
$$\iint_R f(x,y) \, dA = \int_a^b \left( \int_c^d f(x,y) \, dy \right) dx = \int_c^d \left( \int_a^b f(x,y) \, dx \right) dy$$

**Proof Sketch:**
For fixed $x$, $g(x) = \int_c^d f(x,y) dy$ exists (continuity).
Riemann sum for double integral:
$$\sum_{i,j} f(x_i^*, y_j^*) \Delta x \Delta y = \sum_i \left( \sum_j f(x_i^*, y_j^*) \Delta y \right) \Delta x$$
Inner sum $\to \int_c^d f(x_i^*, y) dy$ as $n \to \infty$.
Outer sum $\to \int_a^b \left( \int_c^d f(x,y) dy \right) dx$ as $m \to \infty$.
By symmetry, order doesn't matter.

**General Regions:** Extend by defining $f=0$ outside $R$.

---

### 1.3 Iterated Integrals for General Regions

**Type I:** $R = \{(x,y): a \le x \le b, g_1(x) \le y \le g_2(x)\}$
Define $F(x) = \int_{g_1(x)}^{g_2(x)} f(x,y) dy$. Then $\iint_R f = \int_a^b F(x) dx$.

**Type II:** $R = \{(x,y): c \le y \le d, h_1(y) \le x \le h_2(y)\}$
$$\iint_R f = \int_c^d \left( \int_{h_1(y)}^{h_2(y)} f(x,y) dx \right) dy$$

### 2.1 Transformation
$x = x(u,v), y = y(u,v)$ maps region $S$ in $uv$-plane to $R$ in $xy$-plane.
Assume one-to-one, continuously differentiable.

### 2.2 Jacobian Determinant
$$J = \frac{\partial(x,y)}{\partial(u,v)} = \begin{vmatrix} x_u & x_v \\ y_u & y_v \end{vmatrix} = x_u y_v - x_v y_u$$

**Geometric Meaning:** Area scaling factor.
Parallelogram spanned by $(x_u, y_u) \Delta u$ and $(x_v, y_v) \Delta v$ has area $|J| \Delta u \Delta v$.

### 2.3 Change of Variables Formula
$$\iint_R f(x,y) \, dx \, dy = \iint_S f(x(u,v), y(u,v)) \, |J| \, du \, dv$$

**Proof Sketch:**
Partition $S$ into small rectangles $\Delta u \Delta v$.
Image in $R$ is approximately parallelogram with area $|J| \Delta u \Delta v$.
Riemann sum in $R$: $\sum f(x_i, y_i) \Delta A_i \approx \sum f(x(u_i,v_i), y(u_i,v_i)) |J(u_i,v_i)| \Delta u \Delta v$.
Limit gives the formula.

---

### 2.4 Polar Coordinates
$x = r \cos \theta, y = r \sin \theta$
$J = \begin{vmatrix} \cos\theta & -r\sin\theta \\ \sin\theta & r\cos\theta \end{vmatrix} = r(\cos^2\theta + \sin^2\theta) = r$
$|J| = r$

$$\iint_R f(x,y) dx\,dy = \iint_S f(r\cos\theta, r\sin\theta) \, r \, dr \, d\theta$$

### 3.1 Definition
Partition box $B = [a,b] \times [c,d] \times [r,s]$ into subboxes of volume $\Delta V = \Delta x \Delta y \Delta z$.
Triple integral: $\iiint_B f(x,y,z) dV = \lim \sum f(x_{ijk}^*, y_{ijk}^*, z_{ijk}^*) \Delta V$.

### 3.2 Fubini's Theorem for Triple Integrals
If $f$ continuous on box $B$:
$$\iiint_B f = \int_a^b \int_c^d \int_r^s f(x,y,z) dz\,dy\,dx$$
(Any of the 6 orders works.)

### 3.3 General Regions
**Type 1 (z-simple):** $E = \{(x,y,z): (x,y) \in D, u_1(x,y) \le z \le u_2(x,y)\}$
$$\iiint_E f = \iint_D \left( \int_{u_1(x,y)}^{u_2(x,y)} f(x,y,z) dz \right) dA$$

**Type 2 (y-simple):** $E = \{(x,y,z): (x,z) \in D, v_1(x,z) \le y \le v_2(x,z)\}$
$$\iiint_E f = \iint_D \left( \int_{v_1(x,z)}^{v_2(x,z)} f(x,y,z) dy \right) dA$$

**Type 3 (x-simple):** $E = \{(x,y,z): (y,z) \in D, w_1(y,z) \le x \le w_2(y,z)\}$

### 4.1 Transformation
$x = r \cos \theta, y = r \sin \theta, z = z$
$r \ge 0, 0 \le \theta \le 2\pi, z \in \mathbb{R}$

### 4.2 Jacobian
$$\frac{\partial(x,y,z)}{\partial(r,\theta,z)} = \begin{vmatrix} \cos\theta & -r\sin\theta & 0 \\ \sin\theta & r\cos\theta & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{vmatrix} = r(\cos^2\theta + \sin^2\theta) = r$$

### 4.3 Volume Element
$dV = r \, dz \, dr \, d\theta$ (order can vary)

---


### 5.1 Transformation
$x = \rho \sin \phi \cos \theta$
$y = \rho \sin \phi \sin \theta$
$z = \rho \cos \phi$
where $\rho \ge 0, 0 \le \phi \le \pi, 0 \le \theta \le 2\pi$

### 5.2 Jacobian
$$\frac{\partial(x,y,z)}{\partial(\rho,\theta,\phi)} = \begin{vmatrix}
\sin\phi\cos\theta & -\rho\sin\phi\sin\theta & \rho\cos\phi\cos\theta \\
\sin\phi\sin\theta & \rho\sin\phi\cos\theta & \rho\cos\phi\sin\theta \\
\cos\phi & 0 & -\rho\sin\phi
\end{vmatrix}$$

Expand along third column:
$$= \rho\cos\phi\cos\theta \begin{vmatrix} \sin\phi\sin\theta & \rho\sin\phi\cos\theta \\ \cos\phi & 0 \end{vmatrix}
- \rho\cos\phi\sin\theta \begin{vmatrix} \sin\phi\cos\theta & -\rho\sin\phi\sin\theta \\ \cos\phi & 0 \end{vmatrix}
- \rho\sin\phi \begin{vmatrix} \sin\phi\cos\theta & -\rho\sin\phi\sin\theta \\ \sin\phi\sin\theta & \rho\sin\phi\cos\theta \end{vmatrix}$$

Compute each 2Ã—2:
- First: $-\rho\sin\phi\cos\phi\sin\theta$
- Second: $\rho\sin\phi\cos\phi\cos\theta$
- Third: $\rho\sin^2\phi(\cos^2\theta + \sin^2\theta) = \rho\sin^2\phi$

Result:
$$= \rho\cos\phi\cos\theta (-\rho\sin\phi\cos\phi\sin\theta) - \rho\cos\phi\sin\theta (\rho\sin\phi\cos\phi\cos\theta) - \rho\sin\phi(\rho\sin^2\phi)$$
$$= -\rho^2\sin\phi\cos^2\phi\sin\theta\cos\theta - \rho^2\sin\phi\cos^2\phi\sin\theta\cos\theta - \rho^2\sin^3\phi$$
Wait, better to use known formula: $|J| = \rho^2 \sin \phi$.

**Alternative Derivation:**
Volume of spherical box $\Delta \rho \times \rho \Delta \phi \times \rho \sin \phi \Delta \theta = \rho^2 \sin \phi \Delta \rho \Delta \phi \Delta \theta$.
Thus $|J| = \rho^2 \sin \phi$.

### 5.3 Volume Element
$dV = \rho^2 \sin \phi \, d\rho \, d\phi \, d\theta$

### 6.1 Theorem
Let $T: S \to R$ be a $C^1$ transformation from open set $S \subset \mathbb{R}^n$ to $R \subset \mathbb{R}^n$, one-to-one on $S$, with $\det DT(\mathbf{u}) \neq 0$ on $S$.
Then for integrable $f$:
$$\int_R f(\mathbf{x}) d\mathbf{x} = \int_S f(T(\mathbf{u})) |\det DT(\mathbf{u})| d\mathbf{u}$$

### 6.2 Proof Sketch (Inverse Function Theorem)
1. Partition $S$ into small boxes.
2. $T$ maps each box approximately to a parallelepiped.
3. Volume of image $\approx |\det DT|$ Ã— volume of box.
4. Riemann sums correspond term-by-term in limit.

---


### 7.1 Volume
$$V = \iiint_E 1 \, dV$$

**Example (Sphere radius $a$):**
Spherical: $V = \int_0^{2\pi} \int_0^\pi \int_0^a \rho^2 \sin \phi \, d\rho \, d\phi \, d\theta = 2\pi \cdot 2 \cdot \frac{a^3}{3} = \frac{4}{3}\pi a^3$

### 7.2 Mass and Centre of Mass
Density $\rho(x,y,z)$:
$$m = \iiint \rho \, dV$$
$$\bar{x} = \frac{1}{m} \iiint x \rho \, dV, \quad \bar{y} = \frac{1}{m} \iiint y \rho \, dV, \quad \bar{z} = \frac{1}{m} \iiint z \rho \, dV$$

### 7.3 Moments of Inertia
$$I_x = \iiint (y^2 + z^2) \rho \, dV$$
$$I_y = \iiint (x^2 + z^2) \rho \, dV$$
$$I_z = \iiint (x^2 + y^2) \rho \, dV$$

### 7.4 Probability
Joint density $f(x,y)$: $P((X,Y) \in R) = \iint_R f(x,y) dA$

---


### 8.1 Surface Area
Surface $z = f(x,y)$ over region $D$:
$$A = \iint_D \sqrt{1 + f_x^2 + f_y^2} \, dA$$

**Derivation:** Tangent plane area element $dS = \sqrt{1 + f_x^2 + f_y^2} dA$.

### 8.2 Parametric Surface
$\mathbf{r}(u,v) = \langle x(u,v), y(u,v), z(u,v) \rangle$
$$dS = \|\mathbf{r}_u \times \mathbf{r}_v\| \, du \, dv$$

---


### 9.1 Line Integrals
$$\int_C f(x,y) ds = \int_a^b f(x(t), y(t)) \sqrt{x'(t)^2 + y'(t)^2} \, dt$$

### 9.2 Green's Theorem
$$\oint_C P dx + Q dy = \iint_D \left( \frac{\partial Q}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial P}{\partial y} \right) dA$$

## 10. Summary of Jacobians

| Coordinates | Transformation | Jacobian Determinant | Volume Element |
|-------------|----------------|---------------------|----------------|
| **Polar** | $x=r\cos\theta, y=r\sin\theta$ | $r$ | $r\,dr\,d\theta$ |
| **Cylindrical** | $x=r\cos\theta, y=r\sin\theta, z=z$ | $r$ | $r\,dz\,dr\,d\theta$ |
| **Spherical** | $x=\rho\sin\phi\cos\theta, y=\rho\sin\phi\sin\theta, z=\rho\cos\phi$ | $\rho^2\sin\phi$ | $\rho^2\sin\phi\,d\rho\,d\phi\,d\theta$ |
| **General 2D** | $x=x(u,v), y=y(u,v)$ | $\frac{\partial(x,y)}{\partial(u,v)}$ | $|J|\,du\,dv$ |
| **General 3D** | $x=x(u,v,w), \dots$ | $\frac{\partial(x,y,z)}{\partial(u,v,w)}$ | $|J|\,du\,dv\,dw$ |
