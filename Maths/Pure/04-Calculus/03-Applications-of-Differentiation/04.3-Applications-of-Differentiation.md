
## Definition

Using derivatives to solve real problems: **optimisation** (best values), **related rates** (linked changes), **curve shape** (monotonicity, convexity), **tangents/normals**, and **approximation** (Newton's method, linearisation).

## The Intuition

Hiking up a mountain — the derivative tells you the steepness right where you stand. Slope zero → a peak, a valley, or a flat ridge. Optimisation is finding the highest peak or lowest valley by following the slope information.

## The Toolkit

| Result | Formula | Use for |
|--------|---------|---------|
| Tangent | $y - y_0 = f'(x_0)(x-x_0)$ | line touching a curve |
| Normal | $y - y_0 = -\frac{1}{f'(x_0)}(x-x_0)$ | perpendicular to tangent |
| Rolle's Theorem | $f'(c) = 0$ | $f(a)=f(b)$, continuous/differentiable |
| MVT (Lagrange) | $f'(c) = \frac{f(b)-f(a)}{b-a}$ | average vs instantaneous rate |
| Newton's Method | $x_{n+1} = x_n - \frac{f(x_n)}{f'(x_n)}$ | numerical root finding |
| Second derivative test | $f''(c) > 0$ → min, $f''(c) < 0$ → max | classify critical points |

## Derivation

Tangent/normal come from the line equations with slope $f'(x_0)$. Newton's method: the tangent at $x_n$ crosses the axis at $x_{n+1}$ — from $0 = f(x_n) + f'(x_n)(x_{n+1} - x_n)$. MVT is Rolle's Theorem applied after a linear adjustment. [Full derivations: 04.3-Applications-of-Differentiation-Proofs]


**Optimisation:**
1. Express the quantity to optimise as a function of **one** variable (use the constraint).
2. Find critical points: $f'(x) = 0$ (and where $f'$ is undefined).
3. Classify with $f''$; on a closed interval also **check the endpoints**.

**Related rates:**
1. Write the geometric relationship (e.g. $x^2 + y^2 = 25$).
2. Differentiate **implicitly with respect to $t$**.
3. Substitute known values and rates; solve for the unknown rate.

**Newton's method:** iterate $x_{n+1} = x_n - f(x_n)/f'(x_n)$ from a good initial guess; stops when successive values agree to the required precision.

## Worked Examples

**Setup:** A 5 m ladder slides down a wall. How fast does the top descend when the bottom is 3 m from the wall and moving at 1 m/s?

**Solution:** $x^2 + y^2 = 25$. Differentiate: $2x\frac{dx}{dt} + 2y\frac{dy}{dt} = 0$. At $x=3$, $y=4$: $2(3)(1) + 2(4)\frac{dy}{dt} = 0$, so $\frac{dy}{dt} = -0.75$ m/s (descending).

**Key insight:** Differentiate the geometric relationship with respect to time, substitute, solve.

---

**Setup:** Use Newton's method to approximate $\sqrt{2}$.

**Solution:** $f(x) = x^2 - 2$, $f'(x) = 2x$, $x_{n+1} = \frac{x_n^2+2}{2x_n}$. From $x_0 = 1$: $x_1 = 1.5$, $x_2 \approx 1.4167$, $x_3 \approx 1.4142$.

**Key insight:** Newton's method converges fast but needs a good initial guess and $f'(x) \neq 0$.

## Common Traps

- Forgetting to check **endpoints** on closed intervals
- Second derivative test inconclusive when $f''(c) = 0$ — use a sign table
- Related rates: substituting values *before* differentiating (treat them as constants and the derivative is wrong)
- Dividing by zero in Newton's method ($f'(x_n) = 0$)
- Confusing maximum of $f$ with maximum of $f'$
