Vector calculus extends differentiation and integration to vector fields, providing tools (gradient, divergence, curl) and theorems (Green's, Stokes', Divergence) that connect local behaviour to global quantities. It is the mathematical language of fluid dynamics, electromagnetism, and gravitation. Maxwell's equations, the heat equation, and the wave equation are all expressed in vector calculus notation.

**The Intuition:** Imagine a fluid flowing through space. The velocity field assigns a velocity vector to each point. Divergence tells you where fluid is being created (source) or destroyed (sink). Curl tells you where the fluid is spinning (vorticity). The gradient of pressure tells you which way the fluid wants to flow.

**The Math:** Gradient: $\nabla f = \frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\mathbf{i} + \frac{\partial f}{\partial y}\mathbf{j} + \frac{\partial f}{\partial z}\mathbf{k}$ (scalar $\to$ vector). Divergence: $\nabla \cdot \mathbf{F} = \frac{\partial P}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial Q}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial R}{\partial z}$ (vector $\to$ scalar). Curl: $\nabla \times \mathbf{F}$ (vector $\to$ vector). Laplacian: $\nabla^2 f = \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x^2} + \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial y^2} + \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial z^2}$.

Fundamental theorems: Green's Theorem: $\oint_C P\,dx + Q\,dy = \iint_D (Q_x - P_y)\,dA$ (2D). Stokes' Theorem: $\oint_C \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r} = \iint_S (\nabla \times \mathbf{F}) \cdot d\mathbf{S}$ (3D surface). Divergence Theorem: $\iiint_V \nabla \cdot \mathbf{F}\,dV = \oiint_S \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{S}$ (3D volume).

Conservative field: $\nabla \times \mathbf{F} = \mathbf{0}$ (in simply connected domain) $\iff$ $\mathbf{F} = \nabla f$ for some $f$.

**What does this mean for Pure Mathematics?** The major theorems connect local differential information (what happens at each point) to global integral information (what happens over a region, surface, or volume). They are the multivariable Fundamental Theorem of Calculus.

**Setup:** Is $\mathbf{F} = (2xy + z^2)\mathbf{i} + (x^2 + 2yz)\mathbf{j} + (2xz + y^2)\mathbf{k}$ conservative? **Solution:** Check $\nabla \times \mathbf{F}$: $\frac{\partial R}{\partial y} = 2y = \frac{\partial Q}{\partial z}$, $\frac{\partial P}{\partial z} = 2z = \frac{\partial R}{\partial x}$, $\frac{\partial Q}{\partial x} = 2x = \frac{\partial P}{\partial y}$. All equal, so $\nabla \times \mathbf{F} = \mathbf{0}$ — conservative. **Key insight:** Check all three curl conditions. If all are satisfied and the domain is simply connected, the field is conservative.

**Setup:** Evaluate $\oiint_S \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{S}$ where $\mathbf{F} = x\mathbf{i} + y\mathbf{j} + z\mathbf{k}$ and $S$ is the sphere $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = a^2$. **Solution:** $\nabla \cdot \mathbf{F} = 3$. By divergence theorem: $\iiint_V 3\,dV = 3 \cdot \frac{4}{3}\pi a^3 = 4\pi a^3$. **Key insight:** The divergence theorem says total flux out of a closed surface equals the integral of divergence over the enclosed volume.
