
## Definition

Vector calculus extends differentiation and integration to **vector fields**:

- **Gradient:** $\nabla f = \frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\mathbf{i} + \frac{\partial f}{\partial y}\mathbf{j} + \frac{\partial f}{\partial z}\mathbf{k}$ (scalar → vector)
- **Divergence:** $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{F} = \frac{\partial P}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial Q}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial R}{\partial z}$ (vector → scalar)
- **Curl:** $\nabla\times\mathbf{F}$ (vector → vector)
- **Laplacian:** $\nabla^2 f = \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x^2} + \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial y^2} + \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial z^2}$

The fundamental theorems (Green, Stokes, Divergence) connect **local** behaviour to **global** quantities.

## The Intuition

Fluid flowing through space: the velocity field assigns a velocity vector to each point. **Divergence** says where fluid is created (source) or destroyed (sink); **curl** says where it's spinning; the gradient of pressure says which way it wants to flow.

## The Toolkit

| Theorem | Statement | Dimension |
|---------|-----------|-----------|
| Green's | $\oint_C P\,dx + Q\,dy = \iint_D (Q_x - P_y)\,dA$ | 2D |
| Stokes' | $\oint_C \mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r} = \iint_S (\nabla\times\mathbf{F})\cdot d\mathbf{S}$ | 3D surface |
| Divergence | $\iiint_V \nabla\cdot\mathbf{F}\,dV = \oiint_S \mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{S}$ | 3D volume |
| Conservative field | $\nabla\times\mathbf{F} = \mathbf{0} \iff \mathbf{F} = \nabla f$ | simply-connected domain |

## Derivation

Each theorem is the multivariable **Fundamental Theorem of Calculus**: the integral of a derivative over the interior equals the boundary evaluation. Green: $\oint$ boundary = $\iint$ curl (2D). Stokes: surface curl integrated = circulation around the boundary. Divergence: flux through a closed surface = divergence integrated over the volume. [Full derivations: 04.10-Vector-Calculus-Proofs]

## Method

1. **Conservative field check:** verify the three curl conditions $\frac{\partial P}{\partial y} = \frac{\partial Q}{\partial x}$, etc. If all hold on a simply-connected domain, $\mathbf{F} = \nabla f$ — line integrals become path-independent.
2. **Flux through a closed surface:** the Divergence theorem — compute $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{F}$, integrate over the enclosed volume.
3. **Circulation around a closed curve:** Stokes' theorem — integrate $(\nabla\times\mathbf{F})$ over a spanning surface.
4. **Pick the theorem that matches the data:** boundary vs interior integrals.

## Worked Examples

**Setup:** Is $\mathbf{F} = (2xy+z^2)\mathbf{i} + (x^2+2yz)\mathbf{j} + (2xz+y^2)\mathbf{k}$ conservative?

**Solution:** Check curl: $\frac{\partial R}{\partial y} = 2y = \frac{\partial Q}{\partial z}$, $\frac{\partial P}{\partial z} = 2z = \frac{\partial R}{\partial x}$, $\frac{\partial Q}{\partial x} = 2x = \frac{\partial P}{\partial y}$. All hold → $\nabla\times\mathbf{F} = \mathbf{0}$ → conservative.

**Key insight:** Check all three curl conditions; all satisfied + simply-connected ⇒ conservative.

---

**Setup:** $\oiint_S \mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{S}$ with $\mathbf{F} = x\mathbf{i} + y\mathbf{j} + z\mathbf{k}$ over the sphere $x^2+y^2+z^2 = a^2$.

**Solution:** $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{F} = 3$. Divergence theorem: $\iiint_V 3\,dV = 3\cdot\frac43\pi a^3 = 4\pi a^3$.

**Key insight:** Total flux out of a closed surface = integral of divergence over the enclosed volume.

## Common Traps

- Using Green/Stokes on the wrong side of the equation (boundary vs interior)
- Conservative-field test in a domain with holes (need simply connected)
- Sign/orientation errors — the boundary orientation matters for the theorems
- Computing $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{F}$ vs $\nabla\times\mathbf{F}$ — scalar vs vector results
- Forgetting the unit normal in surface integrals
