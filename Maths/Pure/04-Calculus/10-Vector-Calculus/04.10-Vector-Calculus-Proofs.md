### 1.1 Curl of Gradient
$$\nabla \times (\nabla f) = \mathbf{0}$$
**Proof:**
$$\nabla \times (\nabla f) = \begin{vmatrix} \mathbf{i} & \mathbf{j} & \mathbf{k} \\ \partial_x & \partial_y & \partial_z \\ f_x & f_y & f_z \end{vmatrix} = (f_{zy} - f_{yz})\mathbf{i} + (f_{xz} - f_{zx})\mathbf{j} + (f_{yx} - f_{xy})\mathbf{k} = \mathbf{0}$$
by equality of mixed partials (Clairaut's theorem).

### 1.2 Divergence of Curl
$$\nabla \cdot (\nabla \times \mathbf{F}) = 0$$
**Proof:**
$$\nabla \cdot (\nabla \times \mathbf{F}) = \partial_x(R_y - Q_z) + \partial_y(P_z - R_x) + \partial_z(Q_x - P_y)$$
$$= R_{yx} - Q_{zx} + P_{zy} - R_{xy} + Q_{xz} - P_{yz} = 0$$

### 1.3 Product Rules
| Identity | Proof Sketch |
|----------|-------------|
| $\nabla(fg) = f\nabla g + g\nabla f$ | Component-wise product rule |
| $\nabla \cdot (f\mathbf{F}) = f\nabla \cdot \mathbf{F} + \nabla f \cdot \mathbf{F}$ | $\partial_x(fP) = f_x P + f P_x$, sum over components |
| $\nabla \times (f\mathbf{F}) = f\nabla \times \mathbf{F} + \nabla f \times \mathbf{F}$ | Expand determinant, group terms |
| $\nabla \cdot (\mathbf{F} \times \mathbf{G}) = \mathbf{G} \cdot (\nabla \times \mathbf{F}) - \mathbf{F} \cdot (\nabla \times \mathbf{G})$ | Expand $\partial_i(\epsilon_{ijk}F_j G_k)$ |
| $\nabla \times (\mathbf{F} \times \mathbf{G}) = \mathbf{F}(\nabla \cdot \mathbf{G}) - \mathbf{G}(\nabla \cdot \mathbf{F}) + (\mathbf{G} \cdot \nabla)\mathbf{F} - (\mathbf{F} \cdot \nabla)\mathbf{G}$ | Use vector triple product and product rule |

### 1.4 Double Curl Identity
$$\nabla \times (\nabla \times \mathbf{F}) = \nabla(\nabla \cdot \mathbf{F}) - \nabla^2 \mathbf{F}$$
**Proof (index notation):**
$(\nabla \times (\nabla \times \mathbf{F}))_i = \epsilon_{ijk} \partial_j (\nabla \times \mathbf{F})_k = \epsilon_{ijk} \partial_j \epsilon_{klm} \partial_l F_m$
Using $\epsilon_{ijk}\epsilon_{klm} = \delta_{il}\delta_{jm} - \delta_{im}\delta_{jl}$:
$$= \partial_j \partial_i F_j - \partial_j \partial_j F_i = \partial_i (\partial_j F_j) - \partial_j^2 F_i = (\nabla(\nabla \cdot \mathbf{F}))_i - (\nabla^2 \mathbf{F})_i$$

### 2.1 Characterisation
In simply connected domain $D \subseteq \mathbb{R}^3$, for $\mathbf{F} \in C^1$:
$$\mathbf{F} = \nabla \phi \iff \nabla \times \mathbf{F} = \mathbf{0} \iff \oint_C \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r} = 0 \text{ for all closed } C$$

**Proof:**
$(\Rightarrow)$ If $\mathbf{F} = \nabla \phi$, then $\nabla \times \mathbf{F} = \nabla \times \nabla \phi = \mathbf{0}$.
Also $\oint_C \nabla \phi \cdot d\mathbf{r} = \phi(\text{end}) - \phi(\text{start}) = 0$ for closed $C$.

$(\Leftarrow)$ Assume $\oint_C \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r} = 0$ for all closed $C$. Fix $x_0 \in D$. Define:
$$\phi(x) = \int_{x_0}^x \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r}$$
Path independence follows from $\oint = 0$. Then $\nabla \phi = \mathbf{F}$ by FTC.

### 3.1 Statement
Let $D$ be a bounded region in $\mathbb{R}^2$ with piecewise smooth boundary $C$, oriented counterclockwise. If $P, Q \in C^1$ on $D \cup C$:
$$\oint_C P\,dx + Q\,dy = \iint_D \left( \frac{\partial Q}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial P}{\partial y} \right) dA$$

### 3.2 Proof
For Type I region $D = \{(x,y): a \le x \le b, g_1(x) \le y \le g_2(x)\}$:
$$\iint_D \frac{\partial Q}{\partial x} dA = \int_a^b \int_{g_1(x)}^{g_2(x)} Q_x dy dx = \int_a^b [Q(x, g_2(x)) - Q(x, g_1(x))] dx$$
This equals $\oint_C Q dy$ (right and left boundaries contribute).

Similarly $\iint_D -\frac{\partial P}{\partial y} dA = \oint_C P dx$.
Add the two.

For general regions, partition into Type I and Type II pieces. Internal boundaries cancel.

### 4.1 Statement
Let $S$ be an oriented piecewise smooth surface with boundary curve $C = \partial S$ (positive orientation = right-hand rule). If $\mathbf{F} \in C^1$ on $S$:
$$\oint_C \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r} = \iint_S (\nabla \times \mathbf{F}) \cdot d\mathbf{S}$$

### 4.2 Proof
Parametrise $S$ by $\mathbf{r}(u,v)$ for $(u,v) \in D \subset \mathbb{R}^2$.
Left side:
$$\oint_C \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r} = \oint_{\partial D} \mathbf{F}(\mathbf{r}(u,v)) \cdot \left( \frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial u} du + \frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial v} dv \right)$$
$$= \oint_{\partial D} \left( \mathbf{F} \cdot \frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial u} \right) du + \left( \mathbf{F} \cdot \frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial v} \right) dv$$

Apply Green's Theorem in $uv$-plane:
$$= \iint_D \left[ \frac{\partial}{\partial u} \left( \mathbf{F} \cdot \frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial v} \right) - \frac{\partial}{\partial v} \left( \mathbf{F} \cdot \frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial u} \right) \right] du\,dv$$

Right side:
$$\iint_S (\nabla \times \mathbf{F}) \cdot d\mathbf{S} = \iint_D (\nabla \times \mathbf{F})(\mathbf{r}(u,v)) \cdot \left( \frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial u} \times \frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial v} \right) du\,dv$$

Using vector identity:
$$\frac{\partial}{\partial u} \left( \mathbf{F} \cdot \frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial v} \right) - \frac{\partial}{\partial v} \left( \mathbf{F} \cdot \frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial u} \right) = (\nabla \times \mathbf{F}) \cdot \left( \frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial u} \times \frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial v} \right)$$
Both sides match.

### 5.1 Statement
Let $V$ be a solid region with piecewise smooth boundary $S = \partial V$, outward normal $\mathbf{n}$. If $\mathbf{F} \in C^1$ on $V \cup S$:
$$\iiint_V \nabla \cdot \mathbf{F} \, dV = \iint_S \mathbf{F} \cdot \mathbf{n} \, dS = \iint_S \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{S}$$

### 5.2 Proof (Rectangular Box)
Let $V = [a,b] \times [c,d] \times [e,f]$.
$$\iiint_V \frac{\partial P}{\partial x} dV = \int_c^d \int_e^f \int_a^b \frac{\partial P}{\partial x} dx dy dz = \int_c^d \int_e^f [P(b,y,z) - P(a,y,z)] dy dz$$
$$= \iint_{x=b} P dy dz - \iint_{x=a} P dy dz = \iint_{\text{right}} \mathbf{F} \cdot \mathbf{n} dS + \iint_{\text{left}} \mathbf{F} \cdot \mathbf{n} dS$$

Similarly for $Q_y$ and $R_z$. Summing gives total flux through all six faces.

### 5.3 General Proof
Decompose $V$ into type I, II, III regions (z-simple, y-simple, x-simple).
Internal boundaries have opposite normals, so their contributions cancel.
Take limit of fine partition.

### 6.1 Scale Factors
For transformation $x = x(u_1,u_2,u_3)$, etc.:
$$h_i = \left\| \frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial u_i} \right\|$$
Unit vectors: $\hat{\mathbf{e}}_i = \frac{1}{h_i} \frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial u_i}$

### 6.2 Gradient
$$\nabla f = \frac{1}{h_1} \frac{\partial f}{\partial u_1} \hat{\mathbf{e}}_1 + \frac{1}{h_2} \frac{\partial f}{\partial u_2} \hat{\mathbf{e}}_2 + \frac{1}{h_3} \frac{\partial f}{\partial u_3} \hat{\mathbf{e}}_3$$

### 6.3 Divergence
$$\nabla \cdot \mathbf{F} = \frac{1}{h_1 h_2 h_3} \left[ \frac{\partial}{\partial u_1}(h_2 h_3 F_1) + \frac{\partial}{\partial u_2}(h_3 h_1 F_2) + \frac{\partial}{\partial u_3}(h_1 h_2 F_3) \right]$$

**Proof:** Volume element $dV = h_1 h_2 h_3 du_1 du_2 du_3$. Apply divergence theorem to small box $[u_i, u_i+du_i]$.

### 6.4 Curl
$$\nabla \times \mathbf{F} = \frac{1}{h_1 h_2 h_3} \begin{vmatrix}
h_1 \hat{\mathbf{e}}_1 & h_2 \hat{\mathbf{e}}_2 & h_3 \hat{\mathbf{e}}_3 \\
\frac{\partial}{\partial u_1} & \frac{\partial}{\partial u_2} & \frac{\partial}{\partial u_3} \\
h_1 F_1 & h_2 F_2 & h_3 F_3
\end{vmatrix}$$

### 6.5 Cylindrical Coordinates
$u_1=r, u_2=\theta, u_3=z$; $h_r=1, h_\theta=r, h_z=1$

| Operator | Formula |
|----------|---------|
| $\nabla f$ | $\frac{\partial f}{\partial r}\hat{\mathbf{r}} + \frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial f}{\partial \theta}\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}} + \frac{\partial f}{\partial z}\hat{\mathbf{z}}$ |
| $\nabla \cdot \mathbf{F}$ | $\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}(r F_r) + \frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial F_\theta}{\partial \theta} + \frac{\partial F_z}{\partial z}$ |
| $\nabla \times \mathbf{F}$ | $\frac{1}{r} \begin{vmatrix} \hat{\mathbf{r}} & r\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}} & \hat{\mathbf{z}} \\ \frac{\partial}{\partial r} & \frac{\partial}{\partial \theta} & \frac{\partial}{\partial z} \\ F_r & r F_\theta & F_z \end{vmatrix}$ |

### 6.6 Spherical Coordinates
$u_1=\rho, u_2=\phi, u_3=\theta$; $h_\rho=1, h_\phi=\rho, h_\theta=\rho\sin\phi$

| Operator | Formula |
|----------|---------|
| $\nabla f$ | $\frac{\partial f}{\partial \rho}\hat{\boldsymbol{\rho}} + \frac{1}{\rho}\frac{\partial f}{\partial \phi}\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}} + \frac{1}{\rho\sin\phi}\frac{\partial f}{\partial \theta}\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}$ |
| $\nabla \cdot \mathbf{F}$ | $\frac{1}{\rho^2}\frac{\partial}{\partial \rho}(\rho^2 F_\rho) + \frac{1}{\rho\sin\phi}\frac{\partial}{\partial \phi}(\sin\phi F_\phi) + \frac{1}{\rho\sin\phi}\frac{\partial F_\theta}{\partial \theta}$ |
| $\nabla \times \mathbf{F}$ | $\frac{1}{\rho^2\sin\phi} \begin{vmatrix} \hat{\boldsymbol{\rho}} & \rho\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}} & \rho\sin\phi\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}} \\ \frac{\partial}{\partial \rho} & \frac{\partial}{\partial \phi} & \frac{\partial}{\partial \theta} \\ F_\rho & \rho F_\phi & \rho\sin\phi F_\theta \end{vmatrix}$ |

### 7.1 Surface Element
For $\mathbf{r}(u,v)$:
$$d\mathbf{S} = \left( \frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial u} \times \frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial v} \right) du dv = \mathbf{n} \|\mathbf{r}_u \times \mathbf{r}_v\| du dv$$

**Proof:** $\mathbf{r}_u du$ and $\mathbf{r}_v dv$ span tangent parallelogram. Area = $\|\mathbf{r}_u \times \mathbf{r}_v\| du dv$. Normal = cross product direction.

### 7.2 Arc Length Element
$$\mathbf{r}(t) = \langle x(t), y(t), z(t) \rangle$$
$$ds = \|\mathbf{r}'(t)\| dt, \quad d\mathbf{r} = \mathbf{r}'(t) dt$$

### 8.1 First Identity
$$\int_V (u \nabla^2 v + \nabla u \cdot \nabla v) dV = \oint_S u \frac{\partial v}{\partial n} dS$$

**Proof:** $\nabla \cdot (u \nabla v) = \nabla u \cdot \nabla v + u \nabla^2 v$. Apply Divergence Theorem.

### 8.2 Second Identity
$$\int_V (u \nabla^2 v - v \nabla^2 u) dV = \oint_S \left( u \frac{\partial v}{\partial n} - v \frac{\partial u}{\partial n} \right) dS$$

**Proof:** Apply first identity to $u \nabla^2 v$ and $v \nabla^2 u$, subtract.

### 8.3 Uniqueness of Laplace's Equation
If $\nabla^2 \phi = 0$ in $V$ with $\phi$ or $\partial \phi/\partial n$ given on $\partial V$, solution is unique.

**Proof:** Let $\phi_1, \phi_2$ be two solutions. Set $\psi = \phi_1 - \phi_2$. Then $\nabla^2 \psi = 0$, $\psi = 0$ or $\partial \psi/\partial n = 0$ on $\partial V$.
By first identity: $\int_V \|\nabla \psi\|^2 dV = \oint_S \psi \frac{\partial \psi}{\partial n} dS = 0$.
Thus $\nabla \psi = 0 \Rightarrow \psi = \text{constant} = 0$.

### 9.1 Existence
If $\nabla \cdot \mathbf{F} = 0$ on simply connected domain, then $\exists \mathbf{A}$ such that $\mathbf{F} = \nabla \times \mathbf{A}$.

### 9.2 Construction (Poincaré Lemma)
$$\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{x}) = \int_0^1 \mathbf{F}(t\mathbf{x}) \times \mathbf{x} t dt$$

**Proof:** Compute $\nabla \times \mathbf{A}$:
$$(\nabla \times \mathbf{A})_i = \epsilon_{ijk} \partial_j A_k = \epsilon_{ijk} \partial_j \int_0^1 \epsilon_{klm} F_l(tx) x_m t dt$$
$$= \int_0^1 (\delta_{il}\delta_{jm} - \delta_{im}\delta_{jl}) \partial_j F_l(tx) x_m t dt$$
Using $\nabla \cdot \mathbf{F} = 0$ and chain rule, simplifies to $F_i(\mathbf{x})$.

### 10.1 Maxwell's Equations (Differential Form)
From integral forms using Stokes/Divergence Theorems:

| Integral Form | Differential Form |
|--------------|------------------|
| $\oiint_S \mathbf{E} \cdot d\mathbf{S} = Q/\varepsilon_0$ | $\nabla \cdot \mathbf{E} = \rho/\varepsilon_0$ |
| $\oiint_S \mathbf{B} \cdot d\mathbf{S} = 0$ | $\nabla \cdot \mathbf{B} = 0$ |
| $\oint_C \mathbf{E} \cdot d\mathbf{r} = -d\Phi_B/dt$ | $\nabla \times \mathbf{E} = -\partial \mathbf{B}/\partial t$ |
| $\oint_C \mathbf{B} \cdot d\mathbf{r} = \mu_0 I + \mu_0\varepsilon_0 d\Phi_E/dt$ | $\nabla \times \mathbf{B} = \mu_0 \mathbf{J} + \mu_0\varepsilon_0 \partial \mathbf{E}/\partial t$ |

### 10.2 Fluid Dynamics
- $\nabla \cdot \mathbf{v} = 0$: Incompressible flow
- $\nabla \times \mathbf{v} = \mathbf{0}$: Irrotational (potential) flow
- $\mathbf{v} = \nabla \phi$ for potential flow
- $\nabla^2 \phi = 0$ for incompressible potential flow
