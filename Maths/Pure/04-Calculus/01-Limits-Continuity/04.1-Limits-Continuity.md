
## Definition

$$\lim_{x \to a} f(x) = L \iff f(x) \text{ can be made arbitrarily close to } L \text{ by taking } x \text{ close to } a \ (x \neq a)$$

Formally (ε–δ): for every $\varepsilon > 0$ there exists $\delta > 0$ such that $0 < |x-a| < \delta \Rightarrow |f(x) - L| < \varepsilon$.

**One-sided limits:** $\lim_{x\to a^+}f(x)$ (right) and $\lim_{x\to a^-}f(x)$ (left). The two-sided limit exists **iff both one-sided limits exist and agree**.

**Continuity at $a$** requires all three: $f(a)$ defined, $\lim_{x\to a}f(x)$ exists, and $\lim_{x\to a}f(x) = f(a)$.

## The Intuition

A limit is *guessing where a function is heading without arriving*. Walking toward a wall: you get infinitely close but never touch — yet you can clearly predict where you're heading. Continuity means the graph doesn't jump or vanish at that point. Every derivative and integral is built on this idea.

## The Toolkit

| Result | Formula | Valid when |
|--------|---------|-----------|
| Standard limit | $\lim_{x\to 0}\dfrac{\sin x}{x} = 1$ | radians |
| Standard limit | $\lim_{x\to 0}\dfrac{e^x - 1}{x} = 1$ | — |
| Standard limit | $\lim_{x\to\infty}\left(1+\dfrac{1}{x}\right)^x = e$ | — |
| L'Hôpital's Rule | $\lim\dfrac{f(x)}{g(x)} = \lim\dfrac{f'(x)}{g'(x)}$ | form $0/0$ or $\infty/\infty$, right side exists |
| Squeeze theorem | $g \le f \le h$, $\lim g = \lim h = L \Rightarrow \lim f = L$ | bounding functions |

## Derivation

The ε–δ definition is the precise version of "arbitrarily close." The standard limits follow from geometry (the unit circle sector argument for $\sin x/x$) or from definitions ($e^x$'s limit is the definition of $e$). L'Hôpital's Rule is a consequence of the Mean Value Theorem applied to numerator and denominator. [Full derivations: 04.1-Limits-Continuity-Proofs]

## Method

1. **Try substitution** first — if you get a number, that's the limit.
2. **$0/0$ from a rational function:** factorise and cancel (avoids L'Hôpital).
3. **Trig/exp forms:** manipulate to isolate the standard limits (multiply top/bottom by the angle, etc.).
4. **L'Hôpital:** only for $0/0$ or $\infty/\infty$; re-apply while the form stays indeterminate.
5. **Check one-sided limits** at piecewise boundaries and discontinuities.

## Worked Examples

**Setup:** Evaluate $\lim_{x\to 2}\frac{x^3-8}{x^2-4}$.

**Solution:** Factorise: $\frac{(x-2)(x^2+2x+4)}{(x-2)(x+2)} = \frac{x^2+2x+4}{x+2}$, so the limit is $\frac{12}{4} = 3$.

**Key insight:** Factorisation first — it avoids L'Hôpital entirely.

---

**Setup:** Evaluate $\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{\sin 5x}{\sin 2x}$.

**Solution:** $\frac{5x}{2x}\cdot\frac{\sin 5x}{5x}\cdot\frac{2x}{\sin 2x} = \frac52 \cdot 1 \cdot 1 = \frac52$.

**Key insight:** Manipulate to isolate known standard limits.

---

**Setup:** Evaluate $\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{e^{2x}-1-2x}{x^2}$.

**Solution:** $0/0$. L'Hôpital: $\lim\frac{2e^{2x}-2}{2x}$ — still $0/0$. Again: $\lim\frac{4e^{2x}}{2} = 2$.

**Key insight:** Re-apply L'Hôpital as long as the form remains indeterminate.

## Common Traps

- Using L'Hôpital on forms that aren't $0/0$ or $\infty/\infty$ (e.g. $1^\infty$ needs logarithms first)
- $\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{\sin x}{x} = 1$ requires **radians** — degrees break it
- Forgetting one-sided limits at corners/piecewise points
- Continuity needs all three conditions — one missing means discontinuous
- Dividing by a limit that's zero
