
## Definition

The derivative is the **instantaneous rate of change** — the slope of the tangent:

$$f'(x) = \lim_{h\to 0}\frac{f(x+h) - f(x)}{h}$$

The difference quotient is the slope of a secant; as $h \to 0$ the secant becomes the tangent. Positive slope → increasing, negative → decreasing, zero → stationary.

## The Intuition

Your car's speedometer doesn't show average speed over the trip — it shows your speed *right now*. The derivative is that instant speed: you compute it by shrinking the time interval smaller and smaller.

## The Toolkit

| Rule | Formula |
|------|---------|
| Power rule | $\frac{d}{dx}x^n = nx^{n-1}$ |
| Product rule | $(fg)' = f'g + fg'$ |
| Quotient rule | $\left(\frac{f}{g}\right)' = \frac{f'g - fg'}{g^2}$ |
| Chain rule | $\frac{d}{dx}f(g(x)) = f'(g(x))\,g'(x)$ |
| Exponential | $\frac{d}{dx}e^x = e^x$ |
| Natural log | $\frac{d}{dx}\ln x = \frac{1}{x}$ |
| Trig | $\frac{d}{dx}\sin x = \cos x$, $\frac{d}{dx}\cos x = -\sin x$, $\frac{d}{dx}\tan x = \sec^2 x$ |
| Mean Value Theorem | $f'(c) = \frac{f(b)-f(a)}{b-a}$ for some $c \in (a,b)$ |
| Tangent at $x=a$ | $y - f(a) = f'(a)(x-a)$ |
| Normal at $x=a$ | $y - f(a) = -\frac{1}{f'(a)}(x-a)$ |

## Derivation

Definition: the difference quotient with $h \to 0$. Power rule: expand $(x+h)^n$ and cancel $x^n$. Product rule: add and subtract $f(x+h)g(x)$. Chain rule: cancel $g(x+h) - g(x)$ in the quotient (with care). All other rules follow by algebra on these. [Full derivations: 04.2-Differentiation-Proofs]

## Method

1. **Pick the outermost operation** — that decides the first rule (product, quotient, or chain).
2. **Chain rule:** differentiate outside, keep inside, multiply by inside's derivative.
3. **Implicit differentiation:** every $y$ term gets a $\cdot\frac{dy}{dx}$ factor; then solve.
4. **Logarithmic differentiation:** for $f(x)^{g(x)}$ or heavy products — take $\ln$, differentiate, solve for $y'$.

## Worked Examples

**Setup:** Differentiate $y = x^2\sin(x^3)$.

**Solution:** Product rule: $y' = 2x\sin(x^3) + x^2\cos(x^3)\cdot 3x^2 = 2x\sin(x^3) + 3x^4\cos(x^3)$.

**Key insight:** Product rule first, then chain rule on the composite factor.

---

**Setup:** Find $\frac{dy}{dx}$ for $x^2 + xy + y^2 = 7$.

**Solution:** $2x + y + x\frac{dy}{dx} + 2y\frac{dy}{dx} = 0$, so $\frac{dy}{dx} = -\frac{2x+y}{x+2y}$.

**Key insight:** Treat $y$ as a function of $x$; every $y$ term gets a $\cdot\frac{dy}{dx}$ factor.

---

**Setup:** Differentiate $y = x^x$ ($x > 0$).

**Solution:** $\ln y = x\ln x$. Then $\frac{1}{y}y' = \ln x + 1$, so $y' = x^x(\ln x + 1)$.

**Key insight:** Logarithmic differentiation turns exponents into products and products into sums.

## Common Traps

- Chain rule forgotten on composites (the #1 mistake)
- Quotient rule sign error — $f'g - fg'$, not $f'g + fg'$
- Implicit: missing the $\frac{dy}{dx}$ factor on $y$-terms
- $y = x^x$ — the power rule doesn't apply (the exponent is a variable); use logs
- The derivative of $|x|$ doesn't exist at $0$
