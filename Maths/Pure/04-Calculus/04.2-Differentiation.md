Differentiation finds the rate of change of a function at any point — the slope of the tangent line to the curve. It is the engine behind optimisation, motion analysis, and nearly every applied maths problem that involves change. The derivative $f'(x)$ tells you the slope of the tangent line at any point on the curve. Positive slope means the function is increasing; negative means decreasing; zero means a stationary point.

**The Intuition:** Imagine driving a car. Your speedometer does not show your average speed over the whole trip — it shows your speed right now, at this instant. The derivative is that instant speed. You compute it by looking at what happens over smaller and smaller time intervals.

**The Math:** $f'(x) = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x+h) - f(x)}{h}$. The difference quotient is the slope of a secant line through two nearby points. As $h$ shrinks to zero, the secant becomes the tangent.

Basic rules: power rule $\frac{d}{dx} x^n = nx^{n-1}$, product rule $(fg)' = f'g + fg'$, quotient rule $(f/g)' = \frac{f'g - fg'}{g^2}$, chain rule $\frac{d}{dx} f(g(x)) = f'(g(x)) \cdot g'(x)$. Standard derivatives: $\frac{d}{dx} e^x = e^x$, $\frac{d}{dx} \sin x = \cos x$, $\frac{d}{dx} \cos x = -\sin x$, $\frac{d}{dx} \tan x = \sec^2 x$, $\frac{d}{dx} \ln x = 1/x$.

Mean Value Theorem: $f'(c) = \frac{f(b) - f(a)}{b - a}$ for some $c \in (a,b)$. Tangent at $x = a$: $y - f(a) = f'(a)(x - a)$. Normal at $x = a$: $y - f(a) = -\frac{1}{f'(a)}(x - a)$.

**What does this mean for Pure Mathematics?** The rules of differentiation are mechanical recipes that let you compute derivatives without going back to the limit definition every time. Learning these rules fluently is essential — the real skill is knowing which rule to apply and how to set up the problem.

**Setup:** Differentiate $y = x^2 \sin(x^3)$. **Solution:** Product rule: $y' = 2x \sin(x^3) + x^2 \cos(x^3) \cdot 3x^2 = 2x \sin(x^3) + 3x^4 \cos(x^3)$. **Key insight:** When a product contains a composite function, apply the product rule first, then the chain rule to the relevant factor.

**Setup:** Find $\frac{dy}{dx}$ for $x^2 + xy + y^2 = 7$. **Solution:** Differentiate: $2x + y + x\frac{dy}{dx} + 2y\frac{dy}{dx} = 0$. Solve: $\frac{dy}{dx} = -\frac{2x + y}{x + 2y}$. **Key insight:** Treat $y$ as a function of $x$ throughout. Every $y$ term gets a $\cdot \frac{dy}{dx}$ factor.

**Setup:** Differentiate $y = x^x$ for $x > 0$. **Solution:** Take $\ln$: $\ln y = x \ln x$. Differentiate: $\frac{1}{y} y' = \ln x + 1$. So $y' = x^x(\ln x + 1)$. **Key insight:** Logarithmic differentiation turns exponents into products and products into sums.
