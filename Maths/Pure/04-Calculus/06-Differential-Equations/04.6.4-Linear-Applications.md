# Applications of Linear Differential Equations

Linear differential equations model anything where a rate of change is proportional to the quantity itself (or its derivatives): **exponential growth/decay**, **Newton's cooling**, **simple harmonic motion**, and **RC/RL circuits**. Each application is just one of the linear patterns from the module wearing a real-world costume.

**The Intuition:** All four models share one structure — "how fast something changes depends linearly on how much of it there is." Population grows in proportion to population ($N' = kN$). A hot cup cools in proportion to how hot it is *above the room* ($T' = -k(T-T_s)$). A mass-spring system accelerates in proportion to how far it's displaced ($mx'' = -kx$). A capacitor charges in proportion to the voltage still missing. Learn the patterns once; recognize them everywhere.

## 1 — Exponential growth / decay

$$\frac{dN}{dt} = kN \implies N = N_0 e^{kt}$$

- $k > 0$: growth (populations, investments, bacteria)
- $k < 0$: decay (radioactive half-life, drug elimination)

**Half-life:** if $N(\tau) = N_0/2$, then $e^{k\tau} = \tfrac12 \Rightarrow \tau = \frac{\ln 2}{|k|}$.

**Setup:** A substance decays to 25% in 40 minutes. Find its half-life.

**Solution:** $N = N_0e^{kt}$, and $0.25 = e^{40k} \Rightarrow k = \frac{\ln 0.25}{40}$. Half-life $\tau = \frac{\ln 2}{|k|} = \frac{40\ln 2}{\ln 4} = 20$ minutes.

**Key insight:** 25% = two half-lives, so the half-life is half the 40 minutes — no logs needed once you see it.

## 2 — Newton's cooling

$$\frac{dT}{dt} = -k(T - T_s) \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad T = T_s + (T_0 - T_s)e^{-kt}$$

A first-order linear equation in $u = T - T_s$: $\frac{du}{dt} = -ku$, solved by the exponential pattern.

**Setup:** Coffee at $90^\circ$C cools to $70^\circ$C in 10 min in a $20^\circ$C room. When does it reach $50^\circ$C?

**Solution:** $T = 20 + 70e^{-kt}$. At $t=10$: $70 = 20 + 70e^{-10k} \Rightarrow e^{-10k} = \tfrac{5}{7}$. For $50^\circ$C: $30 = 70e^{-kt} \Rightarrow e^{-kt} = \tfrac{3}{7}$. Then $t = 10\cdot\frac{\ln(3/7)}{\ln(5/7)} \approx 27$ min.

**Key insight:** always measure the *gap* $T - T_s$, not the absolute temperature — that's the quantity that decays exponentially.

## 3 — Simple harmonic motion

$$m\frac{d^2x}{dt^2} = -kx \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad x'' + \omega^2 x = 0, \quad \omega = \sqrt{k/m}$$
$$x(t) = A\cos(\omega t) + B\sin(\omega t) \quad \text{or} \quad x(t) = R\cos(\omega t - \phi)$$

The complex-root case with purely imaginary roots $\pm i\omega$: pure oscillation at natural frequency $\omega$.

**Setup:** A 2 kg mass on a spring with $k = 8$ N/m is pulled 0.3 m and released from rest. Find $x(t)$.

**Solution:** $\omega = \sqrt{8/2} = 2$. $x = A\cos 2t + B\sin 2t$; $x(0) = 0.3 \Rightarrow A = 0.3$; $x'(0) = 0 \Rightarrow B = 0$. So
$$x(t) = 0.3\cos 2t$$

**Key insight:** "released from rest" kills the sine term — the phase $\phi = 0$. Initial displacement sets $A$, initial velocity sets $B$.

## 4 — RC circuit (charging)

$$R\frac{dQ}{dt} + \frac{Q}{C} = E \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad Q' + \frac{1}{RC}Q = \frac{E}{R}$$

A first-order linear equation with constant forcing — integrating factor $\mu = e^{t/(RC)}$:
$$Q(t) = CE\big(1 - e^{-t/(RC)}\big), \qquad I(t) = Q'(t) = \frac{E}{R}e^{-t/(RC)}$$

**Setup:** $R = 1\,\text{k}\Omega$, $C = 1\,\text{mF}$, $E = 10\,\text{V}$. How long to reach 95% charge?

**Solution:** $\tau = RC = 1\,\text{s}$. $Q = 10^{-2}(1 - e^{-t})$. Set $1 - e^{-t} = 0.95 \Rightarrow t = -\ln 0.05 \approx 3\,\text{s} = 3\tau$.

**Key insight:** the time constant $\tau = RC$ is the universal timescale — charging reaches $\sim 63\%$ at $t = \tau$, and is "complete" after about $5\tau$.

## Practice (try before peeking)

1. Which linear pattern fits "the rate a rumor spreads is proportional to the number who haven't heard it"?
2. A mass-spring has $m = 1$, $k = 9$: what's $\omega$ and the general solution?
3. In Newton's cooling, why use $T - T_s$ instead of $T$?

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. It's the *decay* pattern in disguise: if $N$ is the total population and $x$ haven't heard, then $dx/dt = k(N - x)$, i.e. $u = N - x$ satisfies $u' = -ku$ — exponential decay toward saturation. Same structure as Newton's cooling.
2. $\omega = \sqrt{k/m} = 3$, so $x = A\cos 3t + B\sin 3t$ (or $R\cos(3t - \phi)$).
3. Because the *gap* $u = T - T_s$ satisfies the clean exponential equation $u' = -ku$. The absolute temperature has a constant offset that just shifts the solution — measuring the gap isolates the actual dynamics.

</details>

---

**Common traps:**
- Confusing growth ($k>0$) with decay ($k<0$) — check the sign of the proportionality constant
- Using absolute values where the *difference from equilibrium* decays (cooling, rumors, charging)
- Forgetting $\omega = \sqrt{k/m}$ relates to mass — heavier mass → slower oscillation
- Mixing up $RC$ (charge time) with $L/R$ (current time in RL circuits)
