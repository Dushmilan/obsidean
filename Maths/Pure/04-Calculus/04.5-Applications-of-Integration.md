Using definite integrals to compute physical and geometric quantities: areas, volumes, arc lengths, surface areas, work, centroids, and moments of inertia. These are the most common real-world uses of integration in physics and engineering. Integration computes accumulation — when you accumulate height across a width, you get area; when you accumulate cross-sectional area across a length, you get volume.

**The Intuition:** Think of slicing a loaf of bread. Each slice has a small area. If you know the area of each slice and how thick each slice is, you can add up all the slices to get the total volume. Integration does exactly this with infinitely many infinitely thin slices.

**The Math:** Area between curves: $\int_a^b [f(x) - g(x)]\,dx$ (top minus bottom). Volume by discs: $\pi \int_a^b [f(x)]^2\,dx$. Volume by washers: $\pi \int_a^b ([f(x)]^2 - [g(x)]^2)\,dx$. Volume by shells: $2\pi \int_a^b x\,f(x)\,dx$. Arc length: $\int_a^b \sqrt{1 + [f'(x)]^2}\,dx$. Surface area: $2\pi \int_a^b f(x)\sqrt{1 + [f'(x)]^2}\,dx$. Work: $\int_a^b F(x)\,dx$. Centroid: $\bar{x} = \frac{1}{A}\int_a^b x[f(x) - g(x)]\,dx$.

Choosing volume method: disc/washer slices perpendicular to the axis of revolution; shells slice parallel. For revolution about the $y$-axis, shells avoid inverting the function.

**What does this mean for Pure Mathematics?** The key skill is setting up the integral correctly — choosing the right method, the right variable of integration, and the right limits. Once the integral is set up, evaluation is usually straightforward.

**Setup:** Find the area between $y = x^2$ and $y = 2x - x^2$. **Solution:** Intersections: $x^2 = 2x - x^2 \Rightarrow x = 0, 1$. On $[0,1]$: $2x - x^2 \geq x^2$. $A = \int_0^1 (2x - 2x^2)\,dx = [x^2 - \frac{2}{3}x^3]_0^1 = \frac{1}{3}$. **Key insight:** Always find intersection points first to determine limits and which function is on top.

**Setup:** Volume when $y = \sqrt{x}$ from $x = 0$ to $4$ is revolved about the $x$-axis. **Solution:** $V = \pi \int_0^4 (\sqrt{x})^2\,dx = \pi \int_0^4 x\,dx = \pi[\frac{x^2}{2}]_0^4 = 8\pi$. **Key insight:** The disc method is straightforward: square the function, multiply by $\pi$, integrate.
