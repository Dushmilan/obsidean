
## Definition

Partial derivatives extend differentiation to functions of **multiple variables**: differentiate with respect to one variable while **holding the others constant**.

$$f_x = \frac{\partial f}{\partial x} = \lim_{h\to 0}\frac{f(x+h, y) - f(x, y)}{h}$$

Mixed partials: $f_{xy} = \frac{\partial}{\partial y}\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\right)$. For well-behaved functions, $f_{xy} = f_{yx}$ (Clairaut).

## The Intuition

Standing on a hillside: $\partial f/\partial x$ is the slope walking east ($y$ fixed); $\partial f/\partial y$ is the slope walking north ($x$ fixed). The **gradient** $\nabla f = (f_x, f_y)$ points in the direction of steepest ascent.

## The Toolkit

| Result | Formula |
|--------|---------|
| First partials | $f_x = \dfrac{\partial f}{\partial x}$, $f_y = \dfrac{\partial f}{\partial y}$ |
| Chain rule | $\dfrac{dz}{dt} = \dfrac{\partial f}{\partial x}\dfrac{dx}{dt} + \dfrac{\partial f}{\partial y}\dfrac{dy}{dt}$ |
| Gradient | $\nabla f = (f_x, f_y)$ |
| Directional derivative | $D_{\mathbf{u}}f = \nabla f \cdot \mathbf{u}$ |
| Tangent plane | $z - z_0 = f_x(x_0,y_0)(x-x_0) + f_y(x_0,y_0)(y-y_0)$ |
| 2nd-derivative test | $D = f_{xx}f_{yy} - (f_{xy})^2$ |
| Lagrange multipliers | $\nabla f = \lambda\nabla g$ with $g = c$ |

**Second-derivative test:** $D > 0$, $f_{xx} > 0$ → local min; $D > 0$, $f_{xx} < 0$ → local max; $D < 0$ → saddle.

## Derivation

The chain rule extends the single-variable version: $z$ changes through both $x(t)$ and $y(t)$, so add both contributions. The tangent plane is the linear approximation in two variables. Lagrange multipliers: at an extremum subject to $g = c$, the level curves of $f$ and $g$ are tangent, so their gradients are parallel. [Full derivations: 04.8-Partial-Differentiation-Proofs]

## Method

1. **Compute partials:** treat every other variable as a constant; use the usual rules.
2. **Mixed partials:** differentiate the first partial with respect to the second variable.
3. **Chain rule:** sum over all paths the variable flows through.
4. **Constrained extrema (Lagrange):** solve $\nabla f = \lambda\nabla g$ together with $g = c$; $\lambda$ is a byproduct.
5. **Classify critical points** with $D = f_{xx}f_{yy} - (f_{xy})^2$.

## Worked Examples

**Setup:** Find $f_x, f_y, f_{xy}$ for $f(x,y) = x^2y + \sin(xy)$.

**Solution:** $f_x = 2xy + y\cos(xy)$, $f_y = x^2 + x\cos(xy)$, $f_{xy} = 2x + \cos(xy) - xy\sin(xy)$.

**Key insight:** Treat the other variable as constant; for mixed partials differentiate the first partial with respect to the other variable.

---

**Setup:** Maximise $f = xy$ subject to $x^2 + y^2 = 1$.

**Solution:** $\nabla f = (y, x)$, $\nabla g = (2x, 2y)$. $y = 2\lambda x$, $x = 2\lambda y \Rightarrow x^2 = y^2$. Constraint: $2x^2 = 1 \Rightarrow x = \pm\frac{1}{\sqrt2}$. Max $f = \frac12$.

**Key insight:** Solve $\nabla f = \lambda\nabla g$ together with $g = c$; $\lambda$'s value is usually unnecessary.

## Common Traps

- Treating the held variable as a function of $x$ (only implicit differentiation does that)
- Confusing $f_x$ at a point with $f$ itself
- The second-derivative test's $D = f_{xx}f_{yy} - (f_{xy})^2$ — sign of $D$ and $f_{xx}$ both matter
- Chain rule missing a path — every variable that carries $t$ contributes
- Lagrange: forgetting to also satisfy the constraint equation
