# Scalar Product — Full Derivations

## 1. Definition and Equivalence

### 1.1 Geometric Definition
$$\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b} = \|\mathbf{a}\|\|\mathbf{b}\|\cos\theta$$
where $\theta$ is the angle between $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$.

### 1.2 Component Form
$$\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b} = a_1b_1 + a_2b_2 + a_3b_3$$

### 1.3 Equivalence Proof
By cosine law on triangle with sides $\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b}, \mathbf{b}-\mathbf{a}$:
$$\|\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{a}\|^2 = \|\mathbf{a}\|^2 + \|\mathbf{b}\|^2 - 2\|\mathbf{a}\|\|\mathbf{b}\|\cos\theta$$

But $\|\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{a}\|^2 = (b_1-a_1)^2 + (b_2-a_2)^2 + (b_3-a_3)^2$
$$= \|\mathbf{a}\|^2 + \|\mathbf{b}\|^2 - 2(a_1b_1 + a_2b_2 + a_3b_3)$$

Equating: $\|\mathbf{a}\|\|\mathbf{b}\|\cos\theta = a_1b_1 + a_2b_2 + a_3b_3$.

---

## 2. Properties

### 2.1 Commutativity
$$\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b} = \|\mathbf{a}\|\|\mathbf{b}\|\cos\theta = \|\mathbf{b}\|\|\mathbf{a}\|\cos\theta = \mathbf{b} \cdot \mathbf{a}$$

### 2.2 Distributivity
$$\mathbf{a} \cdot (\mathbf{b} + \mathbf{c}) = \mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b} + \mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{c}$$
**Proof:** Component form: $\sum a_i(b_i+c_i) = \sum a_ib_i + \sum a_ic_i$.

### 2.3 Scalar Multiplication
$$(k\mathbf{a}) \cdot \mathbf{b} = k(\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b})$$
**Proof:** $\sum (ka_i)b_i = k\sum a_ib_i$.

### 2.4 Self Product
$$\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{a} = \|\mathbf{a}\|^2$$

### 2.5 Orthogonality
$$\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b} = 0 \iff \mathbf{a} \perp \mathbf{b} \text{ (or one is zero)}$$

---

## 3. Cauchy-Schwarz Inequality

### 3.1 Statement
$$|\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b}| \le \|\mathbf{a}\|\|\mathbf{b}\|$$
Equality $\iff \mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$ are linearly dependent.

### 3.2 Proof
For any $t \in \mathbb{R}$:
$$0 \le \|\mathbf{a} + t\mathbf{b}\|^2 = \|\mathbf{a}\|^2 + 2t(\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b}) + t^2\|\mathbf{b}\|^2$$
This quadratic in $t$ has discriminant $\le 0$:
$$(2\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b})^2 - 4\|\mathbf{a}\|^2\|\mathbf{b}\|^2 \le 0$$
$$4(\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b})^2 \le 4\|\mathbf{a}\|^2\|\mathbf{b}\|^2 \Rightarrow |\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b}| \le \|\mathbf{a}\|\|\mathbf{b}\|$$

---

## 4. Triangle Inequality

### 4.1 Statement
$$\|\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b}\| \le \|\mathbf{a}\| + \|\mathbf{b}\|$$

### 4.2 Proof
$$\|\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b}\|^2 = \|\mathbf{a}\|^2 + \|\mathbf{b}\|^2 + 2\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b} \le \|\mathbf{a}\|^2 + \|\mathbf{b}\|^2 + 2\|\mathbf{a}\|\|\mathbf{b}\| = (\|\mathbf{a}\| + \|\mathbf{b}\|)^2$$
Take square root.

---

## 5. Angle Between Vectors

### 5.1 Formula
$$\cos\theta = \frac{\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b}}{\|\mathbf{a}\|\|\mathbf{b}\|}$$

### 5.2 Validity
By Cauchy-Schwarz, RHS $\in [-1, 1]$, so $\theta$ well-defined.

---

## 6. Projection

### 6.1 Scalar Projection
$$\text{comp}_{\mathbf{b}}\mathbf{a} = \|\mathbf{a}\|\cos\theta = \frac{\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b}}{\|\mathbf{b}\|}$$

### 6.2 Vector Projection
$$\text{proj}_{\mathbf{b}}\mathbf{a} = (\text{comp}_{\mathbf{b}}\mathbf{a})\frac{\mathbf{b}}{\|\mathbf{b}\|} = \frac{\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b}}{\|\mathbf{b}\|^2}\mathbf{b} = \frac{\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b}}{\mathbf{b} \cdot \mathbf{b}}\mathbf{b}$$

**Proof:** Projection is parallel to $\mathbf{b}$, so $\text{proj} = k\mathbf{b}$. Require $\mathbf{a} - k\mathbf{b} \perp \mathbf{b}$:
$$(\mathbf{a} - k\mathbf{b}) \cdot \mathbf{b} = 0 \Rightarrow \mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b} - k\|\mathbf{b}\|^2 = 0 \Rightarrow k = \frac{\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b}}{\|\mathbf{b}\|^2}$$

---

## 7. Direction Cosines

### 7.1 Definition
For $\mathbf{a} = a_1\mathbf{i} + a_2\mathbf{j} + a_3\mathbf{k}$:
$$\cos\alpha = \frac{a_1}{\|\mathbf{a}\|}, \quad \cos\beta = \frac{a_2}{\|\mathbf{a}\|}, \quad \cos\gamma = \frac{a_3}{\|\mathbf{a}\|}$$

### 7.2 Identity
$$\cos^2\alpha + \cos^2\beta + \cos^2\gamma = 1$$

**Proof:** Unit vector $\hat{\mathbf{a}} = \cos\alpha \mathbf{i} + \cos\beta \mathbf{j} + \cos\gamma \mathbf{k}$ has magnitude 1.

---

## 8. Work Done by Force

Force $\mathbf{F}$, displacement $\mathbf{d}$:
$$W = \mathbf{F} \cdot \mathbf{d} = \|\mathbf{F}\|\|\mathbf{d}\|\cos\theta$$

---

## 9. Angle Between Lines

Lines with direction vectors $\mathbf{d}_1, \mathbf{d}_2$:
$$\cos\theta = \frac{|\mathbf{d}_1 \cdot \mathbf{d}_2|}{\|\mathbf{d}_1\|\|\mathbf{d}_2\|}$$
(Absolute value gives acute angle between lines.)
