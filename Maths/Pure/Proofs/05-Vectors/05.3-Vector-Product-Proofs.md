# Vector Product — Full Derivations

## 1. Definition and Component Form

### 1.1 Geometric Definition
$$\mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{b} = \|\mathbf{a}\|\|\mathbf{b}\|\sin\theta \, \hat{\mathbf{n}}$$
where $\hat{\mathbf{n}}$ is unit vector $\perp$ to $\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}$ by right-hand rule.

### 1.2 Component Form Derivation
Using distributive law and basis products:
$$\mathbf{i} \times \mathbf{j} = \mathbf{k}, \quad \mathbf{j} \times \mathbf{k} = \mathbf{i}, \quad \mathbf{k} \times \mathbf{i} = \mathbf{j}$$
$$\mathbf{j} \times \mathbf{i} = -\mathbf{k}, \quad \mathbf{k} \times \mathbf{j} = -\mathbf{i}, \quad \mathbf{i} \times \mathbf{k} = -\mathbf{j}$$
$$\mathbf{i} \times \mathbf{i} = \mathbf{j} \times \mathbf{j} = \mathbf{k} \times \mathbf{k} = \mathbf{0}$$

Expanding $(a_1\mathbf{i}+a_2\mathbf{j}+a_3\mathbf{k}) \times (b_1\mathbf{i}+b_2\mathbf{j}+b_3\mathbf{k})$:
$$= a_1b_2(\mathbf{i} \times \mathbf{j}) + a_1b_3(\mathbf{i} \times \mathbf{k}) + a_2b_1(\mathbf{j} \times \mathbf{i}) + a_2b_3(\mathbf{j} \times \mathbf{k}) + a_3b_1(\mathbf{k} \times \mathbf{i}) + a_3b_2(\mathbf{k} \times \mathbf{j})$$
$$= (a_2b_3 - a_3b_2)\mathbf{i} + (a_3b_1 - a_1b_3)\mathbf{j} + (a_1b_2 - a_2b_1)\mathbf{k}$$

## 2. Properties

### 2.1 Anti-commutativity
$$\mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{b} = -\mathbf{b} \times \mathbf{a}$$
**Proof:** Component form or right-hand rule reversal.

### 2.2 Distributivity
$$\mathbf{a} \times (\mathbf{b} + \mathbf{c}) = \mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{b} + \mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{c}$$
**Proof:** Component-wise or geometric (parallelogram law).

### 2.3 Scalar Multiplication
$$(k\mathbf{a}) \times \mathbf{b} = k(\mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{b}) = \mathbf{a} \times (k\mathbf{b})$$

### 2.4 Parallel Vectors
$$\mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{b} = \mathbf{0} \iff \mathbf{a} \parallel \mathbf{b}$$
**Proof:** $\|\mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{b}\| = \|\mathbf{a}\|\|\mathbf{b}\|\sin\theta = 0 \iff \sin\theta = 0 \iff \theta = 0 \text{ or } \pi$.

---

## 3. Magnitude and Area

### 3.1 Magnitude
$$\|\mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{b}\| = \|\mathbf{a}\|\|\mathbf{b}\|\sin\theta$$

### 3.2 Area of Parallelogram
Vertices: $\mathbf{0}, \mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b}, \mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b}$
Height = $\|\mathbf{b}\|\sin\theta$, base = $\|\mathbf{a}\|$
Area = base × height = $\|\mathbf{a}\|\|\mathbf{b}\|\sin\theta = \|\mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{b}\|$

### 3.3 Area of Triangle
Vertices: $\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b}, \mathbf{c}$
Sides: $\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{c}-\mathbf{a}$
Area = $\frac{1}{2}\|(\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{a}) \times (\mathbf{c}-\mathbf{a})\|$

---

## 4. Perpendicular Vector

### 4.1 Direction
$\mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{b}$ is perpendicular to both $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$.
**Proof:** $(\mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{b}) \cdot \mathbf{a} = 0$, $(\mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{b}) \cdot \mathbf{b} = 0$ (scalar triple product with repeated vector).

### 4.2 Unit Normal
$$\hat{\mathbf{n}} = \frac{\mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{b}}{\|\mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{b}\|}$$

---

## 5. Lagrange's Identity

$$\|\mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{b}\|^2 = \|\mathbf{a}\|^2\|\mathbf{b}\|^2 - (\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b})^2$$

**Proof:**
$$\|\mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{b}\|^2 = \|\mathbf{a}\|^2\|\mathbf{b}\|^2\sin^2\theta = \|\mathbf{a}\|^2\|\mathbf{b}\|^2(1-\cos^2\theta) = \|\mathbf{a}\|^2\|\mathbf{b}\|^2 - (\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b})^2$$

---

## 6. Vector Triple Product (Preview)

$$\mathbf{a} \times (\mathbf{b} \times \mathbf{c}) = (\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{c})\mathbf{b} - (\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b})\mathbf{c}$$

**Proof:** Let $\mathbf{d} = \mathbf{a} \times (\mathbf{b} \times \mathbf{c})$. Then $\mathbf{d} \perp \mathbf{b} \times \mathbf{c}$, so $\mathbf{d}$ lies in plane of $\mathbf{b},\mathbf{c}$.
Write $\mathbf{d} = \alpha\mathbf{b} + \beta\mathbf{c}$. Then $\mathbf{d} \cdot \mathbf{a} = 0 \Rightarrow \alpha(\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b}) + \beta(\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{c}) = 0$.
Solve for $\alpha, \beta$ using $\mathbf{d} \cdot \mathbf{b} = (\mathbf{a} \times (\mathbf{b} \times \mathbf{c})) \cdot \mathbf{b} = \mathbf{a} \cdot ((\mathbf{b} \times \mathbf{c}) \times \mathbf{b})$ etc.

---

## 7. Moment of Force

Force $\mathbf{F}$ applied at point with position vector $\mathbf{r}$:
$$\mathbf{M} = \mathbf{r} \times \mathbf{F}$$

Magnitude: $\|\mathbf{M}\| = \|\mathbf{r}\|\|\mathbf{F}\|\sin\theta$ = force × perpendicular distance.
