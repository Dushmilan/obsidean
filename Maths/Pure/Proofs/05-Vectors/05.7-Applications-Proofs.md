# Applications of Vectors — Full Derivations

## 1. Geometric Theorems

### 1.1 Centroid of Triangle
Vertices $\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b}, \mathbf{c}$. Medians from $\mathbf{a}$ to $\frac{\mathbf{b}+\mathbf{c}}{2}$.
Line: $\mathbf{r} = \mathbf{a} + \lambda\left(\frac{\mathbf{b}+\mathbf{c}}{2} - \mathbf{a}\right) = (1-\lambda)\mathbf{a} + \frac{\lambda}{2}(\mathbf{b}+\mathbf{c})$.
At $\lambda = \frac{2}{3}$: $\mathbf{r} = \frac{1}{3}\mathbf{a} + \frac{1}{3}\mathbf{b} + \frac{1}{3}\mathbf{c}$.
This point lies on all three medians. Hence centroid $\mathbf{g} = \frac{\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b}+\mathbf{c}}{3}$.

### 1.2 Orthocentre
Altitude from $\mathbf{a}$ is line through $\mathbf{a}$ perpendicular to $\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{c}$:
$$\mathbf{r} = \mathbf{a} + \mu(\mathbf{b} - \mathbf{c}) \quad \text{where } \mu \in \mathbb{R}$$
Wait — direction must be perpendicular to $\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{c}$.
Altitude direction: $(\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{c}) \times (\mathbf{c}-\mathbf{a}) \times (\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})$.
Better: Use vector identity. If circumcentre is origin, $\|\mathbf{a}\| = \|\mathbf{b}\| = \|\mathbf{c}\| = R$.
Altitude from $\mathbf{a}$ is line through $\mathbf{a}$ parallel to $\mathbf{b}+\mathbf{c}$:
$(\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{a}) \cdot (\mathbf{b} - \mathbf{c}) = 0$ and $(\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{b}) \cdot (\mathbf{c} - \mathbf{a}) = 0$.
Solution: $\mathbf{r} = \mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b} + \mathbf{c}$.

### 1.3 Parallelogram Law
$$\|\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b}\|^2 + \|\mathbf{a} - \mathbf{b}\|^2 = (\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b})\cdot(\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b}) + (\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})\cdot(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})$$
$$= \|\mathbf{a}\|^2 + \|\mathbf{b}\|^2 + 2\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b} + \|\mathbf{a}\|^2 + \|\mathbf{b}\|^2 - 2\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b} = 2\|\mathbf{a}\|^2 + 2\|\mathbf{b}\|^2$$

### 1.4 Tetrahedron Volume
Edges from $\mathbf{a}$: $\mathbf{u} = \mathbf{b}-\mathbf{a}$, $\mathbf{v} = \mathbf{c}-\mathbf{a}$, $\mathbf{w} = \mathbf{d}-\mathbf{a}$.
Parallelepiped volume = $|\mathbf{u} \cdot (\mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{w})|$.
Tetrahedron is $\frac{1}{6}$ of parallelepiped (6 equal pyramids).
$V = \frac{1}{6}|(\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{a}) \cdot ((\mathbf{c}-\mathbf{a}) \times (\mathbf{d}-\mathbf{a}))|$.

## 2. Mechanics

### 2.1 Moment of a Force
Force $\mathbf{F}$ at point with position vector $\mathbf{r}$ about origin.
Moment arm = perpendicular distance from line of action to origin.
Magnitude: $\|\mathbf{r}\|\|\mathbf{F}\|\sin\theta = \|\mathbf{r} \times \mathbf{F}\|$.
Direction: perpendicular to plane of $\mathbf{r}$ and $\mathbf{F}$ (right-hand rule).
Thus $\boldsymbol{\tau} = \mathbf{r} \times \mathbf{F}$.

### 2.2 Couple
Two forces $\mathbf{F}$ at $\mathbf{r}_1$ and $-\mathbf{F}$ at $\mathbf{r}_2$.
Total moment about origin:
$$\boldsymbol{\tau} = \mathbf{r}_1 \times \mathbf{F} + \mathbf{r}_2 \times (-\mathbf{F}) = (\mathbf{r}_1 - \mathbf{r}_2) \times \mathbf{F}$$
Independent of origin choice. If origin shifts by $\mathbf{a}$:
$$\boldsymbol{\tau}' = (\mathbf{r}_1 - \mathbf{a}) \times \mathbf{F} + (\mathbf{r}_2 - \mathbf{a}) \times (-\mathbf{F}) = \mathbf{r}_1 \times \mathbf{F} - \mathbf{r}_2 \times \mathbf{F} - \mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{F} + \mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{F} = \boldsymbol{\tau}$$

### 2.3 Work Done
$$W = \int_C \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r} = \int_a^b \mathbf{F}(\mathbf{r}(t)) \cdot \mathbf{r}'(t) dt$$
For constant $\mathbf{F}$: $W = \mathbf{F} \cdot \int_a^b \mathbf{r}'(t) dt = \mathbf{F} \cdot (\mathbf{r}_b - \mathbf{r}_a)$.

### 2.4 Circular Motion Kinematics
Position: $\mathbf{r}(t) = R\cos(\omega t)\mathbf{i} + R\sin(\omega t)\mathbf{j}$
Velocity: $\mathbf{v}(t) = -R\omega\sin(\omega t)\mathbf{i} + R\omega\cos(\omega t)\mathbf{j} = \boldsymbol{\omega} \times \mathbf{r}$
where $\boldsymbol{\omega} = \omega\mathbf{k}$.
$$\boldsymbol{\omega} \times \mathbf{r} = \begin{vmatrix} \mathbf{i} & \mathbf{j} & \mathbf{k} \\ 0 & 0 & \omega \\ R\cos\omega t & R\sin\omega t & 0 \end{vmatrix} = -R\omega\sin\omega t \mathbf{i} + R\omega\cos\omega t \mathbf{j} = \mathbf{v}$$

Acceleration:
$$\mathbf{a} = \frac{d\mathbf{v}}{dt} = -R\omega^2\cos(\omega t)\mathbf{i} - R\omega^2\sin(\omega t)\mathbf{j} = -\omega^2 \mathbf{r}$$
Also: $\boldsymbol{\omega} \times \mathbf{v} = \boldsymbol{\omega} \times (\boldsymbol{\omega} \times \mathbf{r}) = \boldsymbol{\omega}(\boldsymbol{\omega} \cdot \mathbf{r}) - \mathbf{r}(\boldsymbol{\omega} \cdot \boldsymbol{\omega}) = -\omega^2\mathbf{r}$ (since $\boldsymbol{\omega} \perp \mathbf{r}$).

### 2.5 General Rotational Motion
$\mathbf{v} = \boldsymbol{\omega} \times \mathbf{r}$
$$\mathbf{a} = \frac{d\mathbf{v}}{dt} = \boldsymbol{\alpha} \times \mathbf{r} + \boldsymbol{\omega} \times \mathbf{v} = \boldsymbol{\alpha} \times \mathbf{r} + \boldsymbol{\omega} \times (\boldsymbol{\omega} \times \mathbf{r})$$
where $\boldsymbol{\alpha} = \dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}}$.

Using vector triple product:
$$\boldsymbol{\omega} \times (\boldsymbol{\omega} \times \mathbf{r}) = \boldsymbol{\omega}(\boldsymbol{\omega} \cdot \mathbf{r}) - \mathbf{r}(\boldsymbol{\omega} \cdot \boldsymbol{\omega})$$
If $\boldsymbol{\omega} \perp \mathbf{r}$: $= -\omega^2\mathbf{r}$.

### 2.6 Angular Momentum
$$\mathbf{L} = \mathbf{r} \times m\mathbf{v}$$
Rate of change:
$$\frac{d\mathbf{L}}{dt} = \dot{\mathbf{r}} \times m\mathbf{v} + \mathbf{r} \times m\dot{\mathbf{v}} = \mathbf{v} \times m\mathbf{v} + \mathbf{r} \times \mathbf{F} = \mathbf{0} + \boldsymbol{\tau} = \boldsymbol{\tau}$$

## 3. Electromagnetism

### 3.1 Lorentz Force
$\mathbf{F} = q(\mathbf{E} + \mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{B})$

**Derivation:** Experimental fact — combination of electric and magnetic forces.

### 3.2 Force on Current Element
$d\mathbf{F} = q\mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{B} = I d\mathbf{l} \times \mathbf{B}$
where $I = qn v A$, $d\mathbf{l} = \mathbf{v} dt$.

### 3.3 Magnetic Moment of Loop
Current $I$, area vector $\mathbf{A}$ (normal to plane, right-hand rule).
Force on element: $d\mathbf{F} = I d\mathbf{l} \times \mathbf{B}$
Torque: $d\boldsymbol{\tau} = \mathbf{r} \times d\mathbf{F} = \mathbf{r} \times (I d\mathbf{l} \times \mathbf{B})$
For uniform $\mathbf{B}$ and planar loop:
$$\boldsymbol{\tau} = I \oint \mathbf{r} \times (d\mathbf{l} \times \mathbf{B}) = I \oint [(\mathbf{r} \cdot \mathbf{B}) d\mathbf{l} - (\mathbf{r} \cdot d\mathbf{l}) \mathbf{B}]$$
Using $\oint \mathbf{r} \cdot d\mathbf{l} = 2 \times \text{area}$ and $\oint d\mathbf{l} = \mathbf{0}$:
$$\boldsymbol{\tau} = \mathbf{B} \times \left(I \oint \frac{1}{2} \mathbf{r} \times d\mathbf{l}\right) = \boldsymbol{\mu} \times \mathbf{B}$$
where $\boldsymbol{\mu} = I \mathbf{A}$ with $\mathbf{A} = \frac{1}{2}\oint \mathbf{r} \times d\mathbf{l}$.

### 3.4 Poynting Vector
Energy density in EM field: $u = \frac{1}{2}\varepsilon_0 E^2 + \frac{1}{2\mu_0} B^2$
Maxwell's equations:
$$\nabla \times \mathbf{E} = -\frac{\partial \mathbf{B}}{\partial t}, \quad \nabla \times \mathbf{B} = \mu_0 \mathbf{J} + \mu_0\varepsilon_0 \frac{\partial \mathbf{E}}{\partial t}$$
Poynting theorem: $\frac{\partial u}{\partial t} + \nabla \cdot \mathbf{S} = -\mathbf{J} \cdot \mathbf{E}$
where $\mathbf{S} = \frac{1}{\mu_0} \mathbf{E} \times \mathbf{B}$.
**Proof:** $\nabla \cdot \mathbf{S} = \frac{1}{\mu_0} \nabla \cdot (\mathbf{E} \times \mathbf{B}) = \frac{1}{\mu_0} (\mathbf{B} \cdot (\nabla \times \mathbf{E}) - \mathbf{E} \cdot (\nabla \times \mathbf{B}))$
Substitute Maxwell's equations:
$$= \frac{1}{\mu_0} \left(\mathbf{B} \cdot \left(-\frac{\partial \mathbf{B}}{\partial t}\right) - \mathbf{E} \cdot \left(\mu_0 \mathbf{J} + \mu_0\varepsilon_0 \frac{\partial \mathbf{E}}{\partial t}\right)\right) = -\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(\frac{B^2}{2\mu_0} + \frac{\varepsilon_0 E^2}{2}\right) - \mathbf{J} \cdot \mathbf{E}$$

## 4. Crystallography

### 4.1 Reciprocal Vectors
Given non-coplanar $\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b}, \mathbf{c}$, define:
$$\mathbf{a}' = \frac{\mathbf{b} \times \mathbf{c}}{[\mathbf{a} \; \mathbf{b} \; \mathbf{c}]}, \quad \mathbf{b}' = \frac{\mathbf{c} \times \mathbf{a}}{[\mathbf{a} \; \mathbf{b} \; \mathbf{c}]}, \quad \mathbf{c}' = \frac{\mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{b}}{[\mathbf{a} \; \mathbf{b} \; \mathbf{c}]}$$
where $[\mathbf{a} \; \mathbf{b} \; \mathbf{c}] = \mathbf{a} \cdot (\mathbf{b} \times \mathbf{c})$.

**Properties:**
- $\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{a}' = \frac{\mathbf{a} \cdot (\mathbf{b} \times \mathbf{c})}{[\mathbf{a} \; \mathbf{b} \; \mathbf{c}]} = 1$
- $\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b}' = \frac{\mathbf{a} \cdot (\mathbf{c} \times \mathbf{a})}{[\mathbf{a} \; \mathbf{b} \; \mathbf{c}]} = 0$ (repeated vector)
- Similarly all cross terms vanish.

**Expansion of arbitrary vector $\mathbf{v}$:**
$$\mathbf{v} = (\mathbf{v} \cdot \mathbf{a}')\mathbf{a} + (\mathbf{v} \cdot \mathbf{b}')\mathbf{b} + (\mathbf{v} \cdot \mathbf{c}')\mathbf{c}$$
**Proof:** Express $\mathbf{v} = x\mathbf{a} + y\mathbf{b} + z\mathbf{c}$.
Dot with $\mathbf{a}'$: $\mathbf{v} \cdot \mathbf{a}' = x\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{a}' + y\mathbf{b} \cdot \mathbf{a}' + z\mathbf{c} \cdot \mathbf{a}' = x \cdot 1 + y \cdot 0 + z \cdot 0 = x$.
So $x = \mathbf{v} \cdot \mathbf{a}'$, etc.

### 4.2 Miller Indices
Plane with intercepts $\frac{a}{h}, \frac{b}{k}, \frac{c}{l}$ has normal parallel to $h\mathbf{a}' + k\mathbf{b}' + l\mathbf{c}'$.

## 5. Navigation and Relative Motion

### 5.1 Relative Velocity
$\mathbf{v}_{A/B} = \mathbf{v}_A - \mathbf{v}_B$

### 5.2 Closest Approach
Objects A, B with constant velocities:
$\mathbf{r}_A(t) = \mathbf{a} + \mathbf{u}t$, $\mathbf{r}_B(t) = \mathbf{b} + \mathbf{v}t$
Separation: $\mathbf{r}(t) = \mathbf{a} - \mathbf{b} + (\mathbf{u} - \mathbf{v})t$
Distance squared: $d^2(t) = \|\mathbf{r}_0 + \mathbf{w}t\|^2 = \|\mathbf{r}_0\|^2 + 2t(\mathbf{r}_0 \cdot \mathbf{w}) + t^2\|\mathbf{w}\|^2$
Minimum at $t = -\frac{\mathbf{r}_0 \cdot \mathbf{w}}{\|\mathbf{w}\|^2}$
Minimum distance: $d_{\text{min}} = \frac{\|\mathbf{r}_0 \times \mathbf{w}\|}{\|\mathbf{w}\|}$

### 5.3 Interception
Ship A at $\mathbf{a}$ with velocity $\mathbf{u}$, Ship B at $\mathbf{b}$ with velocity $\mathbf{v}$.
For A to intercept B: $\exists t > 0$ such that $\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{u}t = \mathbf{b} + \mathbf{v}t$.
Condition: $\mathbf{a} - \mathbf{b}$ parallel to $\mathbf{u} - \mathbf{v}$.
i.e., $(\mathbf{a} - \mathbf{b}) \times (\mathbf{u} - \mathbf{v}) = \mathbf{0}$.

## 6. Computer Graphics

### 6.1 Orthographic Projection
Project onto plane with normal $\hat{\mathbf{n}}$:
$$\mathbf{r}_{\text{proj}} = \mathbf{r} - (\mathbf{r} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{n}})\hat{\mathbf{n}}$$
This subtracts the component along $\hat{\mathbf{n}}$.

### 6.2 Perspective Projection
Eye at $\mathbf{e}$, view plane distance $d$, normal $\hat{\mathbf{n}}$.
For point $\mathbf{r}$:
$$\mathbf{r}_{\text{proj}} = \mathbf{e} + \frac{d}{(\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{e}) \cdot \hat{\mathbf{n}}}(\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{e})$$
Projected point lies on line through $\mathbf{e}$ and $\mathbf{r}$.

### 6.3 Barycentric Coordinates
Point in triangle $\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b}, \mathbf{c}$:
$$\mathbf{p} = \alpha\mathbf{a} + \beta\mathbf{b} + \gamma\mathbf{c}, \quad \alpha+\beta+\gamma=1, \quad \alpha,\beta,\gamma \ge 0$$

### 6.4 Phong Lighting
$$\mathbf{I} = I_a k_a + I_d k_d (\hat{\mathbf{n}} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{l}}) + I_s k_s (\hat{\mathbf{r}} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{v}})^n$$
- $I_a, I_d, I_s$: ambient, diffuse, specular intensities
- $k_a, k_d, k_s$: material coefficients
- $\hat{\mathbf{n}}$: surface normal
- $\hat{\mathbf{l}}$: light direction
- $\hat{\mathbf{r}}$: reflection direction
- $\hat{\mathbf{v}}$: view direction
- $n$: shininess exponent
