# Triple Products — Full Derivations

## 1. Scalar Triple Product

### 1.1 Definition
$$[\mathbf{a} \; \mathbf{b} \; \mathbf{c}] = \mathbf{a} \cdot (\mathbf{b} \times \mathbf{c}) = \begin{vmatrix} a_1 & a_2 & a_3 \\ b_1 & b_2 & b_3 \\ c_1 & c_2 & c_3 \end{vmatrix}$$

### 1.2 Cyclic Permutation
$$\mathbf{a} \cdot (\mathbf{b} \times \mathbf{c}) = \mathbf{b} \cdot (\mathbf{c} \times \mathbf{a}) = \mathbf{c} \cdot (\mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{b})$$

**Proof:** Each equals the determinant of the matrix with rows $\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b},\mathbf{c}$. Cyclic permutation of rows preserves determinant sign.

### 1.3 Anti-cyclic Permutation
$$\mathbf{a} \cdot (\mathbf{b} \times \mathbf{c}) = -\mathbf{a} \cdot (\mathbf{c} \times \mathbf{b})$$

**Proof:** Swapping two rows changes determinant sign.

### 1.4 Dot/Cross Interchange
$$\mathbf{a} \cdot (\mathbf{b} \times \mathbf{c}) = (\mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{b}) \cdot \mathbf{c}$$

**Proof:** $(\mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{b}) \cdot \mathbf{c} = \mathbf{c} \cdot (\mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{b}) = \mathbf{a} \cdot (\mathbf{b} \times \mathbf{c})$ by cyclic permutation.

## 2. Volume of Parallelepiped

### 2.1 Derivation
Edges $\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b}, \mathbf{c}$ from one vertex.
Base area = $\|\mathbf{b} \times \mathbf{c}\|$
Height = projection of $\mathbf{a}$ onto $\hat{\mathbf{n}} = \frac{\mathbf{b} \times \mathbf{c}}{\|\mathbf{b} \times \mathbf{c}\|}$:
$$\text{height} = \mathbf{a} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{n}} = \frac{\mathbf{a} \cdot (\mathbf{b} \times \mathbf{c})}{\|\mathbf{b} \times \mathbf{c}\|}$$
Volume = base × height = $\|\mathbf{b} \times \mathbf{c}\| \cdot \frac{|\mathbf{a} \cdot (\mathbf{b} \times \mathbf{c})|}{\|\mathbf{b} \times \mathbf{c}\|} = |\mathbf{a} \cdot (\mathbf{b} \times \mathbf{c})|$

---

## 3. Coplanarity Test

Three vectors $\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b}, \mathbf{c}$ are coplanar $\iff \mathbf{a} \cdot (\mathbf{b} \times \mathbf{c}) = 0$

**Proof:** Volume of parallelepiped = 0 $\iff$ edges are coplanar $\iff$ vectors are coplanar.

## 4. Vector Triple Product

### 4.1 BAC-CAB Identity
$$\mathbf{a} \times (\mathbf{b} \times \mathbf{c}) = (\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{c})\mathbf{b} - (\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b})\mathbf{c}$$

**Proof 1 (Component):**
Compute $\mathbf{b} \times \mathbf{c}$, then $\mathbf{a} \times (\mathbf{b} \times \mathbf{c})$, compare with RHS.

**Proof 2 (Geometric):**
Let $\mathbf{d} = \mathbf{a} \times (\mathbf{b} \times \mathbf{c})$.
- $\mathbf{d} \perp \mathbf{b} \times \mathbf{c} \Rightarrow \mathbf{d}$ lies in plane of $\mathbf{b}, \mathbf{c}$
- So $\mathbf{d} = \alpha\mathbf{b} + \beta\mathbf{c}$
- $\mathbf{d} \cdot \mathbf{a} = 0 \Rightarrow \alpha(\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b}) + \beta(\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{c}) = 0$
- $\mathbf{d} \cdot \mathbf{b} = \mathbf{a} \cdot ((\mathbf{b} \times \mathbf{c}) \times \mathbf{b}) = \mathbf{a} \cdot ((\mathbf{b}\cdot\mathbf{b})\mathbf{c} - (\mathbf{b}\cdot\mathbf{c})\mathbf{b}) = \|\mathbf{b}\|^2(\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{c}) - (\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b})(\mathbf{b}\cdot\mathbf{c})$
- Also $\mathbf{d} \cdot \mathbf{b} = \alpha\|\mathbf{b}\|^2 + \beta(\mathbf{b}\cdot\mathbf{c})$
- Solving gives $\alpha = \mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{c}, \beta = -\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b}$

### 4.2 Alternative Form
$$(\mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{b}) \times \mathbf{c} = (\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{c})\mathbf{b} - (\mathbf{b} \cdot \mathbf{c})\mathbf{a}$$

**Proof:** Use anti-commutativity and BAC-CAB:
$$(\mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{b}) \times \mathbf{c} = -\mathbf{c} \times (\mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{b}) = -[(\mathbf{c}\cdot\mathbf{b})\mathbf{a} - (\mathbf{c}\cdot\mathbf{a})\mathbf{b}] = (\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{c})\mathbf{b} - (\mathbf{b}\cdot\mathbf{c})\mathbf{a}$$

---

## 5. Non-associativity

$$(\mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{b}) \times \mathbf{c} \neq \mathbf{a} \times (\mathbf{b} \times \mathbf{c}) \text{ in general}$$

**Example:** $\mathbf{i} \times (\mathbf{i} \times \mathbf{j}) = \mathbf{i} \times \mathbf{k} = -\mathbf{j}$
$(\mathbf{i} \times \mathbf{i}) \times \mathbf{j} = \mathbf{0} \times \mathbf{j} = \mathbf{0}$

---

## 6. Four-Vector Products

### 6.1 Scalar Product of Cross Products
$$(\mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{b}) \cdot (\mathbf{c} \times \mathbf{d}) = (\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{c})(\mathbf{b} \cdot \mathbf{d}) - (\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{d})(\mathbf{b} \cdot \mathbf{c})$$

**Proof:** Use determinant identity or expand both sides.

### 6.2 Vector Product of Cross Products
$$(\mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{b}) \times (\mathbf{c} \times \mathbf{d}) = [\mathbf{a} \; \mathbf{b} \; \mathbf{d}]\mathbf{c} - [\mathbf{a} \; \mathbf{b} \; \mathbf{c}]\mathbf{d}$$

**Proof:** Let $\mathbf{u} = \mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{b}$, $\mathbf{v} = \mathbf{c} \times \mathbf{d}$.
Use BAC-CAB: $\mathbf{u} \times \mathbf{v} = (\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{d})\mathbf{c} - (\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{c})\mathbf{d}$.
Then $\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{d} = (\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b})\cdot\mathbf{d} = [\mathbf{a} \; \mathbf{b} \; \mathbf{d}]$, similarly for $\mathbf{c}$.

---

## 7. Reciprocal Vectors

Given non-coplanar $\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b}, \mathbf{c}$, define:
$$\mathbf{a}' = \frac{\mathbf{b} \times \mathbf{c}}{[\mathbf{a} \; \mathbf{b} \; \mathbf{c}]}, \quad \mathbf{b}' = \frac{\mathbf{c} \times \mathbf{a}}{[\mathbf{a} \; \mathbf{b} \; \mathbf{c}]}, \quad \mathbf{c}' = \frac{\mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{b}}{[\mathbf{a} \; \mathbf{b} \; \mathbf{c}]}$$

**Properties:**
- $\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{a}' = \mathbf{b} \cdot \mathbf{b}' = \mathbf{c} \cdot \mathbf{c}' = 1$
- $\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b}' = \mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{c}' = \mathbf{b} \cdot \mathbf{a}' = \dots = 0$

**Proof:** $\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{a}' = \frac{\mathbf{a} \cdot (\mathbf{b} \times \mathbf{c})}{[\mathbf{a} \; \mathbf{b} \; \mathbf{c}]} = 1$.
$\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b}' = \frac{\mathbf{a} \cdot (\mathbf{c} \times \mathbf{a})}{[\dots]} = 0$ (repeated vector).

---

## 8. Volume of Tetrahedron

Vertices $\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b}, \mathbf{c}, \mathbf{d}$:
$$V = \frac{1}{6}|(\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{a}) \cdot ((\mathbf{c}-\mathbf{a}) \times (\mathbf{d}-\mathbf{a}))|$$

**Proof:** Tetrahedron is 1/6 of parallelepiped with edges $\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{c}-\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{d}-\mathbf{a}$.
