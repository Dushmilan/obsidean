# Partial Differentiation â€” Full Derivations

## 1. Partial Derivatives

### 1.1 Definition
$$\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}(x_0, y_0) = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x_0+h, y_0) - f(x_0, y_0)}{h}$$
$$\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}(x_0, y_0) = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x_0, y_0+h) - f(x_0, y_0)}{h}$$

### 1.2 Continuity of Partial Derivatives
If $f_x, f_y$ exist and are continuous at $(x_0, y_0)$, then $f$ is differentiable at $(x_0, y_0)$.

---

## 2. Higher-Order Derivatives and Young's Theorem

### 2.1 Young's Theorem (Clairaut's Theorem)
**Theorem:** If $f_{xy}$ and $f_{yx}$ are continuous on an open set containing $(a,b)$, then $f_{xy}(a,b) = f_{yx}(a,b)$.

**Proof:**
Consider $\Delta(h,k) = f(a+h, b+k) - f(a+h, b) - f(a, b+k) + f(a, b)$.

By Mean Value Theorem:
$$\Delta = f_y(a+\theta h, b+k) h - f_y(a+\theta h, b) h = h k f_{xy}(a+\theta h, b+\phi k)$$
Also:
$$\Delta = f_x(a+h, b+\theta k) k - f_x(a, b+\theta k) k = h k f_{yx}(a+\psi h, b+\theta k)$$

Taking limit as $(h,k) \to (0,0)$, continuity gives $f_{xy} = f_{yx}$.

## 3. Chain Rule

### 3.1 Case 1: $z = f(x,y)$, $x = x(t)$, $y = y(t)$
$$\frac{dz}{dt} = \frac{\partial f}{\partial x} \frac{dx}{dt} + \frac{\partial f}{\partial y} \frac{dy}{dt}$$

**Proof:**
$$\frac{\Delta z}{\Delta t} = \frac{f(x+\Delta x, y+\Delta y) - f(x,y)}{\Delta t}$$
$$= \frac{f(x+\Delta x, y+\Delta y) - f(x, y+\Delta y)}{\Delta t} + \frac{f(x, y+\Delta y) - f(x,y)}{\Delta t}$$
$$= f_x(x+\theta \Delta x, y+\Delta y) \frac{\Delta x}{\Delta t} + f_y(x, y+\phi \Delta y) \frac{\Delta y}{\Delta t}$$
Take limit $\Delta t \to 0$.

### 3.2 Case 2: $z = f(x,y)$, $x = x(u,v)$, $y = y(u,v)$
$$\frac{\partial z}{\partial u} = \frac{\partial f}{\partial x} \frac{\partial x}{\partial u} + \frac{\partial f}{\partial y} \frac{\partial y}{\partial u}$$
$$\frac{\partial z}{\partial v} = \frac{\partial f}{\partial x} \frac{\partial x}{\partial v} + \frac{\partial f}{\partial y} \frac{\partial y}{\partial v}$$

**Proof:** Fix $v$, treat as Case 1.

### 3.3 General Chain Rule (Tree Diagram)
For $z = f(x_1, \dots, x_n)$ where each $x_i = x_i(u_1, \dots, u_m)$:
$$\frac{\partial z}{\partial u_j} = \sum_{i=1}^n \frac{\partial f}{\partial x_i} \frac{\partial x_i}{\partial u_j}$$

## 4. Total Differential and Differentiability

### 4.1 Definition of Differentiability
$f$ is differentiable at $(x_0, y_0)$ if:
$$f(x_0+h, y_0+k) = f(x_0, y_0) + A h + B k + \epsilon(h,k) \sqrt{h^2+k^2}$$
where $\epsilon(h,k) \to 0$ as $(h,k) \to (0,0)$, and $A = f_x$, $B = f_y$.

### 4.2 Total Differential
$$df = f_x dx + f_y dy$$
$$\Delta f \approx df \quad \text{for small } dx, dy$$

### 4.3 Error Bound
If $|f_{xx}|, |f_{xy}|, |f_{yy}| \le M$ near $(x_0, y_0)$, then
$$|\Delta f - df| \le \frac{M}{2}(|dx| + |dy|)^2$$

## 5. Gradient and Directional Derivatives

### 5.1 Gradient
$$\nabla f = \left( \frac{\partial f}{\partial x}, \frac{\partial f}{\partial y} \right)$$

### 5.2 Directional Derivative
$$D_{\mathbf{u}} f = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(\mathbf{r}_0 + h\mathbf{u}) - f(\mathbf{r}_0)}{h} = \nabla f \cdot \mathbf{u}$$
where $\mathbf{u}$ is a unit vector.

**Proof:**
Let $g(h) = f(x_0 + h u_1, y_0 + h u_2)$.
$g'(0) = f_x u_1 + f_y u_2 = \nabla f \cdot \mathbf{u}$.

### 5.3 Maximum Rate of Change
Maximum of $\nabla f \cdot \mathbf{u}$ subject to $\|\mathbf{u}\|=1$ is $\|\nabla f\|$ when $\mathbf{u} = \frac{\nabla f}{\|\nabla f\|}$.
Minimum is $-\|\nabla f\|$ when $\mathbf{u} = -\frac{\nabla f}{\|\nabla f\|}$.

## 6. Tangent Planes and Normal Lines

### 6.1 Surface $z = f(x,y)$
Tangent plane at $(x_0, y_0, z_0)$:
$$z - z_0 = f_x(x_0, y_0)(x - x_0) + f_y(x_0, y_0)(y - y_0)$$

**Derivation:**
Parametric: $\mathbf{r}(x,y) = (x, y, f(x,y))$.
Tangent vectors: $\mathbf{r}_x = (1, 0, f_x)$, $\mathbf{r}_y = (0, 1, f_y)$.
Normal: $\mathbf{n} = \mathbf{r}_x \times \mathbf{r}_y = (-f_x, -f_y, 1)$.
Plane: $\mathbf{n} \cdot (x-x_0, y-y_0, z-z_0) = 0$.

### 6.2 Level Surface $F(x,y,z) = c$
Tangent plane: $\nabla F \cdot (\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}_0) = 0$.
**Proof:** Curve on surface: $F(\mathbf{r}(t)) = c$. Differentiate: $\nabla F \cdot \mathbf{r}' = 0$. Normal is $\nabla F$.

## 7. Extrema of Functions of Two Variables

### 7.1 Necessary Condition (Fermat)
If $f$ has local extremum at interior point $(a,b)$ and partials exist, then $f_x(a,b) = f_y(a,b) = 0$.

**Proof:** Fix $y=b$, $g(x) = f(x,b)$ has extremum at $x=a$, so $g'(a) = f_x(a,b) = 0$. Similarly $f_y = 0$.

### 7.2 Second Derivative Test
Let $D = f_{xx} f_{yy} - (f_{xy})^2$ at critical point.

**Proof (Quadratic Approximation):**
$$f(a+h, b+k) - f(a,b) \approx \frac{1}{2} \left( f_{xx} h^2 + 2f_{xy} hk + f_{yy} k^2 \right)$$
This is a quadratic form $Q(h,k)$.

- $D > 0$, $f_{xx} > 0$: $Q$ positive definite $\Rightarrow$ local minimum
- $D > 0$, $f_{xx} < 0$: $Q$ negative definite $\Rightarrow$ local maximum
- $D < 0$: $Q$ indefinite $\Rightarrow$ saddle point
- $D = 0$: Test inconclusive

## 8. Lagrange Multipliers

### 8.1 One Constraint
**Theorem:** If $f$ has constrained extremum at $(x_0, y_0)$ subject to $g(x,y)=c$, and $\nabla g \neq \mathbf{0}$, then $\exists \lambda$ such that $\nabla f = \lambda \nabla g$.

**Proof:**
Constraint defines curve $\mathbf{r}(t)$ with $\mathbf{r}(t_0) = (x_0, y_0)$, $g(\mathbf{r}(t)) = c$.
Differentiate: $\nabla g \cdot \mathbf{r}'(t) = 0$, so $\mathbf{r}' \perp \nabla g$.
Define $\phi(t) = f(\mathbf{r}(t))$. Extremum $\Rightarrow \phi'(t_0) = \nabla f \cdot \mathbf{r}'(t_0) = 0$.
Thus $\nabla f \perp \mathbf{r}'(t_0)$. Both $\nabla f$ and $\nabla g$ perpendicular to $\mathbf{r}'$, so they are parallel: $\nabla f = \lambda \nabla g$.

### 8.2 Two Constraints
Max/min $f(x,y,z)$ subject to $g(x,y,z)=c$, $h(x,y,z)=d$.
$$\nabla f = \lambda \nabla g + \mu \nabla h$$

## 9. Implicit Differentiation

### 9.1 $F(x,y) = 0$ defines $y = f(x)$
$$\frac{dy}{dx} = -\frac{F_x}{F_y} \quad (F_y \neq 0)$$

**Proof:** $F(x, f(x)) = 0$. Differentiate: $F_x + F_y f'(x) = 0$.

### 9.2 $F(x,y,z) = 0$ defines $z = f(x,y)$
$$\frac{\partial z}{\partial x} = -\frac{F_x}{F_z}, \quad \frac{\partial z}{\partial y} = -\frac{F_y}{F_z} \quad (F_z \neq 0)$$

## 10. Taylor's Theorem for Two Variables

### 10.1 Second-Order Expansion
$$f(x_0+h, y_0+k) = f + f_x h + f_y k + \frac{1}{2}(f_{xx} h^2 + 2f_{xy} hk + f_{yy} k^2) + R_2$$

### 10.2 Lagrange Remainder
$$R_2 = \frac{1}{3!} \left( f_{xxx} h^3 + 3f_{xxy} h^2 k + 3f_{xyy} h k^2 + f_{yyy} k^3 \right)$$
evaluated at some point on line segment.

### 10.3 General Form
$$f(\mathbf{x}_0 + \mathbf{h}) = \sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{1}{n!} \left( \mathbf{h} \cdot \nabla \right)^n f(\mathbf{x}_0)$$
where $(\mathbf{h} \cdot \nabla) = h \frac{\partial}{\partial x} + k \frac{\partial}{\partial y}$.

## 11. Hessian Matrix and Quadratic Forms

### 11.1 Hessian Matrix
$$H = \begin{pmatrix} f_{xx} & f_{xy} \\ f_{yx} & f_{yy} \end{pmatrix}$$

### 11.2 Classification via Eigenvalues
- Both eigenvalues $> 0$: positive definite $\Rightarrow$ local min
- Both eigenvalues $< 0$: negative definite $\Rightarrow$ local max
- Mixed signs: indefinite $\Rightarrow$ saddle point
- One zero: test fails

### 11.3 Principal Minors Test (Sylvester's Criterion)
For $H = \begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ b & c \end{pmatrix}$:
- $a > 0$, $ac - b^2 > 0 \Rightarrow$ positive definite
- $a < 0$, $ac - b^2 > 0 \Rightarrow$ negative definite
- $ac - b^2 < 0 \Rightarrow$ indefinite

This is exactly $D = f_{xx}f_{yy} - f_{xy}^2$.

## 12. Change of Variables in Partial Derivatives

### 12.1 Jacobian
For transformation $u = u(x,y), v = v(x,y)$:
$$J = \frac{\partial(u,v)}{\partial(x,y)} = \begin{vmatrix} u_x & u_y \\ v_x & v_y \end{vmatrix}$$

### 12.2 Chain Rule with Jacobian
$$\frac{\partial(f,g)}{\partial(u,v)} = \frac{\partial(f,g)}{\partial(x,y)} \cdot \frac{\partial(x,y)}{\partial(u,v)}$$

### 12.3 Inverse Transformation
If $J \neq 0$, then $\frac{\partial(x,y)}{\partial(u,v)} = 1/J$.

## 13. Euler's Theorem for Homogeneous Functions

### 13.1 Definition
$f$ is homogeneous of degree $n$ if $f(tx, ty) = t^n f(x,y)$.

### 13.2 Euler's Theorem
$$x \frac{\partial f}{\partial x} + y \frac{\partial f}{\partial y} = n f$$

**Proof:** Differentiate $f(tx, ty) = t^n f(x,y)$ with respect to $t$:
$$x f_x(tx,ty) + y f_y(tx,ty) = n t^{n-1} f(x,y)$$
Set $t = 1$.

### 13.3 Corollary
$$x^2 f_{xx} + 2xy f_{xy} + y^2 f_{yy} = n(n-1) f$$
