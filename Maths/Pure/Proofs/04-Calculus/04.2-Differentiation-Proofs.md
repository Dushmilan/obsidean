# Differentiation — Full Derivations

## 1. Derivative from First Principles

**Definition:** $f'(a) = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(a+h) - f(a)}{h} = \lim_{x \to a} \frac{f(x) - f(a)}{x - a}$

**Differentiability $\Rightarrow$ Continuity:**
If $f'(a)$ exists, then
$$\lim_{x \to a} [f(x) - f(a)] = \lim_{x \to a} \frac{f(x) - f(a)}{x - a} \cdot (x - a) = f'(a) \cdot 0 = 0$$
So $\lim_{x \to a} f(x) = f(a)$.

---

## 2. Basic Derivative Rules

### Constant Rule
$$\frac{d}{dx}(c) = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{c - c}{h} = \lim_{h \to 0} 0 = 0$$

### Power Rule ($x^n$, $n \in \mathbb{N}$)
$$\frac{d}{dx}(x^n) = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{(x+h)^n - x^n}{h}$$
By binomial theorem: $(x+h)^n = x^n + n x^{n-1}h + \binom{n}{2}x^{n-2}h^2 + \cdots + h^n$
$$\frac{(x+h)^n - x^n}{h} = n x^{n-1} + \binom{n}{2}x^{n-2}h + \cdots + h^{n-1} \xrightarrow{h \to 0} n x^{n-1}$$

**Extension to rational $n = p/q$:**
Let $y = x^{p/q} \Rightarrow y^q = x^p$. Differentiate implicitly:
$q y^{q-1} y' = p x^{p-1} \Rightarrow y' = \frac{p}{q} \frac{x^{p-1}}{y^{q-1}} = \frac{p}{q} x^{p-1} x^{-p(q-1)/q} = \frac{p}{q} x^{p/q - 1}$

**Extension to real $n$:** Use $x^n = e^{n \ln x}$, then $(e^{n \ln x})' = e^{n \ln x} \cdot \frac{n}{x} = n x^{n-1}$.

---

## 3. Sum/Difference Rule
$$(f \pm g)' = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{[f(x+h) \pm g(x+h)] - [f(x) \pm g(x)]}{h} = f'(x) \pm g'(x)$$

---

## 4. Constant Multiple Rule
$$(cf)' = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{c f(x+h) - c f(x)}{h} = c \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x+h) - f(x)}{h} = c f'(x)$$

---

## 5. Product Rule

**Theorem:** $(fg)' = f'g + fg'$

**Proof:**
$$\begin{aligned}
(fg)'(x) &= \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x+h)g(x+h) - f(x)g(x)}{h} \\
&= \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x+h)g(x+h) - f(x+h)g(x) + f(x+h)g(x) - f(x)g(x)}{h} \\
&= \lim_{h \to 0} \left[ f(x+h) \frac{g(x+h) - g(x)}{h} + g(x) \frac{f(x+h) - f(x)}{h} \right] \\
&= f(x) g'(x) + g(x) f'(x) = f'g + fg'
\end{aligned}$$
(Requires $f,g$ continuous at $x$, which follows from differentiability.)

---

## 6. Quotient Rule

**Theorem:** $\left(\frac{f}{g}\right)' = \frac{f'g - fg'}{g^2}$ ($g \neq 0$)

**Proof 1 (using product & reciprocal):**
$$\frac{f}{g} = f \cdot \frac{1}{g}$$
$$\left(\frac{1}{g}\right)' = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{\frac{1}{g(x+h)} - \frac{1}{g(x)}}{h} = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{g(x) - g(x+h)}{h g(x+h)g(x)} = -\frac{g'(x)}{[g(x)]^2}$$
Then by product rule:
$$\left(f \cdot \frac{1}{g}\right)' = f' \cdot \frac{1}{g} + f \cdot \left(-\frac{g'}{g^2}\right) = \frac{f'g - fg'}{g^2}$$

**Proof 2 (direct):**
$$\begin{aligned}
\left(\frac{f}{g}\right)' &= \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{\frac{f(x+h)}{g(x+h)} - \frac{f(x)}{g(x)}}{h} \\
&= \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x+h)g(x) - f(x)g(x+h)}{h g(x+h)g(x)} \\
&= \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{[f(x+h)-f(x)]g(x) - f(x)[g(x+h)-g(x)]}{h g(x+h)g(x)} \\
&= \frac{f'(x)g(x) - f(x)g'(x)}{[g(x)]^2}
\end{aligned}$$

## 7. Chain Rule

**Theorem:** If $y = f(u)$, $u = g(x)$, and $f$ differentiable at $g(x)$, $g$ differentiable at $x$, then
$$\frac{dy}{dx} = f'(g(x)) \cdot g'(x)$$

**Proof (Standard):**
Let $\Delta u = g(x+\Delta x) - g(x)$, $\Delta y = f(u+\Delta u) - f(u)$.
$$\frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x} = \frac{\Delta y}{\Delta u} \cdot \frac{\Delta u}{\Delta x} \quad (\text{if } \Delta u \neq 0)$$
As $\Delta x \to 0$, $\Delta u \to 0$ (since $g$ continuous).
$$\lim_{\Delta x \to 0} \frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x} = \lim_{\Delta u \to 0} \frac{\Delta y}{\Delta u} \cdot \lim_{\Delta x \to 0} \frac{\Delta u}{\Delta x} = f'(u) g'(x)$$

**Rigorous Proof (Carathéodory's approach):**
Since $g$ differentiable at $x$, $\exists \phi$ continuous at $x$ such that:
$$g(x+h) - g(x) = \phi(h) h, \quad \phi(0) = g'(x)$$
Since $f$ differentiable at $g(x)$, $\exists \psi$ continuous at $0$ such that:
$$f(g(x)+k) - f(g(x)) = \psi(k) k, \quad \psi(0) = f'(g(x))$$
Let $k = \phi(h) h$. Then:
$$\frac{f(g(x+h)) - f(g(x))}{h} = \psi(\phi(h)h) \cdot \phi(h) \xrightarrow{h \to 0} \psi(0) \phi(0) = f'(g(x)) g'(x)$$

## 8. Derivatives of Standard Functions

### Exponential: $e^x$
$$\frac{d}{dx} e^x = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{e^{x+h} - e^x}{h} = e^x \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{e^h - 1}{h} = e^x \cdot 1 = e^x$$
(Using $\lim_{h \to 0} \frac{e^h - 1}{h} = 1$)

### General Exponential: $a^x$
$$a^x = e^{x \ln a} \Rightarrow \frac{d}{dx} a^x = e^{x \ln a} \cdot \ln a = a^x \ln a$$

### Natural Logarithm: $\ln x$
$$(\ln x)' = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{\ln(x+h) - \ln x}{h} = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{1}{h} \ln\left(1 + \frac{h}{x}\right)$$
Let $u = h/x \to 0$:
$$= \lim_{u \to 0} \frac{1}{xu} \ln(1+u) = \frac{1}{x} \lim_{u \to 0} \frac{\ln(1+u)}{u} = \frac{1}{x} \cdot 1 = \frac{1}{x}$$

### General Logarithm: $\log_a x$
$$\log_a x = \frac{\ln x}{\ln a} \Rightarrow \frac{d}{dx} \log_a x = \frac{1}{x \ln a}$$

---

## 9. Trigonometric Derivatives

### $\sin x$ and $\cos x$
$$\frac{d}{dx} \sin x = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{\sin(x+h) - \sin x}{h} = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{\sin x \cos h + \cos x \sin h - \sin x}{h}$$
$$= \sin x \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{\cos h - 1}{h} + \cos x \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{\sin h}{h} = \sin x \cdot 0 + \cos x \cdot 1 = \cos x$$

$$\frac{d}{dx} \cos x = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{\cos(x+h) - \cos x}{h} = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{\cos x \cos h - \sin x \sin h - \cos x}{h}$$
$$= \cos x \cdot 0 - \sin x \cdot 1 = -\sin x$$

### $\tan x = \sin x / \cos x$
$$\frac{d}{dx} \tan x = \frac{\cos x \cdot \cos x - \sin x \cdot (-\sin x)}{\cos^2 x} = \frac{\cos^2 x + \sin^2 x}{\cos^2 x} = \sec^2 x$$

### $\cot x, \sec x, \csc x$
Similar using quotient rule or reciprocal rule:
- $(\cot x)' = -\csc^2 x$
- $(\sec x)' = \sec x \tan x$
- $(\csc x)' = -\csc x \cot x$

---

## 10. Inverse Trigonometric Derivatives

### $\sin^{-1} x$
Let $y = \sin^{-1} x \Rightarrow x = \sin y$, $-\pi/2 < y < \pi/2$
$$\frac{dx}{dy} = \cos y = \sqrt{1 - \sin^2 y} = \sqrt{1 - x^2}$$
$$\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{1}{dx/dy} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - x^2}} \quad (|x| < 1)$$

### $\cos^{-1} x$
$y = \cos^{-1} x \Rightarrow x = \cos y$, $0 < y < \pi$
$\frac{dx}{dy} = -\sin y = -\sqrt{1 - x^2}$
$\frac{dy}{dx} = -\frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - x^2}}$

### $\tan^{-1} x$
$y = \tan^{-1} x \Rightarrow x = \tan y$, $-\pi/2 < y < \pi/2$
$\frac{dx}{dy} = \sec^2 y = 1 + \tan^2 y = 1 + x^2$
$\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{1}{1 + x^2}$

---

## 11. Implicit Differentiation

Given $F(x,y) = 0$ defining $y$ implicitly as function of $x$:
$$\frac{d}{dx} F(x, y(x)) = \frac{\partial F}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial F}{\partial y} \frac{dy}{dx} = 0$$
$$\frac{dy}{dx} = -\frac{\partial F/\partial x}{\partial F/\partial y} \quad \text{(provided } \partial F/\partial y \neq 0\text{)}$$

**Proof:** By chain rule on composition $x \mapsto (x, y(x)) \mapsto F(x, y(x))$.

---

## 12. Parametric Differentiation

Given $x = f(t)$, $y = g(t)$ with $f'(t) \neq 0$:
$$\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{dy/dt}{dx/dt} = \frac{g'(t)}{f'(t)}$$

**Proof:** By chain rule: $\frac{dy}{dt} = \frac{dy}{dx} \cdot \frac{dx}{dt} \Rightarrow \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{dy/dt}{dx/dt}$.

### Second Derivative
$$\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} = \frac{d}{dx}\left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right) = \frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right) \cdot \frac{dt}{dx} = \frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right) \div \frac{dx}{dt}$$

**Explicit formula:**
$$\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} = \frac{f'(t)g''(t) - g'(t)f''(t)}{[f'(t)]^3}$$
**Proof:** $\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{g'}{f'}$. Differentiate w.r.t. $t$:
$$\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{g'}{f'}\right) = \frac{g''f' - g'f''}{(f')^2}$$
Divide by $f'$: $\frac{g''f' - g'f''}{(f')^3}$.

---

## 13. Logarithmic Differentiation

For $y = f(x) > 0$:
$$\ln y = \ln f(x) \Rightarrow \frac{1}{y} \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{f'(x)}{f(x)} \Rightarrow \frac{dy}{dx} = f(x) \frac{f'(x)}{f(x)} = f'(x)$$

For $y = [u(x)]^{v(x)}$:
$$\ln y = v(x) \ln u(x)$$
$$\frac{1}{y} y' = v'(x) \ln u(x) + v(x) \frac{u'(x)}{u(x)}$$
$$y' = y \left[ v'(x) \ln u(x) + v(x) \frac{u'(x)}{u(x)} \right]$$

---

## 14. Higher-Order Derivatives

### Leibniz Rule (General Product Rule)
$$(fg)^{(n)} = \sum_{k=0}^n \binom{n}{k} f^{(k)} g^{(n-k)}$$

**Proof by induction:**
- Base $n=1$: $(fg)' = f'g + fg'$ ✓
- Inductive step: Assume true for $n$. Then
  $$(fg)^{(n+1)} = \frac{d}{dx} \sum_{k=0}^n \binom{n}{k} f^{(k)} g^{(n-k)}$$
  $$= \sum_{k=0}^n \binom{n}{k} [f^{(k+1)} g^{(n-k)} + f^{(k)} g^{(n-k+1)}]$$
  $$= f^{(n+1)}g + \sum_{k=1}^n \left[\binom{n}{k} + \binom{n}{k-1}\right] f^{(k)} g^{(n+1-k)} + fg^{(n+1)}$$
  $$= \sum_{k=0}^{n+1} \binom{n+1}{k} f^{(k)} g^{(n+1-k)} \quad \text{(using Pascal's identity)}$$

## 15. Mean Value Theorem (MVT)

**Theorem:** If $f$ continuous on $[a,b]$ and differentiable on $(a,b)$, then $\exists c \in (a,b)$ such that
$$f'(c) = \frac{f(b) - f(a)}{b - a}$$

**Proof (Rolle's Theorem as lemma):**
Define $g(x) = f(x) - \frac{f(b)-f(a)}{b-a}(x-a) - f(a)$.
Then $g(a) = g(b) = 0$. $g$ satisfies Rolle's conditions, so $\exists c \in (a,b): g'(c) = 0$.
$$g'(x) = f'(x) - \frac{f(b)-f(a)}{b-a} \Rightarrow f'(c) = \frac{f(b)-f(a)}{b-a}$$

### Rolle's Theorem
If $f$ continuous on $[a,b]$, differentiable on $(a,b)$, and $f(a) = f(b)$, then $\exists c \in (a,b): f'(c) = 0$.

**Proof:** $f$ attains max/min on $[a,b]$ (EVT). If both at endpoints, $f$ constant, $f'=0$ everywhere. Otherwise, interior extremum $\Rightarrow f'(c)=0$ (Fermat's theorem).

### Cauchy Mean Value Theorem
If $f,g$ continuous on $[a,b]$, differentiable on $(a,b)$, $g'(x) \neq 0$, then $\exists c \in (a,b)$:
$$\frac{f'(c)}{g'(c)} = \frac{f(b)-f(a)}{g(b)-g(a)}$$

**Proof:** Apply Rolle's to $h(x) = f(x) - f(a) - \frac{f(b)-f(a)}{g(b)-g(a)}(g(x)-g(a))$.

---

## 16. L'Hôpital's Rule (Formal Proof)

**Theorem:** If $\lim_{x \to a} f(x) = \lim_{x \to a} g(x) = 0$ or $\pm\infty$, and $g'(x) \neq 0$ near $a$, and $\lim_{x \to a} \frac{f'(x)}{g'(x)}$ exists, then
$$\lim_{x \to a} \frac{f(x)}{g(x)} = \lim_{x \to a} \frac{f'(x)}{g'(x)}$$

**Proof for $0/0$ case (Cauchy MVT):**
For $x \neq a$ near $a$, apply Cauchy MVT on $[a,x]$ (or $[x,a]$):
$$\frac{f(x)-f(a)}{g(x)-g(a)} = \frac{f'(c)}{g'(c)} \quad (c \text{ between } a \text{ and } x)$$
Since $f(a)=g(a)=0$, $\frac{f(x)}{g(x)} = \frac{f'(c)}{g'(c)}$.
As $x \to a$, $c \to a$, so RHS $\to \lim_{c \to a} \frac{f'(c)}{g'(c)}$.

---

## 17. Taylor's Theorem (with Lagrange Remainder)

**Theorem:** If $f$ is $n+1$ times differentiable on interval containing $a$, then for any $x$:
$$f(x) = \sum_{k=0}^n \frac{f^{(k)}(a)}{k!}(x-a)^k + R_n(x)$$
where $R_n(x) = \frac{f^{(n+1)}(c)}{(n+1)!}(x-a)^{n+1}$ for some $c$ between $a$ and $x$.

**Proof:** Define $g(t) = f(t) - \sum_{k=0}^n \frac{f^{(k)}(t)}{k!}(x-t)^k - \frac{M}{(n+1)!}(x-t)^{n+1}$
Choose $M$ so $g(a) = 0$. Then $g(x) = 0$. By Rolle's, $g'(c_1)=0$, etc. Eventually $g^{(n+1)}(c)=0 \Rightarrow f^{(n+1)}(c) = M$.

**Maclaurin Series:** Special case $a=0$.
$$f(x) = \sum_{k=0}^n \frac{f^{(k)}(0)}{k!}x^k + \frac{f^{(n+1)}(c)}{(n+1)!}x^{n+1}$$

## 18. Differentiability of Inverse Functions

**Theorem:** If $f$ is strictly monotonic and differentiable on interval $I$, with $f'(x) \neq 0$, then $f^{-1}$ is differentiable on $f(I)$ and
$$(f^{-1})'(y) = \frac{1}{f'(f^{-1}(y))}$$

**Proof:** Let $y = f(x)$, $x = f^{-1}(y)$. For $\Delta y \neq 0$, let $\Delta x = f^{-1}(y+\Delta y) - f^{-1}(y)$.
$$\frac{f^{-1}(y+\Delta y) - f^{-1}(y)}{\Delta y} = \frac{\Delta x}{\Delta y} = \frac{1}{\Delta y / \Delta x}$$
As $\Delta y \to 0$, $\Delta x \to 0$ (by continuity of $f^{-1}$). Then $\frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x} \to f'(x)$.
Thus $\frac{\Delta x}{\Delta y} \to \frac{1}{f'(x)} = \frac{1}{f'(f^{-1}(y))}$.

## 19. Derivative of $|x|$

$|x| = \begin{cases} x & x \ge 0 \\ -x & x < 0 \end{cases}$

$$\frac{d}{dx}|x| = \begin{cases} 1 & x > 0 \\ -1 & x < 0 \end{cases}$$
Not differentiable at $x=0$ (left derivative $-1$, right derivative $1$).

**Alternative:** $|x| = \sqrt{x^2} \Rightarrow \frac{d}{dx}|x| = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{x^2}} \cdot 2x = \frac{x}{|x|} = \text{sgn}(x)$ for $x \neq 0$.

---

## 20. Derivative of $a^x$ (Alternative Proof)

$a^x = e^{x \ln a}$. By chain rule: $(e^{x \ln a})' = e^{x \ln a} \cdot \ln a = a^x \ln a$.

---

## 21. Derivative of $\log_a x$ (Alternative)

$\log_a x = \frac{\ln x}{\ln a} \Rightarrow (\log_a x)' = \frac{1}{x \ln a}$.

---

## 22. Inverse Hyperbolic Functions (Proofs)

### $\sinh^{-1} x$
$y = \sinh^{-1} x \Rightarrow x = \sinh y = \frac{e^y - e^{-y}}{2}$
$2x = e^y - e^{-y} \Rightarrow e^{2y} - 2x e^y - 1 = 0$
$e^y = x + \sqrt{x^2+1}$ (positive root)
$y = \ln(x + \sqrt{x^2+1})$
$\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{1}{x + \sqrt{x^2+1}} \cdot (1 + \frac{x}{\sqrt{x^2+1}}) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{x^2+1}}$

### $\cosh^{-1} x$ ($x \ge 1$)
$y = \cosh^{-1} x \Rightarrow x = \cosh y = \frac{e^y + e^{-y}}{2}$
$e^y = x + \sqrt{x^2-1} \Rightarrow y = \ln(x + \sqrt{x^2-1})$
$\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{x^2-1}}$

### $\tanh^{-1} x$ ($|x| < 1$)
$y = \tanh^{-1} x \Rightarrow x = \tanh y = \frac{e^y - e^{-y}}{e^y + e^{-y}}$
Solve: $x(e^y + e^{-y}) = e^y - e^{-y} \Rightarrow e^{2y} = \frac{1+x}{1-x} \Rightarrow y = \frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{1+x}{1-x}$
$\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{1-x}{1+x} \cdot \frac{2}{(1-x)^2} = \frac{1}{1-x^2}$

---

## 23. Higher-Order Derivatives of Specific Functions

### $e^{ax}$
$\frac{d^n}{dx^n} e^{ax} = a^n e^{ax}$

### $\sin(ax+b)$, $\cos(ax+b)$
$$\frac{d^n}{dx^n} \sin(ax+b) = a^n \sin\left(ax+b + \frac{n\pi}{2}\right)$$
$$\frac{d^n}{dx^n} \cos(ax+b) = a^n \cos\left(ax+b + \frac{n\pi}{2}\right)$$

### $\ln x$
$\frac{d^n}{dx^n} \ln x = (-1)^{n-1} \frac{(n-1)!}{x^n}$ for $n \ge 1$

### $x^m$
$\frac{d^n}{dx^n} x^m = m(m-1)\cdots(m-n+1) x^{m-n} = \frac{m!}{(m-n)!} x^{m-n}$ (for $n \le m$)
$= 0$ for $n > m$ (if $m \in \mathbb{N}$)
